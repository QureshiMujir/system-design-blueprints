Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

All numbers derived from the System Constraints table (§4.1).

1. Per-layer forward FLOPs — linear transform Formula: N × F_in × F_out multiplications. Derivation: default N=10⁵ nodes, F=10³ features, F'=16 hidden (from constraints table). 10⁵ × 10³ × 16 = 1.6 × 10⁹ FLOPs for the first GCN layer transform. For the 2-layer default: Layer 1 (F=1433→16): 2708 × 1433 × 16 ≈ 6.2 × 10⁷; Layer 2 (16→7): 2708 × 16 × 7 ≈ 3 × 10⁵. Cora is small; cost is dominated by Layer 1.

2. Per-layer propagation FLOPs — gather+segment_sum Formula: E × F reads + accumulates (gather is indexed copy; segment_sum is E additions into N bins). Derivation: Cora E=5429, F=16 (after transform): 5429 × 16 ≈ 8.7 × 10⁴ operations per propagation step. Ceiling graph: E=10⁶, F=10³: 10⁹ operations.

3. Activation memory per layer Formula: L × (N × F + E × F_msg) float64 arrays held for the reverse sweep. Derivation (§4.1 constraint "Memory"): L=2 layers, N=10⁵, F=10³: node features 2 × 10⁵ × 10³ × 8 bytes = 1.6 GB. At Cora scale: 2 × 2708 × 1433 × 8 ≈ 62 MB. The constraint table sets the ceiling N≤10⁵, F≤10³, giving 1.6 GB active tensors — fits on a 16 GB workstation.

4. Reverse-pass overhead Formula: ~3× forward eval (from constraints table "Gradient overhead ≤3× forward"). Derivation: forward cost (Cora Layer 1) ≈ 6.2×10⁷ FLOPs → reverse pass ≤ 3 × 6.2×10⁷ ≈ 1.9×10⁸ FLOPs per epoch. At 200 epochs: 3.7×10¹⁰ total FLOPs — sub-second on a modern CPU at ~10¹⁰ FLOP/s.

5. Neighbor sampling working set Formula: fanout[0] × fanout[1] × batch_size nodes touched per minibatch. Derivation (§4.1 "Neighbor sampling [25,10]"): 1 target node → 25 1-hop neighbors → each has 10 2-hop neighbors: 25 × 10 = 250 nodes per target. Batch of 512 targets: 512 × 250 = 128,000 node feature lookups = 128,000 × 1433 × 8 bytes ≈ 1.5 GB per minibatch. This is the practical memory bound for sampled training on Cora-sized graphs.

6. Attention FLOPs per GAT layer Formula: heads × E × (2 × F' + 1) for score computation + heads × E × F' for weighted message. Derivation (§4.1 "GAT default 8 heads × 8 hidden"): E=5429, heads=8, F'=8: score 8 × 5429 × 17 ≈ 7.4×10⁵; weighted msg 8 × 5429 × 8 ≈ 3.5×10⁵. Total: ~1.1×10⁶ FLOPs per GAT layer — ~10× more than a GCN layer at the same hidden dim.

7. segment_softmax memory Formula: 2 × E per segment pass (one for max subtraction, one for sum normalization) + E for output. Derivation: E=5429, float64: 3 × 5429 × 8 = 130 KB. Negligible. At E=10⁶: 24 MB — still fits in L3 cache.

8. Tape memory for one forward pass Formula: num_ops × (output_array_size + closure_overhead). Derivation: a 2-layer GCN forward records ~12 ops (matmul×2, gather×2, scatter×2, relu×1, dropout×1, log_softmax×1, CE×1, mask×1). Per-op overhead ~1 output array: 12 × N × F × 8 bytes. Cora: 12 × 2708 × 16 × 8 ≈ 4 MB. Ceiling: 12 × 10⁵ × 10³ × 8 = 9.6 GB — exceeds RAM; justifies the E≪N² sparse requirement (§4.1).

9. Checkpoint size Formula: Σ param_shapes × 8 bytes (float64 NPZ). Derivation: default 2-layer GCN: W1 (1433×16) + b1 (16) + W2 (16×7) + b2 (7) = 22,928 + 16 + 112 + 7 = 23,063 parameters × 8 bytes = ~185 KB. Even a 10-layer model with F=256 hidden stays under 10 MB — disk is not a constraint.

10. Dense adjacency memory (why we avoid it) Formula: N² × 8 bytes. Derivation: §4.1 ceiling N=10⁵: (10⁵)² × 8 = 80 GB. Comparison to sparse COO: E=10⁶ edges × 2 × 4 bytes (int32 edge_index) = 8 MB. Ratio: 80 GB / 8 MB = 10,000× — this single number justifies ADR-7 completely (P13).
