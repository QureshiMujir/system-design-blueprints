4. System Constraints

Single shard, reference hardware: 64 GB RAM, 8-core / 16-thread x86-64 (AVX2), NVMe SSD (≥2 GB/s sequential read), 10 Gbps NIC, Linux 5.15+ (io_uring available).

The one defining property: query latency is a memory-bandwidth-and-decode-throughput problem on the read path; indexing throughput is a sort-and-merge-amortization problem on the write path. Every codec, merge-policy, and concurrency decision in this document is negotiated against those two facts (§27 Performance Engineering elaborates).

33. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

All numbers derived from §4 (System Constraints); formula shown for each.

  • C1 — Postings-bytes at the single-shard ceiling. bytes = postings × (4B/compression_ratio + tf_bytes). At the §4 ceiling of 1B postings, compression ratio ≥6× (§4) gives doc-id cost ≤0.67 B; PFOR-packed tf adds ~1 B ⇒ ≤1.67 B/posting ⇒ ≤1.67 GB doc-id+tf bytes per shard (positions, when enabled, roughly double this for positional fields — §8.2's separate .pos stream is exactly why non-positional queries never pay it).
  • C2 — Term-dictionary memory (FST). bytes = terms × bytes_per_term_FST. M13/§4 budget ≤2.5 GB at 50M terms ⇒ ≤50 B/term average FST cost — consistent with ADR-02's claimed 5–10× reduction vs. a ~250–500 B flat sorted-string entry.
  • C3 — Indexing ingest bandwidth. bandwidth = docs/sec × avg_doc_bytes. M1's 100,000 docs/sec at the assumed 1 KB/doc (5 fields) ⇒ 100 MB/s raw text ingest — against the §4 NVMe sequential-write floor of ≥2 GB/s, this leaves a 20× margin for simultaneous WAL + segment writes.
  • C4 — Per-shard query-CPU ceiling. queries/core/sec = 1000 ms / cpu_ms_per_query. §4's ≤4 core-ms/query budget ⇒ 250 queries/core/sec; × 8 cores (§4 reference hardware) ⇒ ~2,000 q/s theoretical per-shard ceiling before contention — cross-checked against M4 (5,000 QPS ÷ 4 shards = 1,250/shard), leaving headroom for coordination overhead (§30).
  • C5 — Decode-bound query latency contribution. latency = bytes_decoded / decode_throughput. A term at 1% selectivity in a 1B-posting shard has df≈10M ⇒ ≈78,125 blocks; at M7's 70% BMW skip ratio, ≈23,437 blocks are actually decoded ⇒ ≈6 MB of doc-id+tf bytes; at M5's 5 GiB/s AVX2 decode ⇒ ≈1.1 ms — comfortably inside the §4 p50 budget of 8 ms, leaving room for intersection, scoring, and network.
  • C6 — WAL batch-fsync sizing. batch_bytes = (docs/sec × fsync_window_sec) × avg_record_bytes. At 100,000 docs/sec (M1) and the §18.6 5 ms group-commit window ⇒ 500 docs/batch; at ~200 B/record (analyzed tokens + doc-id) ⇒ ≈100 KB/batch — well inside NVMe's ≥2 GB/s sequential write floor, so fsync=batch (ADR default, §18.6) never becomes the bottleneck at this throughput.
  • C7 — FD budget vs. concurrently open segment sets. max_open_sets = fd_budget / files_per_segment. §4's 4,096-FD ceiling ÷ 8 files/segment (§8.3) ⇒ ≈512 concurrently open segment file-sets — comfortably above the tiered policy's steady-state segment count (§18.2's max_segments_per_tier = 10 × a handful of tiers).
  • C8 — Storage-amplification headroom. transient_overhead = live_bytes × (amplification_ceiling − 1). At §4's ≤1.3× ceiling, a 1 TB live corpus has a transient merge overhead ceiling of ≈300 GB — both must fit inside the §4 5 TB segment-bytes ceiling, leaving a ~3.5× growth margin before re-sharding (§30) becomes necessary.
