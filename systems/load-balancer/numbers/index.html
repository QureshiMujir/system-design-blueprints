35. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Every number below is derived from the §4 System Constraints table, formula shown.

  1. Total connection RAM at max concurrency. 500,000 conns × 4 KiB/idle-conn (§4) = 2,000,000 KiB ≈ 1.9 GiB — just under the 2 GiB RSS ceiling (§4), leaving ~100 MiB headroom for backend descriptors and route tables.
  2. FD budget check. 500,000 conns × 2 FDs/conn (client + upstream) = 1,000,000 FDs vs the 1,048,576 RLIMIT_NOFILE budget (§4) — leaves 48,576 FDs of headroom for listener sockets, health-check connections, and admin-API connections.
  3. Backend descriptor RAM at design ceiling. 10,000 backends × 4 KiB/backend (§4) = 40,000 KiB ≈ 39 MiB — negligible against the 1.9 GiB connection budget (#1).
  4. Route table RAM at design ceiling. 10,000 routes × 1 KiB/route (§4) = 10,000 KiB ≈ 9.8 MiB.
  5. L7 HTTP/1.1 bandwidth at target throughput. 100,000 req/s × 1 KiB response (§4) = 100,000 KiB/s ≈ 800 Mbps — comfortably under a 10 Gbps NIC (12.5× headroom).
  6. L4 TCP bandwidth at target throughput. 1,000,000 req/s × 1 KiB (§4) = 1,000,000 KiB/s ≈ 8 Gbps — saturates a 10 Gbps NIC almost entirely, which is exactly why the L4 path needs SO_REUSEPORT-sharded multi-core accept (§5 UNP ch. 7) rather than a single-core design.
  7. Maglev table-build cost at N=100 backends. M·N operations = 65,537 × 100 = 6,553,700; at ~1 ns/operation (a hash + a comparison) ≈ 6.5 ms (§15.6) — confirms the requirement (I13) that this never run on the request path.
  8. UDP receive-buffer sizing for the L4 UDP target. 1,500,000 pkt/s × 512 B avg (§4) ≈ 768 MB/s inbound; a 4 MiB SO_RCVBUF (§23.2) holds 4 MiB / 512 B ≈ 8,192 packets, i.e. 8,192 / 1,500,000 pkt/s ≈ 5.5 ms of absorption headroom against scheduling jitter — comfortably above a typical <1 ms scheduler tick, validating the 4 MiB choice.
  9. TLS handshake CPU budget under a reconnect storm. At 10,000 new conns/s, a full handshake (25 ms p99, §4) costs 10,000 × 25 ms = 250 CPU-seconds/s if fully serial — far beyond one core; with session resumption (5 ms p99, §4) the cost drops to 10,000 × 5 ms = 50 CPU-seconds/s, still requiring ~50 cores of parallel handshake capacity — this is why resumption (I11) is mandatory at scale, not merely an optimization.
  10. Availability improvement from active-passive failover. Single-node 99.9% (43.2 min/month, §4) vs a VRRP pair with < 3 s RTO (§4): a single failover event now costs 3 s / 2,592,000 s-per-month ≈ 0.00012% of a month — the practical availability ceiling moves to 99.99% (4.32 min/month, §4), bounded by node MTBF, not by RTO.
  11. Access-log storage rate at full sampling. 100,000 req/s × ~300 B/JSON-line × 1.2× amplification (§4) = 36,000,000 B/s = 36 MB/s ≈ 3.1 TB/day per node at 100% sampling — the reason per-route sampling down to 1% (§21.2) is a first-class config knob, not an afterthought.
  12. Cache-locality ceiling for the hot pick path. 10,000 backends × 4 KiB/backend (§4) = 39 MiB (#3) exceeds a typical 24 MiB shared L3 (§4) once other working-set data is counted — this validates keeping the filtered healthy-subset array (§15.7, typically hundreds of entries at realistic per-cluster backend counts) as the structure pick iterates, rather than scanning the full 10,000-entry backend array on every call.
