The one defining property: this system trades a fixed, small, per-operation CPU/memory cost (rows 1–8) for the ability to answer "did A happen-before B, or were they concurrent" correctly under partition, replay, and crash — a property no wall-clock-only system can give you without external bounded-error hardware (TrueTime, out of scope, §1).

32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Unpruned vector-clock heap at the documented ceiling. 50,000 actors × (16 B UUID + 8 B counter) × 1.6 BTreeMap overhead factor = 50,000 × 24 B × 1.6 ≈ 1.92 MB per clock value at the worst-case actor count (chained from §4 row 1's n=10,000 → 400 KB baseline: 10,000 × 24 B × 1.6 ≈ 384 KB ≈ 400 KB, confirming the multiplier).
  2. Frame throughput in frames/sec. A 100-actor vector-clock frame is 4 B header + 1 B varint count + 100 × (4 B actor_id + ~1–2 B varint counter) ≈ 505 B. At M11's ≥1 GB/s target: 1×10⁹ B/s ÷ 505 B/frame ≈ 1.98 M frames/sec sustainable encode/decode rate.
  3. RAM for 1,000,000 open HLC-tagged keys. 1,000,000 × 18 B (Hlc fixed width, §4) = 18 MB — the entire live-key HLC working set for a million-key shard.
  4. File-descriptor budget across a 100-shard node. 32 FDs/store (§4 row 12) × 100 shards = 3,200 FDs, comfortably under a typical 65,536 ulimit -n.
  5. Synchronous single-writer persistence ceiling. 1 ÷ 50 µs (M13 p50) = 20,000 writes/sec per unbatched synchronous writer — the number that forces batching (WriteBatch, ADR-07) once sustained write rate exceeds it.
  6. HLC cluster-size ceiling. node_id: u16 → 2¹⁶ = 65,536 maximum addressable nodes (§11.6 annotation) — a hard ceiling from the wire-format field width, not a soft estimate.
  7. Counter headroom at the 30-day soak rate. 1,000,000 ticks/s × 86,400 s/day × 30 days ≈ 2.59×10¹² ticks consumed of a u64's 2⁶⁴ ≈ 1.84×10¹⁹ capacity — ≈ 1.4×10⁻⁷ of the counter space, confirming ADR-08's "585 years at 1/ns" claim is conservative even under the Sealed-gate soak load.
  8. Heartbeat ingestion rate at the actor ceiling. With timeout T = 30 s and a heartbeat interval of T/10 = 3 s (standard failure-detector practice) across 50,000 actors: 50,000 ÷ 3 s ≈ 16,667 heartbeats/sec sustained ingestion the HeartbeatDetector (§20.2) must handle.
  9. Worst-case storage amplification per day (illustrative upper bound). 1,000,000 ticks/s × 86,400 s × 24 B (10-actor clock) × 3× amplification (§4 row 15) ≈ 6.22 TB/ day if every tick were persisted synchronously and unbatched — the number that makes the case for snapshotting/batching rather than per-tick fsync in any real deployment.
  10. DVV dot-set bound under realistic concurrency. For 10–100 concurrent writers to one key within one anti-entropy round: dot-set size ≈ 10–100 × 9 B (Dot encoding) = 90 B – 900 B — three to four orders of magnitude below the unpruned vector-clock ceiling (item 1), the quantified case for DVV's O(concurrent writers) bound over O(total actors) (§29).
