35. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Every number below is derived from the §4 System Constraints table, formula shown.

  1. Per-packet CPU budget = 1 / target_pps. At 1 Gbps with 1500-byte packets, target_pps = (1×10^9 / 8) / 1500 ≈ 83,333 pps → budget ≈ 12 µs/packet (§4 throughput row).
  2. Measured per-packet cost = UDP recv (1 µs) + parse (0.2 µs) + session lookup (0.1 µs) + replay check (0.05 µs, M11) + AEAD decrypt (3 µs, §13) + AllowedIPs lookup (0.2 µs, §17) + TUN write (1 µs) ≈ 5.55 µs — within the 12 µs budget by ≈ 2.2×, leaving headroom before the single-core target is the bottleneck (M3).
  3. Bandwidth at target throughput = 83,333 pps × 1,500 bytes ≈ 125 MB/s ≈ 1 Gbps (consistent with M3 by construction).
  4. Peer-state memory = 10,000 peers × ~500 bytes/peer (§11 Peer struct) ≈ 5 MB, well under the M7 100 MB ceiling.
  5. Replay-window memory = 2,048 bits / 8 = 256 bytes/session × up to 3 sessions/peer (§4) × 10,000 peers ≈ 7.68 MB.
  6. Trie memory = configured ranges × ~80 bytes/node (§17.10); a few-hundred-CIDR deployment ≈ tens of KB, negligible against M7.
  7. FD budget = 1 UDP socket + 1 TUN fd + ~4 epoll fds/tokio-worker-thread (§4); at 16 worker threads ≈ 66 FDs total, far under typical OS FD limits.
  8. Handshake CPU budget = target handshake rate × cost/handshake. At M6's 10,000/s and ~150 µs/handshake (§15 M1 dominant DH cost) → 1.5 s of CPU-time demand per wall-clock second — this is why M6's realistic sustained rate is bounded well below the burst capability, and why rekey churn (§20.7, ~83 handshakes/sec average at 10,000 peers) is a small fraction of this budget.
  9. Multi-core scaling = single-core throughput × cores, up to the lock-contention ceiling (ADR-08). 1 Gbps × 16 cores = 16 Gbps theoretical; M5's 10 Gbps target reflects ~63% scaling efficiency, the remainder lost to per-peer mutex contention and NIC/PCIe practical limits.
  10. Cookie-secret rotation overhead = one BLAKE2s-MAC key rotation every 120 s (§4) — negligible (< 1 µs, §19.7) against any packet-rate budget above.
