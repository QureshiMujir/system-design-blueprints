  4. How does the psychology-proxy cache avoid becoming a silent source of behavioral drift, and what test catches it if it does? (§11, §19, FM-9)
  5. Why must the ethics gate block dispatch mechanically rather than rely on scenario-author self-certification? (§7 I10, §22 T1–T5, MM6)

  7. Why is Mesa/NetLogo not simply "Polis without LLMs," and what does the LLM substrate cost in reproducibility guarantees that classical ABM never had to pay? (§31, §8.5)

30. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1. "An agent is a state machine wrapped around a stochastic policy; the event loop is its clock." Predicts: barrier-phase design is mandatory once policy calls are asynchronous/expensive. Bug class without it: agents racing across tick boundaries. Applies in §8.2 and §15.

MM2. "Reproducibility is a distributional claim whenever a sampler sits in the loop; only sampler-free layers can be bit-exact." Predicts: environment-resolution-only replay can be exact (ADR-16) while full-run replay cannot. Bug class without it: false bit-exact- replay claims. Applies in §8.5 and §25.

MM3. "A cache is correct only over an equivalence class you can name; naming the wrong equivalence class is silent data corruption, not a crash." Predicts: the psychology-proxy cache needs an explicit, testable equivalence definition. Bug class without it: the cache returns behaviorally-wrong actions with no error signal. Applies in §11 and §19.

MM4. "Emergent behavior is execution-order-sensitive; making the order explicit is cheaper than debugging its absence." Predicts: implicit-ordering systems (Mesa/NetLogo style) become undebuggable at scale. Bug class without it: irreproducible faction-formation results. Applies in §8.4 and §7 (I2/I3).

MM5. "Institutions are the aggregate memory of a population; agent memory alone cannot explain multi-generational drift." Predicts: cultural drift and political formation need environment-level (InstitutionLayer) state, not just per-agent memory. Bug class without it: modeling culture purely as agent memory, which cannot survive agent turnover. Applies in §20 and §21.

MM6. "Ethics gates are structural, not aspirational, only if they block dispatch mechanically." Predicts: a documentation-only gate is bypassed under deadline pressure. Bug class without it: FM-5. Applies in §7 (I10) and §22 (T1–T5).

MM7. "Cost is a first-class architectural input, not a post-launch optimization." Predicts: deferring cost consideration forces expensive later rewrites instead of designing it in from Phase 0. Bug class without it: an economically nonviable Phase 7. Applies in §4, §19, §28.

MM8. "A scenario card is a contract, not a log dump — it must be sufficient to re-derive the run, not merely describe it." Predicts: under-specified cards (missing a model pin or seed) look fine until the first fork attempt fails. Bug class without it: forks that silently diverge from their parent. Applies in §11 (Scenario) and §17.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Epstein, J. M. & Axtell, R., Growing Artificial Societies: Social Science from the Bottom Up, MIT Press, 1996. Extract: the "generative" standard (if you can't grow it, you don't understand it) and the Sugarscape agent/environment separation. Applied in §8 (Agent/Environment split) and Phase 1 (§13).
  • Schelling, T. C., Micromotives and Macrobehavior, Norton, 1978. Extract: aggregate emergent patterns from simple individual rules, and the danger of reading macro patterns as individual intent. Applied in Phase 8 (§20) and Design Principle P6.
  • Henrich, J., The WEIRDest People in the World, Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2020. Extract: cross-cultural psychological variation is structural, not noise around a universal baseline. Applied in PsychologyProfile.cultural_overlay (§11) and Phase 8 (§20).
  • Acemoglu, D. & Robinson, J. A., Why Nations Fail, Crown, 2012. Extract: extractive vs. inclusive institutions as the primary explanatory variable for long-run divergence. Applied in InstitutionLayer state transitions (§11) and Phase 9 (§21).
  • Tetlock, P., Expert Political Judgment: How Good Is It? How Can We Know?, Princeton University Press, 2005. Extract: calibration and resolution as the two axes of forecast quality, and the risk of unfalsifiable narrative. Applied in §26 (Testing & Verification, Reproducibility Rate framing) and the Anti-goals clause of §1.
  • Page, S. E., The Diversity Bonus: How Great Teams Pay Off in the Knowledge Economy, Princeton University Press, 2017. Extract: cognitive-diversity contribution to collective problem-solving, formalized. Applied in Phase 1 psychology-library design (§13) and Design Principle P1.
  • Kleppmann, M., Designing Data-Intensive Applications, O'Reilly, 2017. Extract: log-structured storage and the case for treating a system's event log as its source of truth. Applied in Event as source-of-truth (§11) and Phase 4 (§16).

Reference Implementations

  • vLLM (github.com/vllm-project/vllm) — study vllm/core/block_manager.py and the continuous-batching scheduler. Lesson: paged KV-cache reuse across concurrent requests with shared prefixes. Pitfall: do not adopt vLLM's request-arrival scheduler wholesale — it has no synchronous tick barrier, which Polis's DELIBERATE phase requires. Applied in Phase 3 (§15) and Phase 7, KV-Cache Hierarchy (§19).
  • Mesa (github.com/projectmesa/mesa) — study mesa/model.py and mesa/time.py. Lesson: clean scheduler/model-state separation, a template for the Orchestrator/ Environment split. Pitfall: Mesa's RandomActivation scheduler hides execution-order nondeterminism that Polis's determinism log must make explicit (I2, I3). Applied in §8 (Domain Model) and §31 (Comparative Architecture Table).
  • NetLogo (github.com/NetLogo/NetLogo) — study the turtle/patch execution-order semantics in the core engine. Lesson: emergent-behavior sensitivity to execution order is real and must be controlled. Pitfall: NetLogo's turtle-then-patch order is implicit; Polis makes tick-phase and per-agent order an explicit, replayable log entry. Applied in §8.4 (Event Total Ordering) and §7 (I2, I3).
  • Redis (github.com/redis/redis) — study src/evict.c (eviction policies). Lesson: eviction-policy design for the psychology-proxy cache; LFU-with-decay outperforms plain LRU under skewed hash distributions. Pitfall: naive LRU thrashes when a few "modal psychology" hashes dominate traffic. Applied in Phase 7 (§19) and ADR-06 (§9).
  • ClickHouse (github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse) — study the MergeTree engine family and its columnar compression codecs. Lesson: tick-windowed columnar aggregation is the right shape for the Emergent Metrics Bus sink. Pitfall: ClickHouse penalizes point lookups and small frequent inserts — batch inserts per tick, never per-event. Applied in Phase 4 (§16) and ADR-09 (§9).
  • Kafka (github.com/apache/kafka) — study core/src/main/scala/kafka/log (the partitioned append-only log). Lesson: the append-only, partition-ordered log is the correct structural model for the determinism log. Pitfall: exactly-once semantics require idempotent producers keyed on the full event tuple, not naive retry-on-timeout. Applied in §11 (Event), Phase 3 (§15), and ADR-12 (§9).

Glossary (§36) · 35 terms · site-wide glossary

Action Repertoire
The closed set of ActionTypes an agent may emit; enforced by I1.
Agent
A simulated entity with a declared psychology, memory, action repertoire, and communication protocol.
Agent-hour
One simulated agent advanced through one simulated hour; the unit of compute and of throughput targets (§28).
Barrier Synchronization
The explicit wait-for-all-shards checkpoint between tick phases (P9, I3).
Conformance Suite
The reference test suite an agent class, environment, or memory adapter must pass to ship or be admitted to the Template Store (I13).
Control Plane
The stateless service exposing the scenario API, ethics gate, and budget guard.
Cultural Overlay
The PsychologyProfile field pointing to a Culture record shaping values, beliefs, and drift dynamics (§20).
Cultural Drift
Norm transmission and value convergence/divergence over generations (Phase 8, §20).
Determinism Log
The append-only, totally-ordered event record that is the run's source of truth (§11, I2).
EmergentMetric
A reducer over the event stream, published live and stored durably.
Ethics Gate
The Control-Plane check that runs before a triggering scenario dispatches (I10).
Event
The atomic, totally-ordered unit of a Polis run.
Faction-Formation Reducer
The clustering reducer over PsychologyProfile.beliefs producing faction counts and membership (§21).
Fork
A new run derived from a parent scenario card via a JSONPatch diff.
Fork DAG
The acyclic lineage of scenario forks (I12).
Institution Layer
The environment subsystem modeling currencies, courts, schools, and emergent political assemblies (§21).
KV-Cache
The transformer key/value cache reused across batched inference requests (PagedAttention, §5).
Lens
The post-run analysis and replay surface.
Memory Adapter
The per-agent interface for episodic/semantic memory read, write, and consolidation (§11).
Modal Psychology
A small, fine-tuned model absorbing the bulk of routine agent ticks at low cost.
Perception Digest
The hashed perception payload used as half the psychology-proxy cache key (§11, P12).
Polis Studio
The composer and live-run cockpit web UI.
Proxy Cache (Psychology-Proxy Cache)
The (psychology_hash, perception_digest)-keyed cache short-circuiting behaviorally-equivalent LLM calls (P12, §19).
Psychology Profile
The structured object declaring an agent's traits, values, beliefs, and decision policy.
Reproducibility Rate (RR)
Fraction of published runs whose re-execution lands inside the declared confidence band.
Scenario Card
The serialized, hash-addressable, forkable artefact that fully specifies a run.
Shadow Society
A deferred continuously-running simulation tracking real-world conditions (§35).
Sharding
Partitioning agents across worker processes/nodes by agent_id mod n (ADR-07).
Sim-year
One simulated calendar year of agent activity.
Template Store
The marketplace of civilization templates, gated by conformance and the ethics gate (§21).
Tick
One five-phase simulation step (PERCEIVE, DELIBERATE, ACT, COMMUNICATE, WRITE).
Tick Phase
One of the five ordered stages within a tick (§8.2).
Time-Series Store
ClickHouse (metrics/events) plus TimescaleDB (relationship snapshots).
Total Ordering
The property that (scenario_id, run_id, tick, phase, agent_id, seq) totally orders all events in a run (P10).
Trust Boundary
The structural separation preventing the Environment from reading agent internals (P5, I6).
