In one paragraph

A sensor fusion engine turns noisy, disagreeing sensor readings into one best guess of where a vehicle is, how fast it moves and which way it points. This design is a Rust library, plus a std runner called sfctl, that fuses an inertial unit (accelerometer and gyroscope) with GPS, wheel odometry, a barometer and a magnetometer (§1.1, §1.4). It tracks a 15-number error state and targets a predict step of at most 50 µs on a Cortex-M4F microcontroller, with no heap allocation in the real-time loop (§4). Its defining choice is an error-state Kalman filter as the core, with every measurement checked for plausibility before it can touch the uncertainty estimate (ADR-7, P4). Robotics and navigation engineers embed it (§1.5).

The problem, concretely

You have a vehicle with an inertial unit that reports at 100 Hz, typically, and up to 1000 Hz, and a GPS that reports at 1 to 20 Hz (§4), and you want one pose and velocity estimate at inertial rate (§1.1). Each naive answer breaks in a different way.

Trust the inertial unit alone: integrate the gyro to get orientation, rotate the accelerometer reading into the world frame, subtract gravity, integrate twice for position (§8.3). Sensor biases get integrated too, so an inertial-only estimate drifts (§17.3, §17.5). A parked vehicle that should show zero velocity shows a nonzero residual, and that residual is the accelerometer bias (§8.8, MM8). Without outside correction the uncertainty only grows (§8.10).

Trust GPS alone: it reports only 1 to 20 times a second (§4), it is mounted away from the inertial unit so it measures a point that moves when the vehicle rotates (the lever arm, §8.4, §8.7), and a delayed fix applied "now" instead of at its true time creates an error correlated with how fast the vehicle is moving (MM6). Add a spoofed or glitched sample and, with no gate, one outlier can collapse the covariance, which is the filter's own record of how sure it is (P4).

Blend them carelessly and the maths bites back. Filtering the four numbers of an orientation quaternion directly gives a rank-deficient covariance (P3). Letting the covariance lose symmetry or positive definiteness produces negative variances, then a NaN gain, then divergence (P1). Mixing north-east-down with east-north-up, or two quaternion conventions, flips signs silently (P5, I8).

How it works, end to end

Follow one inertial sample and one late GPS fix through the boxes of §3.1. The drawing below is redrawn from that diagram with the same boxes, arrows and labels, coloured by the four boundaries of §3.3.

  1. Drivers are external. They emit raw, device-unit, device-frame samples with a device timestamp behind SensorSource::poll() -> Option<RawSample>, and all of it is untrusted input (§3.2).
  2. sf-sensor converts a raw sample into a canonical Measurement: SI units, a documented frame, its noise covariance R, and a core Timestamp. It runs on the non-real-time ingest path and takes new measurements through submit(Measurement) (§3.2).
  3. sf-sync holds a fixed-capacity ring called MeasurementBuffer, ordered by timestamp. It absorbs out-of-order and delayed arrivals. When it is full it drops the oldest unapplied item and counts the event (§3.2). The real-time core drains it in order (§3.1).
  4. sf-imu is the predict step: integrate the gyro, rotate the specific force into the navigation frame, remove gravity, integrate to velocity and position, and build the discrete F and Q. It is the only step allowed to run with no aiding measurement (§3.2).
  5. sf-gate computes the innovation y and its covariance S before any update, evaluates a chi-squared test on d² = yᵀS⁻¹y, and accepts, rejects or defers. It also runs the NIS/NEES consistency monitors, divergence detection and adaptive noise, and can veto a measurement (§3.2).
  6. sf-filter holds the Filter trait and the KF, EKF, ESKF and UKF cores, stateless with respect to sensors: (F, Q) come from sf-imu, (h, H, R, z) from sensor models (§3.2).
  7. sf-state owns the authoritative StateVector, Covariance and ErrorState, enforces "quaternion normalised, P symmetric positive definite", and lets only the real-time thread write. Readers get an atomically published double-buffered snapshot (§3.2).
  8. Estimate API publishes position, velocity, attitude, bias estimates, covariance and health flags as a lock-free snapshot for a consumer such as a controller (§3.2).
  9. sf-replay logs inputs and outputs to a versioned binary log, replays them bit-deterministically and scores RMSE, ATE and RPE. sf-calib estimates sensor intrinsics, magnetometer iron effects, extrinsics and time offsets (§3.2). sf-cli (sfctl) and sf-bench complete the 12 crates (§1.6).

Four boundaries organise it: real-time predict, gated update and publish; non-real-time ingest, buffering, calibration and logging; a trust boundary at sf-gate; only sf-replay persists (§3.3).

The core ideas

Strapdown prediction: integrate the inertial unit forward (§8.3, §17)

State: position p, velocity v, attitude quaternion q (body to navigation frame, Hamilton convention), gyro bias b_g and accelerometer bias b_a, 16 scalars in all (§8.1, §1.1). The continuous model is ṗ = v, v̇ = R(q)a + g, q̇ = ½ q ⊗ [0, ω], and the two biases are random walks (§8.3). Three steps per sample (§8.3, §17.2):

  1. Correct the readings: ω = ω_m − b_g, a = a_m − b_a (noise terms are modelled separately, §8.3).
  2. Turn: q_{k+1} = q_k ⊗ Exp(ω Δt), so attitude is updated by a small rotation (§8.3).
  3. Move: v ← v + (R·a + g) Δt, then p ← p + v Δt + ½(…)Δt² with trapezoidal velocity (§17.2).

In parallel the covariance grows: P⁻ = F P Fᵀ + Q, where F ≈ I + F_c Δt + ½(F_c Δt)² and Q comes from the Van Loan method or a trapezoid rule, using noise densities from the datasheet (§8.3, §17.2). A timing spike is capped and Q is raised and flagged, because an uncapped Δt over-integrates attitude (§8.11). The stationary test is the sanity check: a still vehicle should have about zero velocity, and any residual reveals b_a (§8.8, I12).

The error state: filter the small mistake, not the big number (§8.2, §8.5, §19.2, MM2)

The filter keeps a full-precision nominal state x_nom and a small 15-number error δx = [δp, δv, δθ, δb_g, δb_a], where the attitude error δθ is a 3-vector rotation, not a 4-number quaternion (§8.2, P3). That is the error-state Kalman filter (ESKF). A step has four moves (§8.5, §19.2):

Step What happens
predict integrate x_nom; propagate only the error covariance, P⁻ = F P⁺ Fᵀ + Q; error mean stays 0
update y = z − h(x_nom), S = H P⁻ Hᵀ + R, K = P⁻ Hᵀ S⁻¹, δx⁺ = K y
inject x_nom ← x_nom ⊞ δx⁺: position, velocity, biases add; q ← q ⊗ Exp(δθ), then renormalise
reset δx ← 0 and P ← J P Jᵀ, with J = blkdiag(I, I, I − ½[δθ]×, I, I)

Exp(φ) = [cos(‖φ‖/2), sin(‖φ‖/2) φ/‖φ‖], which for a small angle is about [1, ½φ] (§8.6). The error stays small, so one linearisation stays valid, and filtering happens in flat space with the quaternion sphere used only at inject and reset (MM2). Skipping the reset gives a covariance that no longer matches the relinearised state: the symptom is NEES below the expected value, then a blow-up (§19.6). It is called the classic bug site of the method (§19.9).

The update: Joseph form and Cholesky, never a plain inverse (§8.5, MM5, MM7, ADR-29)

For every accepted measurement: S = H P⁻ Hᵀ + R, K = P⁻ Hᵀ S⁻¹, and the covariance is updated in Joseph form, P⁺ = (I − KH) P⁻ (I − KH)ᵀ + K R Kᵀ (§8.5). The plain (I − KH)P is algebraically the same but accumulates rounding error until variances go negative; Joseph form preserves symmetry and positive definiteness by construction (MM5). It costs about twice as much, which the blueprint accepts (P1). S⁻¹ is applied by a Cholesky solve; a Cholesky that fails is an early warning on P or S, answered by regularising and skipping, not by propagating NaN (§8.5, MM7, I9).

Tiny example, §8.8's one-dimensional vertical case: state [p_D, v_D, b_a], initial P = diag(1, 1, 0.01), a barometer reading z = 0 m with R = 0.04. The update is y = z − p_D, S = P_pp + R, K = P_pp / S, then Joseph P⁺ (§8.8). The gain tends to 1 as R → 0 and to 0 as R → ∞, the two limits Phase 4 tests (§16.5).

Each sensor supplies (h, H, R) as a unit testable alone (§8.4, P12). GPS position, for example, predicts p + R(q) l_gps, where l is the lever arm from the inertial unit to the antenna, so its H has an attitude block coupling rotation to position. The blueprint gives that block two signs: R(q)[l]_× in the §8.4 table, ∂(R(q)l)/∂δθ = −R(q)[l]_× in §18.2 and Hard Question 3, and a wrong sign is a named pitfall (§18.6). Every H must pass a central finite-difference check (§18.2, §8.5).

The gate: ask "is this surprising enough to be an error?" (§21, MM4, I5, I10, §8.10)

The innovation y = z − h(x⁻) is everything new a measurement says, and S is how big a surprise to expect. The Mahalanobis distance d² = yᵀS⁻¹y divides one by the other and should follow a chi-squared distribution when the model is right (MM4). sf-gate compares d² to a threshold per degrees of freedom at a configurable confidence such as 0.99 and returns GateResult{accepted, d2, threshold, dof} (§21.2). The acceptance test injects 10×σ GPS spikes and requires false-accept and false-reject rates under 1% at the 0.99 gate (§21.4). Gating reuses the S factorisation the update already needs, so it is nearly free (§21.7).

Two monitors watch the filter over time. NIS (normalised innovation squared) is averaged over a rolling window and should sit near the measurement's degrees of freedom (§21.2, I10). NEES uses ground truth and runs only in tests, in Monte-Carlo batches (§21.2). When NIS stays high the state machine moves Running → Degraded (noise inflated, filter keeps estimating); on detected divergence it moves to Reinit; after a sensor dropout longer than a threshold it moves to DeadReckon (§8.10). A real manoeuvre is told apart from an outlier by persistence (§21.8). A gate too tight starves the filter; one too loose lets covariance collapse (§21.6).

Late measurements: rewind, apply at the true time, replay forward (§8.9, §22.2, MM6)

A GPS fix stamped t0 arrives two inertial ticks late; the core never moves time backwards (I4, P11), so sf-sync handles it (§8.9):

  1. The fix's timestamp is earlier than now, so it takes the out-of-sequence measurement (OOSM) path.
  2. Clone the state stored at t0 from a bounded state history, gate and apply the update there.
  3. Re-propagate through the stored inertial samples to now (§8.9, §22.2).

That is buffer-replay. The alternative is the Larsen/Alspach approximation, a cheaper delayed-update formula; the design chooses replay up to a bounded delay and the approximation beyond it (§22.2). With 200 ms of history at 100 Hz the depth is 20 snapshots of about 1,944 B each (C4), so a replay re-runs at most 20 predicts (M11). The rewind is the tail-latency risk, so its depth is bounded (§22.7, Performance Engineering).

Other back-ends: UKF and the complementary filter (§8.5, §20, C9, ADR-8)

The unscented Kalman filter (UKF) needs no derivatives (§20.1): it pushes 2n+1 sample points (sigma points) through the nonlinear model, with λ = α²(n + κ) − n and weights built from it (§8.5). The UKF update budget is written for 2n+1 = 31 sigma points (§4). The capacity model shows the price: running each of the 31 through the full mechanization is about 209,250 flops, roughly 1.25 ms on the M4F, above the 50 µs predict budget, so C9 says to restrict the UKF to the update step or batch the sigma points with SIMD (C9, §20.6).

The complementary filter is the cheap baseline: q_{k+1} = q_k ⊗ Exp((ω + k_p e) Δt), where e is the gap between measured and predicted gravity or magnetic direction. It has no covariance and is O(1) (§8.5). The blueprint disagrees with itself on two points:

  • Which algorithm. §8.5 labels this equation Mahony's. §16.1 calls it "Mahony/Madgwick-style". The comparison and Alt-3 name xioTechnologies Fusion (Madgwick), a gradient-descent filter, as the baseline used here (Comparative Architecture Table, Alt-3).
  • What it falls back for. §16.2, the comparison and Alt-3 make it the DeadReckon fallback. But §8.10 and the sensor-dropout row define DeadReckon as predict-only with growing P. §16.10 makes it the fallback when the ESKF enters Reinit (§8.10, §16.2, §16.10).

All of these sit behind one Filter trait, swappable at construction and comparable on one dataset (§1.1, ADR-8).

The trade-offs — where engineers disagree

Core filter: error-state KF (ADR-7)

Chosen: ESKF, for minimal attitude degrees of freedom, a valid linearisation, and production use (§9). Rejected: a full-state EKF and a particle filter. When the rejected choice is right: a full-state EKF when attitude uncertainty is always small (an attitude-only unit with no large initial errors); a particle filter when the belief is strongly multimodal, as in an urban canyon or a kidnapped robot (§9).

Linear algebra: an in-house fixed-size matrix type (ADR-2)

Chosen: an own Matrix<R,C> with const-generic sizes, for no_std, zero allocation, compile-time dimensions and control over float determinism (§9, P13). Rejected: nalgebra, ndarray, Eigen through FFI. When the rejected choice is right: nalgebra when the state size is dynamic (a sliding-window filter with a variable number of clones); Eigen through FFI when integrating with a Python or C++ toolchain for offline batch optimisation (§9).

Floating-point contraction: off by default (ADR-4)

Chosen: fused multiply-add contraction disabled unless a target feature is set explicitly, for reproducible replay (§9, I7). Rejected: allowing the compiler to contract. When the rejected choice is right: when bit-exact replay across different CPUs is not required and throughput dominates, such as offline batch processing (§9).

Attitude representation: Hamilton quaternion plus a 3-DOF error (ADR-5)

Chosen: one Hamilton convention, scalar first, single-sourced in sf-math, with the error state avoiding the norm constraint (§9, §8.6). Rejected: JPL quaternions, Euler angles, a rotation matrix alone. When the rejected choice is right: JPL, in aerospace software that already uses it throughout, to avoid sign-flip adapter bugs (§9).

Observability handling: first-estimates Jacobians, optional (ADR-30)

Chosen: an optional first-estimates Jacobians (FEJ) mode in the EKF and ESKF, which evaluates Jacobians at the first estimate so linearisation drift cannot invent information about unobservable directions (§9, MM3). Rejected: no such handling. When the rejected choice is right: when the sensor suite observes every state all the time, for example an inertial unit plus full 6-DOF motion capture, so linearisation-point drift is negligible (§9).

Roads not taken (Alternative Architectures, Deferred Features Register)

Factor graph or batch smoother (Alt-1): estimate the whole trajectory at once from preintegrated inertial, GPS and landmark factors. It wins for post-processing and offline smoothing, such as underwater or aerial survey, where loop closure is mandatory and hard real-time is not. It is rejected here because it reads future measurements, needs a heap and a dynamic graph, and cannot run at inertial rate; the ESKF estimate can feed any offline smoother.

Particle filter (Alt-2): keep many weighted samples of the state. It wins for non-Gaussian, multimodal beliefs such as urban-canyon GPS or kidnapped-robot recovery, and needs no Jacobian. It is rejected because 500 to 10,000 particles for 15 degrees of freedom cost roughly ten times the UKF, with no bounded worst case, and the target microcontroller cannot host it.

Attitude-only complementary filter (Alt-3): blend integrated gyro with an accelerometer and magnetometer tilt estimate. It wins for a gimbal or VR headset, under 2 KB of memory and a 30-line implementation. It is rejected as the core because it has no position, velocity or covariance, but it is kept as the Phase 4 baseline.

Tightly coupled INS/GNSS (Alt-4): feed raw satellite ranges and Doppler to the filter. It wins for precision work such as surveying or autonomous landing, where a loosely coupled solution is not good enough. It is rejected because raw ranges are not available from the documented receiver interface, per-satellite states raise the dimension to about 27 or more, and the geometry and ionosphere modelling add an order of magnitude of work.

Deferred (Deferred Features Register): visual-inertial MSCKF, SIMD-batched sigma points, PTP/PPS sync, a fixed-lag smoother, RTK, thermal compensation and online extrinsics, each with an un-defer trigger.

What must never be false

  • I1: ‖q‖ = 1 ± 1e-6, checked by a norm test after every inject or Exp (quat_norm_err, §7).
  • I2, I3: P is symmetric and positive definite. Noticed by max|P − Pᵀ| after every update (cov_asymmetry) and by a failing Cholesky probe (cov_not_pd_total) (§7).
  • I4: core time never decreases; the predict guard catches dt < 0 (time_regression_total, §7).
  • I5: every accepted measurement passed the chi-squared gate. A gate-bypass assertion fires; gated_total and accepted_total are the counters (§7).
  • I6: biases stay within physical bounds; a breach clamps and flags (bias_clamp_total, §7).
  • I7: output is deterministic given inputs; a replay-hash mismatch is the alarm (replay_hash, §7).
  • I8: no NED/ENU or Hamilton/JPL mixing. Caught by the type system, Frame tags and unit or property tests, with no run-time telemetry (§7).
  • I9: the innovation covariance S is positive definite; a failed Cholesky flags it (S_not_pd_total, §7).
  • I10: NIS stays within chi-squared bounds over a rolling window. Noticed by the window statistic; nis_violation_ratio (§7).
  • I11: no heap allocation on the real-time path; an allocation-counter test needs rt_alloc_total = 0 (§7).
  • I12: gravity and lever-arm signs are fixed per frame. A static-vehicle bias test catches a wrong sign, with no telemetry listed (§7).

What breaks in production

Rows below are from Failure Modes and Recovery; the blueprint gives them class names, not numbers.

  • Filter divergence. Symptom: estimates diverge with huge residuals. Causes: a bad Jacobian, a too-small Q, an unmodelled bias, or outliers. Detected by a sustained NIS breach or covariance trend. Recovery: the gate and adaptive noise, then the Reinit state automatically; reset and re-tune Q/R by hand (Failure Modes and Recovery).
  • Covariance collapse. Symptom: the filter is overconfident and rejects valid measurements. Causes: an earlier gate too loose, R too small, or a missing reset Jacobian. Detected as low NIS followed by high attitude error and a rising reject rate. Recovery: inflate P when its trace falls below a floor, and use FEJ; manually reinitialise the covariance block (Failure Modes and Recovery, §19.6).
  • Observability loss. Symptom: yaw or scale drifts silently. Causes: stationary without a heading source, or planar motion. Detected by Gramian rank or a variance-floor breach. Recovery: hold the variance and use FEJ automatically; add an aiding sensor or move manually (Failure Modes and Recovery).
  • Sensor dropout. Symptom: aiding stops and P grows. Detected as no measurements for longer than T. Recovery: the predict-only DeadReckon state (Failure Modes and Recovery, §8.10).
  • Calibration or stale parameters. Symptom: a constant bias, a yaw offset, or motion-correlated error. Causes: a wrong lever arm or extrinsics, a stale parameter set, declination drift, or double bias correction. Detected by a post-calibration stationary residual, a yaw-versus-GPS-course check, or a param_set_version mismatch. Recovery: flag low observability and reject anomalous calibrations; re-calibrate or roll back the parameter set (Failure Modes and Recovery).
  • OOSM history overflow. Symptom: a delayed measurement is applied approximately, with silent accuracy loss. Cause: delay exceeding the bounded history depth. Detected by history_overflow_total and delay-versus-depth telemetry. Recovery: fall back to the Larsen approximation and flag it; size the history to the largest delay (Failure Modes and Recovery, §22.5, §22.6).

Threats a reader should know (Threat Model): spoofed GPS, a fake fix to mislead the estimate, met by the chi-squared gate, an IMU dead-reckoning cross-check and multi-sensor consistency, and detected as sustained high GPS innovation (gated_total, gps_innovation). Replayed GPS, an old valid fix sent again, met by timestamp monotonicity and a freshness window. Filter-divergence denial of service, forcing expensive rewinds, met by bounding history depth and rewind frequency (oosm_rewind_depth). Time spoofing, manipulating timestamps to reorder or skew, met by the monotonic core clock (I4) and ingest discontinuity detection. Malformed measurement, NaN or garbage fields, met by strict parsing and range validation that never propagates a NaN (parse_error_total). The trust boundary is the gate plus the monotonic-time core; the library exposes no network surface (Threat Model, P4).

How it compares

  • PX4 EKF2 chose a full-state EKF with a richer state (wind, terrain altitude) and hand-derived Jacobians. That is fine in autopilot firmware where deep hardware integration (sensor fault flags, airspeed fusion) outweighs portability, though yaw observability is a known maintenance burden (Comparative Architecture Table).
  • robot_localization chose a configurable mapping from ROS topics to state elements, so any sensor wires up with no code. That suits quick prototyping in ENU ground-robot stacks, at the cost of no_std use, delayed-measurement handling and an error-state treatment of attitude (Comparative Architecture Table).
  • OpenVINS chose a multi-state-constraint filter (MSCKF) that keeps camera-pose clones in a sliding window for tightly coupled vision. It wins for GPS-denied visual-inertial accuracy and was rejected here because it needs a visual front end, ROS, Eigen and a heap (Comparative Architecture Table, §1.3).
  • xioTechnologies Fusion (Madgwick) chose gradient descent with no covariance: a small AHRS that fits under 1 KB of RAM on a Cortex-M0 and needs no Q/R tuning. It gives no position or velocity and no outlier rejection or consistency monitoring, which is why it is only a baseline and fallback here (Comparative Architecture Table, Alt-3).

Questions to test yourself

  1. Why does the ESKF need a reset Jacobian after inject, and what failure mode appears when it is omitted? — Hint: §19.2 gives the equation and §19.6 and §19.9 give the symptom.
  2. Derive the memory ceiling for running ESKF at 15-DOF with 200 ms OOSM history on Cortex-M4F, showing each term. — Hint: §4 and the capacity rows C1 to C5.
  3. Walk through the state-machine transitions that occur when a GPS spoofing attack drives d² > threshold for 20 consecutive updates, and identify every telemetry counter that fires. — Hint: §8.10, the Threat Model row for spoofed GPS, and §21.2.
  4. Compare the OOSM buffer-replay approach to the Larsen/Alspach approximation in terms of computational cost, accuracy loss, and the condition under which each is chosen. — Hint: §22.2 and §22.7.
  5. A robot runs the ESKF for 2 h on a flat floor (planar motion). Explain exactly which state(s) will have variance not shrinking, why, and what sensor or excitation would restore observability. — Hint: §8.11 and §21.2 (the observability Gramian), with the heading rows of §8.4.

If you only remember three things

The estimate is a belief with an honest uncertainty, so every step exists to keep that uncertainty symmetric, positive definite and consistent with reality (MM1, P1, I2, I3). Filter the small error about an integrated nominal state, not the quaternion itself, and remember the reset that follows every inject (MM2, ADR-7, §19.2). Time is an input too: gate every measurement before it touches the covariance, and apply late ones at their true timestamp (P4, MM6, §22.2).

Words used here

  • Strapdown mechanization: integrating a body-mounted inertial unit forward to get orientation, velocity and position.
  • Innovation: the gap between a measurement and what the filter predicted it would be.
  • Innovation covariance S: how large that gap is expected to be, H P Hᵀ + R.
  • Mahalanobis distance: the innovation measured in units of its expected size, yᵀS⁻¹y; the gate's test statistic.
  • NIS / NEES: normalised innovation squared (online) and normalised estimation error squared (tests with ground truth); both should land near their degrees of freedom.
  • ESKF: a Kalman filter on the small error around an integrated nominal state.
  • Joseph form: a covariance update arranged so it cannot lose symmetry or positive definiteness.
  • SPD: symmetric positive definite, the shape every covariance must have.
  • Lever arm: the offset of a sensor from the inertial unit, in the body frame.
  • OOSM: out-of-sequence measurement, one that arrives after later-stamped data.
  • FEJ: first-estimates Jacobians, evaluated at the first estimate so no false information appears in unobservable directions.
  • NED / ENU: north-east-down and east-north-up local navigation frames.
