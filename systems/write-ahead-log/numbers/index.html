32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Record overhead per update (physiological, small payload): 32 B header (§4) + ~40 B typical payload (page id, offset, before/after lengths, small before/after images) + 4 B CRC = ≈ 76 B/update record — well under the 8 KiB ceiling.
  2. Records per transaction (typical OLTP txn, 5 updates): 1 Begin + 5 Update + 1 Commit = 7 records × ≈76 B avg = ≈ 532 B/txn logged.
  3. Segment lifetime at steady 200 MB/s (§4 M3) sustained write: 16 MiB segment ÷ 200 MB/s = ≈ 84 ms/segment at peak throughput — informs how aggressively retention (§21) must track replica acks under sustained load.
  4. Checkpoint interval in records, at 64 MiB trigger (§4) and ≈76 B/record: 64 MiB ÷ 76 B ≈ ≈ 883,000 records between checkpoints at steady small-record load — directly bounds Analysis-pass scan length (§29 bottleneck #1).
  5. Recovery time for a full checkpoint interval, at M6's 150 MB/s scan floor: 64 MiB ÷ 150 MB/s ≈ ≈ 448 ms Analysis+Redo scan time — well under §4's RTO budget (5 s/GiB), confirming the checkpoint interval is conservatively sized.
  6. Group-commit amortization at 50 concurrent committers (§15.4): worst case 1 force() per 2 ms window regardless of committer count ⇒ at 500 commits/sec aggregate demand, ≤ 1 force() per ≥ 40 requests/force() — exceeds M9's ≤1-per-10 target with margin.
  7. Dirty Page Table memory footprint at §4's 100,000-page ceiling: 100,000 × (8 B page_id + 8 B rec_lsn) = 1.6 MB — negligible relative to any modern process's memory budget.
  8. Transaction Table memory footprint at §4's 10,000-txn ceiling: 10,000 × (8 B txn_id + 1 B status + 8 B last_lsn + 9 B Option<Lsn>) ≈ ≈ 260 KB — similarly negligible.
  9. Undo work for a single large loser transaction (10,000 updates): 10,000 CLRs × ≈76 B/record ≈ ≈ 760 KB of additional log volume generated purely by rolling back one transaction — the concrete cost P3's idempotency guarantee is paid for.
  10. Replica catch-up time after a 5-segment (80 MiB, §4's lag budget) gap, at M11's healthy-network streaming rate: assuming streaming throughput matches local append throughput (200 MB/s, M3) once caught up to steady state, 80 MiB ÷ 200 MB/s ≈ ≈ 400 ms to fully catch up from the maximum tolerated lag — well inside a typical retention-gate patience window.
  11. Segments on disk at steady state with a 5-segment replica-lag budget (§4): 1 active + up to 5 pending-replica-ack + 1 pending-checkpoint ≈ ≈ 7 segments × 16 MiB = 112 MiB typical disk footprint before older segments become eligible for deletion (P12).
  12. CRC compute cost at M3's 200 MB/s target, illustrative CRC-32C throughput ≈2 GB/s per core (hardware-accelerated): 200 MB/s ÷ 2 GB/s ≈ 10% of one core dedicated to checksumming at peak throughput — informs M5's ≤5%-of-append-cost target as achievable with hardware CRC instructions, tighter without them.
