Hard questions (§37)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does re-bencoding a parsed .torrent risk producing the wrong infohash, and which two sections jointly explain the fix? (§8 domain model, P3/§14 Phase 2)
  2. Why is the DHT bucket-split rule "only the bucket containing our own ID splits" rather than "split any full bucket," and what breaks under the naive rule? (§20 Phase 8, §7 I12)
  3. Why must the choke tick fire at exactly 10,000 ms rather than "approximately" that cadence, and what invariant enforces it? (§6 P4, §7 I4, §18 Phase 6)
  4. Why is LEDBAT's throughput degradation under competing TCP traffic correct behavior rather than a bug? (§22 Phase 10 pitfalls, MM6, RFC 6817 in §5)
  5. Why does the Transport trait make µTP, TCP, and WebRTC interchangeable to the picker and choke algorithm, and where would that break if transport were an enum instead? (§6 P9, §7 I7, §34 MM5)
  6. Why can BitTorrent v2's merkle verification isolate a failure to a single 16 KiB block while v1 can only isolate to a whole piece — and where in the document is this traded off against v1's simplicity? (§24 Phase 12, §9 ADR-04)
  7. Why is the DHT treated as a hint source rather than a trust source, and which two sections establish the mechanism that makes that safe? (§26 Threat 5, §34 MM3)
  8. Why does endgame mode trigger at 95% completion and not, say, 50%, and what would break at an earlier threshold? (§18 Phase 6, MM7, §32)
  9. Why is private-torrent quarantine enforced at exactly one checkpoint rather than inside each subsystem (PEX, LSD, DHT) individually? (§6 P12, §7 I10, §19 Phase 7)
  10. Why does the resume database only need to store bitfields and partial-piece state, not the payload bytes themselves — and what invariant guarantees this is sufficient after a crash? (I11, §17 Phase 5, §28)
  11. Why does this design choose horizontal (per-host) scaling with zero cross-instance coordination rather than sharding torrents across a coordinated cluster? (§33, §38 Alternative 1 contrast)
  12. Why is Cohen's tit-for-tat choking algorithm still the locked choice despite Piatek 2007's documented critique that it is exploitable? (§5 Papers, §18 Phase 6, §9 ADR discussion implicit in P4)
  13. Under what condition does the rejected alternative in ADR-03 (serde_bencode) actually win, and why doesn't that condition apply to this project? (§9 ADR-03, §1 Goal & Scope)
  14. Why does BEP 52's hash-exchange ride on base-protocol opcodes 21–23 rather than the BEP-10 extension protocol, and what interop failure would result from getting this wrong? (§8 domain model, §24 Phase 12 pitfalls)
  15. Why is the FD budget (§4) exactly 4,004 out of a 4,096 ceiling, and what happens to the system if max_global_connections were raised without also raising the FD ceiling? (§36 Capacity Model item 6, §4)
  16. Why does WebTorrent's WebRTC/WebSocket-tracker protocol carry no official BEP number, and why does this matter for how this blueprint cites it? (§5 Required Reading, §25 Phase 13 pitfalls)
  17. Why does a hybrid v1/v2 torrent need BEP 47 padding files, and what would go wrong for a multi-file torrent without them? (§24 Phase 12, §8 domain model)
  18. Why is the practical scaling ceiling of this design peer-count-per-host rather than an algorithmic complexity limit, and what two levers exist beyond that ceiling? (§33 Scalability Analysis, §36 Capacity Model item 2)

34. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — A torrent is a distributed integrity manifest, not a file. Predicts that hash trees generalize cleanly (flat SHA-1 list in v1, merkle tree in v2, §8). Bug class without it: engineers trust file names/paths instead of the pieces/piece layers data, and silently accept renamed-but-corrupted content. Applies at §8, §14 (Phase 2), §24 (Phase 12).
  • MM2 — The choke algorithm is a repeated iterated prisoner's dilemma, not a scheduler. Predicts optimistic unchoke is structurally required, not a tunable nicety. Bug class without it: engineers "simplify" by removing the optimistic slot and new peers can never demonstrate reciprocity, killing swarm growth. Applies at P4, §18 (Phase 6).
  • MM3 — Kademlia's XOR metric bounds routing-table maintenance by ID-space bit-length, not peer count. Predicts O(log N) lookup hops regardless of network size. Bug class without it: engineers implement an O(N) flood-style discovery expecting it to scale, and it doesn't. Applies at §8, §20 (Phase 8), §33.
  • MM4 — The bencode canonical form is the infohash's identity function. The captured bytes are the hash input, not a value the hash merely describes. Predicts re-serialization breaks compatibility. Bug class without it: silent infohash mismatches against real-world non- canonical torrents. Applies at §8, P3, §14 (Phase 2).
  • MM5 — A Transport is a capability, not a socket type. Predicts choking/picking code stays transport-agnostic across TCP/µTP/WebRTC. Bug class without it: business logic accretes transport-specific special cases that eventually leak into the picker. Applies at P9, §11, §22 (Phase 10), §25 (Phase 13).
  • MM6 — LEDBAT's target delay is a fairness contract with TCP, not a speed knob. Predicts µTP throughput should degrade under TCP competition — that is correct, designed behavior. Bug class without it: engineers "fix" perceived slowness by raising the target delay and quietly break the background-transport promise RFC 6817 makes. Applies at §22 (Phase 10), §5 (RFC 6817).
  • MM7 — Endgame trades bandwidth for tail latency, an explicit admission the picker's model breaks down near completion. Predicts endgame amplifies upload exactly when marginal benefit is lowest, so it must stay capped. Applies at §18 (Phase 6), §32.
  • MM8 — A magnet link is a hash-addressed promise, not a location. Predicts metadata must be re-verified against the hash before any byte is trusted, regardless of which peer supplied it. Bug class without it: engineers trust the first peer's metadata blindly. Applies at §8, §21 (Phase 9).

5. Required Reading

Books

  • Kleppmann, M. — Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017). Ch. 5 (Replication) and Ch. 6 (Partitioning) for the intuition behind sharding an ID space across nodes, directly reused for the DHT's bucket partitioning. Applied in: §8, §20 (Phase 8).
  • Stevens, W. R., Fenner, B., Rudoff, A. M. — UNIX Network Programming, Vol. 1, 3rd ed. (Addison-Wesley, 2003). Ch. 2 (transport-layer overview) and Ch. 8 (UDP sockets) for the socket-level discipline the UDP tracker, DHT, and µTP transport all depend on. Applied in: §15 (Phase 3), §20 (Phase 8), §22 (Phase 10).
  • Tanenbaum, A. S., Wetherall, D. — Computer Networks, 5th ed. (Pearson, 2010). Ch. 5, section 5.3 (congestion control) as the background theory LEDBAT specializes. Applied in: §22 (Phase 10).
  • Klabnik, S., Nichols, C. — The Rust Programming Language, 2nd ed. (No Starch Press, 2023). Ch. 10 (traits & generics) and Ch. 16 (fearless concurrency) for the Transport / Storage / Picker trait design. Applied in: §11, §16 (Phase 4).
  • Blandy, J., Orendorff, J., Tindall, L. N. — Programming Rust, 2nd ed. (O'Reilly, 2021). Ch. 19 (concurrency) and Ch. 20 (async) for the task-per-peer connection model. Applied in: §16 (Phase 4), §23 (Phase 11).
  • Steinmetz, R., Wehrle, K. (eds.) — Peer-to-Peer Systems and Applications, LNCS 3485 (Springer, 2005). The Kademlia chapter and the BitTorrent chapter are the direct domain literature this blueprint specializes. Applied in: §8, §18 (Phase 6), §20 (Phase 8).

Papers

  • Cohen, B. — "Incentives Build Robustness in BitTorrent" (P2PEcon workshop, 2003). The choking algorithm's original justification: tit-for-tat, optimistic unchoke, the four-slot rule. Applied in: P4, §18 (Phase 6).
  • Maymounkov, P., Mazières, D. — "Kademlia: A Peer-to-peer Information System Based on the XOR Metric" (IPTPS, 2002). The DHT routing-table paper. Applied in: §8, §20 (Phase 8).
  • Shalunov, S., Hazel, G., Iyengar, J., Kuehlewind, M. — RFC 6817, "Low Extra Delay Background Transport (LEDBAT)" (IETF, 2012). The congestion-control algorithm µTP runs. Applied in: §22 (Phase 10).
  • Stoica, I., Morris, R., Karger, D., Kaashoek, M. F., Balakrishnan, H. — "Chord: A Scalable Peer-to-peer Lookup Service for Internet Applications" (SIGCOMM, 2001). Comparative DHT design; the Comparative Architecture Table (§35) draws the Chord-vs-Kademlia routing contrast from it. Applied in: §35.
  • Rowstron, A., Druschel, P. — "Pastry: Scalable, Decentralized Object Location and Routing for Large-Scale Peer-to-Peer Systems" (Middleware, 2001). A second comparative DHT design. Applied in: §35, §38.
  • Legout, A., Urvoy-Keller, G., Michiardi, P. — "Rarest First and Choke Algorithms Are Enough" (IMC, 2006). Empirical validation that plain rarest-first + Cohen choking is sufficient without further cleverness. Applied in: §18 (Phase 6), §37 (Hard Questions).
  • Legout, A., Liogkas, N., Kohler, E., Zhang, Z. — "Clustering and Sharing Incentives in BitTorrent Systems" (SIGMETRICS, 2007). Measurement study of tit-for-tat clustering effects. Applied in: §26 (Threat Model), §33 (Scalability Analysis).
  • Piatek, M., Isdal, T., Anderson, T., Krishnamurthy, A., Venkataramani, A. — "Do Incentives Build Robustness in BitTorrent?" (NSDI, 2007). The influential critique of tit-for-tat exploitability (BitTyrant). Applied in: §26 (Threat Model), §37 (Hard Questions).
  • Bharambe, A. R., Herley, C., Padmanabhan, V. N. — "Analyzing and Improving a BitTorrent Network's Performance Mechanisms" (INFOCOM, 2006). Performance analysis of rarest-first and choke tuning. Applied in: §32 (Performance Engineering).

Reference Implementations

  • libtorrent-rasterbar (github.com/arvidn/libtorrent) — src/peer_connection.cpp, src/bt_peer_connection.cpp, src/piece_picker.cpp, src/kademlia/. Lesson: the proactor I/O model scales to huge peer counts; pitfall not to copy: its mmap-based disk cache learned the hard way (circa 2014) that mmap'd writes silently corrupt under page-cache eviction — use pwrite instead (P6, Phase 5 Pitfall). Applied in: §17 (Phase 5), §20 (Phase 8).
  • Transmission (github.com/transmission/transmission) — libtransmission/peer-mgr.c, libtransmission/tr-dht.c. Lesson: a small, dependency-light C daemon is a legitimate design point for resource-constrained hardware. Applied in: §23 (Phase 11), §35.
  • qBittorrent (github.com/qbittorrent/qBittorrent) — src/base/bittorrent/. Lesson: operator-facing status semantics (when a torrent is "stalled" vs "downloading") are a design surface in their own right, not an afterthought. Applied in: §23 (Phase 11), §28.
  • librqbit (github.com/ikatson/rqbit) — a pure-Rust client; fellow-traveller for async patterns idiomatic in Rust but absent from the C++ references. Applied in: §16 (Phase 4), §22 (Phase 10).
  • WebTorrent (github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent) — lib/torrent.js, lib/peer.js. Lesson: the peer-wire protocol is transport-agnostic enough to run over WebRTC data channels with zero changes to message semantics. Applied in: §25 (Phase 13), §35.

Glossary (§40) · 47 terms · site-wide glossary

Allowed fast set
the small set of pieces (BEP 6, conventionally k=10) a peer will serve even while choking the requester, used to bootstrap new connections faster.
Announce
a tracker request reporting peer state and requesting peers.
Bandwidth accountant
the session-owned component that meters upload/download at the transport-trait boundary (P11, I9).
Bencode
the length-prefixed, sorted-dict serialization format underlying all BitTorrent metadata and control messages.
Bitfield
the first-message bitmap advertising which pieces a peer holds.
Block
a sub-piece unit requested/transferred over the wire, conventionally 16 KiB.
Choke
a peer-connection state meaning "I will not send you data right now."
Compact peer list
the 6-byte (IPv4) or 18-byte (IPv6) per-peer tracker response encoding (BEP 23/BEP 7).
Connection ID
the per-session identifier used by both the UDP tracker protocol (BEP 15) and µTP to scope a handshake.
Endgame mode
the one-way transition, near completion, to requesting outstanding blocks from every peer that has them.
Extension protocol (LTEP)
BEP 10's extended (opcode 20) message framing for negotiated sub-protocols.
Fast extension
BEP 6's have-all/have-none/suggest/reject/allowed-fast message set.
File tree
BEP 52's recursive per-path metainfo structure for v2 torrents.
get_peers
the Kademlia RPC that returns either peers or closer nodes for an infohash.
Handshake
the fixed 68-byte exchange that opens every peer-wire connection.
Hash reject / hash request / hashes
BEP 52's base-protocol opcodes (23/21/22) for exchanging merkle-tree layer hashes.
Have message
announces that a peer has just completed and verified a piece.
Holepunch extension (BEP 55)
the ut_holepunch rendezvous/connect/error protocol for NAT-blocked peer pairs.
Hybrid torrent
a torrent carrying both v1 (pieces) and v2 (file tree) fields, joinable in both swarms.
Infohash
the SHA-1 (v1) or SHA-256 (v2) hash of the bencoded info dict, computed once from captured bytes (I3).
Interested / not interested
a peer-connection state expressing desire for at least one block the other side has.
K-bucket
a Kademlia routing-table bucket holding up to K=8 known nodes at a given XOR-distance range.
Kademlia
the XOR-metric DHT algorithm Mainline DHT implements.
LEDBAT
RFC 6817's delay-based, TCP-yielding congestion control algorithm, run by µTP.
LSD
Local Service Discovery (BEP 14), UDP-multicast peer discovery on a local network.
Merkle root / pieces root
the SHA-256 root of a per-file leaf-block tree in BEP 52.
Meta version
the metainfo field distinguishing v1 (absent/1) from v2 (2).
Metainfo
the parsed structure of a .torrent file.
µTP
BEP 29's UDP-based reliable transport running LEDBAT congestion control.
Node ID
a 160-bit Kademlia identifier for a DHT participant.
One-way delay
the LEDBAT-measured queuing delay used to drive the congestion window.
Optimistic unchoke
the rotating unchoke slot given regardless of upload rate, to let new peers demonstrate reciprocity.
Piece
the top-level content-addressed unit a torrent is divided into.
Piece layers
BEP 52's top-level metainfo dict of concatenated merkle layer-hashes.
Piece length
the configured size of each piece, a power of two between 16 KiB and 16 MiB.
Private torrent
a torrent with info.private=1, quarantined from DHT/PEX/LSD (P12, I10).
Quarantined piece
a piece that failed hash verification and awaits re-request from a different peer.
Rarest-first
the piece-selection policy prioritizing the least-available piece a peer holds.
RC4 drop-1024
the MSE mitigation discarding the first 1,024 bytes of RC4 keystream.
Resume database
the sled-backed persistent store of bitfields and partial-piece state (ADR-05).
Scrape
a tracker query for aggregate swarm stats without changing peer state.
Sequential mode
an opt-in piece-selection policy for streaming playback.
Sparse file
a pre-allocated file whose disk usage tracks only verified bytes written.
Super-seeding
BEP 16's lone-seeder bootstrap optimization (deferred, §39).
Tracker
a server coordinating peer discovery for a swarm via HTTP or UDP announce/scrape.
Transport
the trait abstracting TCP, µTP, and WebRTC behind one AsyncRead+AsyncWrite contract (P9, I7).
Write-token
the Kademlia anti-spoofing token required before announce_peer is honored, rotated every 5 minutes. ---
