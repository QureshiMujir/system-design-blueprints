30. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — A flow is a control loop around a pipe with a queue. Insight: sender = controller, network = plant, ACKs = feedback. Predicts: stability/oscillation follow control theory (gain, delay). Bug class without it: tuning constants blindly and getting sawtooth instability. Resolves in: §6 (P1), §3.
  • MM2 — BDP is the memory of the network. Insight: BDP = BtlBw · RTprop bytes are "in flight" with no queue. Predicts: cwnd below BDP under-utilizes; above BDP fills the buffer. Bug class: sizing cwnd/buffers wrong → under-use or bufferbloat. Resolves in: §8 (worked example), §32.
  • MM3 — Loss-based CC fills the buffer by design. Insight: AIMD only backs off after a drop, so it must first fill and overflow the queue. Predicts: CUBIC bufferbloat on deep buffers (FM-1). Bug class: expecting low latency from a loss-based algorithm on a dumb deep buffer. Resolves in: §15 (Phase 3), §19 (Phase 7).
  • MM4 — Two numbers describe the path (Kleinrock's optimal point). Insight: BtlBw and RTprop fully characterize the pipe; operating at exactly BDP inflight maximizes rate and minimizes delay simultaneously. Predicts: BBR's target. Bug class: believing you must trade throughput for latency. Resolves in: §17 (Phase 5), I4.
  • MM5 — You can't measure max-BW and min-RTT at the same time. Insight: probing bandwidth builds a queue (inflates RTT); measuring RTprop needs an empty queue (sacrifices rate) — so you must alternate. Predicts: BBR's ProbeBW/ProbeRTT split; Vegas's delay signal. Bug class: trusting an RTT sample taken while probing up. Resolves in: §17 (Phase 5), I3/I13.
  • MM6 — ACKs are the clock; break the clock and you break the controller. Insight: self-clocking (P1) times sends to the network's own pace. Predicts: ACK compression/thinning destabilizes rate estimates (FM-7). Bug class: estimating rate over ACK-arrival time. Resolves in: §13 (Phase 1, I5), §8.
  • MM7 — Fairness is emergent, not guaranteed. Insight: fairness arises from all flows sharing the same feedback law; mix laws (BBR + CUBIC) and one starves the other. Predicts: BBR-v1 vs CUBIC starvation (Ware 2019, FM-4). Bug class: deploying a new algorithm and assuming it coexists. Resolves in: §23 (FM-4), §17 (Phase 5), P11.
  • MM8 — cwnd is a budget; pacing is a rate. Insight: cwnd bounds how much is in flight; pacing controls how fast it leaves — decoupling them removes bursts. Predicts: cwnd-only senders burst at line rate and self-inflict loss. Bug class: shipping cwnd without pacing on a high-BDP path. Resolves in: §16 (Phase 4, I14), P14.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • W. R. Stevens, TCP/IP Illustrated, Vol. 1 (2nd ed., Fall & Stevens). Ch. on TCP timeout/retransmission, congestion control, and window management. Extract: exact on-wire ACK/RTT/RTO behavior. Applied in §8, §13 (Phase 1), §18 (Phase 6).
  • L. Peterson & B. Davie, Computer Networks: A Systems Approach (6th ed.). Ch. 6 (Congestion Control & Resource Allocation). Extract: AIMD fairness geometry, RED, TCP Vegas. Applied in §14 (Phase 2), §31.
  • C. Benvenuti, Understanding Linux Network Internals (O'Reilly). Parts on the TCP stack and traffic control. Extract: how tcp_congestion_ops and qdiscs are wired. Applied in §21 (Phase 9), ADR-16.
  • B. Gregg, Systems Performance (2nd ed.). Ch. on CPUs, latency, and observability (perf, BPF, USE method). Extract: tail-latency and flame-graph methodology. Applied in §28, §19 (Phase 7).
  • G. Varghese, Network Algorithmics. Ch. on timing wheels and efficient timers. Extract: hashed/hierarchical timer-wheel design for pacing. Applied in §16 (Phase 4), §11.
  • J. Kurose & K. Ross, Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach (8th ed.). Ch. 3 transport + congestion control. Extract: end-to-end control-loop intuition and BDP. Applied in §30 (MM1, MM2), §2.
  • D. Comer, Internetworking with TCP/IP, Vol. 1 (6th ed.). TCP flow/congestion chapters. Extract: reference framing of slow-start and recovery states. Applied in §14 (Phase 2), §8.

Papers

  • V. Jacobson, "Congestion Avoidance and Control," SIGCOMM 1988. Contribution: slow-start, AIMD, RTT variance estimator, self-clocking. Relevance: the AIMD kernel of cc-reno. Applied in §14 (Phase 2), P1, MM6.
  • S. Ha, I. Rhee, L. Xu, "CUBIC: A New TCP-Friendly High-Speed TCP Variant," ACM SIGOPS OSR 2008. Contribution: cubic window-growth function, TCP-friendly region, fast convergence. Relevance: the growth law of cc-cubic. Applied in §15 (Phase 3), §8 (state machine + W(t)).
  • N. Cardwell, Y. Cheng, C. S. Gunn, S. H. Yeganeh, V. Jacobson, "BBR: Congestion-Based Congestion Control," ACM Queue 2016 / CACM 2017. Contribution: BtlBw/RTprop model, pacing_gain/cwnd_gain, ProbeBW/ProbeRTT. Relevance: the model of cc-bbr. Applied in §17 (Phase 5), I4, MM4.
  • K. Nichols & V. Jacobson, "Controlling Queue Delay" (CoDel), ACM Queue 2012. Contribution: sojourn-time AQM without tuning knobs. Relevance: the AQM the harness pairs with. Applied in §19 (Phase 7), FM-1.
  • M. Alizadeh et al., "Data Center TCP (DCTCP)," SIGCOMM 2010. Contribution: ECN-proportional window reduction (alpha). Relevance: the ECN response in cc-bbr/L4S mode. Applied in §19 (Phase 7), I11.
  • M. Mathis, J. Semke, J. Mahdavi, T. Ott, "The Macroscopic Behavior of the TCP Congestion Avoidance Algorithm," CCR 1997. Contribution: throughput ≈ MSS/(RTT·√p) model. Relevance: sanity oracle for loss-based throughput. Applied in §32, §26.
  • S. Floyd & V. Jacobson, "Random Early Detection Gateways for Congestion Avoidance," IEEE/ACM ToN 1993. Contribution: probabilistic early drop. Relevance: the AQM baseline compared in §31. Applied in §31, §34 (ALT-3).
  • L. Brakmo & L. Peterson, "TCP Vegas: End-to-End Congestion Avoidance on a Global Internet," IEEE JSAC 1995. Contribution: delay-based control (RTT gradient). Relevance: the delay-signal ancestor of BBR's RTprop. Applied in §30 (MM5), §31.
  • N. Dukkipati et al., "An Argument for Increasing TCP's Initial Congestion Window," CCR 2010. Contribution: IW10 startup. Relevance: slow-start init in cc-reno/cc-cubic. Applied in §14 (Phase 2), N.8 edge cases.
  • R. Ware, M. Mukerjee, S. Seshan, J. Sherry, "Modeling BBR's Interactions with Loss-Based Congestion Control," IMC 2019. Contribution: BBR's inflight cap explains CUBIC starvation. Relevance: the fairness failure cc-bbr must be measured against. Applied in §23 (FM-4), §17 (Phase 5).

Reference Implementations

  • Linux net/ipv4/tcp_cong.c + tcp_cubic.c. Study: tcp_congestion_ops vtable and CUBIC epoch math. Lesson: the pluggable-CC ABI. Pitfall not to copy: integer-fixed-point scaling that overflows at 100 Gbps. Applied in §21 (Phase 9), §15 (Phase 3), ADR-16.
  • Linux net/ipv4/tcp_bbr.c. Study: state machine, bbr_pacing_gain cycle, ProbeRTT. Lesson: BtlBw windowed-max filter. Pitfall: v1's fairness against CUBIC (FM-4). Applied in §17 (Phase 5).
  • Linux net/ipv4/tcp_rate.c + tcp_recovery.c. Study: delivery-rate sampling and RACK. Lesson: rate sample bookkeeping. Pitfall: assuming ACKs are un-aggregated. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), §18 (Phase 6), I5, I15.
  • google/bbr (GitHub) + BBRv2/v3 drafts. Study: the reference ProbeBW cycle and v2 loss/ECN response. Lesson: gain schedule. Pitfall: copying v2 constants without the v1 baseline first. Applied in §17 (Phase 5), §35 (BBRv3 deferral).
  • Chromium/QUICHE quiche/quic/core/congestion_control/ (bbr_sender.cc, cubic_bytes.cc, pacing_sender.cc). Study: CC decoupled from the kernel, byte-based cwnd. Lesson: the exact trait boundary this project adopts (P8). Pitfall: QUIC-specific ACK-frame assumptions. Applied in §21 (Phase 9), §11, §8 (byte-based cwnd to match QUICHE).
  • ns-3 src/internet/model/tcp-* (e.g. tcp-cubic, tcp-bbr, tcp-linux-reno). Study: discrete-event transport modeling. Lesson: deterministic simulation structure. Pitfall: its RTT estimator differs from Linux — do not treat as the differential oracle. Applied in §20 (Phase 8), ADR-18.

Glossary (§36) · 49 terms · site-wide glossary

ACK compression
ACKs bunched by cross-traffic/aggregation so they arrive in a burst, inflating naive delivery-rate estimates (FM-7).
AIMD
Additive-Increase/Multiplicative-Decrease, the Reno control law (Jacobson 1988).
App-limited
an interval where the sender had no data to send; its rate samples must not lower BtlBw (I4).
AQM
Active Queue Management (RED, CoDel, PIE); drops/marks before the queue is full.
BDP
Bandwidth-Delay Product = BtlBw · RTprop; bytes in flight with an empty queue (MM2).
BBR
Bottleneck Bandwidth and Round-trip propagation time; model-based CC (Cardwell 2016).
BtlBw
estimated bottleneck bandwidth (windowed-max delivery rate, I4).
Bufferbloat
excessive standing queue in an over-large buffer causing high latency (FM-1).
Congestion collapse
throughput → 0 under retransmission storms (Jacobson 1988, FM-2).
Congestion window (cwnd)
sender's upper bound on in-flight bytes (a budget, MM8, I1).
Congestion avoidance
the additive-increase phase after slow-start (RFC 5681).
CUBIC
cubic-window loss-based CC, Linux default (RFC 9438).
DCTCP
Data Center TCP; ECN-proportional window reduction via alpha (Alizadeh 2010, I11).
Delivery rate
bytes delivered per unit send-time interval (tcp_rate.c model, I5).
DSACK
Duplicate SACK; signals a spurious retransmission, enabling undo (I15).
ECN
Explicit Congestion Notification; routers mark instead of drop (RFC 3168).
ECT(0)/ECT(1)/CE
ECN codepoints; ECT(1) marks L4S-scalable traffic (RFC 9331).
Fast convergence
CUBIC's extra W_max reduction to yield bandwidth to new flows (Ha 2008).
Fast recovery
post-loss phase that avoids returning to slow-start (NewReno, RFC 6582).
Flow actor
the single-writer owner of one flow's state (I8, ADR-05).
fq / fq_codel
Linux fair-queue / fair-queue-CoDel qdiscs; pacing + AQM.
hrtimer / timerfd
high-resolution kernel timers used by the pacing backend (ADR-07).
Inflight bytes
sent but unacknowledged bytes; must stay ≤ cwnd (I1).
IW10
initial window of 10 segments at connection start (Dukkipati 2010).
Jain's fairness index
a 0–1 measure of how equally a bottleneck is shared (P11).
Kleinrock's optimal point
inflight = BDP: max throughput and min delay together (MM4).
L4S
Low Latency, Low Loss, Scalable throughput architecture (RFC 9330).
min-RTT / RTprop
the propagation-delay estimate (windowed-min RTT, I3).
Monotonic clock
non-decreasing, NTP-immune time source (CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW, I7).
Optimistic ACK
an ACK for data not yet received; an attack/defect (T2).
Pacing
spacing packet sends at a target rate rather than bursting (P14, MM8).
Pacing gain / cwnd gain
BBR multipliers on rate and window (I14, §8).
Packet conservation
inject new data only as old data leaves the network (P1).
ProbeBW / ProbeRTT
BBR states that probe bandwidth vs refresh RTprop (§17, I13).
QUIC
encrypted userspace transport; CC per RFC 9002.
RACK-TLP
time-based loss detection + Tail Loss Probe (RFC 8985, I15).
RED
Random Early Detection AQM (Floyd/Jacobson 1993).
Replay divergence
a replayed run producing a different trace than recorded (FM-6, I9).
RTO
Retransmission TimeOut (SRTT + 4·RTTVAR, floor 1 s, RFC 6298, I12).
RTT
round-trip time (one delivery-rate/RTT sample per ACK).
SACK
Selective Acknowledgment (RFC 2018); enables precise loss detection.
Self-clocking
using ACK arrivals as the transmit clock (MM6, P1).
Slow start
exponential cwnd growth at connection start until ssthresh (RFC 5681).
Sojourn time
how long a packet sits in a queue; CoDel's control signal (Nichols 2012).
ssthresh
slow-start threshold separating slow-start from congestion avoidance.
Tail latency
high-percentile (p99/p999) latency behavior (Gregg; M1/M2).
TCP-friendly region
CUBIC's fallback so it is never slower than Reno (RFC 9438).
Timer wheel
hashed/hierarchical O(1) timer structure for pacing (Varghese, §16).
Windowed max/min filter
O(1) filter tracking max/min over a time/RTT window (I3, I4).
