34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Methodology: every number below re-derives a §4 constraint (or extends one) from first principles, formula shown inline, not just the answer.

1. Adapter artifact size at rank r. Formula: size(r) = 2 × r × d_model × modules_per_layer × layers × bytes_per_param (the 2 is the A+B matrix pair per module). d_model=4096, modules_per_layer=4 (q_proj/k_proj/v_proj/o_proj), layers=32 → modules_total = 4×32 = 128.

  • r=8: 2×8×4096×128×2 bytes → 16×4096=65,536; ×128=8,388,608; ×2=16,777,216 bytes ≈16.0 MiB payload → C1 (16.8 MB with ~40 KB manifest/index overhead).
  • r=16: double r=8 → 33,554,432 bytes ≈32.0 MiB → C2 (33.6 MB w/ overhead).
  • r=64: 4× r=16 → 134,217,728 bytes ≈128.0 MiB → C3 (134.2 MB w/ overhead).

2. Resident adapters per GPU (C4). Formula: resident_count = adapter_pool_bytes ÷ artifact_size. 80 GB HBM under C10's ceiling, minus base (bf16 7B ≈14 GB) plus KV-cache reserve, leaves ≈40 GB adapter pool. 40 GB ÷ 33.6 MB (r=16, C2) = 1,190.5 → rounded down for index/bookkeeping overhead ≈1,200 (C4).

3. Adapter swap latency p50 (C5). Formula: latency = artifact_size ÷ effective_PCIe_bandwidth. Effective PCIe4 x16 bandwidth ≈25.6 GB/s (≈80% of raw 32 GB/s). 33.6 MB ÷ 25.6 GB/s = 0.0336 GB ÷ 25.6 GB/s ≈1.31 ms ≈ C5's 1.3 ms p50.

4. Extra GEMM FLOPs per token under composition. Per-module LoRA forward is two GEMV ops: r_out = A·x (r×d_model by d_model×1 → 2×r×d_model FLOPs), then h_delta = B·r_out (d_model×r by r×1 → 2×d_model×r FLOPs); total per module-instance = 4 × r × d_model. Across modules_total=128 for one active adapter at r=16: 4×16×4096×128 → 64×4096=262,144; ×128=33,554,432 ≈33.55M FLOPs/token/adapter. At C11's cap of 4 composed adapters: 4×33.55M ≈134.2M FLOPs/token. Against a 7B-parameter base's ≈14 GFLOPs/token (≈2×params, standard forward-pass estimate): 134.2M ÷ 14G ≈0.96% raw-compute overhead — far under C6's 8% ceiling, confirming C6's budget is dominated by memory/kernel-launch cost (§17), not raw FLOPs (Hard Question 18, §35).

5. Registry storage growth, daily and annualized (C12). Formula: raw_growth = new_artifacts_per_day × avg_artifact_size; net_growth = raw_growth × (1 − dedup_rate). 1,000 new r=16 artifacts/day × 33.6 MB = 33.6 GB/day raw; ×(1−0.40) = 20.16 GB/day net (C12). Annualized: 20.16 GB/day × 365 ≈7.36 TB/year net registry growth at steady-state publish rate.

6. Verification throughput and cold-start burst. Formula: cold_verify_time = artifact_size ÷ hashing_throughput. At ≈1 GB/s SHA-256 throughput: r=64 (134 MB, C3) → 134 ms (C9 cold); r=16 (33.6 MB) → 33.6 ms. For a cache-miss burst of K=100 freshly-pulled r=16 adapters serialized on one hashing core: 100 × 33.6 ms = 3.36 s; parallelized across 8 hashing threads (typical verifier worker pool): 3.36 s ÷ 8 ≈0.42 s burst cold-start latency.

7. Verification throughput, checks/s per core. Formula: throughput = 1 ÷ per_check_latency. Cold path: 1 ÷ 0.134 s ≈7.46 checks/s/core (C9 cold). Warm path (cached-digest short-circuit, ≤2 ms, C9 warm): 1 ÷ 0.002 s = 500 checks/s/core — a 67x throughput gap that is the entire justification for P4's TTL-bounded warm-cache carve-out.

8. Fleet-wide adapter-residency capacity (§31). Formula: fleet_capacity = nodes × C4. A 10-node serving fleet: 10 × 1,200 = 12,000 resident adapters fleet-wide, before any single node needs a cache-miss cold pull (§31; C4 and C12 jointly determine how fast catalog growth outruns this ceiling).
