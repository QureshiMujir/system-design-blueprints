34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Every figure below is derived, with the formula and substitution shown, chained from the §4 System Constraints table (or from a number that table itself already fixes, e.g. RTT_p99 used in §8.2).

C1 — Guard interval: G ≥ 2δ + RTT_p99. Substituting δ = 200 ms (§4), RTT_p99 = 20 ms (§8.2): G ≥ 2(200) + 20 = 420 ms, rounded up to the §4 operational default of 500 ms.

C2 — Failover-time ceiling: L + G. With defaults L = 10 s, G = 0.5 s (§4): 10 + 0.5 = 10.5 s, matching the §4/§16.4 ≤ 10.5 s target exactly.

C3 — Session-expiry detection ceiling: heartbeat_interval × missed_heartbeat_threshold. Defaults 2 s × 3 (§4): 2 × 3 = 6 s, matching the §4/§18.4 ≤ 6 s target.

C4 — Renewal traffic at max concurrency, default L: renewal_rate = N ÷ (L / 3). At N = 1,000,000 (§4 max concurrent leases), L = 10 s (§4 default): renewal_interval = 10/3 ≈ 3.33 s; renewal_rate = 1,000,000 ÷ 3.33 ≈ 300,000/s — six times over the §4 sustained-renewal target of 50,000/s. The max-concurrency ceiling and the default lease duration cannot both hold at full load.

C5 — Minimum average L to reconcile C4: solving renewal_rate = 3N / L for L at the target throughput: L_min = 3N ÷ renewal_target = 3(1,000,000) ÷ 50,000 = 60 s. Sustaining 1,000,000 concurrent leases inside the 50,000/s renewal budget needs an average L six times the 10 s default — a fact P11's tradeoff discussion must carry explicitly.

C6 — Fencing-issuance floor, additive check: grant_rate + renewal_hand-off_rate = 5,000/s (§4) + 50,000/s (§4) = 55,000/s, exactly matching the §4 stated ≥ 55,000 tokens/s floor — confirming the four §4 rows are mutually consistent.

C7 — Consensus in-flight concurrency (Little's Law, L = λW): λ ≈ 55,000/s (C6), W = 15 ms (§4 consensus-round p99, 3-node). in_flight ≈ 55,000 × 0.015 = 825. The backing ConsensusStore (§19) must sustain roughly 825 concurrent in-flight proposals at steady state to hold the stated p99.

C8 — Timer-wheel throughput requirement, chained from C4: every renewal reschedules its Timer Wheel entry (§13.2), so the wheel sustains ≈ 300,000 schedule+cancel ops/s at max concurrency under default L. At O(1) amortized per op (ADR-15) that is 300,000 constant-time ops/s; the rejected O(log n) sorted-heap alternative at n = 1,000,000 (log2(1,000,000) ≈ 20) costs ≈ 20× more comparison work per op — an effective 6,000,000 comparison-equivalents/s — quantifying ADR-15's rejection.

C9 — Session fan-out ceiling vs. flat lease ceiling: max_sessions × max_leases_per_session = 200,000 × 100 = 20,000,000 (§4) nominal attach-slots, twenty times the flat max-concurrent-leases ceiling of 1,000,000 (§4). The binding constraint on total addressable resource leases is the flat ceiling, never the session-fan-out product; §18/§31 capacity planning must size against it first.

C10 — RTO-driven minimum replay throughput: RTO < 2 min = 120 s (§4) covers coordinator failover and lease-store recovery together. At the max-concurrency ceiling of 1,000,000 leases (§4), a Phase-7 replay (§19.2) needs a minimum sustained replay-application rate of 1,000,000 ÷ 120 ≈ 8,334 records/s merely to stay inside the RTO budget, before any other recovery cost shares that same window.
