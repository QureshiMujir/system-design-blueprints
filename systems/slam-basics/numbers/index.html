Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Numbers an engineer computes before building, each derived from the System Constraints table (§4). Formula shown; inputs are the §4 quantities.

  • B1 — Beam endpoints processed per second (frontend throughput). beams/scan × scan_rate. At the §4 mid-load point (1081 beams, 20 Hz): 1081 × 20 = 21,620 endpoints/s. At the dense ceiling (1081 beams, 40 Hz): ≈ 43,240/s. Sizes the per-scan endpoint-transform + ray-cast budget.

  • B2 — Per-scan frontend time budget. 1 / scan_rate. At 20 Hz: 1/20 = 50 ms wall per scan; §4 allocates ≤ 30 ms of it to scan-match + association + map-update, leaving ~20 ms headroom for ingest/publish. Confirms the frontend deadline is feasible only if the correlative matcher (the dominant cost, Phase 3) stays under ~30 ms.

  • B3 — Occupancy-grid memory (single grid). extent_x/res × extent_y/res × bytes_per_cell. For the §4 example (100 m × 100 m @ 5 cm, i16 log-odds): (100/0.05) × (100/0.05) × 2 = 2000 × 2000 × 2 = 8 MB. The §4 row counts 4·10⁶ cells; at 2 B/cell that is the 8 MB working-set for one map.

  • B4 — FastSLAM total map memory. particles × single-grid-bytes (B3). At the §4 particle band (30–200) with the 8 MB grid: 30 × 8 MB = 240 MB (floor) to 200 × 8 MB = 1.6 GB (ceiling, before copy-on-write). This is why §4 names "particles × per-particle grid" as the dominant FastSLAM term and Phase 6 mandates copy-on-write grids.

  • B5 — EKF-SLAM covariance memory at the wall. (3 + 2N)² × 8 B for dense f64 Σ. At the §4 landmark wall (N = 1000): (3 + 2000)² × 8 = 2003² × 8 ≈ 32 MB, and per-step update cost scales with that ~4·10⁶-entry matrix. Doubling N to 2000 quadruples it to ~128 MB — the O(N²) wall §4 and Phase 5 cite, motivating the switch to FastSLAM/graph.

  • B6 — Pose-graph state-vector size. poses × DOF. At the §4 graph ceiling (10⁵ poses, SE(2) 3-DOF): 10⁵ × 3 = 3·10⁵ unknowns; in 3D (6-DOF): 6·10⁵. Sets the dimension of H (and thus the Cholesky working set) the solver must factor.

  • B7 — Sparse H nonzeros (odometry backbone). A pure chain of P poses each with one between-factor gives a block-tridiagonal H: ≈ (3P − 2) diagonal/off-diagonal d×d blocks, d = DOF. At P = 10⁴, SE(2): ≈ 3·10⁴ blocks × 3×3 = 9 = ~2.7·10⁵ nonzeros — versus a dense (3·10⁴)² ≈ 9·10⁸. The ~3000× gap is exactly why Principle 2 forbids dense solves.

  • B8 — Loop-closure fill-in pressure. Each non-sequential loop edge adds an off-diagonal block at graph distance Δ; naive elimination can fill the band between, adding up to O(Δ × d²) nonzeros. For a loop spanning Δ = 5000 poses (SE(2)): up to 5000 × 9 ≈ 4.5·10⁴ extra nonzeros from one bad ordering — which AMD (I8, Phase 8) exists to suppress. Quantifies why "loops densify H" (Scalability Analysis).

  • B9 — Full-solve time order of magnitude. Sparse Cholesky cost ≈ O(nnz(L)); §4 budgets "full GN/LM solve ≤ a few seconds at 10⁴ poses." With well-ordered nnz(L) on the order of 10⁶ and ~5–10 GN/LM iterations, that is ~10⁷ flops/iter × ~10 iters ≈ 10⁸ flops — sub-second on a host CPU, consistent with the §4 budget; a poor ordering inflating nnz(L) 10–100× is what blows it past "a few seconds."

  • B10 — Loop-closure database size. keyframes × (descriptor_bytes + ring_key_bytes). With scan-context rings×sectors ~ 20 × 60 = 1200 floats (9.6 KB) per keyframe and 10⁵ keyframes: 10⁵ × 9.6 KB ≈ 960 MB of descriptors — bounding when keyframe culling (Principle 9, Phase 11) becomes mandatory and why retrieval uses a compact ring key + KD-tree (O(log n)), not full-descriptor scan.

  • B11 — Map-rebuild cost after optimization. keyframes × beams × avg_ray_cells. At 10³ keyframes, 1081 beams, ~40 cells/ray (≈ 2 m range @ 5 cm): 10³ × 1081 × 40 ≈ 4.3·10⁷ cell updates — tens of ms, so a full rebuild on every loop closure (I9, Phase 11) is affordable; incremental update between closures keeps it off the per-scan path.

  • B12 — Log-odds saturation headroom. With per-hit increment Δl ≈ 0.85 and clamp |l| ≤ 50 (§4): a cell saturates after 50 / 0.85 ≈ 59 consistent observations. Confirms the §4 clamp keeps the map responsive (a previously-occupied cell can be cleared in ~59 contradicting observations rather than never), the quantitative basis for Principle 11 / I12.
