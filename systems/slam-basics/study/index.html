  • HQ2. A loop closure is accepted and the map folds. Walk the chain that should have prevented it and name every defense that failed. (Principle 13 + I6 + Phase 9 + Phase 10; MM5)
  • HQ3. Your sparse solve that took 0.4 s at 5·10³ poses takes 40 s at 1·10⁴ after a long loop. What changed, how do you confirm it, and what's the fix? (§8.7 + I8 + Phase 8 + Back-of-Envelope B8/B9; MM2)
  • HQ4. Cholesky fails on a graph that "looks fine." Give the two most likely causes and how you distinguish gauge rank-deficiency from genuine ill-conditioning. (Principle 3 + 3b + I2 + I3 + Phase 8; MM4)
  • HQ5. Why must a scan matcher return an information matrix, and what specifically goes wrong in a corridor if it returns an isotropic one? (Principle 10 + I11 + Phase 3 + §8.3; MM7)

  • HQ8. Why is X⊞ξ = X·Exp(ξ) (right) rather than left, and what breaks across modules if one factor uses the other convention? (§8.1 + ADR 8 + Principle 1 + Phase 1; MM3)

Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed intuitions an expert carries into SLAM. Each: the insight, what it predicts, the bug you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

  • MM1 — SLAM is one big sparse nonlinear least-squares problem; everything else is a frontend or an approximation of it. The "map + trajectory" posterior, under Gaussian noise, is argmin Σ ρ(‖r_i‖²_Ω). Predicts: filter back-ends (EKF, FastSLAM) are just specific marginalization/sampling schedules over the same factors; their differences are scaling tradeoffs, not different physics. Bug without it: treating EKF and graph SLAM as unrelated systems and re-deriving each measurement model twice with inconsistent conventions. Applied: §8.6–8.7, Phase 7, Phase 8.

  • MM2 — The information matrix is the adjacency matrix of the factor graph. H = JᵀΩJ has a nonzero block (i,j) exactly when some factor touches both variable i and j. Predicts: odometry gives a banded H; each loop closure adds an off-diagonal block far from the diagonal; fill-in during elimination is determined by graph topology, not values. Bug without it: choosing a variable ordering blind to topology and getting an O(n³) solve from a graph that should take seconds. Applied: §8.7, Phase 8 (ordering, I8), Comparative Architecture Table.

  • MM3 — A pose lives on a manifold; the update lives in the tangent space. You never add to a rotation — you retract: X⊞ξ = X·Exp(ξ). Predicts: the state dimension (3 for SE(2), 6 for SE(3)) is the tangent dimension, not the matrix element count; Jacobians are ∂r/∂ξ, not ∂r/∂R. Bug without it: optimizing 9 matrix entries freely and drifting off SO(3), or linearly averaging angles and exploding at ±π. Applied: §8.1, Principle 1, Phase 1, Phase 7.

  • MM4 — A pose graph has a free gauge; without an anchor the problem is singular by exactly the gauge dimension. Relative measurements fix everything except the global frame (3-DOF in SE(2), 6 in SE(3)). Predicts: H is rank-deficient by the gauge dimension until a prior or fixed vertex is added; the chosen anchor changes no relative geometry. Bug without it: a Cholesky that fails mysteriously, "fixed" by random regularization that biases the whole map. Applied: Principle 3b, I3, §8.8, Phase 8, Phase 9.

  • MM5 — Appearance proposes, geometry disposes; a single accepted false loop closure destroys the entire map. Loop closure is the one place a local error becomes global. Predicts: recall is cheap and precision is everything; the right architecture is defense-in-depth (geometric χ² gate and a robust kernel), not one perfect detector. Bug without it: an appearance-only closure on perceptual aliasing that folds the map, with no second line of defense. Applied: Principle 13, I6, Phase 9 (robust kernels), Phase 10 (geometric gate).

  • MM6 — Conditioning on the trajectory makes the map factorize (Rao-Blackwellization). Given a known path, landmark/cell estimates become independent. Predicts: FastSLAM can carry an analytic map per particle and scale as O(M·N) instead of EKF's O(N²) — at the cost of one full map per particle (the real ceiling is memory, not compute). Bug without it: sharing one map across particles, or running a joint filter over path+map and hitting the quadratic wall needlessly. Applied: §8.5, Phase 6, Scalability Analysis.

  • MM7 — A scan matcher must return uncertainty, not just a transform; an under-constrained direction is information, not noise. A corridor constrains lateral position but not longitudinal. Predicts: the match Hessian is anisotropic; its small eigenvalue is the corridor axis; the back-end must weight that edge accordingly. Bug without it: feeding an isotropic, over-confident constraint and getting longitudinal drift the optimizer can't see. Applied: Principle 10, I11, Phase 3 (information from Hessian), §8.3.

  • MM8 — Linearization is a lie that's only locally true; trust-region damping is how you survive it. GN assumes the residual is linear over the step; far from the optimum it isn't. Predicts: GN can increase cost and diverge on a poor init; LM's λ interpolates between Gauss-Newton (small λ) and gradient descent (large λ) and rejects bad steps. Bug without it: shipping GN-only, watching it oscillate on real data, and blaming the Jacobians. Applied: §8.7, MM-anchored Principle 12, Phase 8 (LM default, ADR 28).

  • MM9 — Determinism is a feature you architect in, not a property you hope for. Float reduction order, FMA contraction, and unseeded RNG each break bit-reproducibility. Predicts: without fixed reduction order + pinned codegen + seeded RNG, two "identical" runs diverge and no incident is replayable. Bug without it: a heisenbug in the particle filter that vanishes under a debugger and a conformance suite that flakes. Applied: Principle 6, I5, ADR 9, ADR 24, Phase 0, Phase 12.

  • MM10 — Marginalization is not deletion; removing a variable folds its information into its neighbors and creates fill-in. Dropping an old pose in a sliding window must Schur-complement it out. Predicts: a "delete old poses to bound memory" shortcut silently discards information and biases the estimate; correct bounding densifies the remaining block. Bug without it: a lifelong-SLAM window that grows over-confident and inconsistent as it forgets. Applied: Principle 8, §8.7 (Schur), Scalability Analysis (sliding window deferred).

Glossary · 60 terms · site-wide glossary

SLAM
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping: jointly estimate a robot trajectory and a map from sensor data.
SE(2) / SE(3)
Special Euclidean groups: rigid-body transforms in 2D (3-DOF) / 3D (6-DOF).
SO(2) / SO(3)
Special Orthogonal groups: rotations in 2D / 3D.
Lie group / Lie algebra
A smooth group (SE(n)) and its tangent space at identity (se(n)); Exp/Log map between them.
Exp / Log map
Exp: tangent vector → group element; Log: inverse. On-manifold updates use Exp.
hat / vee
hat: tangent vector → matrix-algebra element (∧); vee: inverse (∨).
Adjoint Ad_X
Linear map relating left and right perturbations: Exp(Ad_X ξ)=X Exp(ξ) X⁻¹.
⊞ / ⊟ (box-plus/minus)
Retraction X⊞ξ=X·Exp(ξ) and its inverse Y⊟X=Log(X⁻¹Y); on-manifold "add/subtract".
Right perturbation
Convention X⊞ξ=X·Exp(ξ) (perturbation on the right); locked here (§8.1).
Quaternion (Hamilton)
Scalar-first unit quaternion [w,x,y,z], i j=k; SO(3) representation.
Occupancy grid
Discretized map of cell occupancy probabilities, stored as log-odds.
Log-odds
log(p/(1−p)); additive Bayesian update of occupancy; clamped for responsiveness.
Inverse sensor model
Maps a measurement to per-cell occupancy evidence (occupied/free/unknown).
Beam model
Mixture sensor model (p_hit/p_short/p_max/p_rand) for range likelihood.
Likelihood field
Distance-to-nearest-obstacle field used to score scans (smooth, fast).
ICP
Iterative Closest Point: register two point sets by alternating correspondence + transform.
Point-to-line ICP (PLICP)
ICP variant using point-to-line residuals (surface normals); better in structured scenes.
Correlative scan matching
Exhaustive/multi-resolution grid search over the scan-likelihood field for the best pose.
NDT
Normal Distributions Transform: model the target as per-cell Gaussians; optimize scan fit.
Scan matching
Estimating the relative transform between scans (or scan-to-map); yields a constraint + information.
Motion model
Probabilistic model of pose change given control/odometry (odometry or velocity model).
Dead reckoning
Pose estimation from odometry/IMU alone, without map corrections.
EKF-SLAM
SLAM via a single Extended Kalman Filter over pose + all landmarks (dense, O(N²)).
State augmentation
Adding a newly-observed landmark to the EKF state + covariance.
Data association
Matching observations to map elements (landmarks/cells); the dominant failure point.
Mahalanobis distance
d²=yᵀS⁻¹y; χ²-distributed; basis of association gating.
FastSLAM / RBPF
Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filter: particles carry trajectories; maps estimated analytically per particle.
Rao-Blackwellization
Marginalizing part of a posterior analytically (the map) while sampling the rest (the trajectory).
Importance weight
Particle weight `∝ p(z
Resampling
Replacing low-weight particles with copies of high-weight ones; on low N_eff.
N_eff
Effective sample size 1/Σw²; resampling trigger; depletion indicator.
Factor graph
Bipartite graph of variables (poses/landmarks) and factors (constraints); MAP = least squares over it.
Variable / factor
A quantity to estimate (on a manifold) / a probabilistic constraint over a variable subset.
Residual
r(θ)=z⊟h(θ); the error a factor penalizes.
Whitening / sqrt-information
Scaling a residual by Ω^{1/2} so weighted cost becomes plain Σ‖r̃‖².
Information matrix Ω
Inverse covariance Σ⁻¹; the weight of a factor (g2o stores upper-triangular Ω).
Nonlinear least squares
Minimizing Σ‖r(θ)‖²_Ω over a manifold; the SLAM back-end.
Normal equations
(JᵀΩJ)δ=−JᵀΩr, i.e. Hδ=b; the linearized step.
Gauss-Newton
NLS method solving the normal equations and stepping; fast near the optimum.
Levenberg-Marquardt
Damped GN (H+λ diag H)δ=b; trust-region behavior; robust to poor init (default solver).
Sparse Cholesky
H=LLᵀ exploiting sparsity; the linear solve at each iteration.
Fill-in
New nonzeros created in L during factorization; controlled by ordering.
AMD / COLAMD
Approximate Minimum Degree (Column) ordering: fill-reducing variable permutations.
Schur complement
Eliminating a variable block (landmarks) to reduce the linear system size.
Marginalization
Removing a variable while folding its information into neighbors (Schur); creates fill-in.
Robust kernel
M-estimator (Huber/Cauchy/DCS) down-weighting outlier residuals via IRLS.
Huber / Cauchy / DCS
Robust loss functions; Huber (mild), Cauchy + DCS (redescending, strong outlier suppression).
IRLS
Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares: realize a robust kernel via per-iteration Ω reweighting.
Pose graph
Factor graph of poses + relative-pose constraints (no landmarks); optimized for global consistency.
Loop closure
Recognizing a revisited place and adding a constraint that corrects accumulated drift.
Scan context
Rotation-invariant LiDAR place descriptor (ring/sector grid) for loop-closure proposal.
Geometric verification
Confirming a loop candidate by scan matching + χ² consistency before insertion.
Gauge freedom
The free global frame of a pose graph (3-DOF SE(2) / 6-DOF SE(3)); fixed by an anchor prior.
Relocalization
Recovering pose against an existing map after tracking loss / kidnap.
Kidnapped robot
The robot is teleported; the estimate must recover via global matching.
ATE
Absolute Trajectory Error: RMSE of estimated vs ground-truth poses after alignment.
RPE
Relative Pose Error: drift over fixed-length segments (e.g. translational % per 100 m).
NEES / NIS
Normalized Estimation Error Squared (x−x̂)ᵀP⁻¹(x−x̂) — filter consistency vs ground truth (test); Normalized Innovation Squared yᵀS⁻¹y — online consistency metric.
.g2o
g2o pose-graph/factor-graph interchange format (vertices, edges, upper-triangular information).
Keyframe
A selected pose (on motion/information threshold) anchoring the graph and storing a scan.
