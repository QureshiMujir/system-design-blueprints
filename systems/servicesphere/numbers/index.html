34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. End-to-end latency decomposition. A naive sum of five hops each at their standalone p99 budget (150ms, M3) gives 5 × 150ms = 750ms, already exceeding the 400ms end-to-end target (M5). The actual budget that meets M5: gateway 50ms + auth introspection (cached) 30ms + business-service 80ms (typical, below its 150ms ceiling) + cache 10ms + DB read 80ms = 250ms, leaving 150ms margin for network and serialization — this is the real arithmetic behind M5, not the naive sum, and it is why P6 bounds call-chain depth rather than only budgeting each hop independently.

  2. Event backbone bandwidth. At 8,000 events/s sustained (M6) and an average envelope size of ~1.5KB (JSON payload plus CloudEvents metadata headers): 8,000 × 1.5KB = 12MB/s sustained; at the 25,000 events/s burst ceiling: 25,000 × 1.5KB ≈ 37.5MB/s.

  3. Event retention storage. At 12MB/s sustained average and a 7-day operational replay window: 12MB/s × 86,400s/day × 7 days ≈ 7.05TB per week of retained event log across all topics, sized against the Kafka cluster's disk budget.

  4. Database connection math. 27 services × up to 6 instances/service × 20 pooled connections/instance = 3,240 theoretical maximum connections, against a 500-connection PgBouncer ceiling per cluster (§4): 120/service (the §4 per-service budget) × 27 services = 3,240, far exceeding 500 — meaning no more than roughly 4 services can sit at their full 120-connection ceiling simultaneously against one shared PgBouncer tier, which is why PgBouncer is deployed per-service-group rather than as one platform-wide singleton.

  5. Cache memory and hit-ratio arithmetic. To hold the ≥85% hit-ratio target (M8) against an assumed 2,000 QPS origin read rate for the busiest service (jobs-service listings): the cache must serve 1,700 QPS, leaving 300 QPS to the database — comfortably within a single Postgres read replica's steady-state capacity, versus 2,000 QPS uncached, which would require multiple read replicas.

  6. Composed availability. A five-hop chain of services each at 99.9% (0.999) composes to 0.999^5 ≈ 0.995 — already at the committed 99.5% edge SLO (§26) with zero margin. At ten hops, 0.999^10 ≈ 0.990, already below the edge SLO on composed availability alone, independent of any single service's own reliability — the exact arithmetic that forces async decoupling (P6) for any workflow that would otherwise need more than roughly five synchronous hops.

  7. Trace storage. At an average of 8 spans per end-to-end request, 5,000 req/s (M1), and ~500 bytes/span: raw span volume = 5,000 × 8 × 500B = 20MB/s; at the 10% head-based sampling rate chosen in Phase 10 (§22.7): retained storage = 2MB/s ≈ 173GB/day, sized against the Jaeger backend's storage budget.

  8. Consumer lag headroom. At 8,000 events/s sustained (M6) and an assumed 10,000 events/s processing capacity per consumer instance: steady-state needs ⌈8,000/10,000⌉ = 1 instance; the 25,000 events/s burst ceiling needs ⌈25,000/10,000⌉ = 3 instances — sizing the HPA floor/ceiling for every event-consuming service (worker-service, search-service, notifications-service) directly from the §4 throughput targets.

  9. Gateway connection concurrency. At 5,000 req/s sustained (M1) and an average per-hop service time of 80ms (blended compute/DB-bound, M3/M4), Little's Law (L = λW) gives the expected in-flight request count: L = 5,000/s × 0.08s = 400 concurrent in-flight requests at the gateway alone — sizing the gateway's connection-pool and file-descriptor budget directly from the throughput and latency targets rather than guessing a round number.

  10. PgBouncer connection multiplexing benefit. Given the ADR-26/§4 tension in item 4 (120/service × 27 services = 3,240 against a 500 ceiling), multiplexing via PgBouncer's transaction-pooling mode lets each of the ~120 logical per-service connections share a much smaller pool of ~20-30 actual PostgreSQL backend connections per service group: 27 services × 20 backend connections ≈ 540, still above 500 but within striking distance — the residual gap is closed by grouping low-traffic services (content-gen-service, fact-check-service, complaint-assist-service — all stateless, §3) onto a shared low-priority pool rather than the full per-service allocation.

  11. Blast-radius arithmetic. With auth-service excluded as a special root-of-trust case (§35 Q5), a typical business service has at most 3 direct synchronous dependents by design (I5's depth-3 bound composed with typical fan-out) — so I9's "≤3 degraded dependents" bound is not an arbitrary policy number but the direct algebraic consequence of the call-depth invariant applied to the platform's actual dependency graph shape.
