34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Raw keystroke-edit stream: 300 keystrokes/minute active typing, ~2 bytes/keystroke delta average (small EditDelta CBOR overhead) → 300 × 2 = 600 bytes/min → 36,000 bytes/hour ≈ 35 KB/hour — small relative to the 25 MB/hour ceiling (M7), confirming keystrokes are not the dominant raw-stream contributor.

  2. Buffer-save deltas: 20 saves/hour, average edit-since-last-save 4 KB → 20 × 4 KB = 80 KB/hour — still well under M7's ceiling, confirming terminal/build output (below) dominates raw growth, not editor activity.

  3. Terminal volume: a moderately chatty build/test loop producing 5 KB/s of terminal output for 10 minutes/hour of active session time → 5 KB/s × 600 s = 3 MB/hour — the dominant raw-stream contributor, consistent with M7's 25 MB/hour ceiling existing mostly to bound terminal-heavy sessions.

  4. Raw pre-dedupe total: 35 KB (keystrokes) + 80 KB (saves) + 3 MB (terminal) + ~1 MB/hour assumed debugger/VCS overhead ≈ 4.1 MB/hour typical, up to M7's 25 MB/hour ceiling for a terminal-output-heavy hour (repeated full test-suite runs).

  5. Post-dedupe, pre-compression: applying M9's ≥4:1 chunk-dedupe target to the 25 MB/hour worst case → 25 MB ÷ 4 = 6.25 MB/hour (repeated terminal banners and build output dedupe well).

  6. Post-compression session size: applying M9's ≥3:1 zstd ratio to the 6.25 MB residual → 6.25 MB ÷ 3 ≈ 2.08 MB/hour, comfortably under M8's 4 MB/hour target, leaving headroom for a worse-than-typical dedupe ratio.

  7. Index RAM footprint: 8 KiB average chunk size (§8) → a 4 MB/hour post-dedupe session over 24h (M13's ceiling) references ≈ 96 MB ÷ 8 KiB ≈ 12,000 unique chunks; at 32 bytes/digest (SHA-256 truncated reference, ADR-04) + ~24 bytes/index-entry overhead ≈ 56 bytes/entry → 12,000 × 56 bytes ≈ 672 KB — trivially within M11's ≤2%-of-raw-size ceiling at this scale.

  8. Keyframe cost: cadence of one Full snapshot per 2 minutes (§8) → 30 keyframes/hour; assume 15 open buffers averaging 8 KB each per keyframe → 15 × 8 KB = 120 KB/keyframe → 30 × 120 KB = 3.6 MB/hour of raw keyframe bytes before dedupe — a meaningful fraction of the pre-dedupe 25 MB ceiling, explaining why keyframes are chunked and deduped like any other blob (P6), never treated as an exempt special case.

  9. Replay seek cost: worst case is a target immediately before the next keyframe, i.e. up to 200 accumulated deltas (ADR-18's threshold) since the last one; at an assumed 0.5 ms/delta apply cost (structural diff apply, Phase 7/8) → 200 × 0.5 ms = 100 ms, comfortably under M10's 150 ms p95 budget with ~50 ms of margin for index-lookup overhead.

  10. 24-hour session ceiling check: 2.08 MB/hour (step 6, typical) × 24h ≈ 50 MB typical sealed size, or up to 25 MB/hour raw worst-case × 24h = 600 MB pre-dedupe → ÷12 combined ratio (M9's 4:1 × 3:1) = 50 MB worst-case post-compression too — both scenarios land far under M13's 8 GB size-based rotation trigger, confirming the 24h wall-clock threshold, not the 8 GB size threshold, is the binding rotation trigger for a typical developer session (only an unusually terminal-heavy session approaches the size trigger first).
