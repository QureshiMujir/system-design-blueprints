34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Max resident stacks per 4 GiB, worst case (every page touched). 4 GiB / 64 KiB = 65,536 stacks (§4).
  2. Max resident stacks per 4 GiB, typical case (3 of 16 pages touched, 4 KiB pages). 4 GiB / (3 × 4 KiB) = 4 GiB / 12 KiB ≈ 349,525 stacks — shows the 1M-coroutine target (M4) is a virtual-memory story, not a worst-case-physical-memory one.
  3. Virtual-address-space ceiling, 64 KiB stacks. 2⁴⁷ B (typical x86-64 48-bit user VA half) / 2¹⁶ B = 2³¹ ≈ 2.1 billion stacks, confirming §4's "~10⁹, address-space-bound" figure.
  4. Per-core context-switch throughput ceiling. 1 / 30 ns (M1) = 33.3 million switches/sec/core.
  5. 16-core aggregate switch ceiling (ideal, uncontended). 16 × 33.3M ≈ 533 million switches/sec.
  6. mmap-only cost if every one of 1M spawns were a pool miss. 1,000,000 × 5 µs = 5.0 s — nearly the entire M4 10 s budget, which is exactly why the ≥95% pool-hit target (M8) exists: at 95% hit rate only 50,000 calls miss, costing 0.25 s, leaving headroom for the remaining work.
  7. Channel bandwidth at the M9 throughput target, 64 B payload. 10,000,000 msgs/sec × 64 B = 640 MB/s per channel pair.
  8. Guard-page virtual-address overhead at 1M coroutines. 1,000,000 × 4 KiB = 4 GiB of VA reserved for guard pages alone (PROT_NONE, not physically resident, §14).
  9. Stack-zero cost per pool retire (I10), 64 KiB at ≈20 GB/s memset bandwidth. 65,536 B / 20×10⁹ B/s ≈ 3.3 µs — the dominant component of the "≤ 1 µs p50" pool-hit spawn target (M3), which is why that target is stated in microseconds rather than nanoseconds.
  10. Cross-core steal-attempt ceiling, 16 workers at 500 ns/steal (M7). 1/500 ns = 2M/sec per worker; 16 × 2M = 32 million steal attempts/sec aggregate ceiling, which is why the half-stealing heuristic (§19.2) — amortizing one round trip across multiple stolen tasks — matters more as worker count grows.

All ten numbers are derived directly from the System Constraints table (§4); none is asserted without the formula shown above it.
