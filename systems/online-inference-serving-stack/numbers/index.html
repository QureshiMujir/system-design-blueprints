32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  • C1 — FD budget check. 50,000 max concurrent requests/node × 4 FDs/request = 200,000 FDs, matching the 200k FD budget (§4) exactly.
  • C2 — Per-tenant queue memory ceiling. 10,000 max queue depth/tenant × 2 KB/AdmissionTicket = 20 MB per fully-saturated tenant queue, inside per-node memory (§4).
  • C3 — Cluster-wide tenant queue memory, worst case. 50,000 max tenants × 20 MB (C2) = 1,000 GB theoretical ceiling, bounded in practice by the per-node concurrent-tenant fraction, not raw tenant count.
  • C4 — Model-artifact NVMe cache footprint. 20 GB max artifact size × 10 concurrently resident models/node = 200 GB, inside the 512 GB max node memory (§4), leaving 312 GB headroom.
  • C5 — Worker startup transfer time. 20 GB artifact ÷ 1.5 GB/s effective NVMe-to-HBM transfer = 13.3 s, inside the 15 s worker-startup budget (§4).
  • C6 — Scheduler heartbeat fanout cost. 2,000 max cluster workers × 1 heartbeat/2s = 1,000 heartbeats/s per scheduler replica; 1,000 × 500 µs decision budget (§4) = 0.5 CPU-s/s, 50% of one core.
  • C7 — Replay-write bandwidth. 50,000 max concurrent requests/node × 1 KB/ReplayRecord ÷ 12 ms mean p50 residency (§4) = 4.17M records/s theoretical peak, driving the compaction requirement (§19.2 step 14).
  • C8 — GPU fragmentation ceiling, absolute. 24 GB GPU × 15% max fragmentation (§4) = 3.6 GB unusable-but-allocated headroom the residency scorer (§16) must plan around.
  • C9 — Storage amplification, absolute cost. 20 GB max artifact × 1.8x amplification target (§4) = 36 GB total stored per artifact version across the NVMe cache and object-store replica.
  • C10 — Error budget in request-equivalents. 21.6 min/month error budget × 60 s/min × 50,000 req/s peak throughput (§4) = 64.8M request-equivalents of monthly budget, sized against the 99.95% availability target.
