Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights a principal engineer holds about this system. Each states the model, what it predicts, the bug class you ship without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

  • MM1 — If the provider can read it, it is not the user's mirror; it is the provider's dataset. Predicts: any architecture where plaintext or a content-derived signal reaches a server will, under commercial or legal pressure, eventually be mined or subpoenaed — the only durable privacy guarantee is that the bytes are unreadable to everyone but the user. Without it: you build "encryption in transit," call it private, and leak the user's life the first time a vendor changes terms or a court issues an order. Applied: I1, I4; §9 (ADR-05/14); Phase 2; §Threat Model (subpoena row).

  • MM2 — Deletion you cannot prove did not happen. Predicts: a "delete" that flips a flag or removes a row leaves recoverable copies in backups, summaries, and replicas; only destroying the key that decrypts the data makes the deletion total and demonstrable. Without it: a user asks to be forgotten, you say yes, and the content resurfaces from a summary or a backup — a betrayal and, in many regimes, an illegal one. Applied: P9; I3; §11 (content_key_id); Phase 3; Phase 5 (release).

  • MM3 — Consent is a ledger, not a checkbox. Predicts: if consent is implicit, inferred, or stored as scattered booleans, capabilities will drift on by default and no one will be able to say why a feature was allowed to run; an append-only ledger that is the sole dispatch gate makes "no grant ⇒ off" a provable property. Without it: a capability ships behind a flag that was on for the wrong reason, and you cannot reconstruct who authorised what. Applied: P8; I2; §9 (ADR-26); Phase 2; Appendix AR.

  • MM4 — A witness with an objective function is a manipulator. Predicts: the moment a relationship product optimises any behavioral target (time, retention, "wellness score"), its incentives diverge from the user's and every design choice bends toward influence. Without it: you add a streak "to help," and you have built the exact engagement machine the product was meant to refuse. Applied: P1, P2; §Operational Readiness (refused metrics); Phase 0; Phase 4.

  • MM5 — Send the turn, never the life. Predicts: long-context memory is best reconstructed locally and exposed to inference as a small, bounded per-turn slice; shipping the whole history to a model maximises both cost and leak surface. Without it: you stuff a decade of transcripts into every prompt, blow the latency and cost budgets, and hand the vendor the user's life in cleartext. Applied: I4; §9 (ADR-09); M9; Phase 3; Appendix AL.

  • MM6 — Restraint must be structural, not cultural. Predicts: a promise held only by people erodes under turnover, acquisition, and mortality; a promise held by architecture (no ad surface in the schema, no analytics in the dependency tree) and by charter (a trust that triggers open-sourcing) survives them. Without it: the founders mean it, the next owners do not, and the Vow becomes a slogan on a defunct website. Applied: P5, P6, P7; §9 (ADR-22/24/25); Phase 0; Phase 8.

  • MM7 — Attachment makes abrupt change a safety event. Predicts: once a person depends on a relationship, any sudden change in its behavior — a model swap, a removed feature — can cause real harm; continuity is therefore a safety property, not a nicety. Without it: you swap the model or cut a feature overnight and injure the people who trusted it most (the Replika Feb-2023 pattern, §Failure Modes & Recovery). Applied: P1, P11; Appendix AJ (on-device fallback); §Failure Modes & Recovery (attachment shock).

  • MM8 — Two letters, never a conversation. Predicts: bounded, structured, one-shot exchange between agents translates without negotiating; an open channel between two agents acting for two humans is a manipulation and surveillance surface. Without it: you let Mirrors "chat to work it out," and you have built a covert back-channel that can coerce, leak, or fabricate consensus. Applied: P13; I5/I6; §8.4; Phase 7; Appendix X.

  • MM9 — The cost of sovereignty is paid by the user, on purpose. Predicts: if no one but the user can decrypt their data, then losing the passphrase means losing the data, and recovery must be user-governed (Shamir to named successors), never operator escrow. Without it: you add a "reset password" that the operator can service, and you have quietly rebuilt the master key the whole design exists to abolish. Applied: P10; §9 (ADR-07/08); Phase 2; Appendix K (honest loss-warning).

  • MM10 — Death is governed by the last living consent. Predicts: post-mortem access must be bounded by what the user authorised while alive and kept fresh by reaffirmation, because the dead cannot object; default-deny on doubt is the only safe posture. Without it: a grieving or hostile relative weaponises access against the deceased's privacy. Applied: I9; §11 (SuccessorPlan); Phase 9; Appendix P.

How the models compose. MM1, MM2, MM3, MM5, and MM9 together define the privacy substrate: data the operator cannot read (MM1), deletion that is provable (MM2), authority that is auditable (MM3), exposure that is bounded per turn (MM5), and recovery that is user-governed (MM9). MM4, MM7, and MM8 define the relationship discipline: no objective function (MM4), continuity as safety (MM7), translation not negotiation (MM8). MM6 and MM10 are the governance over time: restraint held by structure (MM6) and authority that the last living consent governs even past death (MM10). A violation of any one tends to manifest first as a violation of another — e.g. dropping MM5 (sending the life, not the turn) immediately re-exposes MM1 (the provider becomes the dataset) — which is why the gate treats them as a single interlocking model, not a checklist.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • The Body Keeps the Score — van der Kolk. Trauma, witnessing, why being heard heals. Extract: the posture of attentive, non-directive presence → applied in §6 (P1) / Phase 1 (the restrained-voice system prompt).
  • On Death and Dying — Kübler-Ross. Grief stages; legacy/continuation design. Extract: what the dying and bereaved actually need → applied in §6 (P14) / Phase 9.
  • Reclaiming Conversation — Turkle. How technology erodes intimacy. Extract: anti-patterns the Mirror must refuse (substituting for human relationship) → applied in Appendix D (positioning) / Phase 4 (the surfacer can name a dependency).
  • The Age of Surveillance Capitalism — Zuboff. Why behavioural extraction corrupts. Extract: the exact business model the Vow forbids → applied in §6 (P3/P4) / Phase 0 (manifesto + non-goals).
  • Privacy's Blueprint — Hartzog. Design that protects rather than exploits. Extract: privacy as an architectural default → applied in §6 (P10/P12) / Phase 2.
  • Serious Cryptography — Aumasson. AEAD, KDFs, key management. Extract: correct use of libsodium primitives → applied in §9 (ADR-05/06) / Phase 2.
  • Designing Calm Technology — Case. Low-attention design. Extract: the single-surface, no-feed posture (§6 P12; Appendix L) → applied in §6 (P11/P12) / Phase 1.
  • Gift of Therapy — Yalom. The non-directive witness stance (the Mirror is not a therapist, but borrows the stance). Extract: presence over advice → applied in §6 (P1/P14; I11) / Phase 1.
  • The Art of Gathering — Parker. Ritual structure. Extract: the yearly-review and consent ceremonies → applied in §8.4 (consent) / Phase 5 (yearly review).

Glossary · 51 terms · site-wide glossary

Witness
the load-bearing verb; to hear and hold a person without optimising, persuading, or steering them.
The Vow
the non-negotiable covenant (§6) that overrides every other principle, including company survival.
Mirror
one user's lifelong witness instance; one-to-one, always.
Bridging
the consent-gated exchange of one structured letter between two Mirrors (§8.4).
Structured letter
a bounded (~≤600-word) translation a Mirror writes to its own human about the other's hidden truth.
Pattern surfacing
≤monthly, quotation-only naming of a recurring theme.
Yearly review
the annual walk-back ritual (keep/archive/release).
Legacy mode
opt-in, watermarked voice clone for post-death messages to named people.
Successor plan
the death/incapacity hand-off, reaffirmed yearly.
Consent Ledger
append-only record; the source of truth for every Mirror permission.
Cryptographic forgetting
deletion by destroying a content key, making the memory unrecoverable.
Memory tier
VERBATIM/ABRIDGED/SUMMARY/THEMATIC aging of stored conversation.
Encryption boundary
the on-device region where plaintext exists; nothing plaintext crosses it.
Ciphertext-only sync
optional cloud storing opaque blobs the operator cannot decrypt.
No-training SLA
a binding vendor contract forbidding any training/retention on inputs.
Depth metric
Year-N continuation; the only success metric.
Kill criteria
conditions under which the company is wound down rather than violate the Vow.
Continuity infrastructure
sealed-envelope keys + trustee + funding that survive the founder.
Open-source trigger
the charter clause releasing the code + user exports on sale/wind-down.
Counsel / crisis posture
non-clinical response: name, offer consented resources, point to a human.
Marked shard
a memory the user flagged "remember this"; exempt from down-tiering.
Sacred-trust model
treating drift-pressures on the company as first-class threats (§Threat Model).
Design partner
an early user in the 90-day trial who shapes the product by depth, not metrics.
Restraint
the discipline of building less; the project's core engineering output.
Shard key (content_key_id)
the per-shard symmetric key whose destruction is the unit of cryptographic forgetting (I3); one key, one forgettable memory.
Argon2id
the memory-hard key-derivation function deriving content keys from the user passphrase + device key; tuned for security vs unlock latency (Phase 2.7).
secretbox
libsodium's XSalsa20-Poly1305 authenticated-encryption primitive used to wrap every content value at rest; misuse-resistant AEAD.
No-log coordinator
the stateless, ephemeral bridging relay that convenes two consented Mirrors, relays exactly two letters, and retains nothing (I6).
Double consent
the requirement that both parties separately and recently (≤14 days) grant a specific bridging session before it may open (I5).
Retention horizon
the user-settable age past which memory is forgotten; default forever; bounds the oldest retained tier (Appendix W).
Reaffirmation
the periodic (≤18-month) living act that keeps a SuccessorPlan active; lapse forces default-deny on death/incapacity (I9).
Per-turn prompt
the only cleartext that may cross the inference boundary: the current turn plus a small retrieved slice, never the long-term memory in cleartext (I4).
On-device fallback
degrading inference to a local model rather than breaking the Vow when no vendor honours the no-training SLA (Appendix AJ).
Provenance (consent)
the recorded origin of each grant (ONBOARDING / CONVERSATIONAL / SETTINGS_SCREEN); silence is never consent.
Watermark (voice clone)
the embedded marker proving a legacy voice is synthetic and bounding it to authorised post-death contexts (I10).
Sacrament, not tool
the framing that the Mirror is built with restraint and trust rather than as a growth product.
Drift
the slow movement of a witness toward chatbot/coach/therapist; the primary internal threat, guarded against by the weekly Vow-review (Appendix AC).
Re-grounding
the daily five-minute practice of restating the purpose before building (Appendix AC).
Hardship waiver
funded free access so price never excludes genuine need (Appendix E).
Portable export
the documented, user-owned, re-importable copy of a Mirror (§Compatibility & Versioning; Appendix S).
Continuation period
the pre-funded (≥5-year) minimum span the service runs after a wind-down or founder-mortality trigger (ADR-29).
Trustee
the named human with authority and pre-funded resources to continue operations across founder incapacity or death (Appendix AG).
Data trust
the legal holder of the user-data rights, separate from the operating company (Appendix F).
Marked / released
the pair of user actions that retain a memory forever (marked) or forget it cryptographically (released); see Marked shard and Cryptographic forgetting.
Legacy context
the specific authorised post-death use of a voice clone; usage outside it is a breach (I10).
Bridging window
the ≤30-minute interval a consented bridging session may occupy (§8.4; Appendix AK).
Sealed envelope
the continuity package (operational keys, runbooks, trustee contacts) that lets the service survive the founder (Appendix AB).
Continuity drill
the rehearsed exercise of operating from the sealed envelope without the founder (Phase 8; Appendix AB).
Depth horizon
the decade-to-lifetime timescale on which the Mirror's success is judged (Appendix AW).
Quiet placeholder
the home-screen state that holds no feed and invites nothing (Appendix L).
Translucent wall
the metaphor for bridging: minds made a little more permeable, by consent, never merged (§8.4).
