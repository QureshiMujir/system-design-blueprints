35. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Packrat memory, worked case. Formula: input_bytes × memoised_rules × bytes_per_entry. The §20 Pratt/arithmetic benchmark's 1,000,000-token expression, memoising 4 rules (expr/term/factor/one auxiliary) at ≈ 32 bytes/entry (C5): 1,000,000 × 4 × 32 = 128 MB. This is why packrat is opt-in (P7, ADR-05) — memoising indiscriminately on a 4 GiB input (C1) at even 5 rules would be 4.29×10⁹ × 5 × 32 ≈ 687 GB, obviously infeasible.
  2. Cores to saturate a 10 GbE link with JSON parsing. Formula: link_bytes_per_sec / single_core_throughput. 10 GbE ≈ 1.25 GB/s; M1's 500 MB/s per core ⇒ 1.25×10⁹ / 5×10⁸ = 2.5 → 3 cores, each independent (C10, I5/I11 — no shared state needed).
  3. HTTP request-rate ceiling from parse throughput alone. Formula: throughput_bytes_per_sec / avg_request_bytes. At M2's 100 MB/s and a ≈700-byte average request-line+headers: 10⁸ / 700 ≈ 142,857 requests/s of pure parse work per core (excludes IO/syscall overhead, which dominates in practice).
  4. Error-tree stack footprint at concurrency. Formula: C14_bytes × thread_count. At 192 bytes/error (C14) × 64 threads = 12.3 KB aggregate — negligible; confirms the error-tree design does not bound concurrency.
  5. Recursion stack budget vs. a pathological JSON blob. Formula: depth × bytes_per_ frame. Default limit 256 × 2 KiB (C3) = 512 KiB, comfortably under an 8 MiB thread stack (15× headroom); an unguarded 10,000-level-deep blob would need 10,000 × 2 KiB = 20 MiB — 2.5× the default stack, which is exactly why T1's 256 default exists.
  6. Left-recursion cost collapse from memoisation. Formula (unmemoised worst case): rules × input_length² (each grow step re-parses from scratch, Frost/Hafiz/Callaghan 2007). For the 1M-token benchmark with 4 rules: 4 × (10⁶)² = 4×10¹² operations unmemoised. With packrat (Ford 2002): rules × input_length = 4×10⁶ operations — a 10⁶× reduction, the entire point of combining Ford's linear-time packrat with Frost/Hafiz/Callaghan's seed-and-grow.
  7. Compile-time scaling, mono vs. erased. From §23's worked numbers (90 s mono / 8 s erased at 50 rules): per-rule compile cost ≈ 1.8 s (mono) vs. 0.16 s (erased). Extrapolating to a 200-rule grammar (total ≈ per_rule × rule_count): mono ≈ 360 s (6 min — likely CI-unacceptable), erased ≈ 32 s — the concrete argument behind ADR-02's "past ~40 rules, prefer erasure" guidance.
  8. Fuzzing execution budget for Sealed. Formula: days × seconds_per_day × execs_per_sec. At 30 days (M10) and an assumed ≈2,000 execs/s (typical libFuzzer throughput on a small grammar target): 30 × 86,400 × 2,000 ≈ 5.18×10⁹ executions before Sealed.
  9. Conformance suite per-bucket time budget. Formula: total_budget / bucket_count. M8's ≤90 s aggregate over 23 buckets (§24.5) ⇒ ≤ 3.9 s/bucket average — flags any single bucket consuming disproportionate wall-clock for investigation.
  10. many0 vs. fold0 allocation churn. Formula: N × sizeof(O) × growth_factor. For N=10,000 matches of an 8-byte output with Vec's ≈1.5× amortized geometric growth: 10,000 × 8 × 1.5 ≈ 120 KB transient Vec churn per parse for many0; fold0's accumulator lives in a register/local ⇒ ≈0 bytes — the concrete argument for P8's JSON-literal-fast-path preference (§22).
