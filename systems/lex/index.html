Agent Runtime (untrusted). Runs the agent process; may be malicious, buggy, or compromised. Never crosses the trust boundary holding a private key (P14, §6); the SDK signs using a KMS/HSM-rooted key the runtime cannot extract (ADR-16, §9).

SDK (signing boundary). Constructs and signs ActionEnvelopes (§8), maintains the local hash chain, ships to the Aggregator synchronously, batched, or end-of-session.

Passport Issuer. Mints AgentPassport Verifiable Credentials bound to a DID; HSM-signed (ADR-16); publishes a revocation endpoint with a 60 s propagation SLO (I11, M10).

DID Registry. Resolves did:lex:/did:web:/did:key: identifiers to the public key material used to verify passport and envelope signatures (I3).

Aggregator. Append-only, ingest-time signature and hash-chain verification (I1–I3), periodic Merkle-root anchoring of disputed outcomes only (P4), never every action.

Constitution Registry. Content-addressed store (I5) for JSON-LD Constitutions; publishes by BLAKE3(JCS(canonical_form)) hash, never a mutable identifier (P11).

Negotiation Broker. Carries offer/counter/accept messages between two agents' SDKs and emits a signed MicroContract on mutual acceptance; optional — agents may negotiate peer-to-peer without a broker (Phase 5, §17).

Mesh Coordinator + Judge Pool. Selects a stake-weighted quorum (I7) of independently operated model judges per DisputeCase; each judge emits a structured opinion plus a zk proof of evaluation (I10); ties default to inconclusive (I13).

Insurance API / Regulator Dashboard / Court Evidence Package. Three read-only consumers of AdjudicationOutcome: a carrier claims trigger, a jurisdictionally-scoped aggregate dashboard, and a courtroom-admissible evidence bundle (Phase 8–9, §20–§21).

Worked Example

  1. Buyer agent 0x9f1c and seller agent 0x4b2e bind Constitution 8f4b... (clauses C1 payment cap, C2 delivery obligation).
  2. Buyer's SDK signs an ActionEnvelope for payments.execute (240 USD, under the C1 cap); Aggregator verifies I1–I3, accepts at sequence=4821.
  3. Negotiation Broker emits MicroContract 1c2e... referencing C1/C2 and the 10-widget order.
  4. Day 8: no delivery. Buyer files DisputeCase 5e7d... alleging breach of C2, evidence = seller's ActionLog slice seq 1200–1340, within the 30-day dispute_window (I9).
  5. Mesh Coordinator draws a 7-judge quorum (I7) excluding any judge whose operator is Acme or BetaCo (I8); 6 of 7 opinions find breached on C2, 1 upheld; majority stands (I13 not triggered); AdjudicationOutcome b09a... signed, remedy refund:240USD, anchored per P4.

Malformed-Input Example

A submitted ActionEnvelope with sequence=4822 but prior_hash pointing to entry 4820 (skipping 4821) violates I1/I2. The Aggregator's chain-walk detects the discontinuity at ingest, rejects with actionlog_chain_break_total{agent_did=...} incremented, and quarantines the entry (FM-1, §23) rather than appending a best-effort record (P10 fail-closed).

Failure-Path Example

During OpinionsCollecting for DisputeCase 5e7d..., the zk-Proof Verifier rejects judge J4's proof (I10 violation): J4's operator shares infrastructure with two other quorum members (detected via the operator-diversity check, T4/FM-4, §22–§23). The Coordinator excludes J4's opinion from the tally, redraws one replacement judge from a disjoint operator cluster, and the outcome proceeds once 7 valid opinions are collected — the dispute is never silently resolved on a tainted quorum.

Design principles (§6)

  • P1 — Neutrality above all. Lex must be acceptable to a frontier lab, a sovereign government, an open-source project, and a small enterprise simultaneously. Tradeoff: forfeit exclusive deals and preferential integrations. Operational implication: every partnership contract is reviewed against this principle before signature. Violating it: the spec becomes one vendor's internal tool, destroying the standards-capture moat (§2).
  • P2 — Spec before surface. Every commercial feature is downstream of a published, versioned spec; no closed-source feature requiring a closed-source format. Tradeoff: slower time-to-revenue. Violating it: the spec stops being the moat and becomes marketing collateral for a product.
  • P3 — Cryptographic finality. Every artifact (passport, log entry, constitution, microcontract, outcome) is signed and independently verifiable offline with only public material. Tradeoff: no "trust us" convenience features. Violating it: any "trust the dashboard" surface becomes a single point of forgeable truth.
  • P4 — Anchor only what is disputed. Default path is signed-but-not-anchored; only disputed outcomes and constitution registrations get an external-chain anchor. Tradeoff: routine actions have no chain-level finality, only aggregator-level. Violating it: cost and latency scale linearly with total action volume instead of dispute volume (§32 C8).
  • P5 — Jurisdictional honesty. Constitutions and disputes name a jurisdiction; Lex never pretends to override sovereign law. Tradeoff: some disputes remain gated on slow sovereign process. Violating it: outcomes become legally unenforceable theater.
  • P6 — No agent left behind. The free tier of the SDK and reference mesh must be usable by a hobbyist agent; identity is never gated behind a credit card. Tradeoff: subsidizes low-value usage. Violating it: Lex becomes an enterprise-only tool and loses the long-tail adoption that builds citation authority (§2).
  • P7 — Adjudication transparency. Every AdjudicationOutcome publishes a redacted reasoning trace; black-box arbitration is not arbitration. Tradeoff: reasoning-trace storage and redaction engineering cost. Violating it: outcomes are unappealable fiat, defeating the court-admissibility goal (§21).
  • P8 — Backwards compatibility from v1.0. Once a spec (Passport, ActionLog, Constitution, MicroContract) hits 1.0, it is extended, never broken; breaking changes ship as vN.0 with a 24-month dual-stack window. Tradeoff: legacy fields accumulate. Violating it: every integrator must re-certify on every release, destroying the "Lex Conformant" mark's value.
  • P9 — Standards-track syntax from day one. Every spec is written to be IETF-publishable from its first draft. Tradeoff: more upfront rigor than a move-fast internal format needs. Violating it: Phase 9 standards submission requires a rewrite, wasting the accumulated citation authority.
  • P10 — Fail-closed validation. Malformed or unverifiable envelopes are rejected at ingest, never logged as best-effort. Tradeoff: a buggy SDK's entries are dropped, not salvaged. Violating it: I1–I3 (§7) become advisory instead of enforced, and the aggregator's append-only guarantee is meaningless.
  • P11 — Content-addressing over mutable identifiers. Constitutions and MicroContracts are identified by the hash of their canonical form, never mutated in place. Tradeoff: any amendment is a new document with a new address. Violating it: I5 (§7) cannot hold, and two parties can silently diverge on "the same" agreement's text.
  • P12 — Federated judge diversity over throughput. Quorum selection favors operator and jurisdictional diversity over minimizing latency. Tradeoff: higher Time-to-Outcome (M7–M8) than a centralized judge pool would achieve. Violating it: a fast but concentrated judge pool is trivially capturable (T4, FM-4, §22–§23).
  • P13 — Delegation depth bounding. Capability delegation chains are bounded to depth 5 (I6, §7). Tradeoff: some legitimate deep-delegation use cases (agent-of-agent-of-agent) are unsupported. Violating it: an unbounded chain makes blast-radius analysis after a compromise computationally unbounded.
  • P14 — Minimal trusted computing base at the signing boundary. The agent runtime is always untrusted; only KMS/HSM-rooted key material crosses the signing boundary (ADR-16, §9). Tradeoff: raw-software-key mode exists for hobbyists but is flagged in the passport. Violating it: a single runtime compromise (T9, §22) becomes a silent, undetectable identity theft instead of a flagged, revocable event.
