30. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — A passport is a claim, not a fact. The insight: identity documents assert, they don't guarantee; a passport's truth depends on the issuer's continued trustworthiness and the revocation channel's freshness. Predicts: revocation and issuer-chain trust must be first-class, not afterthoughts. Bug class without it: treating identity as permanent once issued. Applies in §13 (Phase 1 Issuer), I11 (§7).
  • MM2 — An action log is a ledger of intent, not truth. The insight: a signed entry proves the agent asserted an action happened, not that the physical/business outcome was as claimed. Predicts: disputes center on interpretation of a truthful-but-incomplete log, not just forged entries. Bug class without it: assuming "signed" means "undisputable." Applies in §8 Domain Model, §22 Threat Model.
  • MM3 — Anchoring is a scarcity decision. The insight: writing to an external chain is never free, so a protocol must decide what's worth that cost. Predicts: the anchor-only- disputed design (P4) is not an optimization, it's the model. Bug class without it: cost scaling linearly with ingest instead of dispute volume. Applies in §4 System Constraints, §32 Capacity Model, §21 Phase 9.
  • MM4 — Quorum size trades latency for capture-resistance. The insight: every additional judge in a quorum raises the cost of collusion but also raises fan-out latency. Predicts a latency/security curve, not a single "right" quorum size. Bug class without it: optimizing quorum size for speed alone, reintroducing capture risk. Applies in I7 (§7), §18 Phase 6.
  • MM5 — A constitution is a compiled contract, not prose. The insight: natural-language agreements are ambiguous at machine speed; a formal predicate layer (LCL) is what makes automated evaluation possible. Predicts the need for a grammar with deterministic semantics. Bug class without it: two agents disagreeing on what "the same" clause means. Applies in §8 Domain Model, §16 Phase 4.
  • MM6 — A zk proof proves computation happened, not that it was correct. The insight: Groth16/BN254 attests input-binding, not judgment quality. Predicts collusion risk persists even with zk integration. Bug class without it: over-trusting zk as a correctness oracle instead of a process-integrity check. Applies in §19 Phase 7.
  • MM7 — Delegation is capability erosion. The insight: every hop in a delegation chain is a chance for scope creep or compromise amplification. Predicts deeper chains carry proportionally higher risk, justifying a hard depth bound. Bug class without it: unbounded blast-radius analysis after a compromise. Applies in P13 (§6), §22 Threat Model.
  • MM8 — Standards capture is a lagging, not leading, indicator. The insight: citation authority (ISO/IETF adoption) follows deployed usage; it cannot be won by lobbying alone. Predicts premature standards engagement wastes scarce founder attention before Phase 8-9 telemetry exists to point to. Bug class without it: chasing prestige before proof. Applies in §2 Why Build This, §21 Phase 9.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Boneh, D. & Shoup, V., A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography (free online, updated editions) — ch. 13 (digital signatures), ch. 8 (hash functions). Extract: EdDSA construction and security proof sketch, hash-function collision resistance requirements. Applied in §13, §14 (Phase 1–2 signing/hashing primitives).
  • Kaufman, C., Perlman, R. & Speciner, M., Network Security: Private Communication in a Public World, 2nd ed. — chapters on PKI and certificate revocation. Extract: revocation- list design tradeoffs (push vs. pull), certificate-chain validation pitfalls. Applied in §13 (Phase 1 Issuer + revocation).
  • Lessig, L., Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace — the "code is law" argument. Extract: why a machine-readable rule set changes enforceability compared to prose. Applied in §8 Domain Model and §16 (Phase 4 Constitution format).
  • Wong, D., Real-World Cryptography — ch. 13 (zero-knowledge proofs). Extract: practical deployment tradeoffs of zk-SNARK systems (trusted setup, proof size, verifier cost). Applied in §19 (Phase 7 zk-proof integration).
  • Thaler, J., Proofs, Arguments, and Zero-Knowledge — chapters on SNARK construction and the sumcheck protocol. Extract: what a zk proof actually attests (computation happened on claimed inputs) vs. what it does not (that the computation's judgment was correct). Applied in §19, MM6 §30.
  • Kleppmann, M., Designing Data-Intensive Applications — ch. 5 (replication), ch. 11 (stream processing/append-only logs). Extract: append-only log design, replication-lag tradeoffs. Applied in §15 (Phase 3 Aggregator) and §18 (Phase 6 Mesh Coordinator).
  • Antonopoulos, A., Mastering Bitcoin, 3rd ed. — chapters on Merkle trees and transaction fees. Extract: Merkle-proof construction, fee-market economics for anchoring decisions. Applied in §15 (Merkle anchor scheduler) and §20 (Phase 8 dual-anchor strategy).
  • Birman, K., Guide to Reliable Distributed Systems — chapters on quorum systems and Byzantine fault tolerance. Extract: quorum-intersection requirements under adversarial nodes. Applied in §18 (Phase 6 judge-selection quorum design).

Papers

  • Lamport, L., Shostak, R. & Pease, M., "The Byzantine Generals Problem," ACM TOPLAS, 1982. Contribution: formalizes agreement under malicious actors. Applied in §18 (Phase 6 quorum design against colluding judges).
  • Castro, M. & Liskov, B., "Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance," OSDI, 1999. Contribution: a practical BFT protocol with bounded message complexity. Applied in §18, §20 (Phase 8 SLA tiering).
  • Groth, J., "On the Size of Pairing-Based Non-interactive Arguments," EUROCRYPT, 2016. Contribution: the Groth16 zk-SNARK construction Lex adopts as its initial proving system. Applied in §19 (Phase 7).
  • Ben-Sasson, E. et al., "Scalable, Transparent, and Post-Quantum Secure Computational Integrity," IACR ePrint 2018/046 (STARKs). Contribution: transparent-setup, post-quantum proof system. Applied as the migration alternative discussed in §19.10 and the Deferred Features Register (§35).
  • Sporny, M. et al. (eds.), "Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0," W3C Recommendation. Contribution: the credential envelope Agent Passport is built on. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Reed, D. et al. (eds.), "Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) v1.0," W3C Recommendation. Contribution: the DID syntax and resolution model did:lex extends. Applied in §13.
  • Merkle, R., "A Digital Signature Based on a Conventional Encryption Function," CRYPTO,
  • Contribution: hash trees for efficient batch authentication. Applied in §15 (Merkle anchor scheduler).
  • Ongaro, D. & Ousterhout, J., "In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm (Raft)," USENIX ATC, 2014. Contribution: an understandable replicated-log consensus protocol. Applied in §15 (Phase 3 Aggregator replication).
  • Douceur, J., "The Sybil Attack," IPTPS, 2002. Contribution: formalizes the cost of identity in open systems without a trusted issuer. Applied directly against T5 (§22) and §13 (Phase 1 issuer reputation controls).
  • Back, A., "Hashcash — A Denial of Service Counter-Measure," 2002. Contribution: proof-of-work as a rate-limiting primitive. Applied in §17 (Phase 5 bonding) and §18 (Phase 6 stake-to-file dispute bonding).

Reference Implementations

  • W3C did-core + digitalbazaar/did-method-key-js — DID method resolution reference. Lesson: method registration is separate from resolution security. Pitfall: do not conflate "registered with W3C" with "safe to resolve without pinning." Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Hyperledger Aries (hyperledger/aries-framework-go) — verifiable-credential exchange and revocation-list protocols. Lesson: batched revocation-list publication avoids leaking per-check metadata. Pitfall: naive per-credential revocation checks create a timing side channel. Applied in §13 (Phase 1 revocation).
  • Sigstore / Rekor (sigstore/rekor) — append-only, Merkle-tree-backed transparency log for software signing. Lesson: transparency-log design for the Aggregator (§15). Pitfall: consistency-proof cost grows with log size if not batched. Applied in §15 (Phase 3).
  • Certificate Transparency (google/certificate-transparency-go, RFC 6962/9162) — gossip and audit protocols that detect log equivocation. Lesson: split-view attacks on an append-only log require independent auditors, not just one aggregator. Applied in §15, T2/FM-1 (§22–§23).
  • circom / snarkjs (iden3/circom, iden3/snarkjs) — Groth16 circuit compiler and prover toolchain. Lesson: circuit-design constraints for zk statements. Pitfall: trusted- setup ceremony risk if not run as a multi-party computation. Applied in §19 (Phase 7).
  • Tendermint / CometBFT (cometbft/cometbft) — BFT consensus reference implementation with slashing. Lesson: quorum-based Byzantine consensus with economic penalties. Pitfall: long-range attacks without weak-subjectivity checkpoints. Applied in §18 (Phase 6), FM-4 (§23).

Glossary (§36) · 46 terms · site-wide glossary

AAP — Active Agent Passport:
a passport with at least one signed action in the last 7 days.
Adjudication Mesh:
the federated network of model-judge operators that resolves disputes per a constitution's adjudication_policy (§18).
AdjudicationOutcome:
the signed, content-addressed verdict produced by the mesh (§8, §11).
ActionEnvelope:
the signed, hash-chained unit an SDK produces per agent action (§11).
Agent Passport:
the DID-rooted, Verifiable-Credential-bundled identity of an autonomous agent.
Anchor:
publication of a content hash to an external chain for finality (P4).
BFT — Byzantine Fault Tolerance:
the property of reaching agreement despite malicious participants (Lamport/Shostak/Pease, §5).
Bloom Filter:
a probabilistic set membership structure used for the revocation-list working set (§32 C9).
CIF — Constitutions In Force:
registered constitutions whose effective date has passed and whose expiry has not.
Constitution:
a JSON-LD document specifying the rules of engagement between two parties' agents (§8, §16).
Content-address:
an identifier equal to the hash of a document's canonical form (I5).
Controller:
the human or organization legally responsible for an agent (controller_did).
DID — Decentralized Identifier:
W3C-standard URI scheme for self-sovereign identity.
Delegation Chain:
the ordered list of capability-delegation links bounded to depth 5 (I6).
DisputeCase:
a filed allegation that a MicroContract's terms have been breached (§8).
Ed25519:
the default digital-signature scheme used for passport and action-log signing.
Groth16:
the zk-SNARK proving system used for opinion-evaluation proofs (ADR-22, §19).
Hash-chain:
the linked-by-hash structure making an ActionLog tamper-evident (I1).
HSM — Hardware Security Module:
a physical device custodying signing keys (ADR-16).
Issuer:
an entity authorized to mint Verifiable Credentials for agent passports (§13).
JCS — JSON Canonicalization Scheme:
RFC 8785, the deterministic serialization used for content-addressing (I5).
KMS — Key Management Service:
a cloud-hosted signing-key custody service (ADR-16).
LCL — Lex Clause Language:
the Datalog-subset formal predicate language for constitution clauses (§8, §16).
Merkle Root:
the single hash summarizing a batch of entries for anchoring (§32 C10).
Mesh Coordinator:
the service that draws quorums and tallies opinions (§18).
Mesh Node:
an independently operated participant in the Adjudication Mesh.
MicroContract:
a binding executable agreement between two agents under a constitution (§8, §17).
NSM — North-Star Metric:
SignedActionLog entries per week from production (non-pilot) agents (§4).
Nonce:
a single-use value preventing offer/action replay (§17.8).
Passport Issuer:
a service that mints Agent Passport VCs (§13).
PKCS#11:
the standard interface for HSM-backed signing operations (ADR-16).
Quorum:
the constitution-defined number of mesh judges required for an outcome (I7).
Revocation List:
issuer-published list of passports no longer valid (§13, I11).
RTO — Recovery Time Objective:
maximum acceptable time to restore service after failure (§24).
Self-hash:
the hash of an ActionLog entry's own content, chained by the next entry's prior_hash (§11).
Sequence:
the strictly increasing per-agent entry counter (I2).
Sigstore/Rekor:
the transparency-log reference system informing Aggregator design (§5, §31).
SignedActionLog:
the per-agent, hash-chained, signed log of agent actions.
Slashing:
the economic penalty applied to a judge's staked collateral for proven malfeasance (§19).
Stake-weighting:
biasing quorum selection probability by a judge's posted collateral (§18).
Sybil Attack:
the creation of many cheap fake identities to gain disproportionate influence (Douceur, §5; T5, §22).
TSC — Technical Steering Committee:
the governance body holding the merge bit on spec changes (§27).
TTO — Time-to-Outcome:
elapsed time from DisputeCase filing to AdjudicationOutcome.
VC — Verifiable Credential:
W3C-standard cryptographically verifiable claim.
Weak Subjectivity:
the checkpoint mechanism preventing long-range consensus-history rewrites (Tendermint/CometBFT lesson, §5, §18).
zk proof:
a zero-knowledge proof asserting a statement without revealing the underlying witness — here, that a judge evaluated the supplied inputs (I10, MM6 §30).
