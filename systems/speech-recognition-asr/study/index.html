34. Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights a principal ASR engineer holds. Each: the model, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

  • MM1 — The front-end is a lossy, deliberate projection: waveform → a sequence of decorrelated, perceptually-warped, log-compressed vectors. Every step discards information on purpose (phase, fine spectral detail, channel mean) so the acoustic model sees only what distinguishes phones. Predicts: changing any front-end parameter (mel formula, DCT norm, lifter, CMVN axis) shifts WER systematically with no crash; the front-end caps achievable accuracy. Bug class without it: "my model trains fine but WER is 8% worse than the paper" — silent train/serve feature skew or an oracle-mismatched convention. Applied: Phase 1–2 (§13–§14), ADR-10/ADR-31, I1.
  • MM2 — EM is coordinate ascent on a lower bound; the E-step fills in the latent variable, the M-step re-fits, and the data likelihood never decreases. GMM responsibilities and HMM occupancy γ are the same idea at two scales. Predicts: a correct implementation's training likelihood is monotone non-decreasing; any drop is a bug, not bad luck. Bug class without it: treating an oscillating likelihood as "needs more iterations" instead of finding the sign/accumulator error. Applied: Phase 3–4 (§15–§16), I6, P5.
  • MM3 — Baum-Welch over a concatenated transcript-HMM learns phone models from un-segmented audio: the alignment is a hidden variable you marginalize, not data you need. Embedded training is why you never hand-label phone boundaries. Predicts: given only audio + word transcripts + a lexicon, the system can bootstrap from a flat (uniform) start. Bug class without it: building a pipeline that demands phone-level labels (which don't exist at scale). Applied: Phase 5 (§17), MM2.
  • MM4 — Context explodes combinatorially; tying is the bias-variance dial that buys it back. |phones|³ triphones can't each get data; a phonetic decision tree pools acoustically-similar states to share parameters. Predicts: too-fine a tree (low min-occupancy) overfits; too-coarse underfits; there is a leaf budget that minimizes held-out WER. Bug class without it: either monophone-only (underfit, leaves accuracy on the table) or untied triphones (most contexts unseen → garbage). Applied: Phase 6 (§18), I15, ADR-8.
  • MM5 — Decoding is single-source shortest-path on a weighted graph that is the composition of four transducers (H∘C∘L∘G). The acoustic model, context, lexicon, and grammar are all weighted automata; recognition is one Viterbi search over their product. Predicts: every decode decision (beam, LM weight, word penalty, token recombination) is a graph-search parameter, not a model parameter; pruning trades search errors for speed but never changes the model. Bug class without it: welding the LM into the AM, or "fixing" deletions by retraining instead of widening the beam. Applied: Phase 9 (§21), I8, ADR-12.
  • MM6 — CTC is the alignment-free dual of Baum-Welch: a blank symbol turns "no boundaries" into a sum over all monotonic alignments, computed by the same forward-backward DP. Predicts: CTC and HMM forward-backward share numerics (log-space, scaling) and failure modes (underflow); the gradient is posterior − expected-occupancy. Bug class without it: doing per-frame argmax without blank semantics → duplicated/dropped tokens; or computing CTC in the linear domain → NaN on any real utterance. Applied: Phase 11 (§23, shared with #387), I4/I5, MM2.
  • MM7 — A weak per-frame sensor plus a strong sequence prior beats either alone: the LM rescues the acoustic model. The acoustic posterior is noisy and locally confusable ("yes"/"yas"); the LM supplies global plausibility, fused as an additive log-score in beam search. Predicts: an LM-weight sweep has a WER minimum; too little LM → acoustic noise dominates (substitutions), too much → the LM hallucinates (deletions/insertions imbalance). Bug class without it: tuning the AM to fix errors the LM should fix, or shipping with an untuned acoustic scale. Applied: Phase 8–9 (§20–§21), P7, the WER-reduction gate (§4).
  • MM8 — Everything probabilistic lives in log-space, and the only honest correctness promise is a WER tolerance, not bit-equality. Products of thousands of small probabilities underflow; log-sum-exp + per-frame scaling keep them finite, but FP reduction order then makes bit-reproducibility impossible across thread counts. Predicts: a parallel decode will differ from a serial one in the last ULP but not in WER; promising bits is a contract you'll break on any GEMM/FFT reorder. Bug class without it: linear-domain underflow to zero → NaN; or a flaky CI that fails on a legitimate numeric reorder because it asserted bit-equality. Applied: §4 numeric boundary, P2/P10, ADR-14/ADR-15, I2/I4.
  • MM9 — Acoustic and LM scores live on different scales; the acoustic scale is the knob that makes them commensurable. Acoustic log-likelihoods (summed over ~100 frames/word) dwarf LM log-probs (one per word), so they must be reweighted (acoustic_scale, or equivalently lmwt) before they can be added in beam search. Predicts: the WER-vs-lmwt curve is U-shaped with a single minimum; the word-insertion penalty trades insertions against deletions orthogonally. Bug class without it: "the LM does nothing" (scale wrong) or "the decoder only outputs short utterances" (penalty wrong) — tuning the AM to fix a scale bug. Applied: Phase 9 (§21), §21.2 lm_fusion, MM7, the M3 gate.
  • MM10 — The lattice is the hypothesis space; the 1-best is one path through it, and confidence is its posterior mass. Decoding doesn't produce an answer, it produces a weighted graph of plausible answers; keeping it enables rescoring with a stronger LM, N-best extraction, and per-word confidence — all without re-decoding. Predicts: a word whose lattice posterior is spread across competitors is low-confidence even if it's the 1-best; rescoring only helps if the true word is in the lattice (so lattice oracle-WER lower-bounds achievable WER). Bug class without it: shipping 1-best-only then discovering you can't add confidence or rescore; or trusting a 1-best word the model was 51/49 on. Applied: Phase 9 (§21), ADR-13, I8/I10, §8.9.

Required reading (§5)

5.1 Books

  • Rabiner & Juang, Fundamentals of Speech Recognition — the classical bible: MFCC, HMM, Baum-Welch, Viterbi, the whole GMM-HMM pipeline. Applied: the semantics oracle for the front-end (§13–§14) through the decoder (§21); directly grounds Phase 4 forward-backward (§16) and Phase 5 Viterbi (§17).
  • Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3e draft) — ch. on ASR, HMM, n-gram LMs (backoff/Kneser-Ney), WFST decoding, WER. Applied: the n-gram LM (Phase 8, §20), WFST decode (Phase 9, §21), and WER metric (Phase 12, §24); the integrative reference shared with #387.
  • Huang, Acero, Hon, Spoken Language Processing — front-end, acoustic modeling, decoding, search. Applied: the engineering depth behind the front-end (§13–§14) and search/decoder (§21); informs the Threat Model (§26) resource-bound thinking.
  • Gold, Morgan, Ellis, Speech and Audio Signal Processing — DSP foundations: framing, windowing, spectra, filterbanks, the cepstrum. Applied: the #369-facing front-end in Phase 1 (§13) and Phase 2 (§14); MM1.
  • Rabiner, "A Tutorial on Hidden Markov Models…" (Proc. IEEE 1989) — the HMM tutorial: the three problems (evaluation/decoding/training), scaling, re-estimation. Applied: Phase 4 scaled forward-backward + Baum-Welch (§16) and Phase 5 Viterbi (§17); invariants I2/I6; MM2.
  • Bishop, Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning — ch.9 (EM, GMM), ch.13 (sequential data, HMM forward-backward — the parent of CTC). Applied: Phase 3 GMM EM (§15) and the EM→Baum-Welch→CTC arc (§16, §23); MM2/MM6.
  • Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, Deep Learning — ch.9–10 (CNN/RNN-LSTM). Applied: the neural encoder in Phase 10 (§22) and CTC training context in Phase 11 (§23).
  • Mohri, Pereira, Riley, "Weighted Finite-State Transducers in Speech Recognition" (Computer Speech & Language 2002) — the WFST framework (H/C/L/G, composition, determinization). Applied: the decoder's HCLG theory in Phase 9 (§21); the deferred full-WFST feature (§39); MM5.

5.2 Papers

  • Davis & Mermelstein, "Comparison of Parametric Representations…MFCC" (IEEE ASSP 1980) — the MFCC front-end origin. Applied: Phase 2 MFCC (§14); MM1.
  • Baum, Petrie, Soules, Weiss, "A Maximization Technique… (Baum-Welch)" (Annals Math. Stat. 1970) — the HMM EM re-estimation. Applied: Phase 4 Baum-Welch (§16); invariant I6; MM2.
  • Viterbi, "Error Bounds for Convolutional Codes… (Viterbi algorithm)" (IEEE IT 1967) — the max-probability path DP. Applied: Phase 5 Viterbi decode/align (§17) and the token-passing search (§21); MM5.
  • Young, Odell, Woodland, "Tree-Based State Tying for High-Accuracy Acoustic Modelling" (HLT 1994) — phonetic decision-tree triphone state tying. Applied: Phase 6 (§18); invariant I15; ADR-8; MM4.
  • Graves et al., "Connectionist Temporal Classification" (ICML 2006) — CTC. Applied: Phase 11 CTC loss/decode (§23, shared with #387); invariants I4/I5; MM6.
  • Graves & Jaitly, "Towards End-to-End Speech Recognition with RNNs" (ICML 2014) & Hannun et al., "Deep Speech" (2014) — CNN/RNN+CTC ASR + LM beam decoding. Applied: the neural encoder + CTC + LM-fusion decode in Phase 10–11 (§22–§23); the DNN+CTC alternative (§38 R2).
  • Povey et al., "The Kaldi Speech Recognition Toolkit" (ASRU 2011) — the WFST decoder + the differential oracle. Applied: Phase 9 decoder design (§21) and the conformance oracle (Phase 13, §25); ADR-12/ADR-21.
  • Chen & Goodman, "An Empirical Study of Smoothing Techniques for Language Modeling" (1998) — Kneser-Ney and friends. Applied: Phase 8 LM smoothing (§20); ADR-11.
  • Gales, "Maximum Likelihood Linear Transforms for HMM-Based Speech Recognition" (Computer Speech & Language 1998) — MLLR/fMLLR speaker adaptation. Applied: the theory behind the deferred adaptation feature (§39); informs the GMM-HMM re-estimation in Phase 4 (§16).
  • Hermansky, "Perceptual Linear Prediction (PLP)" (JASA 1990) — the alternative front-end. Applied: the rejected-front-end regime in ADR-10 (informative; MFCC default in Phase 2, §14).

5.3 Reference Implementations

  • Kaldi (src/gmm, src/hmm, src/tree decision-tree tying, src/decoder, src/fstext/egs/* recipes) — the differential oracle + architecture source. Study GMM est, tree tying, the WFST decoder, the s5 recipe. Applied: GMM (§15), tree tying (§18), decoder (§21), conformance oracle (§25). Study; don't copy (Apache-2.0; we re-derive in Rust). Pitfall: deep OpenFst coupling — we do HCLG-lite (§39).
  • HTK (HTKLib/HRec.c, HERest, HVite, HDecode) — the canonical GMM-HMM toolkit: embedded training, token passing. Applied: the embedded-training recipe (Phase 5, §17) and token-passing decoder (Phase 9, §21); the differential oracle (§25). Pitfall: dated C, licensing.
  • wav2letter / flashlight (src/criterion CTC/ASG, decoder) — clean CTC + beam decoder reference. Applied: the CTC criterion + lexicon-constrained beam in Phase 11 (§23).
  • Mozilla DeepSpeech (acoustic model + CTC + KenLM scorer) — CTC + n-gram-LM shallow-fusion decoder. Applied: the LM-fusion beam recipe in Phase 11 (§23, shared with #387); the DNN+CTC alternative (§38 R2).
  • librosa / python_speech_features — MFCC/filterbank reference for the front-end. Applied: the pinned-param oracle in Phase 2 (§14, ADR-31). Study; don't depend ("from scratch"). Pitfall: defaults differ (we pin ours, document).
  • KenLM / SRILM — n-gram estimation + ARPA + query. Applied: the Kneser-Ney + ARPA reference in Phase 8 (§20). Pitfall: heavy C++.
  • warp-ctc — numerically-stable log-space CTC forward-backward. Applied: the ctc crate reference in Phase 11 (§23, shared with #387); invariant I4.

Glossary (§40) · 60 terms · site-wide glossary

ARPA
text format for n-gram LMs (log-prob + backoff).
Backoff / Kneser-Ney
n-gram smoothing: redistribute probability to unseen n-grams via lower orders / continuation counts.
Baum-Welch
the HMM EM: forward-backward occupancy → re-estimate transitions + emissions.
Beam search
pruned best-first/Viterbi search keeping the top hypotheses.
CMVN
Cepstral Mean (and Variance) Normalization of features.
CTC
Connectionist Temporal Classification; alignment-free sequence loss with a blank label (shared with #387).
CTM
time-marked output format (utt chan tbeg dur word conf).
Decision-tree state tying
clustering acoustically-similar triphone states to share a GMM (Young 1994).
DCT
Discrete Cosine Transform; decorrelates log-mel energies → MFCC.
Embedded training
training HMMs from un-segmented utterances by concatenating phone models along the transcript.
Forced alignment
Viterbi against a known transcript to get phone/word time boundaries.
Forward-backward
the DP computing α (forward) and β (backward) occupancy in an HMM/CTC.
GMM
Gaussian Mixture Model; the diagonal-covariance HMM-state emission.
HCLG
the composed WFST H∘C∘L∘G (HMM ∘ context ∘ lexicon ∘ grammar) searched at decode.
HMM
Hidden Markov Model; left-to-right states with transition + emission probabilities.
Lattice
word graph of competing hypotheses with acoustic + LM weights.
Log-mel / MFCC
log mel-filterbank energies / their DCT (the front-end features).
Mel scale
perceptual frequency warping for the filterbank.
Perplexity
exp(−avg log P); the LM-quality metric.
Pre-emphasis
a high-pass filter boosting high frequencies before framing.
RTF
Real-Time Factor = decode time ÷ audio duration.
Scaling (forward-backward)
per-frame normalization to avoid underflow in α/β.
Token passing
the HMM/WFST Viterbi decode where tokens propagate along arcs carrying score + backpointer.
Triphone
a phone in its left/right context (context-dependent model).
Viterbi
the max-probability path DP (decoding/alignment).
WER / CER
Word / Character Error Rate = edit distance ÷ reference length.
WFST
Weighted Finite-State Transducer; the algebra unifying AM/lexicon/LM for decoding.
G2P
grapheme-to-phoneme; predicts pronunciations for OOV words.
Senone / tied state
a clustered context-dependent HMM state sharing a GMM (a decision-tree leaf).
Monophone / triphone
a context-independent / left-right-context-dependent phone model.
Flat start
uniform-segmentation initialization for the first monophone training pass.
Spectrogram
the time × frequency power representation from the framed, windowed FFT.
Windowing
tapering each frame (Hamming/Hann) before the FFT to reduce spectral leakage.
Cepstrum / liftering
the transform of the log spectrum (MFCCs are mel-cepstral) / scaling its coefficients.
Frame / hop
the analysis window (≈25 ms) and its advance step (≈10 ms).
Dithering
adding tiny noise before quantization to decorrelate quantization error.
Acoustic scale
the weight balancing acoustic vs LM scores during decoding.
Token recombination
merging decoder tokens that reach the same state, keeping the best (bounds the beam).
Shallow fusion
adding the LM log-prob to the acoustic score during beam search (CTC path).
Sufficient statistics
the accumulated occupancy (γ) and transition (ξ) counts re-estimation needs.
OOV
out-of-vocabulary word (absent from the lexicon/LM); handled via G2P / backoff / ⟨unk⟩.
VAD
voice activity detection — speech vs silence/noise segmentation.
Lattice rescoring
re-weighting a decoded lattice with a stronger (e.g. larger-order or neural) LM.
Endpointing
detecting utterance start/end for streaming recognition (deferred §39).
Real speech vs synthetic gap
the accuracy drop from training on synthetic/clean audio and testing on real recordings.
Bakis topology
the standard left-to-right HMM with self-loops and single-step forward transitions (no skips).
Senone
a tied context-dependent HMM state (a decision-tree leaf); synonym for tied state (Kaldi pdf-id).
PDF id
the integer index of a tied-state probability density function (the GMM/posterior the state shares).
LVCSR
Large-Vocabulary Continuous Speech Recognition — the unconstrained-vocabulary decoding the decoder targets.
Tropical (log) semiring
the (min, +) algebra in which WFST weights combine for Viterbi shortest-path.
Epsilon (ε) arc
a WFST transition consuming no input/output symbol (silence, backoff) requiring an ε-filter in composition.
Blank (CTC)
the extra output symbol separating repeated labels and absorbing non-emitting frames; the key to alignment-free training.
Prefix beam search
CTC decoding that tracks blank/non-blank prefix probabilities to merge alignments mapping to the same label string.
Shallow vs deep LM fusion
adding LM log-prob during beam search (shallow) vs integrating the LM into the network (deep); this design uses shallow.
Acoustic scale / LM weight
the multiplier reconciling per-frame acoustic scores with per-word LM scores in decoding (MM9).
Word-insertion penalty
a per-word decode bias trading insertions against deletions.
Lattice beam
the (≤ search-beam) threshold deciding which competing arcs are retained in the output lattice.
Mixture splitting (mixing up)
doubling GMM components (μ ± ε·σ) then re-training, growing model capacity (HTK recipe).
Variance flooring
clamping GMM variances to a minimum to prevent a component collapsing onto one frame (→ ∞ likelihood).
Forward variable α / backward variable β
the partial probabilities of the prefix/suffix observations in the forward-backward DP.
