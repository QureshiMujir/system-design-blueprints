33. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. L1 working-set fit (ADR-03's justification). A binary kernel (2 inputs + 1 output) over i32 at the default 2048-row vector: 3 × 2048 rows × 4 B = 24,576 B ≈ 24 KiB, fitting within the 32 KiB L1 (§4) with headroom for validity bitmaps and register spill.
  2. ClickHouse's 65536-row Block by the same formula. 3 × 65536 × 4 B ≈ 768 KiB — exceeds both L1 (32 KiB) and most of L2 (1 MiB, §4), confirming ClickHouse deliberately trades cache residency for lower per-batch dispatch overhead (§32).
  3. Morsel dispatch-overhead ceiling. At a ~200 ns per-morsel dispatch cost (queue pop + push
    • branch) and 100,000 rows/morsel (ADR-04): 100,000 rows / 200 ns ≈ 5 × 10¹¹ rows/s ceiling from dispatch alone — roughly 500× above the ~10⁹ rows/s operator targets (M8), confirming morsel size is not the throughput bottleneck.
  4. Memory-bandwidth scan ceiling. At 20 GB/s sustained per NUMA node (§4) over an i64 column (8 B/value): 20 × 10⁹ / 8 ≈ 2.5 × 10⁹ rows/s per node — leaves ≈ 2.5× headroom over the M8 target (≥ 1B rows/s scan + agg) for the additional arithmetic passes over the same bytes.
  5. SIMD lane-width speedup ceiling. AVX2 (256-bit) over i32 (32-bit): 256 / 32 = 8 lanes → theoretical 8× speedup. M4's ≥ 4× target captures roughly 4/8 = 50% vectorization efficiency, the realistic gap from bounds checks, tail loops, and load/store bubbles (§14.7).
  6. TPC-H Q1 hash-table footprint. Q1's GROUP BY (l_returnflag, l_linestatus) has at most 4 distinct groups at any scale factor — hash-table memory is O(1), a few KB regardless of SF, contrasted with #7 below.
  7. A high-cardinality group-by's footprint. GROUP BY l_orderkey at SF=1 (≈ 1.5M orders): (1.5 × 10⁶ / 0.7 load factor) × 64 B/bucket ÷ 4 keys/bucket ≈ 137 MB — comfortably within the 4 GiB default budget (§4) without spilling.
  8. Hash-join build-side footprint at SF=100. orders as the build side (≈ 150M rows, SF × 1.5M): (150 × 10⁶ / 0.7) × 16 B/row (amortized bucket share) ≈ 3.4 GB — approaches the 4 GiB default per-query budget (§4), explaining why SF=100 benchmarks (M9/M10) are exactly the regime that exercises spill (§20) in practice, not a corner case.
  9. Spill partition-count sizing (the formula §20.7 promised). To keep each grace-hash sub-partition within a 256 MB target, given #8's 3.4 GB build side: ceil(3.4 GB / 256 MB) = 14, rounded up to the next power of two → 16 partitions.
  10. FD-budget validation. 16 partitions (#9) × up to 4 concurrently spilling operators/query (§4's FD assumption) = 64 open files for one query — the stated ≤ 256 FD budget/query (§4) covers up to 4 such joins spilling simultaneously within one plan, validating that number against a realistic worst case rather than an arbitrary pick.
  11. Cross-NUMA steal overhead. At a ~200 ns cross-NUMA latency delta (§4) and a 100,000-row morsel taking ≈ 100 µs to process locally (from #4's per-node throughput), an occasional stolen morsel processed with some fraction of remote cache misses adds only a low single-digit percentage to that morsel's cost — why I8's 90%-local threshold (not 99%) is an engineered tradeoff, not an arbitrary number.
  12. Worst-case spill disk provisioning. Storage amplification ≤ 1.3× (§4) applied to a ~100 GB SF=100 dataset gives ≈ 130 GB per full spill; combined with the 10× spill quota (§20.2), disk provisioning guidance for a production deployment should budget toward the upper end of that range per concurrently spilling query, not the typical (unspilled) case.
