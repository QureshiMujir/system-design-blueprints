32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Sandbox-pool node capacity. 16 vCPU / 64 GiB node ÷ (2 vCPU / 4 GiB per sandbox) = 8 concurrent sandboxes/node (§4 row 6).
  2. Nodes needed for 20 concurrent pipelines/org (§4 row 5), ~2 sandboxes in flight per pipeline on average → 20 × 2 = 40 concurrent sandboxes ÷ 8/node = 5 worker nodes at peak per org-cohort of this size.
  3. Artifact storage growth/day. Assume 100 pipelines/day × 4 artifacts/pipeline × avg 5 MB (well under the 100 MB cap, §4) = 100 × 4 × 5 MB = 2 GB/day raw, × 1.3 amplification (§4 row: storage amplification) ≈ 2.6 GB/day.
  4. (continuing numbering) Postgres row growth/day. 100 pipelines × ~6 steps × (1 Run.steps[] row + 1 audit event) = 100 × 6 × 2 = 1,200 rows/day control-plane-side — trivially small until org count reaches ~10,000× this.
  5. Audit-write throughput required at 100 pipelines/day. 1,200 events ÷ 86,400 s ≈ 0.014 events/s average — far under the M5 p99 < 10 ms budget; headroom is ~5–10k events/s (§29), i.e. ~6 orders of magnitude of growth before this becomes the bottleneck.
  6. LLM token spend/pipeline (Coder+Tester+DevOps+Reviewer in-budget, §4 row 12). (60k+30k)+(40k+20k)+(50k+25k)+(80k+10k) = 90k+60k+75k+90k = 315k tokens/pipeline ceiling (actual usage is typically well under ceiling).
  7. FD budget check. 65,536 FDs/worker (§4) ÷ (~6 FDs per in-flight sandbox connection: Docker socket + stdio pipes + S3 + Postgres) ≈ ~10,900 concurrent sandbox connections/worker theoretical ceiling — never binding before the 8-sandboxes/node CPU/mem ceiling (item 1) is hit first.
  8. Monitor polling load per 1,000 deployed services. 1,000 services ÷ 15-min cycle (§21.2, ADR-20) = 1,000/900 s ≈ 1.1 CloudWatch metric-pull calls/s sustained — batched per customer account (§21.7) to avoid linear STS-call scaling.
