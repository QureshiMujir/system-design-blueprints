32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — Every synthesis technique is a bandwidth-management problem. You are always deciding where the energy above Nyquist goes: suppressed by construction (BLIT/PolyBLEP, P2), pushed above the audible range by oversampling (Phase 9), or allowed to fold back as measurable, budgeted aliasing (§4 floor). Without this model you write oscillators that "sound fine" until a high note exposes silent, uncontrolled folding. Resolved in §8, §14, §21.

MM2 — A filter is a feedback system, so every exposed parameter is a stability claim. Cutoff and resonance are not just tone knobs; they are coordinates in a stability region you must prove, not assume (P4). Without this model you ship a filter that works at demo settings and blows up at a user's extreme setting. Resolved in §7 (I2), §16.

MM3 — Real-time is a worst-case contract, not an average-case one. A callback that meets its deadline 999,999 times out of 1,000,000 still has an xrun budget violation if that one miss happens (§4 SLO). Without this model you optimize mean CPU and ship tail-latency regressions. Resolved in §23, §30.

MM4 — The control/audio thread boundary is the one true concurrency boundary. Everything else in the system can be reasoned about sequentially once you accept that only the SPSC queues and atomic snapshot swaps cross it (P9). Without this model you introduce a shared-mutable-state bug that is a segfault waiting for the right race. Resolved in §3, §6 (P9), §17.

MM5 — A click is always a discontinuity, and a discontinuity always has a named cause. Zipper noise (unsmoothed parameter), voice-stealing clicks (truncated envelope), aliasing (uncorrected discontinuity) are the same underlying bug class wearing different clothes. Without this model you chase each click as a one-off instead of routing every new parameter/event through the existing smoothing/ramping contracts. Resolved in §6 (P3, P8), §15, §18.

MM6 — Block processing is a scheduling artifact, not a synthesis concept. The math (§8) is defined per-sample; blocks exist only to amortize call overhead. A node that leaks block-size dependence into its output is confusing an implementation detail for a contract. Resolved in §8, §17.

MM7 — Reproducibility is the only trustworthy form of correctness at scale. "It sounds right" does not scale past a handful of manual listens; a differential test against a closed-form or reference implementation does (P10). Without this model, regressions hide until a user with extreme settings finds them. Resolved in §7 (I13), §28.

MM8 — Every deferred feature is a scope boundary, not a missing feature. Naming what this library does not do (convolution reverb, plug-in hosting, wavetable synthesis) is as load-bearing as naming what it does — it prevents scope creep from silently violating the real-time/numerical-correctness contracts the built features were held to. Resolved in §1, §37.

MM9 — Latency and CPU headroom are the same budget viewed from two ends. Every microsecond spent processing shrinks the deadline's remaining margin (§4); every sample of added buffering (oversampling, smoothing, lookahead) shrinks the latency budget instead (P13). A design decision that ignores one while optimizing the other is only half-reasoned. Resolved in §4, §30, §34.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Smith, The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing — ch. 6, 8, 19–21 (convolution, the DFT, IIR filters). Extract: transfer-function intuition, the convolution theorem. Applied in §8.
  • Smith, Introduction to Digital Filters with Audio Applications (CCRMA) — chapters on the bilinear transform and biquad design. Extract: pole/zero placement, cookbook-style biquad derivation. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Zölzer, DAFX: Digital Audio Effects, 2nd ed. — ch. 2 (Filters), ch. 4 (Modulators/delay-based effects), ch. 5 (Nonlinear processing). Extract: delay-line effect topologies, waveshaping. Applied in §20 and §21 (Phases 8/9).
  • Pirkle, Designing Software Synthesizer Plug-Ins in C++ — chapters on voice management and the modulation matrix. Extract: voice-stealing structures, mod-matrix routing tables. Applied in §18 and §19 (Phases 6/7).
  • Roads, The Computer Music Tutorial — ch. 4 (FM synthesis), ch. 5 (waveshaping). Extract: FM index/sideband math, synthesis taxonomy. Applied in §8 and §36.
  • Oppenheim & Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing — ch. 2 (z-transform), ch. 5 (transfer functions). Extract: formal z-transform machinery, stability criteria. Applied in §8.
  • Zavalishin, The Art of VA Filter Design (Native Instruments, freely published) — chapter on the TPT one-pole/SVF. Extract: the trapezoidal integrator, zero-delay-feedback solution. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).

Papers

  • Moore 1977, "Table Lookup Noise for Sinusoidal Digital Music Synthesis" — wavetable interpolation noise floor. Applied in §14 (Phase 2).
  • Chowning 1973, "The Synthesis of Complex Audio Spectra by Means of Frequency Modulation" (JAES) — FM sideband spectrum via Bessel functions. Applied in §8 and §21 (Phase 9).
  • Stilson & Smith 1996, "Alias-Free Digital Synthesis of Classic Analog Waveforms" (ICMC) — BLIT. Applied in §14 (Phase 2).
  • Brandt 2001, "Hard Sync Without Aliasing" (ICMC) — minBLEP. Applied in §14 (Phase 2).
  • Välimäki & Huovilainen 2006, "Oscillator and Filter Algorithms for Virtual Analog Synthesis" (Computer Music Journal) — antialiased oscillator/VA filter survey. Applied in §14 and §16.
  • Välimäki et al. 2010, antialiasing survey — cost/quality comparison across antialiasing methods. Applied in §14 and §4 (aliasing-floor target).
  • Schroeder 1962, "Natural Sounding Artificial Reverberation" (JAES) — comb/allpass reverb topology. Applied in §20 (Phase 8).
  • Jot & Chaigne 1991 (feedback delay networks, AES) — FDN stability via an orthogonal/unitary feedback matrix. Applied in §20 (Phase 8) and §8 (domain model).
  • Moorer 1979, "About This Reverberation Business" (Computer Music Journal) — practical reverb design refinements over Schroeder. Applied in §20 (Phase 8).
  • Smith & Angell 1982 (Karplus-Strong extension). Applied in §36 (physical-modeling alternative).
  • Karplus & Strong 1983 (Computer Music Journal) — plucked-string synthesis. Applied in §36.
  • Yeh, Abel & Smith 2010 (nonlinear virtual analog) — nonlinear diode-ladder analysis. Applied in §21 (Phase 9).
  • Bristow-Johnson, "Audio EQ Cookbook" (published reference) — closed-form biquad coefficient formulas per filter type. Applied in §16 (Phase 4) and §8 (domain model).

Reference Implementations

  • SuperCollider — server/plugins/ (UGen implementations, e.g. Filter.cpp, Osc.cpp). Lesson: block-based UGen calc-function dispatch by rate (audio/control). Pitfall: do not copy its plugin-API macro complexity wholesale — it targets a C++ ABI stability this project does not need. Applied in §17 (Phase 5).
  • Csound — the opcode source tree (Opcodes/, OOps/ugens*.c). Lesson: decades-stable oscillator/envelope opcode designs. Pitfall: Csound's orchestra/score language conflates scheduling and DSP — keep this project's graph and automation layers separate. Applied in §19 (Phase 7).
  • FAUST — its compiler backend translating a functional DSP description into a specialized C++ kernel. Lesson: compiling a graph ahead of time removes interpretation overhead. Pitfall: do not adopt FAUST's whole-graph static compilation model — this blueprint keeps the graph dynamically patchable (ADR-04). Applied in §17 (Phase 5) and §36.
  • JUCE — modules/juce_dsp/ (e.g. juce_IIRFilter.h, juce_Oversampling.h). Lesson: clean separation between coefficient computation and per-sample state update. Pitfall: JUCE's ProcessorChain templates assume compile-time-fixed topology, unlike this project's runtime graph. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Surge XT — src/common/dsp/ oscillator and filter implementations. Lesson: production-grade PolyBLEP and ladder-filter code with measured alias floors. Pitfall: Surge inlines heavy per-voice branching that this project's block-processing contract must avoid on the hot path. Applied in §14 (Phase 2) and §21 (Phase 9).
  • scipy.signal — butter/iirfilter/freqz. Lesson: the reference numeric implementation this project's differential filter tests validate against. Pitfall: scipy is offline/batch — never import it into the audio-thread hot path (ADR-01, ADR-16... see ADR-21 for testing use). Applied in §16 (Phase 4) and §28.
  • librosa — STFT/spectral utilities. Lesson: reference spectral-analysis routines useful for conformance-fixture generation (alias energy, THD+N measurement). Pitfall: analysis-only, non-real-time — used only in conformance//benchmarks/, never in the runtime graph. Applied in §28.

RFCs / Specifications

  • WAV/RIFF format specification — chunk layout, fmt/data chunks. Applied in §12 (Phase 0) and §28 (fuzzing).
  • IEEE 754 — floating-point semantics, denormals. Applied in §4 and §16 (Phase 4), and I6.

Glossary (§38) · 50 terms · site-wide glossary

Nyquist frequency
half the sample rate; the highest frequency representable without aliasing.
Aliasing
a frequency above Nyquist folding back into the audible band as a false, lower frequency.
Band-limited
a signal containing no energy above Nyquist by construction.
BLIT
band-limited impulse train, a closed-form antialiased pulse generator (Stilson & Smith 1996).
minBLEP
minimum-phase band-limited step correction via table convolution (Brandt 2001).
PolyBLEP
polynomial band-limited step approximation applied at a waveform discontinuity.
Biquad
a two-pole, two-zero IIR filter section.
Pole
a root of a transfer function's denominator; governs feedback/resonance and stability.
Zero
a root of a transfer function's numerator; shapes the frequency response's nulls.
Unit circle
the |z|=1 boundary in the z-plane; poles inside it are stable.
Transfer function
H(z) = Y(z)/X(z), the frequency-domain description of an LTI system.
TPT integrator
topology-preserving-transform (trapezoidal) integrator used to build zero-delay-feedback filters (Zavalishin 2012).
State-variable filter (SVF)
a filter topology exposing low-pass/band- pass/high-pass simultaneously from shared integrator state.
Resonance / Q
the parameter controlling a filter's peak sharpness near cutoff; at extremes, self-oscillation.
ADSR
Attack-Decay-Sustain-Release envelope.
Zipper noise
audible stepping artifacts from an unsmoothed, block-rate parameter change.
Voice stealing
reallocating an already-sounding voice to a new note when the polyphony ceiling is reached.
Modulation matrix
a bounded source×destination×depth routing table for modulation signals.
Sample-accurate automation
a parameter change applied at an exact sample index rather than quantized to a block boundary.
Block rate vs. control rate
the frequency at which audio-rate processing (per sample) versus modulation/control processing (per block) occurs.
Denormal
a floating-point value so close to zero it loses normalized precision, historically costly on some CPUs.
DC offset
an unwanted constant (0 Hz) component in a signal, often from an asymmetric nonlinearity.
Over- sampling
processing a signal at a higher internal rate to push aliasing above the eventual output Nyquist before downsampling.
FDN reverb
feedback delay network reverb, N delay lines coupled through a stability-proven feedback matrix (Jot & Chaigne 1991).
Schroeder allpass
an allpass filter section used in classic reverb topologies (Schroeder 1962).
Comb filter
a delay-with-feedback structure producing evenly-spaced frequency-response notches/peaks.
Impulse response
a system's output when driven by a unit impulse; fully characterizes an LTI system.
THD+N
total harmonic distortion plus noise, a measured signal-purity metric.
Xrun
a buffer underrun/overrun in the audio callback, audible as a click or dropout.
Real-time safety
the property that a code path never allocates, locks, or blocks unpredictably.
RMS
root-mean-square, a signal-level measure.
dBFS
decibels relative to full scale, the digital audio level unit.
SPSC
single-producer single-consumer, a lock-free queue discipline.
Arena allocator
a pre-reserved memory pool sized once, never grown at runtime.
FTZ/DAZ
flush-to-zero / denormals-are-zero, CPU flags forcing denormal values to zero in hardware.
RIFF
the container format WAV files use (Resource Interchange File Format).
Running status
a MIDI 1.0 optimization omitting a repeated status byte across consecutive same-type messages.
UMP
Universal MIDI Packet, the MIDI 2.0 wire format.
Legato
reusing an already-sounding voice for a new note rather than retriggering, for a smooth pitch transition.
Key tracking
scaling a filter's cutoff by the played note's frequency.
Spectral radius
the largest eigenvalue magnitude of a matrix; governs feedback- system stability.
Bessel function
the special function giving FM synthesis's sideband amplitudes (Chowning 1973).
Waveshaping
a nonlinear, memoryless transfer function applied to a signal for distortion/timbre shaping.
Diode-ladder filter
a nonlinear filter topology modeling analog diode-ladder circuits (Yeh, Abel & Smith 2010).
Polyphase filter
an efficient multirate filter structure used in up/downsampling.
Topological sort
an ordering of a DAG's nodes such that every edge points from earlier to later in the order.
Node protocol
the structural interface (process/static_size/reset) every graph node implements.
RTO
recovery time objective, the target maximum time to restore service after a failure.
SLO
service-level objective, a quantified reliability target.
Latency budget
the total accounted delay a system may introduce, allocated across its buffering/processing stages. ---
