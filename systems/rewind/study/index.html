Hard questions (§33)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does the forward path budget itself in single-digit milliseconds while the rewind path budgets in tens of seconds, and what would break if those budgets were swapped? (§4, P3)
  2. Under what conditions does Mode A (logical-decoding plugin) become strictly better than Mode B (wire-protocol proxy), and what operational cost does that regime impose? (§9 ADR-02, §13.2)
  3. Why is idempotency mandatory for inverses but only optional for forwards — what invariant would break if inverses were merely "best-effort" idempotent? (§6 P4, §7 I3)
  4. How does the FLP impossibility result justify a bounded-retry saga-abandonment policy rather than "just wait for consensus"? (§5 papers, §19.6, P14)
  5. Why does a Raft-replicated saga registry (Phase 7) not, by itself, guarantee that a saga's compensation converges? (§19.2, §30 MM7)
  6. What is the precise difference between the guarantee a hash chain provides and the guarantee an operator might assume it provides, and where does that gap get closed operationally? (§12.2, §22 T3/T4)
  7. Trace what happens, end to end, when a rewind is interrupted by SIGKILL at 40% completion — which invariant guarantees a safe resume, and which telemetry signal would an operator see first? (§8.4, §16.9)
  8. Why does the runtime treat every SDK-originated message as adversarial input even though the SDK ships from the same vendor as the runtime? (§6 P12, §7 I8, §22 T1)
  9. Why is "reversibility coverage" deliberately never reported as 100%, and what principle would be violated if it were? (§6 P7, §30 MM8)
  10. What decision in ADR-03 (Postgres hot tier) would need revisiting first if a single tenant's write volume exceeded 200,000 ops/sec, and how much headroom does §32 say we have before that happens? (§9 ADR-03, §32 #8)
  11. Why does the agent safety layer (Phase 9) reuse the exact same Operation/Inverse machinery as human-driven writes instead of a parallel agent-specific pipeline? (§21.2, §21.9)
  12. Under what circumstances does the conflict resolver's "quiesce-replay" strategy get invoked instead of "skip" or "force," and what does it cost relative to the other two? (§17.2, §11.2's strategy enumeration)
  13. Why does a two-party co-sign requirement apply to rewinds touching auth/permission tables specifically, rather than to every rewind above a row-count threshold alone? (§7 I6, §22 T2)
  14. How would you extend the Chandy-Lamport-inspired Checkpoint model if Rewind needed to support a distributed, multi-shard tenant, and what would break in the current single-checkpoint design? (§11.4, §20.2)
  15. Why does Rewind's comparative positioning against Temporal rest on "who authors the compensation," and what does that decision cost Rewind that Temporal does not pay? (§31)
  16. What quantitative assumption in the sandbox spin-up derivation (§32 #5) would most change the < 5-minute target if it were wrong by 2×, and which phase's acceptance criteria would then fail? (§32, §20.4)
  17. Why is the inverse log described as "Tier-1 data," architecturally equal to the customer's own database, rather than as an audit/logging concern? (§6 P2, §9 ADR-03)
  18. Trace how a single forward-only declaration (e.g. SendGrid email, §14.2) propagates through a rewind window's dry-run manifest, the operator's decision, and the eventual RPW-Verified metric. (§8.3, §16.2)
  19. Why does the blueprint reject "just replicate the inverse log via Kafka" (ADR-03's rejected alternative) even though Kafka's raw throughput ceiling is higher than Postgres's? (§9 ADR-03, §29)
  20. What is the actual attack an insider (T3) could execute if the hash chain did not exist, and why is a write-only runtime role not sufficient by itself to stop it? (§22 T3, §7 I2)

30. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — An inverse is a proof obligation, not a convenience. Predicts: any verb without a provably-correct inverse must be declared forward-only, never shipped as an optimistic guess. Bug class without it: a rewind silently "succeeds" against a verb that never actually restored prior state. Applies in §14.2, §11.6.

MM2 — Causal order, not wall-clock order, is the only order a rewind may trust (Lamport 1978, §5). Predicts: any sequencing decision keyed on a timestamp instead of the causal DAG is a latent bug. Bug class: incorrect replay order under clock skew. Applies in §8.2, §16.2.

MM3 — A hash chain proves tampering happened; it does not prevent it. Predicts: detection-and-response framing, never "the log cannot be tampered with." Bug class: false security theater that skips building the response path (quarantine, restore). Applies in §12.2, §22 (T3/T4).

MM4 — Idempotency is a property of the operation, not the network. Predicts: an inverse must be safe under at-least-once delivery regardless of transport guarantees. Bug class: double-application under a retry storm. Applies in §15.2, §7 (I3).

MM5 — The rewind command is more dangerous than the data it touches. Predicts: privilege modeling must scope the verb (rewind-authority), not just the table. Bug class: privilege escalation via a legitimate-looking rewind. Applies in §17.2, §22 (T2).

MM6 — A saga is a state machine with an explicit abandon state, not an infinite retry loop. Predicts: any compensation path needs a bounded-retry contract wherever it composes across services. Bug class: resource-starving infinite retries. Applies in §15.2, §19.2, P14.

MM7 — A consensus protocol guarantees agreement on state, not correctness of the workflow built atop it. Predicts: conflating etcd's Raft guarantee with saga-level convergence is the exact pitfall a distributed saga coordinator must avoid. Bug class: "the registry is healthy" mistaken for "the saga converged." Applies in §19.2, §19.6.

MM8 — Reversibility coverage has an honest ceiling and is never reported as 100%. Predicts: forward-only operations are declared explicitly, never optimistically assumed reversible. Bug class: a false sense of safety that encourages riskier action because "there's an undo button." Applies in §14.2, P7.

MM9 — A checkpoint is a consistent cut, not a backup (Chandy & Lamport 1985, §5). Predicts: a checkpoint alone cannot restore state without the log segment it anchors. Bug class: assuming checkpoints are self-sufficient restore points. Applies in §11.4, §20.2.

MM10 — The forward path optimizes for speed; the rewind path optimizes for proof. Predicts: every performance tradeoff resolves by asking which path it sits on. Bug class: shaving correctness checks off the rewind path to hit a speed target. Applies in §4, P3.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017) — ch. 7 (Transactions), ch. 9 (Consistency and Consensus). Extract: the cost/consistency tradeoff of 2PC vs. compensating actions. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §13 Phase 1.
  • Petrov, Database Internals (O'Reilly, 2019) — ch. 3 (Files and Indexes / WAL), ch. 10 (Replication). Extract: WAL structure and how logical decoding surfaces it. Applied in §13 Phase 1 (pre-image extraction, ADR-02).
  • Shostack, Threat Modeling: Designing for Security (Wiley, 2014) — ch. 4 (STRIDE). Extract: a repeatable per-trust-boundary threat walk. Applied in §22 Threat Model.
  • Hohpe & Woolf, Enterprise Integration Patterns (Addison-Wesley, 2003) — the Compensating Action pattern. Extract: compensations as first-class messaging citizens, not exception handlers. Applied in §14 Phase 2 (verb catalogue design).
  • Burns, Designing Distributed Systems (O'Reilly, 2018) — the Ambassador pattern chapter. Extract: a sidecar/proxy that transparently augments a protocol it doesn't own. Applied in §13 Phase 1 (wire-protocol proxy, ADR-02).
  • Nygard, Release It! (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2007; 2nd ed. 2018) — Circuit Breaker and Bulkhead chapters. Extract: bounding blast radius structurally, not by hoping. Applied in §17 Phase 5 (blast-radius caps), §28 Performance Engineering.

Papers

  • Garcia-Molina & Salem, "Sagas" (ACM SIGMOD, 1987) — the originating saga model. Applied in §15 Phase 3, §19 Phase 7.
  • Gray, "The Transaction Concept: Virtues and Limitations" (VLDB, 1981) — introduces compensating transactions as an alternative to long-lived locks. Applied in §8 Domain Model, P1/P4 (§6).
  • Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" (CACM, 1978) — causal ordering without synchronized clocks. Applied in §8 (causal predecessors), I4 (§7).
  • Chandy & Lamport, "Distributed Snapshots: Determining Global States of Distributed Systems" (ACM TOCS, 1985) — consistent global snapshots. Applied in §11 (Checkpoint type), §20 Phase 8 (sandbox base image).
  • Fischer, Lynch, Paterson, "Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process" (JACM, 1985) — the FLP result. Applied in §19 Phase 7 (why convergence needs bounded retries, not infinite consensus-seeking), P14 (§6).
  • Ongaro & Ousterhout, "In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm" (USENIX ATC, 2014) — Raft. Applied in §19 Phase 7 (etcd-backed saga registry), ADR-07.
  • Brewer, "CAP Twelve Years Later: How the 'Rules' Have Changed" (IEEE Computer, 2012) — refined CAP tradeoffs. Applied in §29 Scalability Analysis, ADR-03.
  • Helland, "Life Beyond Distributed Transactions: An Apostate's Opinion" (CIDR, 2007) — forward/inverse contracts over cross-system 2PC. Applied in §6 (P3, P4), §8.

Reference Implementations

  • Debezium (github.com/debezium/debezium, dir debezium-connector-postgres) — lesson: consuming Postgres logical decoding reliably at scale; pitfall not to copy: Debezium targets append-only event streams, not inverse capture — it never has to compute a pre-image. Applied in §13 Phase 1.
  • Temporal (github.com/temporalio/temporal, dir service/history) — lesson: a production saga/workflow state machine; pitfall not to copy: Temporal requires the developer to author every compensation by hand — Rewind auto-derives them where possible. Applied in §15 Phase 3, §19 Phase 7.
  • etcd (github.com/etcd-io/etcd, dir raft/) — lesson: an embeddable, audited Raft library; pitfall not to copy: etcd's own storage engine (bbolt) is not tuned for our append-heavy inverse-log workload, so we do not reuse it for the log itself. Applied in §19 Phase 7, ADR-07.
  • pg_query_go (github.com/pganalyze/pg_query_go) — lesson: PostgreSQL-grammar-exact SQL parsing without a live PG connection; pitfall not to copy: the parser trails new PG major-version grammar by weeks — pin and test explicitly. Applied in §13 Phase 1, ADR-08.
  • Vitess (github.com/vitessio/vitess, dir go/vt/vttablet) — lesson: a production-grade proxy-in-front-of-a-database pattern (pooling, query rewriting); pitfall not to copy: Vitess's sharding-aware routing is unneeded complexity for Rewind's single-upstream proxy. Applied in §13 Phase 1, ADR-02.

Glossary (§36) · 34 terms · site-wide glossary

Agent action envelope
the AgentAction record (§11.7) wrapping an autonomous agent's operation with agentId/policyRef for retroactive enforcement.
Blast-radius cap
a declared ceiling on rows/records a single verb or agent action may touch (P10, I6, I12).
Causal predecessor
an operation whose effects are a precondition for another's correctness; drives the saga coordinator's reverse-order computation (I4).
Checkpoint
a consistent cross-system anchor (LSN + operation cursor + inverse cursor + hash anchor) written every 60s (§11.4); a dependency-graph cut point and sandbox base image, never a backup by itself (MM9).
Co-sign
the second-operator signature required before a sensitive rewind executes (I6).
Compensating action
a forward operation whose effect is the inverse of a previously-recorded operation (Gray 1981).
Conflict resolver
the component detecting when an inverse's pre-image disagrees with current state (§17.2).
Conformance suite
the mandatory test pack every verb must pass before release (§14.5, §26).
Distributed saga registry
the etcd/Raft-replicated store of saga/operation state across rewindd instances (§19.2).
Dry-run
a simulated rewind producing a manifest without applying it; mandatory by default for production rewinds (I5, P9).
Forward-only operation
an operation explicitly declared as having no inverse (P7); excluded from rewind windows, surfaced at dry-run.
Hash chain
the integrity scheme where every log record references the hash of its predecessor (I2); proves tampering happened, does not prevent it (MM3).
Idempotency key
a deterministic key guarding against double-application of an inverse (§11.5, I3).
Inverse
the compensating action attached to a forward operation (§11.2); one of four strategies (compensating, tombstone, quiesce-replay, forward-only).
Inverse log
the append-only, hash-chained record of every operation and its inverse (P2).
Logical decoding
Postgres's mechanism for streaming committed changes; the basis of interceptor Mode A (§5, §13.2).
Operation
any side effect crossing the Rewind boundary (§11.1): a Postgres write, an HTTP call, a queue publish.
PITR
point-in-time recovery; a delegated precondition for the Rewind sandbox (§1, §20.2), never reimplemented by Rewind.
Pre-image
the row state read before a Postgres UPDATE/DELETE is forwarded, used to derive the inverse SQL (§13.2).
Quiesce-replay
an inverse strategy that quiesces a partition, restores from PITR, and replays surviving forward operations; slow, last-resort (§17.2).
Retention conflict
a detected clash between a rewind and a configured retention/compliance policy (I13, FM-9).
Reversibility contract
the ReversibleOperation interface (§11.6) every reversible operation satisfies.
Reverse-topological sort
the ordering algorithm applying inverses in strict reverse causal order (§16.2, P8).
Rewind
the act of replaying inverses across a window of operations to revert state (§16).
RPW-Verified
Rewinds-per-week with verified post-rewind state restoration; the metric that only increments on a signed, invariant-passing manifest (I10).
Saga
a named sequence of operations sharing a saga context and an all-or-nothing inverse semantics (§11.3, §8.1).
Saga coordinator
the component ordering inverses across services with conflict detection and bounded-retry convergence (§19.2).
Sandbox
a time-boxed, redacted, isolated environment materializing production state at a past timestamp (§20).
Schema-version dispatch
the reader-side mechanism selecting the correct decoder for a record's __rewindVersion (I7).
Tombstone
an idempotent marker indicating an operation should be treated as never-having-happened by downstream consumers (§14.2).
ULID
a lexicographically-sortable, monotonic identifier used for Operation.id (ADR-22), avoiding wall-clock ordering ambiguity.
Verb
a catalogued operation (e.g. pg.update.orders, stripe.charges.create) with declared forward, inverse, and idempotency semantics (§11.6, §14).
Verb catalogue
the registry of all supported verbs and their inverse strategies (§11.6, moat per §2).
Wire-protocol proxy
the Mode-B Postgres interceptor transparently passing frontend/backend protocol traffic while deriving inverses in flight (§13.2, ADR-02).
