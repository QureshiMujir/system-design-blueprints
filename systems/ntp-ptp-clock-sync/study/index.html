Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed intuitions an expert holds about clock synchronization. Each: the insight, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it. Every cited § resolves.

  • MM1 — Time sync produces a bounded interval, never a point. The deliverable is [earliest, latest] (true time lies inside with high confidence), not a scalar "the time". The width is the error budget Λ = E + Δ/2; a point estimate is just the interval's midpoint with its uncertainty thrown away. Predicts: a consumer that treats the clock as exact (consensus lease, commit-wait, MVCC) will violate its safety property exactly when the interval is wide and it assumed it was narrow. Bug without it: reporting offset with no max_error, so callers assume false precision and ordering guarantees silently collapse. Applied: §8.4 (Λ), I2, P2 of §6, Phase 12; this is the Spanner/TrueTime insight (§Comparative Architecture).
  • MM2 — Sync is a feedback control loop, and the oscillator is the plant. Offset is the phase error, frequency is the integral term, and the loop filter is a controller disciplining a physical (drifting, noisy) oscillator — the same math as a PLL in an RF receiver. Predicts: gains too high → the loop amplifies measurement noise (PDV) into the clock and oscillates; gains too low → it never catches a drift. Bug without it: tuning the servo by trial-and-error rather than damping/time-constant, getting a clock that hops. Applied: §8.6, P4 of §6, I4/I9, Phase 4 & Phase 12.
  • MM3 — A single source is never trustworthy; truth is the majority intersection. One server — even one that lies consistently and internally-consistently — must never move your clock. Correctness comes from overlapping confidence intervals (Marzullo), not averaging. Predicts: a naive mean of N sources is walked by one distant liar in proportion to its distance; an intersection rejects it outright. Bug without it: trusting one NTP server, or averaging, so a compromised/misconfigured source walks the fleet. Applied: §8.5/§8.16, P3 of §6, I3, Phase 3.
  • MM4 — Offset and delay are computed from four timestamps under one load-bearing assumption: path symmetry. θ = ((T2−T1)+(T3−T4))/2 and δ = (T4−T1)−(T3−T2) are exact algebra, but θ's correctness rests entirely on the forward and reverse path delays being equal. Predicts: any asymmetry (routing, queueing, a deliberate one-way delay) injects a constant offset bias of half the asymmetry that no algorithm can detect from the timestamps alone. Bug without it: trusting a sub-ms offset over an asymmetric WAN/satellite path, or missing the delay-asymmetry attack. Applied: §8.4/§8.9, T5, Phase 1 & Phase 10, §Scalability Analysis (asymmetry floor).
  • MM5 — A min/min-delay filter extracts signal because queueing only ever adds delay. The sample with the smallest round-trip delay suffered the least queueing, so its offset is the least corrupted — the truth is at the extreme, not the average. RTprop and BtlBw analogues: the minimum delay and the cleanest offset. Predicts: averaging the 8 filter samples is worse than picking the min-delay one; a window that never expires sticks to a stale minimum after a route change. Bug without it: smoothing the filter window and degrading accuracy, or never re-learning after a path change. Applied: §8.5 step 1, I12, Phase 2, ADR-10.
  • MM6 — Slewing preserves monotonicity; stepping breaks it — so the default must be slew. Apps assume gettimeofday never goes backward (log ordering, DB MVCC, TLS validity, TOTP). A frequency nudge (slew) keeps time monotonic; a step can move it backward. Predicts: a system that steps on every correction produces duplicate timestamps, "certificate not yet valid" errors, and MVCC corruption; the worst case is a leap second naively stepped backward. Bug without it: calling settimeofday to "fix" offset, breaking every monotonic consumer. Applied: §8.6, P1 of §6, I1, Phase 5 & Phase 7; the Cloudflare 2017 leap bug (§Failure Modes) is the canonical violation.
  • MM7 — Every received packet is hostile until proven otherwise, and the loop's inputs are the attack surface. NTP is spoofable UDP; a forged reply, a replayed packet, or a length-field lie drives the control loop or the parser. Predicts: trusting pkt.org without checking it echoes your last transmit lets an off-path attacker inject offset; allocating from an attacker's length field is a DoS; a non-constant-time MAC compare is a timing oracle. Bug without it: the entire ntpd CVE class (mode-7 monlist amplification CVE-2013-5211; parser overflows). Applied: §8.17, P7/P8 of §6, I10/I11/I15, Phase 1/6/8, §Threat Model.
  • MM8 — Estimator and controller are different machines; keep measurement pure and actuation isolated. Filter→select→combine is a pure function of samples (no side effects); discipline→actuate is the only thing that touches the clock. Predicts: tangle them and you cannot test the estimator without a real clock, cannot replay a servo bug deterministically, and risk two code paths fighting over CLOCK_REALTIME. Bug without it: an untestable feedback loop and dueling disciplinarians (ntpd + systemd-timesyncd). Applied: P11/P12 of §6, I13, §3 (architecture), Phase 5 (virtual vs sys clock), §11.4 (ClockBackend).
  • MM9 — Wire time is exact fixed-point; float belongs only inside the controller. NTP 32.32 and PTP 48.32 represent sub-ns exactly; IEEE-754 f64 cannot represent the NTP fraction exactly once the seconds field is large (~µs error near 2^32 s). Predicts: serialize an f64 timestamp and you bias every offset by a sub-µs-to-µs amount that grows with the epoch; do servo math in fixed-point and you lose nothing but reproducibility risk. Bug without it: silent precision loss that looks like a stable but wrong offset. Applied: P13 of §6, I14, §8.3, ADR-04/05, Phase 0.
  • MM10 — When you lose your sources, your clock keeps running but your honesty is the deliverable. Holdover means extrapolating from the last frequency estimate while growing max_error at the worst-case drift rate — the time is no longer trustworthy at the old bound and the interval must say so. Predicts: a holdover that keeps reporting the pre-loss error bound presents drift as accuracy and a consumer trusts a clock that is silently wrong; an honest one widens the interval until a consumer can refuse it. Bug without it: false confidence after source loss (the most dangerous failure — confident wrongness). Applied: P9/P14 of §6, I2, Phase 12 (holdover/controller), §Failure Modes (holdover drift).

5. Required Reading

Every entry names the section/phase where its idea is applied.

Books

  • Mills, "Computer Network Time Synchronization: The Network Time Protocol on Earth and in Space" (2nd ed.) — Ch. on the clock filter, intersection/selection, clustering, combining; Ch. on the clock discipline algorithm (hybrid PLL/FLL); Ch. on error budget (root delay/dispersion). Extract: the entire mitigation pipeline + discipline math, normatively. Applied: §8.5 pipeline, §8.6 discipline, Phases 2–4.
  • Franklin, Powell, Emami-Naeini, "Feedback Control of Dynamic Systems" — Ch. root locus, PI/PID, discrete-time control, stability margins. Extract: loop gain, time constant, damping, why conservative gains avoid overshoot. Applied: §8.6, P4 of §6, Phase 4 (loop filter) and Phase 12 (PI servo).
  • Eidson, "Measurement, Control, and Communication Using IEEE 1588" — Ch. on delay request-response, peer-delay, BMCA, transparent/boundary clocks, correctionField. Extract: PTP semantics + timestamp model. Applied: §8.7–§8.12, Phases 9–11.
  • Lamport-adjacent: "Distributed Systems: Concepts and Design" (Coulouris) — Ch. time & global state. Extract: logical vs physical time, why physical sync is bounded-error not exact. Applied: §2 (cross-links), I2 (error interval), MM1.
  • "Numerical Recipes" (Press et al.) — Ch. robust estimation, linear regression, median. Extract: min/median filters, regression for frequency (chrony-style), numerical stability. Applied: §8.5 clock filter, Phase 2 (filter), ADR-10 (regression alternative).
  • Kerrisk, "The Linux Programming Interface" — Ch. time (clock_gettime, adjtimex, timers), sockets, ancillary data. Extract: timex struct, SO_TIMESTAMPING cmsg, clock_adjtime ops. Applied: Phase 5 (clock/adjtimex), Phase 9 (net/timestamping), ADR-11.
  • Stevens, "UNIX Network Programming, Vol 1" — Ch. UDP, ancillary data, recvmsg. Extract: cmsg parsing, raw sockets. Applied: Phase 0 (net/udp), Phase 9 (cmsg/L2), ADR-03.

Papers

  • Mills, "Internet Time Synchronization: the Network Time Protocol", IEEE Trans. Communications, 1991 — origin of the on-wire offset/delay equations + filter; relevance: normative algorithms. Applied: §8.4 equations, Phase 1.
  • Mills, "Improved Algorithms for Synchronizing Computer Network Clocks", IEEE/ACM ToN, 1995 — refined discipline + dispersion; relevance: error budget. Applied: §8.6 discipline, §8.4 dispersion, Phase 4.
  • Marzullo & Owicki, "Maintaining the Time in a Distributed System", PODC 1983 — the intersection algorithm; relevance: Byzantine-tolerant source selection (truechimers vs falsetickers). Applied: §8.5 step 2, §8.16, Phase 3, P3 of §6.
  • Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System", CACM 1978 — happens-before; relevance: why bounded physical clocks matter for ordering. Applied: §2 (cross-links to consensus/snapshots), MM1.
  • Corbett et al., "Spanner: Google's Globally-Distributed Database", OSDI 2012 — TrueTime TT.now()→[earliest,latest], commit-wait; relevance: uncertainty interval is the product, not a point. Applied: §2, I2, P2 of §6, §Comparative Architecture, Phase 12 (uncertainty).
  • Veitch, Babu, Pàsztor, "Robust Synchronization of Software Clocks…", IMC 2004 — software clock estimation under PDV; relevance: filtering noisy paths. Applied: §8.5 filter, ADR-10, Phase 2, MM5.
  • Mills, "Network Time Protocol Version 4: Theory and Algorithms" (RFC-companion report 06-6-1) — full algorithm reference. Applied: §8.5/§8.6 (normative cross-check), Phases 2–4.
  • Lévesque & Tipper, "A Survey of Clock Synchronization Over Packet-Switched Networks", IEEE Comm. Surveys 2016 — NTP vs PTP landscape; relevance: design tradeoffs. Applied: §Comparative Architecture, §Alternative Architectures.
  • Lee et al., "Globally Synchronized Time via Datacenter Networks" (DTP), SIGCOMM 2016 — physical-layer sync; relevance: ceiling of packet-based sync. Applied: §Scalability Analysis (fundamental ceiling), §Alternative Architectures (A2).
  • Geng et al., "Exploiting a Natural Network Effect for Scalable, Fine-grained Clock Synchronization" (Sundial/Huygens), NSDI 2018 — coded probes + SVM; relevance: advanced PDV filtering. Applied: §Alternative Architectures (A3), §Scalability Analysis (PDV ceiling).

Reference Implementations

  • ntpd (ntp.org reference) — ntp_proto.c (receive/transmit, packet processing), ntp_loopfilter.c (discipline). Lessons: the canonical pipeline. Pitfalls: mode 7 monlist amplification (CVE-2013-5211), sprawling C, restrict-list complexity. Applied: §8.5/§8.6 pipeline, Phases 2–4/6, §Comparative Architecture.
  • chrony — sourcestats.c (linear regression frequency est), regress.c, refclock.c, ntp_core.c. Lessons: regression beats simple filter on jittery WAN; fast initial convergence. Pitfalls: more complex statistics to get right. Applied: ADR-10, Phase 2 (filter alternative), §13 differential baseline, §Comparative Architecture.
  • NTPsec — hardened ntpd fork; removed mode 7/Autokey. Lessons: attack-surface reduction; what to delete. Applied: §1 non-goals (mode 7/Autokey), P7/P8 of §6, ADR-15/16.
  • linuxptp (ptp4l, phc2sys) — port.c (port state machine), bmc.c (BMCA), servo/* (PI, linreg, NTP servo), clock.c. Lessons: PTP done right; servo abstraction; phc2sys for PHC↔system. Pitfalls: profile config sprawl; HW dependence. Applied: §8.12, Phases 9–12, ADR-08, §13 PTP differential baseline.
  • Meta time (fbclock / ntp / ptp4u, sptp) — large-scale NTP/PTP serving; ptp4u stateless server, fbclock API exposing error bound. Lessons: serve at scale, expose wphc/error bound to apps like TrueTime. Pitfalls: infra-specific assumptions. Applied: Phase 6 (server), §Scalability Analysis (anycast model), I2/uncertainty.
  • OpenNTPD (OpenBSD) — minimal, security-first NTP client. Lessons: simplicity + privsep. Pitfalls: limited accuracy, no server-grade features. Applied: §1 deployment (capability-dropped), T11 (privsep), §Comparative Architecture.
  • timemaster (RedHat) — orchestrates chrony + ptp4l. Lessons: combining NTP + PTP sources operationally. Applied: Phase 12 (combined NTP/PTP selection), I12/I13.

Glossary · 50 terms · site-wide glossary

NTP
Network Time Protocol; UDP/123 packet-based sync with mitigation + discipline (RFC 5905)
SNTP
Simple NTP; stateless single-exchange subset (RFC 4330)
PTP
Precision Time Protocol; IEEE 1588 sub-µs LAN sync
IEEE 1588
The PTP standard (2008, 2019 editions)
Stratum
Distance from reference clock; 1=primary, 2..15 secondary, 16=unsynchronized
Offset (θ)
Estimated correction local→reference = ((T2−T1)+(T3−T4))/2
Delay (δ)
Round-trip delay = (T4−T1)−(T3−T2)
Dispersion (ε)
Accumulated error from precision + drift + age
Jitter (ψ)
RMS variation of offset samples
Root delay/dispersion
Total delay/dispersion to the reference clock (propagated in header)
Max error (Λ)
Bound on true offset: E + Δ/2; defines [earliest, latest]
Clock filter
8-sample shift register picking min-delay sample (NTP)
Marzullo
Algorithm for the intersection of confidence intervals; Byzantine-tolerant
Truechimer
A source whose interval is in the agreeing majority (accepted)
Falseticker
A source outside the majority intersection (rejected)
Intersection algorithm
Selection step finding the largest overlapping clique of intervals
Clustering
Culling statistical outliers among truechimers by jitter
Combining
Inverse-variance weighted average of survivors → system offset
PLL
Phase-Locked Loop; tracks phase error
FLL
Frequency-Locked Loop; tracks rate of change of offset
Loop filter
The control filter converting offset → frequency/phase correction
PI servo
Proportional-Integral controller (PTP discipline)
Step / slew
Step = jump clock; slew = gradual frequency nudge
Panic threshold
1000 s; offset beyond ⇒ refuse + alarm
STEP threshold
125 ms; offset beyond ⇒ step instead of slew
Poll interval
Time between requests; NTP 2^4..2^17 s
Reachability
8-bit register of recent successful polls
KoD
Kiss-o'-Death; server rate-control/deny packet (stratum 0 + code)
RefID
Reference identifier (refclock code or server IP/hash)
Leap second
±1 s UTC adjustment at 23:59:60 to track Earth rotation
Smearing
Spreading a leap second over a window to preserve monotonicity
TAI / UTC
International Atomic Time (no leaps) vs Coordinated Universal Time (with leaps)
Era
NTP 136-year epoch (seconds-field wrap), 2036 boundary
BMCA
Best Master Clock Algorithm; PTP master election
Grandmaster (GM)
BMCA-elected root clock of a PTP domain
Ordinary clock (OC)
Single-port PTP clock (master or slave)
Boundary clock (BC)
Multi-port PTP clock isolating domains; slave up, master down
Transparent clock (TC)
Forwards PTP updating correctionField with residence time
One-step / two-step
t1 in Sync (one-step) vs in Follow_Up (two-step)
correctionField
PTP Q48.16 ns accumulating residence + asymmetry corrections
meanPathDelay
((t2−t1)+(t4−t3))/2; assumes symmetric path
offsetFromMaster
((t2−t1)−(t4−t3))/2; slave's correction
Peer delay
P2P per-link delay measurement (Pdelay mechanism)
Holdover
Free-running on last frequency when sources lost; error grows
Allan variance / ADEV
Stability metric of a clock vs averaging time τ
NTS
Network Time Security; TLS-derived AEAD auth for NTP (RFC 8915)
NTS-KE
NTS Key Establishment (TLS handshake → cookies + keys)
SO_TIMESTAMPING
Kernel socket option capturing RX/TX timestamps
PHC
PTP Hardware Clock (NIC clock); HW timestamping source (deferred)
Truetime
Google's bounded-uncertainty time API ([earliest, latest])
