Hard questions (§37)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. A metric p99 spike's bucket has an exemplar, but resolving it returns nothing. Walk every reason this is not a bug and what the engine must return instead. (§4.3 + §8.3 + Phase 5 §17 + Phase 8 §20; I3.)
  2. Why does one unbounded label (user_id) threaten the metrics store, the log index, and span attributes simultaneously, and where is the single enforcement point that bounds all three? (§4.1 + §8.14 + Phase 7 §19; I6, MM2/MM3.)
  3. Derive the tail-sampling buffer's memory bound, identify what dominates it, and explain why a 30 s decision window alone is insufficient without a hard memory cap. (§4.2 + Phase 8 §20 + §36 C6; I9, MM4.)
  4. Two query nodes shard a sum by() over 50k series differently and disagree in the last ULP. Is determinism violated? What guarantee actually holds, and how is it enforced? (§8.12 + Phase 6 §18; I12, MM6.)
  5. Explain how a query stays correct while a writer is cutting a block and the compactor is merging — name the two mechanisms and the invariant each upholds. (§3.3 + §3.5 + Phase 6 §18 + Phase 10 §22; I11.)
  6. A trace is dropped by tail sampling at 7 days but a metric exemplar pointing at it survives to 13 months. What does the pivot return, and how do retention + sampling + correlation interact to avoid a silent dangling link? (§4.3 + Phase 8 §20 + Phase 10 §22; I3, MM7.)
  7. Why is trace_id promoted to a first-class indexed label in the log store instead of left in the body, and what latency target forces this? (§8.2 + §4 + Phase 4 §16; I5, C7.)
  8. A slow log store is filling its lane. Prove that metrics and trace ingestion are unaffected, and state what happens when the log lane's queue is full. (§3.5 + Phase 1 §13 + Phase 4 §16; I4, MM8.)
  9. Reconstruct the full anomaly→trace→log pivot as a single query plan, naming the correlation link used at each hop and the snapshot guarantee that keeps the three stores consistent. (§3.4 + §8.11 + Phase 5 §17 + Phase 6 §18; I3/I5/I12.)
  10. Why does a single-window SLO burn-rate alert force a choice between flapping and late detection, and how does the multi-window recipe escape it? Give concrete fast/slow windows. (Phase 9 §21 + §28; MM9.)
  11. A derived RED metric disagrees with the traces it summarizes. List the determinism conditions that must hold for derivation, and how a re-derivation test catches the bug. (§8.6 + Phase 5 §17 + §30; I8, MM10.)
  12. Where does identity normalization happen, why exactly once, and what is the failure signature when one store skips it (e.g. hex vs bytes trace_id)? (§8.2 + Phase 0 §12 + Phase 5 §17; I1/I2, MM1.)
  13. Compute the metrics head RAM at the cardinality ceiling and explain why doubling the sample rate is cheap while adding a label is not. (§4.1 + §36 C2 + Phase 2 §14; MM2.)
  14. Why are immutable blocks the right call here (tie it to the query-determinism guarantee), and name the one workload where mutable-in-place would beat them. (§3.3 + §9 ADR-17 + Phase 6 §18; I11/I12, the ADR's "When the Rejected Choice Wins".)
  15. Trace a malformed OTLP protobuf from the wire to its rejection, naming every validation gate and the invariant each enforces, and explain why size is checked before allocation. (§8.7 + Phase 1 §13 + §26; I7/I14.)
  16. How does the platform guarantee a tenant cannot read another tenant's data structurally rather than by policy, and what would a policy-gated design trade off? (§3.3 + §8.13 + Phase 10 §22 + ADR-19; I10.)
  17. Duplicate OTLP arrives from a client retry. Show why metrics don't double-count, name the idempotency key, and the one regime where at-least-once-with-dup is the better call. (§8.10 + Phase 1 §13 + ADR-22; I15.)
  18. Why is correlation cost independent of total data volume at scale, and what specifically must stay O(log n) for that to hold? (§33 + §4.3 + §36 C7; MM1.)
  19. A late span arrives after its trace's tail-sampling decision. Enumerate the possible outcomes and why a partial-trace outcome must be impossible. (§8.10 + Phase 8 §20; I9.)
  20. Why does this design keep three physical storage layouts instead of Honeycomb's single wide-event store, and in what regime would the wide-event design win? (§35 + §38 + §8.4; the columnar-events alternative.)
  21. Explain the WAL-replay startup budget: derive the required decode throughput from the head size and the startup target. (§4 + §36 C2/C8 + Phase 2 §14.)
  22. A query matches millions of series. What bounds the result, where is the limit enforced, and why must the ABI not buffer the whole result? (§36 C11 + Phase 6 §18 + Phase 11 §23; I16.)

34. Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an expert in observability backends holds. Each: the model, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

  • MM1 — A telemetry signal is an identity stamped on a time series; correlation is just resolving that identity in another store. Predicts: if you normalize trace_id/resource once and index it as a first-class key, every cross-signal pivot is an O(log n) point lookup, not an ETL. Without it: you build three silos and "correlate" by scanning/parsing at query time — the join is slow, lossy, and drifts. Applied: §2.7, §8.2, §8.3, Phase 5 (§17).
  • MM2 — Cardinality, not data volume, is the binding cost of a metrics store. Predicts: a TSDB's RAM ≈ active series × (index entry + open chunk); doubling sample rate is cheap, adding one unbounded label is fatal. Without it: you let user_id become a metric label and OOM the head; you size capacity off bytes/sec and miss the real wall. Applied: §4.1, §8.14, Phase 7 (§19), I6.
  • MM3 — The right home for high-cardinality data is a high-cardinality store, not a label. Predicts: user_id/request_id belong in trace/log attributes or an exemplar, never a metric label or an indexed log term; that placement is what keeps each store bounded. Without it: the same value explodes the metrics series index and the #78 log index and span attributes at once. Applied: §4.1, §8.2 (trace_id indexed, body scanned), ADR-6/ADR-16.
  • MM4 — A tail-sampling buffer is bounded by in-flight traces, not total traffic. Predicts: buffer RAM ≈ in_flight_traces × spans/trace × bytes/span; it scales with concurrency and trace duration, capped by the decision window — a few long traces, not throughput, dominate occupancy. Without it: you size the buffer off ingest rate, a 25 s long-tail trace pins memory, and you OOM (or drop partial traces). Applied: §4.2, Phase 8 (§20), I9, principle 5.
  • MM5 — An immutable, atomically-published block is what makes a query reproducible. Predicts: if writers only ever publish whole immutable blocks and a query pins a snapshot (block set + head epoch), the same (query, snapshot) is bit-stable forever — the basis of differential testing. Without it: queries read half-written state, results flicker, and you can't diff against an oracle. Applied: §3.3 (persistence boundary), Phase 6 (§18), I11/I12.
  • MM6 — Float aggregation is order-sensitive, so determinism requires a fixed evaluation order. Predicts: sum/rate/histogram_quantile over the same series will differ by ULPs unless you sort (by series id) before reducing; cross-implementation equality is only ever "within ε". Without it: query sharding silently changes results, golden tests flake, and "the same query" disagrees with itself. Applied: §8.12, Phase 6 (§18 fixed eval order), I12.
  • MM7 — An exemplar is a pointer, and a pointer can dangle. Predicts: a metric bucket's exemplar trace_id may reference a trace that tail-sampling dropped or retention expired; the only correct outcomes are resolve or an explicit SampledOut/NotFound — never a silent miss. Without it: the pivot 404s with no explanation, on-call loses trust, and sampling/retention silently break correlation. Applied: §4.3, §8.3, Phase 5 (§17 ExemplarResult), Phase 8 (§20 keep_if_exemplar_ref), I3.
  • MM8 — Backpressure is a per-lane property; a slow store must not stall the fast ones. Predicts: with bounded MPSC queues + fan-out per signal, a slow log store backpressures only its own writer; metrics/traces keep flowing, and the system sheds-or-blocks per lane, never grows unbounded. Without it: one slow consumer fills a shared queue, head-of-line blocks every signal, and the whole data plane OOMs or stalls. Applied: §3.5, Phase 1 (§13 backpressure), I4, principle 4.
  • MM9 — A single-window burn-rate alert is forced to choose between flapping and detecting late; two windows escape the dilemma. Predicts: a short window catches acute burns but flaps on blips; a long window is stable but slow — requiring both a short and a long window to burn fires fast on real incidents and stays quiet on transients. Without it: you ship a noisy pager (short window) or miss an SLO breach for hours (long window). Applied: Phase 9 (§21 multi_window), §28 (SLOs).
  • MM10 — A derived metric must be reproducible from its source, or the correlation lies. Predicts: RED/USE series derived from spans must re-derive identically over the same span window (fixed window boundaries + order); only then does "the metric spiked" agree with "these spans were slow". Without it: the derived metric disagrees with the trace reality it points at, and the pivot misleads. Applied: §8.6, Phase 5 (§17 span_to_metrics), I8, principle 10.

Required reading (§5)

5.1 Books

  • Charity Majors, Liz Fong-Jones, George Miranda, Observability Engineering (O'Reilly) — ch. on the three pillars, high-cardinality, and correlation; the conceptual spine. Applied: §2.7 (the correlation imperative), §8.3 (the correlation links), Phase 5 (the correlation layer).
  • Cindy Sridharan, Distributed Systems Observability (O'Reilly report) — metrics vs logs vs traces, when each fails, why correlation matters. Applied: §1.1 (the unification thesis), §8.1 (the unified signal model).
  • Betsy Beyer et al., Site Reliability Engineering (Google) — ch.4–6 (SLIs/SLOs/error budgets), the basis for SLO burn-rate alerting. Applied: Phase 9 (§21, multi-window multi-burn-rate), §28 (SLOs).
  • Alex Petrov, Database Internals — Part I (storage engines: LSM, B-trees, columnar) — the TSDB/log-store layouts + compaction. Applied: §8.4 (per-signal storage), Phase 2 (§14 TSDB), Phase 4 (§16 log store), Phase 10 (§22 compaction).
  • Martin Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications — ch.3 (storage + retrieval), ch.11 (stream processing) — the ingestion + per-signal storage framing. Applied: Phase 1 (§13 pipeline), §8.7 (OTLP ingest), Phase 5 (§17 span→metric streaming).
  • Tyler Akidau, Slava Chernyak, Reuven Lax, Streaming Systems — windowing, watermarks, exactly-once — the RED/USE derivation + tail-sampling windows. Applied: Phase 5 (§17 span→RED windows, I8), Phase 8 (§20 decision window, I9).
  • Brendan Gregg, Systems Performance (2e) — the USE method (ch.2); what to measure and why span/host metrics derive into USE. Applied: §8.6 (USE derivation), Phase 5 (§17 span→USE).

  • Grafana Labs, "Loki" design (label index + chunked logs) — logs-as-streams: index labels, not full text; the log-store split. Applied: §8.4 (log layout), Phase 4 (§16 #78 index + #85 body), ADR-6.
  • Grafana Labs, "Tempo" / trace storage by trace_id — cheap object-store trace storage keyed by trace_id; the trace-lookup pivot. Applied: Phase 3 (§15 trace_index), §3.4 (the pivot), ADR-7.
  • OpenTelemetry "OTLP" + the OTel data model spec — the wire format + the unified Resource/Scope/signal model (the normative contract). Applied: §8.1 (the unified model), Phase 0 (§12 obs-core), Phase 1 (§13 receivers).
  • OpenMetrics exposition + exemplar spec (CNCF) — the exact {trace_id,value,ts} exemplar attached to a bucket; the metric→trace link. Applied: §8.3/§8.5 (exemplar link, I3), Phase 2 (§14 exemplar slots).

  • Dean & Barroso, "The Tail at Scale" (CACM 2013) — why p99 latency dominates; the query-latency targets and the value of per-request tracing. Applied: §4 (latency targets), §32 (tail-latency analysis), §2.7 (why the pivot matters).
  • Pelkonen/Honeycomb-style high-cardinality columnar events (Honeycomb engineering writeups) — events-as-wide-rows, arbitrary-cardinality query; informs the columnar body + cross-signal query thinking. Applied: §8.4 (columnar body via #85), Phase 4 (§16), the Alternative Architectures (wide-events design).

Applied: §4 (multi-tenant + scaling targets), §33 (Scalability Analysis), the Comparative Architecture Table (vs Monarch's push/regional model).

5.3 Reference Implementations

  • Grafana Mimir / Cortex (pkg/ingester, pkg/querier, TSDB blocks) — horizontally-scalable Prometheus storage. Lesson: ingester head + block compaction, query sharding. Pitfall: deep microservice split (we stay coarser). Applied: Phase 2 (§14 head/WAL/blocks), §33 (ingest scaling), ADR-1/ADR-2.
  • Grafana Loki (pkg/ingester, pkg/chunkenc, pkg/storage) — label-index + chunked logs. Lesson: index labels not text, stream model. Study; do not copy its Go GC patterns. Applied: Phase 4 (§16 stream + label_index), ADR-6.
  • Grafana Tempo (tempodb, the trace-by-id path) — trace storage keyed by trace_id, search by tag. Lesson: cheap append + index for the pivot. Applied: Phase 3 (§15 trace_index/search_index), §3.4 (the pivot), ADR-7.
  • OpenTelemetry Collector (receiver/, processor/, exporter/, the pipeline model) — the ingestion pipeline + processors + backpressure. Lesson: the receiver→processor→exporter pipeline shape (our ingest). Applied: Phase 1 (§13 receiver→batch→transform→fan-out), ADR-11.
  • OpenTelemetry Collector — tailsamplingprocessor — the canonical tail-sampling buffer + policies. Lesson: decision window, policy composition, memory bounding. Applied: Phase 8 (§20 buffer/decision_window/policy), §4.2, ADR-13.
  • Prometheus (tsdb/, promql/) — the TSDB (Gorilla encoding, head, WAL, blocks) + the PromQL engine. Lesson: chunk layout, WAL replay, the query engine; exemplar storage. Reused via #83. Applied: Phase 2 (§14), Phase 6 (§18 promql), ADR-5/ADR-9.
  • VictoriaMetrics — an alternative high-perf TSDB + MetricsQL. Lesson: aggressive perf/cardinality handling. Pitfall: its bespoke query dialect. Applied: Phase 7 (§19 cardinality), Phase 12 (§24 perf), the Comparative Architecture Table.
  • Jaeger (cmd/collector, storage backends) — trace ingestion + storage + dependency derivation. Lesson: span model, the service-graph/RED derivation idea. Applied: Phase 3 (§15 span_ingest), Phase 5 (§17 span_to_metrics, I8).
  • Elasticsearch / OpenSearch (inverted index + columnar doc values) — the log-search lineage. Lesson: inverted index + doc-values columnar; the #78/#85 split. Pitfall: full-text on everything explodes cost (we index labels, body is columnar). Applied: Phase 4 (§16), ADR-6, the Alternative Architectures (full-text design).

Glossary (§40) · 36 terms · site-wide glossary

Active series
a time series currently receiving samples; the metrics-store's binding cost (cardinality).
Backpressure
bounded-queue + shed/block scheme that prevents the ingest plane from OOMing under a slow consumer.
Block
an immutable, atomically-published, time-ranged unit of per-signal storage (queries read these).
Burn rate
the rate at which an SLO's error budget is consumed; the basis of SLO alerting.
Cardinality
the number of distinct series/streams produced by a label set; the platform's existential constraint.
Columnar body (#85)
the log message + high-cardinality fields stored column-wise + compressed (scanned, not indexed).
Compactor
the background process doing compaction, downsampling, rollups, and retention.
Correlation
joining metrics, logs, and traces by shared identity (resource, trace_id) — the platform's defining feature.
Cross-signal join (pivot)
one logical query touching multiple signals joined by identity/time (metric→trace→logs).
Decision window
the max time tail sampling buffers a trace before deciding keep/drop.
Downsampling
producing lower-resolution (5m/1h) aggregated series for cheap long-range queries.
Exemplar
a {trace_id, span_id, value, ts} attached to a metric bucket; the metric→trace bridge.
Head
the in-memory, recent, append-target portion of a per-signal store (backed by a WAL).
Inverted index (#78)
label name=value → postings; the log/metric selector path.
LogQL
the log query language subset (stream selector + line/label filters + log-range aggregations).
Multi-window multi-burn-rate
an SLO alert combining fast and slow windows to catch acute + chronic without flapping.
OTLP
the OpenTelemetry Protocol; the gRPC/HTTP protobuf format the platform ingests.
Pillar
one of metrics, logs, traces (the three telemetry signals this platform unifies).
PromQL
the metric query language subset (instant/range vectors, rate, histogram_quantile, aggregations).
RED metrics
Rate, Errors, Duration; service metrics derivable from a span stream.
Relabel
ingest-time rule to drop/keep/rename/hash labels (the cardinality-control hook).
Resource
the OpenTelemetry entity identity (service.name, k8s., host.); the cross-signal resource join key.
Retention
the per-tenant per-signal TTL after which blocks are dropped.
Rollup
a recording-rule-style precomputed series (e.g. per-service RED at 1m).
Scrape
pull-based metric collection (Prometheus-style) from a target's /metrics.
Snapshot
a consistent set of published blocks + a head epoch a query reads (the determinism basis).
Span
a single timed operation in a trace; {trace_id, span_id, parent, timing, status, attrs}.
Staleness
marking a series with no recent sample as stale (no phantom carry-forward).
Stream (log)
a unique label set; the unit of log indexing/chunking.
Tail-based sampling
deciding keep/drop after the whole trace arrives (vs head sampling at the root).
Tenant
an isolated data partition (tenant_id); the multi-tenancy unit.
Trace
all spans sharing a trace_id, assembled into a parent/child tree.
Trace Context (W3C)
the traceparent/tracestate standard defining trace_id/span_id format.
TSDB (#83)
the time-series database backend (Gorilla chunks, head, WAL, blocks).
USE metrics
Utilization, Saturation, Errors; resource metrics (Brendan Gregg's method).
WAL
write-ahead log; durability for the in-memory head before blocks are cut.
