Hard questions

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does simplex search only vertices, never the interior (MM1, §3), and what does that imply about the feasible-set structure (Phase 4)?
  2. Why is "a basis" equivalent to "a vertex," and how does one pivot move between adjacent vertices (MM2, Phase 3/4)?
  3. Derive the entering-variable rule from reduced costs and state the optimality condition (MM3, Phase 4, I4).
  4. Why does the ratio test keep the new basis feasible (MM4, Phase 4), and what does a tie signify (MM5)?
  5. Why can simplex cycle on degenerate problems, and how do Bland's rule and lexicographic perturbation each prevent it (MM5, Phase 6, ADR-09)?
  6. Why factor B with LU rather than maintain B⁻¹ explicitly (MM6, ADR-06, Phase 9/10), and what does Forrest-Tomlin preserve?
  7. How does duality let you verify an optimum independently of how it was found (MM7, Phase 7/11, §4 KKT)?
  8. Why is comparing an f64 to exact 0.0 forbidden, and why must pivot_tol < feasibility_tol < optimality_tol (MM8, §4.2, P6)?
  9. Why is two-phase preferred over Big-M (ADR-10, Phase 5), and what numerical problem does Big-M introduce?
  10. Why does native bounded-variable simplex keep the basis smaller than adding explicit bound rows (ADR-11, Phase 8, §4 memory)?
  11. When does the dual simplex beat re-solving from scratch (ADR-12, Phase 11), and why is it essential for a branch-and-bound parent (Capacity Model warm-start)?
  12. Why refactorize every 100–200 pivots or when fill > 5× or cond > 1e8 (§4, Phase 10) rather than never or every pivot?
  13. Compute dense-tableau memory for m=n=1000 and revised-basis memory for m=10K, and explain why the revised path is mandatory above 1000 (Capacity Model, §4).
  14. Why keep the dense tableau as a reference oracle even though it's slower (ADR-05, Phase 3, §4 differential test) — what bug class does it catch?
  15. Why does presolve achieve ≥ 20% reduction (ADR-13, Phase 12, §4), and how does it interact with certificate validity (MM7)?
  16. How do primal-ray and dual-ray certificates classify unboundedness and infeasibility (Phase 7, §4 certificate verification, MM7)?
  17. Why is geometric-mean scaling O(nnz), and how does it lower the condition number without dense operations (ADR-14, Phase 12, Capacity Model)?
  18. Why is the solver core single-threaded with parallelism only at the batch-solve level (ADR-20, §4), and what about the inner loop resists parallelization?
  19. When would an interior-point method beat simplex (Comparative HiGHS, Deferred Register), and which problem structure flips the choice?
  20. When is SoPlex's exact-rational mode (Comparative) required over this engine's 1e-6 tolerance, and what §4 assumption makes floating point acceptable for v1?

Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — An LP optimum always sits at a vertex of the feasible polytope, so simplex walks vertices, never the interior. Predicts: the search space is the finite set of bases, not a continuum. Bug without it: trying to optimize over the interior (that's interior-point, a different method). Applied: §3, Phase 4, A1.
  • MM2 — A basis is the solution. Choosing m basic variables fixes a vertex; a pivot swaps one basic for one nonbasic, moving to an adjacent vertex. Predicts: the whole algorithm is bookkeeping over which variables are basic. Bug without it: recomputing from scratch each step instead of updating the basis. Applied: Phase 3/4, ADR-06.
  • MM3 — Reduced cost is the price signal. It gives the per-unit objective change of bringing a nonbasic variable in; optimality = no improving reduced cost. Predicts: pricing is choosing the best signal. Bug without it: a wrong entering-variable rule → stalls or wrong optimum. Applied: Phase 4, ADR-08, I4.
  • MM4 — The ratio test enforces feasibility. It picks the leaving variable that hits its bound first, bounding how far the entering variable can grow. Predicts: a tie in the ratio test = degeneracy = potential cycling. Bug without it: stepping too far → an infeasible basis. Applied: Phase 4/6, MM5.
  • MM5 — Degeneracy (a zero-length step) is what causes cycling; anti-cycling rules guarantee termination. Predicts: without Bland/lexicographic rules, simplex can loop forever on degenerate problems. Bug without it: relying on "it usually terminates" → an infinite loop on adversarial input. Applied: Phase 6, ADR-09, I7.
  • MM6 — Don't invert B; factor it. FTRAN/BTRAN solve Bx=b and Bᵀy=c via LU, and rank-1 updates (Forrest-Tomlin) keep the factorization cheap across pivots. Predicts: explicit B⁻¹ is dense and unstable; LU is sparse and stable. Bug without it: maintaining an explicit inverse → fill-in and numerical drift. Applied: Phase 9/10, ADR-06/07.
  • MM7 — Duality gives a certificate. Every optimal primal has a dual proving optimality; an infeasible/unbounded problem has a Farkas/ray certificate. Predicts: you can verify an answer independent of how you found it (KKT check). Bug without it: trusting the solver's "optimal" without a certificate → silent wrong answers. Applied: Phase 7/11, §4 (KKT verification).
  • MM8 — In floating point, never compare to exact zero — compare to a tolerance, and order the tolerances. Predicts: pivot_tol < feasibility_tol < optimality_tol is a hierarchy; mixing them causes wrong classifications. Bug without it: x == 0.0 on an f64 → spurious infeasibility or near-singular pivots. Applied: §4.2, P6, Phase 3.

5. Required Reading

Books

B1 — Dantzig — Linear Programming and Extensions (Princeton, 1963)

  • Chapters: 1–5 (simplex derivation, tableau mechanics, standard form); 7 (duality); 17 (degenerate pivots); 20 (bounded-variable extensions)
  • Concepts: Original pivot rule derivation; standard-form convention (slack + surplus + artificial); primal/dual complementarity; bounded-variable extension; the geometric interpretation of a basis as a vertex of the feasible polytope
  • Why read: Every subsequent textbook and implementation descends from this; reading it directly prevents cargo-culting intermediate sources; the bounded-variable chapter is the direct ancestor of lp-bounds

B2 — Chvátal — Linear Programming (W.H. Freeman, 1983)

  • Chapters: 3 (simplex algorithm); 4–5 (duality, complementary slackness); 7 (sensitivity analysis); 9 (Bland's rule proof + degeneracy); 10 (two-phase method); 11 (Big-M)
  • Concepts: Exact finite-termination proof of Bland's rule (required for I7); sensitivity ranging derivation for cost coefficients and RHS intervals (lp-sensitivity); complementary slackness conditions used to construct Certificate::KktOptimal (P1, P5); two-phase feasibility vs. Big-M tradeoffs
  • Why read: Canonical teaching text; proofs are rigorous and directly implementable; Chapter 9 is the definitive anti-cycling reference; sensitivity chapter maps directly onto Sensitivity return type

B3 — Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis — Introduction to Linear Optimization (Athena Scientific, 1997)

  • Chapters: 3 (simplex); 4 (duality, Farkas lemma); 5 (revised simplex, FTRAN/BTRAN); 6 (sensitivity); 7 (degeneracy, anti-cycling, perturbation method, complexity); 9 (network simplex — context only)
  • Concepts: Revised simplex derivation from first principles; formal FTRAN/BTRAN framework as triangular solves against an LU basis; lexicographic anti-cycling; perturbation method; Farkas' lemma proof for Certificate::DualRay (I8, P5); complexity bound argument
  • Why read: Best formal treatment of revised simplex and FTRAN/BTRAN; Farkas' lemma chapter underpins the infeasibility certificate; anti-cycling chapter gives the lexicographic method as an alternative to Bland

B4 — Maros — Computational Techniques of the Simplex Method (Kluwer, 2003)

  • Chapters: 2 (data structures for basis/tableau, CSC storage); 3 (pricing: Dantzig, Devex, steepest-edge); 4 (ratio test, stability); 5 (bounded-variable simplex); 6 (LU factorization, Forrest-Tomlin, Bartels-Golub updates, refactorization); 7 (numerical stability, pivot thresholds, Markowitz criterion); 8 (presolve + Curtis-Reid scaling); 9 (dual simplex)
  • Concepts: THE implementation reference; all of lp-lu, lp-bounds, lp-pricing, lp-presolve draw directly from here; refactorization trigger criteria (condition estimate, fill factor); Devex weight update formula; bounded-variable ratio test with bound flips; Markowitz pivot selection for fill reduction; Curtis-Reid iterative scaling
  • Why read: No other single source covers all the implementation decisions in this project; Chapters 5–7 are required reading before writing a single line of lp-lu or lp-bounds

B5 — Golub & Van Loan — Matrix Computations, 4th ed. (Johns Hopkins, 2013)

  • Chapters: 3 (LU factorization, partial pivoting, growth factor bounds); 4 (rank-1 updates, Sherman-Morrison); 5.6 (sparse triangular solves); 12 (sparse systems)
  • Concepts: Stability guarantees for LU with partial pivoting; growth-factor bound for PIVOT_TOL selection; triangular solve algorithms for FTRAN/BTRAN; numerical conditioning theory underlying P2
  • Why read: Provides the theoretical grounding that justifies lp-lu's design; growth-factor analysis explains why PIVOT_TOL ≥ 0.01 is necessary

B6 — Nocedal & Wright — Numerical Optimization, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2006)

  • Chapters: 13 (LP theory, vertex enumeration); 14 (simplex internals, degeneracy); 16 (active-set methods — context for bounded variables)
  • Concepts: KKT conditions stated precisely for LP; optimality tolerance justification; reduced-gradient interpretation of Dantzig pricing; connection between KKT and Certificate::KktOptimal
  • Why read: Bridges LP and nonlinear optimization; Chapter 14's implementation discussion complements Maros (B4) with a modern algorithmic perspective

B7 — Schrijver — Theory of Linear and Integer Programming (Wiley, 1986)

  • Chapters: 7 (Farkas' lemma and alternative systems); 8 (duality theorems, strong duality); 9 (LP algorithms and complexity)
  • Concepts: Rigorous Farkas' lemma derivation for Certificate::DualRay; strong duality theorem underlying I5; Fourier-Motzkin elimination as a presolve concept
  • Why read: Most complete theoretical treatment of infeasibility certificates; I8 verification logic requires this level of rigor

B8 — Vanderbei — Linear Programming: Foundations and Extensions, 4th ed. (Springer, 2014)

  • Chapters: 5 (sensitivity analysis — cost-ranging and RHS-ranging derivations); 13 (revised simplex); 18 (degeneracy and anti-cycling); 21 (bounded variables)
  • Concepts: Shadow-price derivation aligned directly with Sensitivity.shadow_prices; cost-coefficient ranging formula; RHS ranging; degenerate-case sensitivity caveats (ranges may degenerate to a point — must be handled in lp-sensitivity)
  • Why read: Most accessible sensitivity-analysis derivation; Chapter 18 covers perturbation method as a practical alternative to Bland

B9 — Wright — Primal-Dual Interior-Point Methods (SIAM, 1997)

  • Chapters: 1–2 (LP geometry, polytope); 5 (complementarity, duality gap); Appendix A (presolve reductions transferable to simplex)
  • Concepts: Interior-point stretch-goal framing; complementary slackness overlap with KKT; shared presolve reductions (singleton rows, bound tightening) applicable to lp-presolve
  • Why read: Provides context for the stretch-goal interior-point path; presolve appendix directly informs lp-presolve's reduction list

Papers

P1 — Dantzig (1951), Cowles Commission Monograph 13

  • Title: "Maximization of a linear function of variables subject to linear inequalities"
  • Contribution: First formal simplex algorithm
  • Relevance: Origin of lp-tableau pivot loop; standard-form convention; optimality termination criterion

P2 — Bland (1977), Mathematics of Operations Research 2(2)

  • Title: "New finite pivoting rules for the simplex method"
  • Contribution: Smallest-index entering and leaving variable guarantees finite termination

  • Title: "Updated Triangular Factors of the Basis to Maintain Sparsity in the Product Form Simplex Method"

  • Relevance: lp-lu update path; fill-control strategy during a pivot sequence; refactorization trigger when fill degrades; the key advance over Bartels-Golub for sparse matrices

P5 — Harris (1973), Mathematical Programming 5

  • Title: "Pivot selection methods of the Devex LP code"

P6 — Goldfarb & Forrest (1992), Mathematical Programming 57

  • Title: "Steepest-edge simplex algorithms for linear programming"

P7 — Reid (1982), Mathematical Programming 24

P8 — Suhl & Suhl (1993), ORSA Journal on Computing 5(3)

  • Title: "Computing sparse LU factorizations for large-scale LP bases"
  • Contribution: Practical pivot heuristics for sparse LP basis LU; update fill management
  • Relevance: lp-lu Markowitz pivot selection; memory layout for eta vectors; update count threshold for triggering refactorization

P9 — Curtis & Reid (1972), Journal of the Institute of Mathematics and Its Applications 10

  • Title: "On the automatic scaling of matrices for Gaussian elimination"
  • Contribution: Curtis-Reid iterative scaling algorithm; equilibrates row/column norms in the log-mean sense
  • Relevance: lp-presolve scaling implementation; convergence criterion for iterative scaling loop; reduces condition number before factorization

P10 — Andersen & Andersen (1995), Mathematical Programming 71

  • Title: "Presolving in Linear Programming"
  • Contribution: Systematic presolve reductions: empty rows/cols, singleton rows, bound tightening, forcing constraints, redundancy detection

P11 — Tomlin (1974), Mathematical Programming 6

  • Title: "On pricing and backward transformation in linear programming"
  • Contribution: BTRAN pricing for the dual simplex; reduced-cost computation as rc = c - y A after BTRAN
  • Relevance: lp-dual pricing path; dual ratio test setup; the computational advantage of BTRAN over re-computing from scratch

P12 — Koberstein (2005), PhD thesis, Universität Paderborn

  • Title: "The dual simplex method, techniques for a fast and stable implementation"
  • Contribution: Dual simplex ratio test stability; bound-flipping ratio test for dual; refactorization schedule; warm-start pivot sequence

P13 — Gill, Murray, Saunders, Wright (1987), Linear Algebra and Its Applications 88/89

  • Title: "Maintaining LU factors of a general sparse matrix"
  • Contribution: Stable rank-1 updates to LU factors under numerical perturbation; growth-factor monitoring
  • Relevance: lp-lu update stability; when to abandon eta updates and refactorize; growth-factor criteria for P2

P14 — Tomlin (1972), Mathematical Programming 2

  • Title: "Updated triangular factors of the basis" (companion to Forrest-Tomlin)
  • Contribution: Systematic comparison of product-form vs. updated-factor methods; refactorization frequency analysis

RFCs / Specifications

S1 — MPS Format Specification (IBM MPS + Extended MPS)

  • Sections: ROWS (free-row N, LE, GE, EQ types); COLUMNS (variable entries, integer markers); RHS; BOUNDS (LO, UP, FX, FR, MI, BV, LI, UI types); RANGES (modifies constraint bounds to create intervals); OBJSENSE (overrides minimize default)
  • Usage: lp-parse must handle all bound types; RANGES row creates [rhs, rhs + |range|] or [rhs - |range|, rhs] intervals depending on sign and constraint type; reference glpmps.c (R1) for edge cases including blank-field handling and integer markers

S2 — LP Format (CPLEX/lp_solve dialect)

  • Sections: Objective section (minimize/maximize keyword); Constraints section (>=, <=, = with named constraints); Bounds section (default 0 ≤ x < +∞; free keyword for unbounded; explicit l ≤ x ≤ u); General / Binary / SOS sections; \ comment syntax
  • Usage: lp-parse LP reader; free-row naming (obj: prefix); multi-line constraint continuation; variable name parsing (alphanumeric + _); whitespace handling in >= vs. > =

S3 — IEEE 754-2019 Floating-Point Arithmetic

  • Sections: Sec. 4 (infinity and NaN representation and propagation); Sec. 5.7 (comparison predicates — distinguishes <, ≤, =, unordered); Sec. 7 (exceptions: overflow, underflow, invalid, division by zero, inexact)
  • Usage: f64::INFINITY used for free-variable bounds; f64::NEG_INFINITY for lower bound of free vars; NaN in matrix operations is a correctness failure — all FTRAN/BTRAN results must be checked for NaN after debug builds; infinity propagation in ratio test must be guarded (bound of +inf → ratio = +inf → never the minimum ratio); all comparisons use named tolerances not literal 0.0 (P6)

S4 — KKT Optimality Conditions for LP

  • Reference: Chvátal Ch. 5 (B2); Bertsimas Ch. 4 (B3); Nocedal & Wright Ch. 13 (B6)
  • Statement: Primal feasibility (Ax = b, l ≤ x ≤ u); dual feasibility (rc = c - y A with rcⱼ ≥ 0 for NonbasicLower, rcⱼ ≤ 0 for NonbasicUpper, rcⱼ = 0 for basic); complementary slackness ((xⱼ - lⱼ)·rcⱼ = 0, (uⱼ - xⱼ)·rcⱼ = 0)
  • Usage: Certificate::KktOptimal construction; P1 post-solve verification; I4 and I5 simultaneous check; all three conditions checked within their respective tolerances (FEAS_TOL, OPT_TOL)

S5 — Farkas' Lemma

  • Reference: Schrijver Ch. 7 (B7); Bertsimas Ch. 4 (B3)
  • Statement (infeasibility form): {Ax = b, x ≥ 0} infeasible iff ∃y: yᵀA ≥ 0, yᵀb < 0
  • Usage: Certificate::DualRay for Status::Infeasible; I8 certificate validity check; lp-dual and lp-simplex Phase I infeasibility detection and ray extraction; dual-ray direction extracted from the Phase I auxiliary problem's optimal dual solution

Glossary · 65 terms · site-wide glossary

Artificial variable
Added in Phase I (Phase 5) to rows with Ge or Eq relations to obtain an initial basis. - Counted in StandardForm.n_artificial; must be driven to zero for primal feasibility. - Removed before Phase II; if any remain positive at Phase I optimum: Status::Infeasible.
Bartels-Golub update
Rank-1 update to basis LU after a pivot; modifies U via row operations; O(m²) worst case. - Stored as an eta vector appended to LuFactorization.l_eta; increments n_updates. - Reference: Bartels & Golub, CACM 1969; implemented in lp-lu/src/update.rs (Phase 10).
Basic feasible solution (BFS)
A vertex of the feasible polytope corresponding to an invertible m × m basis submatrix B. - Stored as Basis + primal solution x: Vec<f64>; degenerate when any basic variable equals a bound (zero step in ratio test). - Simplex (lp-simplex) traverses adjacent BFSs; each pivot moves along one edge of the polytope.
Basic variable
A variable currently in the basis (VarStatus::Basic); one per constraint row. - Value determined by the triangular solve B x_B = b - N x_N; exposed by Basis::basic_solution. - Leaving the basis means it becomes NonbasicLower or NonbasicUpper.
Basis
The m × m invertible submatrix of A defined by Basis.basic: Vec<VarId>. - Stored with factorization: Basis { basic, status, factor: LuFactorization }. - Must satisfy I1 (nonsingularity) at all times; violation triggers refactorization or pivot rejection.
Big-M method
Alternative to two-phase: penalize artificials by M >> 0 in the objective. - Documented in lp-simplex/src/phase1.rs; not the primary path (two-phase is preferred). - Numerical issues arise when M is large relative to problem coefficients; use two-phase instead.
Bland's rule
Anti-cycling: enter the smallest-index variable with r_j < -OPTIMALITY_TOL; break ties on leaving by smallest index. - Guarantees finite termination (I7) by preventing basis repetition. - Enum PivotRule::Bland in lp-pricing; activated by stall detection in lp-simplex driver (Phase 6).
Bound flip
Bounded-variable simplex (Phase 8, lp-bounds): entering nonbasic variable hits its opposite bound before any basic variable hits a bound. - RatioTestResult { leaving: None, step: delta, bound_flip: true }; objective updates, basis unchanged. - Handled by apply_bound_flip; flips VarStatus from NonbasicLower to NonbasicUpper or vice versa.
BTRAN (backward transformation)
Solves B^T y = c_B via reverse triangular solves on the LU factors. - Produces dual variables / row prices y = c_B B^{-1} used to compute reduced costs. - Implemented in lp-lu/src/btran.rs; the pricing side of the revised simplex (Phase 9).
Certificate
Proof accompanying every terminal Status: - Certificate::KktOptimal — primal feasibility + dual feasibility + complementary slackness. - Certificate::PrimalRay(Vec<f64>) — improving direction proving Status::Unbounded. - Certificate::DualRay(Vec<f64>) — Farkas witness proving Status::Infeasible. - Required by P5; verified in kkt_verifier.rs and integration tests (Phase 14).
Complementary slackness
KKT condition: for each variable j, r_j * (x_j - bound_j) = 0 within OPTIMALITY_TOL. - Equivalent formulation: nonbasic variables at their active bound, basic variables with zero reduced cost. - Verified by kkt_verifier.rs (Phase 14) for every Status::Optimal result.
Constraint
A single row a_i . x {<=, =, >=} b_i; identified by ConstraintId(u32). - Relation enum encodes the comparison: Le, Eq, Ge. - Stored in LpProblem.matrix (CSC column-major) and LpProblem.rhs: Vec<f64>.
CSC (compressed sparse column)
Storage format for matrix A in SparseMatrix: values: Vec<f64>, row_indices: Vec<usize>, col_ptrs: Vec<usize>. - Column j spans row_indices[col_ptrs[j]..col_ptrs[j+1]]; enables O(nnz_j) column access for FTRAN. - Used throughout; avoids dense m × n allocation for large sparse problems.
Curtis-Reid scaling
Iterative row/column scaling in lp-presolve: minimizes 2-norm of log-scaled entries to convergence. - Superior to geometric scaling for highly unbalanced matrices; applied before LU factorization. - Scale vectors r: Vec<f64> (rows), c: Vec<f64> (columns) stored for postsolve unscaling.
Dantzig rule
Standard pricing: enter argmin_{j: r_j < -OPTIMALITY_TOL} r_j (most negative reduced cost). - Greedy; reduces objective most per pivot step but may cycle on degenerate problems. - Enum PivotRule::Dantzig; fastest per-pivot selection; default before stall detection.
Degeneracy
BFS where one or more basic variables equal a bound; produces RatioTestResult.step == 0. - Zero-step pivots make no primal progress but may still change basis; repeated degenerate pivots can cycle. - Handled by Phase 6 anti-cycling (Bland / perturbation); degenerate pivot count tracked in driver.
Devex / steepest-edge pricing
Approximate steepest-edge: weight entering selection by estimated w_j ≈ ||B^{-1} a_j||². - Score: r_j² / w_j; weights initialized to 1.0, updated per pivot, reset on refactorization. - Enum PivotRule::Devex in lp-pricing; typically reduces pivot count vs Dantzig on large problems.
Dual feasibility
Reduced costs have correct signs: r_j >= 0 for NonbasicLower, r_j <= 0 for NonbasicUpper. - Maintained throughout lp-dual (I3); checked within OPTIMALITY_TOL. - Necessary condition for KKT optimality (I4); verified before accepting warm-start basis.
Dual ray
Direction d with A^T d >= 0 (standard-form minimization) and b . d < 0. - Proves infeasibility via Farkas' lemma; emitted as Certificate::DualRay(Vec<f64>). - Validity checked in tests/integration/infeasible.rs (Phase 14).
Dual simplex
Maintains dual feasibility; restores primal feasibility by pivoting. - Leaving variable: most infeasible basic (x_B_r < lb_r or x_B_r > ub_r). - Entering variable: dual ratio test ensuring dual feasibility is preserved. - Implemented in lp-dual (Phase 11); ideal for warm-start reoptimization after bound/RHS changes (P8).
Duality
Every LP min c.x s.t. A x >= b has dual max b.y s.t. A^T y <= c, y >= 0. - Shadow prices are dual optimal values; feasible primal+dual with equal objectives proves optimality. - Strong duality (I5): at optimum, c.x* = b.y* within OPTIMALITY_TOL.
Eta vector
Sparse encoding of a single pivot update to L or U in the LU factorization. - LuFactorization.l_eta: Vec<EtaVector>; each entry records the column index and nonzero values. - Accumulation degrades FTRAN/BTRAN performance; triggers refactorization when n_updates > REFACTOR_LIMIT.
Farkas' lemma
Theorem: LP is infeasible iff b . d < 0 for some d with A^T d >= 0. - Constructive: dual simplex's dual ray satisfies this; forms Certificate::DualRay. - Reference: Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis §3.5; also Chvatal Chapter 7.
Feasibility tolerance
Named constant FEASIBILITY_TOL (~1e-7) in lp-core/src/tolerance.rs. - Threshold for |a_i . x - b_i| <= FEASIBILITY_TOL in primal-feasibility checks. - Used consistently in ratio test, postsolve verification, Phase I residual check (P6).
Feasible region
Polytope { x : A x = b, l <= x <= u } in standard form; convex. - Vertices are BFSs; edges connect adjacent vertices (bases differing by one column). - Empty feasible region → Status::Infeasible; unbounded recession direction → Status::Unbounded.
Fill-in
New nonzeros created in L or U during LU factorization or eta-vector updates. - Monitored by REFACTOR_FILL_RATIO; excessive fill triggers refactorization (I1, P2). - Markowitz ordering in lp-lu/src/factor.rs minimizes fill during initial factorization.
Forrest-Tomlin update
Rank-1 LU update preserving L factor, modifying only U; more stable than Bartels-Golub. - Primary update in lp-lu/src/update.rs (Phase 10). - Reference: Forrest & Tomlin, Mathematical Programming, 1972.
FTRAN (forward transformation)
Solves B d = a_j for the entering column's representation in the current basis. - Used in ratio test (compute step sizes) and basis update (eta vector construction). - Implemented in lp-lu/src/ftran.rs via forward triangular solve with permutations.
Geometric scaling
Scale row i by sqrt(max_j |a_ij| / min_j |a_ij|), column j similarly; O(nnz). - Good first-pass before Curtis-Reid; reduces extreme value ranges in A. - Applied in lp-presolve/src/scale.rs; vectors stored for postsolve unscaling.
Infeasible
Status::Infeasible: no point satisfies all constraints. - Proven by Phase I (lp-simplex/src/phase1.rs): sum-of-artificials objective > 0 at optimum. - Accompanied by Certificate::DualRay; I8 requires the ray satisfies Farkas' system.
KKT conditions
Necessary and sufficient for LP optimality: (1) primal feasibility, (2) dual feasibility, (3) complementary slackness. - Verified to emit Certificate::KktOptimal; all three checked within named tolerances (P1, P6). - Verified in kkt_verifier.rs for every Status::Optimal result in the conformance suite.
LpProblem
Root problem type: { sense: Sense, objective: Vec<f64>, matrix: SparseMatrix, rhs: Vec<f64>, relations: Vec<Relation>, bounds: Vec<Bound> }. - Built via ProblemBuilder; validated on build() (dimension agreement, bound sanity). - Passed to lp-presolve then converted to StandardForm by lp-model.
LU factorization
Decomposition P A Q = L U with row/column permutations for numerical stability. - Stored as LuFactorization { l_eta: Vec<EtaVector>, u: SparseTriangular, row_perm, col_perm, n_updates }. - Enables O(m²) FTRAN/BTRAN; updated by Forrest-Tomlin; refactorized when condition degrades (P2, I1).
Markowitz ordering
Pivot-selection heuristic during sparse LU factorization minimizing fill-in. - Score for entry (i, j): (nnz(row i) - 1) * (nnz(col j) - 1); choose minimum-score pivot. - Implemented in lp-lu/src/factor.rs; reduces eta-vector density and FTRAN/BTRAN cost.
MPS format
Standard LP serialization (fixed-column and free variants). - Sections: NAME, ROWS, COLUMNS, RHS, BOUNDS (UP/LO/FX/FR/MI), RANGES, OBJSENSE. - Parsed by lp-parse/src/mps.rs; Netlib test set uses this format.
Netlib LP test set
Public benchmark: ~80 LP instances at netlib.org/lp; sizes from tens to thousands of rows. - Reference optima in tests/conformance/netlib_expected.csv; objective must match within 1e-6. - Primary conformance criterion for Phase 14; includes infeasible and unbounded cases.
Nonbasic variable
Variable not in the basis; fixed at lb (NonbasicLower) or ub (NonbasicUpper). - Contributes c_j * x_j to the objective; reduced cost r_j governs whether it should enter. - Free nonbasic (VarStatus::Free): only in revised path; price r_j = 0 to enter if nonzero gradient.
Objective function
Linear form c . x; stored in LpProblem.objective: Vec<f64>. - Maximization normalized to minimization in StandardForm by negating c (Phase 2). - Optimal value reported in Solution.objective (in original sense after postsolve).
Optimality tolerance
Named constant OPTIMALITY_TOL (~1e-9) in lp-core/src/tolerance.rs. - Threshold for r_j >= -OPTIMALITY_TOL in Phase II termination check (I4). - Also used in KKT verification and dual feasibility checks; consistent across all modules (P6).
Partial pivoting
During LU factorization: swap rows to ensure |pivot| >= PIVOT_TOL * max(|col|). - Stabilizes U entries against catastrophic cancellation; row permutation stored in row_perm. - Implemented in lp-lu/src/factor.rs; prevents Status::Numerical from ill-conditioned pivots.
Perturbation method
Anti-cycling alternative to Bland: add small epsilon to RHS entries to remove degeneracy. - All BFSs become non-degenerate; no zero-step pivots possible; removed in postsolve. - Documented in lp-simplex/src/phase2.rs; reference: Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis §3.5.
Pivot
Exchange of one basic variable (leaving) and one nonbasic variable (entering). - Dense: Gaussian elimination on pivot row/column in lp-tableau; O(m·n). - Revised: FTRAN for entering column + LU update; O(m²) amortized; production path (P4).
Pivot tolerance
Named constant PIVOT_TOL in lp-core/src/tolerance.rs. - Minimum |d_r| before accepting the ratio-test leaving variable; prevents near-singular updates. - If no eligible leaving variable has |d_r| >= PIVOT_TOL: refactorize or skip this entering variable.
Postsolve
Reverse application of the presolve operation stack in lp-presolve/src/postsolve.rs. - Maps reduced Solution.x back to original variable space; unscales using stored scale vectors. - Must produce a feasible original solution with the same objective; verified by I9.
Primal feasibility
All constraints satisfied: A x = b (standard form) and l <= x <= u within FEASIBILITY_TOL. - Maintained throughout Phase II (I2); checked in postsolve and check subcommand. - kkt_verifier.rs verifies this as condition (1) of KKT.
Primal ray
Direction d with c . d < 0 (minimization) and A d = 0, d >= 0 (standard form). - Proves Status::Unbounded; emitted as Certificate::PrimalRay(Vec<f64>). - Constructed when ratio test finds no eligible leaving variable; validated in integration tests.
Primal simplex
Maintains primal feasibility; improves objective by entering variables with negative reduced cost. - Dense path: lp-tableau (Phases 3–7); revised path: lp-revised + lp-lu (Phases 9–10). - Two-phase structure: Phase I achieves feasibility, Phase II achieves optimality.
Ratio test
Determines the leaving variable: theta_i = (x_B_i - lb_i) / d_i for d_i > 0. - Take min theta_i; in bounded-variable case also consider theta = (ub_j - lb_j) for entering variable. - Result RatioTestResult { leaving: Option<usize>, step: f64, bound_flip: bool }; I6 guarantees correctness.
Reduced cost
r_j = c_j - y . a_j where y = c_B B^{-1} (dual prices from BTRAN). - Negative r_j (minimization): variable j can improve the objective; candidate for entering. - Zero for basic variables (complementary slackness); stored in Solution.reduced_costs: Vec<f64>.
Refactorization
Full recomputation of basis LU from scratch in lp-lu/src/refactor.rs. - Triggered when n_updates > REFACTOR_LIMIT or fill ratio > REFACTOR_FILL_RATIO. - Resets n_updates = 0; restores numerical accuracy; maintains I1 and P2.
Revised simplex
Avoids materializing the full m × n tableau; uses FTRAN/BTRAN on LU for all operations. - Memory: O(nnz(A) + m²) vs O(m·n) for dense; critical for large sparse problems. - Implemented in lp-revised (Phase 9); the production path per P4.
Scaling
Row/column diagonal rescaling of A, b, c to reduce condition number. - Methods: geometric (one pass) or Curtis-Reid (iterative); implemented in lp-presolve/src/scale.rs. - Applied before factorization; reversed in postsolve; improves pivot quality and reduces fill.
Sensitivity analysis
Post-optimal ranging: how far can c_j (cost) or b_i (RHS) change while keeping current basis optimal? - Cost range: [c_j - delta_lo, c_j + delta_hi] where basis optimality (dual feasibility) is maintained. - RHS range: [b_i - mu_lo, b_i + mu_hi] where basis primal feasibility is maintained. - Shadow prices (duals y_i) quantify objective change per unit RHS change; stored in Sensitivity.
Shadow price
Dual variable y_i for constraint i; y_i = dz*/db_i at optimum. - Stored in Solution.duals_y: Vec<f64> and Sensitivity.shadow_prices: Vec<f64>. - Zero when constraint is not tight (positive slack); sign depends on constraint direction (Le vs Ge).
Singleton row
Constraint row with exactly one nonzero entry; substituted directly in presolve. - Eliminates a variable and a constraint; operation saved in presolve stack for postsolve recovery. - Handled in lp-presolve/src/presolve.rs (Phase 12).
Slack variable
Added to Le constraint: a_i . x + s_i = b_i, s_i >= 0. - VarStatus::NonbasicLower (zero) when constraint is tight; counted in StandardForm.n_slack. - Provides an initial basic variable for Phase I; positive slack indicates the constraint is not binding.
Solution
Terminal result type: { status: Status, objective: f64, x: Vec<f64>, duals_y: Vec<f64>, reduced_costs: Vec<f64> }. - Accompanied by Certificate; valid iff status = Optimal satisfies I2, I3, I4, I5. - Written to output by lp-parse/src/writer.rs; verified by lp-cli check subcommand.
StandardForm
Canonical form min c x, A x = b, x >= 0 produced by lp-model/src/standard_form.rs. - Fields: { A_std, b, c, n_struct, n_slack, n_artificial, var_map }. - Equivalent to the original LP by I10; var_map enables postsolve recovery of original variables.
Strong duality
At optimum: c.x* = b.y* within OPTIMALITY_TOL (I5). - Primal and dual objectives must agree; checked as part of KKT verification. - Failure indicates numerical error in dual computation or postsolve unscaling.
Surplus variable
Added to Ge constraint: a_i . x - s_i = b_i, s_i >= 0. - Paired with an artificial variable for Phase I basis; counted in StandardForm.n_slack. - Positive surplus indicates the constraint is satisfied strictly (not binding).
Two-phase method
Phase I (Phase 5): minimize sum of artificials; drives to feasible BFS or proves infeasibility. - Phase II (Phase 4): optimize original objective from the feasible basis obtained by Phase I. - Primary feasibility strategy; Big-M is documented as an alternative in phase1.rs.
Unbounded
Status::Unbounded: objective can be decreased without bound (minimization). - Detected in Phase II when ratio test finds no eligible leaving variable (leaving = None). - Accompanied by Certificate::PrimalRay; I8 requires the ray to be a valid unbounded direction.
VarId / ConstraintId
Newtype wrappers VarId(u32) and ConstraintId(u32) providing type-safe dense indices. - Index into LpProblem.objective (VarId) and LpProblem.rhs (ConstraintId). - Prevent accidental mixing of row and column indices at compile time.
VarStatus
Enum encoding a variable's current role: Basic, NonbasicLower, NonbasicUpper, Free. - Stored in Basis.status: Vec<VarStatus>; governs reduced-cost sign check for I4. - Updated on every pivot and bound flip; must remain consistent with Basis.basic membership.
Warm start
Supply a saved Basis to lp-dual::warm_start to reoptimize without re-running Phase I + II. - Basis must be dual-feasible; warm.rs checks this before entering the dual loop (P8). - Enables fast reoptimization after small RHS or bound changes; tested in reopt.rs. ---
