33. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Default per-connection memory footprint = rx_buf(64 KiB) + tx_buf(64 KiB) + TCB/retransmit-queue/timer overhead(~16 KiB) = 144 KiB. Chains into #2.
  2. Max concurrent connections at 16 GiB (§4) = 16×1024×1024 KiB / 144 KiB ≈ 116,508; applying ~15% allocator-fragmentation headroom yields the M11 target of ≥ 100,000.
  3. Loopback bandwidth-delay product at 1 Gbps (§4 M1), RTT ≈ 200 µs (§4) = 1e9 bit/s × 200e-6 s / 8 = 25,000 bytes ≈ 24.4 KiB — below the default 64 KiB window, so no window scaling is required to saturate loopback.
  4. WAN bandwidth-delay product at 1 Gbps, 100 ms RTT (§4 congestion test profile) = 1e9 × 0.1 / 8 = 12,500,000 bytes ≈ 11.9 MiB — far above the 64 KiB default window, which is exactly why RFC 7323 window scaling (up to shift 14, §8.5) is mandatory for WAN throughput, not a nice-to-have (PMTUD deferral, §36, notwithstanding).
  5. ARP cache size at a /24 subnet: 254 hosts × ~40 bytes/entry (IP+MAC+timestamp+ intrusive pointers) ≈ 10.2 KiB — trivially small versus the 16 GiB budget, which is why a flat hash map (not a compressed trie) suffices (§13.7).
  6. IPv4 routing table at end-host scale (~1,000 entries, never a full BGP table — §1 non-goals) × ~64 bytes/trie node ≈ 64 KiB — confirms a simple trie, not a hardware TCAM, suffices for an end-host stack.
  7. Timer wheel sizing: covering the RTO range 200 ms–60 s (§4) at 10 ms tick granularity needs 6,000 buckets at the finest hierarchy level (Varghese & Lauck); a 3-level cascade (250 ms/tick × 64 buckets per level) trades ~40 bytes/timer-slot overhead for O(1) amortized insert (ADR-04).
  8. UDP echo pps ceiling: at M8's 1.5 Gbps with 1500-byte frames, 1.5e9/8/1500 ≈ 125,000 pps; at a typical ~150 ns/syscall pair (recvfrom+sendto), that's ≤ 18.75 ms/s consumed in syscalls — headroom exists, but explains why batched syscalls (recvmmsg/sendmmsg, a future extension) become necessary above ~500,000 pps.
  9. Fuzz corpus throughput: at a typical ~50,000 exec/s single-core libFuzzer rate for a parser this size, a 24 h canary (M13) executes ≈ 4.32 billion iterations — the basis for the ≥95% line-coverage target being achievable in the stated window.
  10. TIME_WAIT table size under churn: at C connections/sec × 2×MSL(60 s) = C×60 concurrent TIME_WAIT entries; at M3's 50,000/s sustained, that's 3,000,000 entries × ~200 bytes/entry ≈ 572 MiB — a material fraction of the 16 GiB budget, which is why RFC 6191 reuse (I10) matters operationally, not merely for correctness.
