Hard questions (§33)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why must a participant's Yes vote be durable before it is sent, and what specifically breaks if the send happens first? (§6 P1, §7 I5, §15)
  2. Why does presumed abort require the coordinator to log nothing on the common path, rather than logging Preparing explicitly? (§6 P4, §16)
  3. Why is 2PC's blocking problem not fixable by simply adding a participant- side timeout that unilaterally decides? (§6 P5, §17)
  4. What exact structural fact about reaching PreCommitted makes 3PC's autonomous-commit timeout safe, when the analogous move is unsafe in 2PC? (§6 P6, §7 I6, §18)
  5. Why does 3PC's termination protocol need a quorum, not just a second opinion from one peer? (§6 P7, §7 I13, §19)
  6. In what precise sense is 3PC "non-blocking" — non-blocking against which fault class, and why does that stop being true under partitions? (§6 P11, §19, §23 FM-7)
  7. Why does a network partition attack 3PC's liveness rather than its safety, and how would you demonstrate that distinction with a fixture? (§7 I7 vs. I13, §19, §30 MM5)
  8. What would have to change about the log format (§11) to support the deferred presumed-commit optimization, and why wasn't it built in v1? (§9 ADR-04, §35)
  9. Why is Paxos Commit's fix (replicate the coordinator) a fundamentally different technique from 3PC's fix (add a phase), even though both are framed as "solving 2PC's problem"? (§20, §30 MM8, §34)
  10. What assumption would have to be violated for the fail-stop/omission fault model (ADR-06) to be insufficient for this project's safety proofs, and what would replace it? (§7 I10, §22 T10, §34)
  11. Why does idempotency have to live in the message handler rather than being solved by making the transport exactly-once? (§6 P9, §30 MM7, §15)
  12. Why is a transaction's cohort fixed at begin() rather than allowed to change mid-transaction, and what alternative architecture accepts that tradeoff differently? (§9 ADR-10, §34)
  13. Trace the exact log-write and message sequence that distinguishes "2PC coordinator crashed before deciding" from "2PC coordinator crashed after deciding, before broadcasting" — why do these two crash points require different recovery actions? (§8.5–§8.6, §16.9)
  14. Why does the message-count formula (4N for 2PC, 6N for 3PC) matter operationally, not just theoretically? (§32 items 6–7, §29)
  15. Why does PostgreSQL accept 2PC's blocking risk in PREPARE TRANSACTION rather than adopting a non-blocking protocol? (§31, §5 R1)
  16. Why does Percolator/TiKV need no dedicated coordinator process at all, and what property of its underlying storage layer makes that safe? (§31, §34, §5 R4/P9)
  17. What is the operational difference between a transaction that is "stably blocked" (FM-1/FM-7) and one that has actually violated a safety invariant (FM-6), and why must CI treat them differently? (§21.6, §21.9, §23)
  18. Why does linear-scan log recovery become the first-named scalability bottleneck rather than coordinator fan-out, at this project's specific constraint values? (§29, §32 item 8, §4)
  19. If Byzantine participants were introduced, which specific invariant (I1–I13) would be the first to fail, and why does PBFT's 3f+1 replication factor address exactly that failure? (§7, §22 T10, §34)
  20. Why is the Sagas pattern not simply "a worse atomic commit protocol," but a different transaction model entirely — and under what regime does that difference make it the better choice? (§23, §34)

30. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — "Prepared is a promise, not a state." Compressed insight: once a participant votes Yes, it has made an external commitment it cannot retract, not merely entered a label called Prepared. Predicts: any design letting Prepared participants "change their mind" is unsafe. Bug class without it: ad-hoc unilateral-abort timeouts (§17.6). Resolving §: §7 (I3), §17.

  • MM2 — "Durability must precede disclosure." Compressed insight: never tell another node something your own crash could make untrue (P1). Predicts: every safe protocol has a force-write immediately before every promise-carrying send. Bug class without it: FM-2's log-order violations. Resolving §: §6 (P1), §13.

  • MM3 — "Silence needs a default, and the default is a design choice." Compressed insight: presumed abort isn't "no logging" — it's choosing which outcome silence means (Mohan et al. 1986). Predicts: any recovery scheme needs an explicit default-on-missing-record rule or it's ambiguous, not merely "simple." Bug class without it: recovery that can't distinguish "never started" from "committed, log lost." Resolving §: §6 (P4), §16.

  • MM4 — "One more phase buys a witness, not a guarantee." Compressed insight: 3PC's third phase doesn't eliminate the coordinator's importance — it ensures a participant is never the sole holder of decision- adjacent knowledge (Skeen 1981). Predicts: 3PC's protection degrades exactly when "ask a witness" becomes impossible (partition). Bug class without it: believing 3PC is unconditionally non-blocking. Resolving §: §6 (P6/P7), §18, §19.

  • MM5 — "Safety and liveness are different axes — partitions attack liveness first, safety only if you let them." Compressed insight: 3PC under partition blocks (loses liveness) by design so it does not disagree (lose safety) (I13). Predicts: any "fix" that makes a blocked minority decide anyway trades a liveness problem for a safety problem — strictly worse. Bug class without it: quorum-bypass "fixes" (FM-6). Resolving §: §7 (I7/I13), §19.

  • MM6 — "The coordinator's crash-recovery decision must be computable from its own log alone." Compressed insight: recovery cannot re-poll votes that were never durable (they're gone) — it can only trust what it wrote (P3). Predicts: any recovery design that "re-asks participants how they voted" as its primary mechanism is solving the wrong problem (that's the termination protocol's job, not ordinary recovery). Resolving §: §6 (P3), §16.

  • MM7 — "Idempotency is a property of the handler, not the transport." Compressed insight: you cannot rely on the network to deliver exactly once (I10); the handler itself must be safe under redelivery (P9). Predicts: any "we'll just make delivery reliable" plan still needs idempotent handlers, because reliable ≠ exactly-once. Bug class without it: FM-8's duplicate-apply bugs. Resolving §: §6 (P9), §15.

  • MM8 — "Adding fault tolerance to the coordinator is a different fix than adding a phase to the protocol." Compressed insight: Paxos Commit (Gray & Lamport 2006) solves coordinator-crash-tolerance by replicating the coordinator; Skeen's 3PC solves participant-blocking by adding a phase — these are orthogonal techniques answering different questions. Predicts: combining them (a replicated 3PC coordinator) is a coherent, real design point, not a contradiction. Resolving §: §20, §31, §34.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Bernstein, Hadzilacos & Goodman, Concurrency Control and Recovery in Database Systems (Addison-Wesley, 1987), ch. 7 "Distributed Recovery" — extract: formal 2PC/3PC state-transition diagrams, presumed-abort proof sketch. Applied in §8 / Phase 3 (§15).
  • Gray & Reuter, Transaction Processing: Concepts and Techniques (Morgan Kaufmann, 1993), ch. 7 ("Two-Phase Commit") — extract: coordinator/ participant log-write ordering, heuristic-decision hazards. Applied in §9 / Phase 4 (§16).
  • Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017), ch. 9 "Consistency and Consensus" — extract: 2PC-as-consensus-with-a-single- point-of-failure framing, contrast with real consensus. Applied in §31 / Phase 8 (§20).
  • Lynch, Distributed Algorithms (Morgan Kaufmann, 1996), ch. 7 "Commit Protocols" — extract: formal safety/liveness statements for 2PC and 3PC. Applied in §7 / Phase 6 (§18).
  • Tanenbaum & Van Steen, Distributed Systems: Principles and Paradigms (2nd ed., Pearson, 2007), ch. 8 "Fault Tolerance" — extract: participant termination-protocol table (what to do on timeout in each state). Applied in Phase 5 (§17) / Phase 7 (§19).
  • Weikum & Vossen, Transactional Information Systems (Morgan Kaufmann, 2001), ch. 19 "Coordinator/Participant Failure and Recovery" — extract: recovery-protocol classification (independent vs. cooperative). Applied in §23 / Phase 5 (§17).

  • Skeen, D. & Stonebraker, M. (1983). "A Formal Model of Crash Recovery in a Distributed System." IEEE TSE, SE-9(3) — formal failure-mode taxonomy for commit protocols. Applied in §23 / §22.

  • Gray, J. & Lamport, L. (2006). "Consensus on Transaction Commit." ACM TODS 31(1), 2006 — Paxos Commit, replicated coordinator. Applied in Phase 8 (§20) / §34.
  • Lamport, L. (1998). "The Part-Time Parliament." ACM TOCS 16(2) — Paxos, the consensus core Paxos Commit runs per decision. Applied in §34 / §20.
  • Castro, M. & Liskov, B. (1999). "Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance." OSDI — Byzantine alternative to the fail-stop model this project uses. Applied in §34.

  • Peng, D. & Dabek, F. (2010). "Large-scale Incremental Processing Using Distributed Transactions and Notifications." OSDI — Percolator, lock-table-based decentralized 2PC. Applied in §34 / §31.

Reference Implementations

  • PostgreSQL, src/backend/access/transam/twophase.c — study PREPARE TRANSACTION / COMMIT PREPARED record layout. Lesson: 2-phase state survives server restart via the same WAL used for ordinary commits. Pitfall: orphaned prepared transactions hold locks/vacuum horizon indefinitely if never resolved (mirrors FM-1). Applied in §31.
  • MySQL/InnoDB, storage/innobase XA support (trx0trx.cc) — study XA PREPARE/XA COMMIT state transitions. Lesson: XA maps directly onto 2PC's Prepare/Commit verbs (§11). Pitfall: coordinator log loss orphans prepared InnoDB transactions (mirrors FM-1). Applied in §9 (ADR-21).
  • Apache Kafka, core/.../coordinator/transaction/TransactionCoordinator.scala — study the transaction-state log (__transaction_state topic) as the coordinator's durable log. Lesson: a replicated log can be the coordinator's DurableLog (§13). Applied in §31.
  • TiKV (TiDB storage engine), percolator-style transaction layer — study the two-phase prewrite/commit implementation. Lesson: a lock table in shared storage removes the need for a dedicated coordinator process (Percolator model). Applied in §34.
  • CockroachDB, parallel-commits design — study how it collapses 2PC's two round trips into effectively one for the common case. Lesson: the count of phases is a latency knob, not a safety requirement, if you change what "prepared" means. Applied in §29.

RFCs / Specifications

  • X/Open Company Ltd., Distributed Transaction Processing: The XA Specification (X/Open CAE Specification, 1991) — canonical xa_prepare/xa_commit/xa_rollback verb set. Applied in §9 (ADR-21) / §11.

Glossary (§36) · 47 terms · site-wide glossary

2PC
Two-Phase Commit; atomic commit protocol with a Prepare vote phase and a Commit/Abort decision phase (Gray 1978).
3PC
Three-Phase Commit; Skeen's non-blocking-under-fail-stop variant of 2PC adding a PreCommit phase (Skeen 1981).
Ack
acknowledgment message confirming a participant applied the coordinator's decision.
Acceptor
Paxos role that votes on proposed values; used per-decision in Paxos Commit (Gray & Lamport 2006).
Atomic commit
property that all participants in a transaction reach the same outcome (all commit or all abort).
Blocking problem
2PC's namesake failure mode: a participant that voted Yes cannot safely proceed without the coordinator's decision.
Byzantine fault
arbitrary or malicious node behavior, beyond simple crash-stop (Castro & Liskov 1999); out of scope here (I10).
CanCommit
3PC's first-phase message asking participants if they can commit (analog of 2PC's Prepare).
Chaos matrix
the Phase 9 fixture set covering every fault-injector variant × crash-boundary combination.
Cohort
the fixed set of participants in a single transaction (I12).
Coordinator
the node driving a transaction's vote collection and decision.
CrashNode
a FaultInjector variant simulating a node crash at a given tick.
DoCommit
3PC's third-phase message instructing participants to commit after PreCommit acks are collected.
DurableLog
the append-only, force-write-barriered log abstraction each node owns (§11).
Fail-stop
a fault model where nodes either operate correctly or halt completely, never behaving incorrectly while "alive."
Fixture
a JSON file under fixtures/ specifying a scenario and its expected outcome, used for conformance testing.
Force / force()
the simulated durability barrier standing in for a real fsync() call (ADR-08).
FSM
finite state machine; the implementation pattern for both coordinator and participant roles.
Happy path
the zero-fault execution of a protocol round.
Heuristic decision
(historical term, not implemented here) a participant unilaterally deciding without coordinator authority; the scenario 2PC's design forbids (I3).
I1–I13
this document's numbered system invariants (§7).
Idempotent handler
a message handler safe to invoke multiple times with the same input without changing behavior beyond the first call (P9).
LogRecord
the tagged-union type for every durable log entry (§11).
Logical clock
the seeded, tick-based timing source all timeouts read (P10, ADR-07), never wall-clock time.
M1–M12
this document's numbered performance targets (§28).
Non-blocking
a protocol property where no correctly-operating node is forced to wait indefinitely for a crashed node, under a stated fault model (here: fail-stop, no partition — I6).
Omission fault
a message drop, delay, duplication, or reorder, but never corruption (I10).
P1–P13
this document's numbered design principles (§6).
Participant
a node that votes on and applies a transaction's outcome.
Partition
a network fault preventing communication between two node groups while both remain individually alive.
Paxos Commit
Gray & Lamport's (2006) atomic commit protocol replacing the single coordinator with a Paxos-replicated one.
Percolator
Google's lock-table-based decentralized transaction model (Peng & Dabek 2010).
Prepare
2PC's first-phase message asking participants to vote.
PreCommit
3PC's second-phase message, sent after unanimous Yes votes, before final commit.
Prepared (state)
a participant's post-Yes-vote, pre-decision state.
Presumed abort
the optimization where a missing coordinator log record on recovery is interpreted as Abort (Mohan et al. 1986, P4).
Presumed commit
the inverse optimization (missing record ⇒ Commit), deferred in this project (ADR-04).
Quorum
a majority (or otherwise defined threshold) subset of a cohort, required by 3PC's termination protocol before deciding (P7).
Recovery
the process of rebuilding FSM state from the durable log after a crash.
Sagas
a compensating-transaction alternative to atomic commit for long-lived transactions (Garcia-Molina & Salem 1987).
Skeen's algorithm
the specific non-blocking commit protocol this project's "3PC" implements (Skeen 1981).
Termination protocol
the cooperative, quorum-bounded procedure participants run on coordinator timeout in 3PC (P7).
TxnId
the unique identifier for a transaction (§11).
Unilateral abort
a participant deciding Abort on its own authority, safe only before voting (I2).
Vote
a participant's Yes/No response to Prepare/CanCommit.
Wire format
the serialized representation of protocol messages, tag-based per ADR-13.
XA
the X/Open Distributed Transaction Processing standard's prepare/commit/rollback verb set (X/Open, 1991). ---
