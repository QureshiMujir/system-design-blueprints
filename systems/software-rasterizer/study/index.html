32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — Rasterization is a point-in-triangle test evaluated on a lattice. Every quality rule — fill conventions, interpolation, antialiasing — is a statement about how that lattice is sampled. It predicts that any visual artifact traces back to either where the lattice samples are placed (MSAA, P10) or how "inside" is decided at a sample (the fill rule, P1). Without it, a developer treats cracks and seams as separate bug classes instead of the same underlying sampling-consistency question. Resolved in section 8 and Phase 2.

MM2 — The whole pipeline is a memory-access-pattern problem wearing geometry clothes. Tiling exists because cache misses, not FLOPs, dominate a flat framebuffer's cost (Drepper 2007). It predicts that the biggest single-phase speedup (Phase 10) comes from a data-layout change, not a math optimization. Without it, a developer chases SIMD and algebraic optimizations first and is surprised when tiling alone dwarfs them. Resolved in Phase 10 and section 30.

MM3 — Homogeneous coordinates make projection a matrix, at the cost of a divide you must not skip or reorder. w carries the information that perspective divide will later consume; treating a clip-space vector as if it were already Euclidean (dividing early, or clipping late) is the single most common structural bug class in this domain. It predicts every w≤0/w=0 failure mode in Phase 5. Resolved in section 8 (P2) and Phase 5.

MM4 — Perspective-correctness is a statement about what's linear in which space. Screen-space position is not linear in barycentric-space attributes once projection is involved; 1/w is the quantity that restores linearity. It predicts the exact shape of the "swimming texture" bug and why it's invisible on fronto-parallel geometry. Resolved in section 8 (P3) and Phase 4.

MM5 — A tile boundary is a second, coarser fill rule. Everything I1 says about shared triangle edges applies again, one level up, to shared tile edges — the same discipline (canonical shared arithmetic) that prevents Phase 2 cracks is what prevents Phase 10 tile-boundary cracks. It predicts that Phase 10's hardest bugs are Phase 2's bugs recurring at a different granularity, not new bugs. Resolved in Phase 10 (fx_10_tile_boundary_coverage).

MM6 — Depth precision is a resource allocated by the projection matrix, not a property of the depth buffer's bit width. Where that precision concentrates (near the near plane, under the standard OpenGL mapping) is a design choice, not a law of nature — reverse-Z reallocates it. It predicts that widening the depth buffer's bit width without changing near/far or the mapping barely helps z-fighting. Resolved in section 8 and Phase 3 (P4).

MM7 — LOD selection is a local, differential quantity, not a global, distance-based one. Screen-space derivatives measure how fast texture coordinates change per screen pixel, which is what aliasing actually depends on — camera distance is a confound, not the cause. It predicts that a grazing-angle surface at a fixed distance needs a different mip level than a fronto-parallel surface at the same distance. Resolved in section 8 (P6) and Phase 8.

MM8 — Determinism is a design decision, enforced continuously, not a property you get for free from "the same code." Floating-point non-associativity, FMA contraction, and thread/SIMD scheduling all silently break bit-identical output unless explicitly pinned. It predicts that I7/I8 regressions are almost always toolchain/scheduling changes, not logic bugs. Resolved in section 27 (ADR-24) and Phase 10/11.

MM9 — MSAA buys antialiasing cheaply only because shading and coverage are decoupled. The moment shading happens per-sample instead of per-pixel, the "cheap" is gone and you've reinvented supersampling with extra bookkeeping. It predicts the exact failure signature of P10 violations (shader-invocation-count fixture). Resolved in section 8 and Phase 9.

Glossary (§38) · 47 terms · site-wide glossary

Anisotropic filtering
: sampling a texture with more samples along the elongated axis of its screen-space footprint; deferred (section 37).
Backface culling
: discarding a triangle whose screen-space winding indicates it faces away from the camera (ADR-07).
Barycentric coordinate
: a triangle-relative weight (λ0, λ1, λ2), summing to 1, expressing a point as a combination of the triangle's three vertices.
Bilinear filtering
: sampling a texture as a weighted average of the four nearest texels.
Binning
: assigning primitives to the tiles their bounding box overlaps, prior to per-tile rasterization.
Clip space
: the coordinate space immediately after the projection matrix is applied, before perspective divide.
Coverage mask
: a per-pixel bitmask indicating which MSAA sample positions are inside a given triangle.
Depth buffer
: a per-pixel buffer storing the closest interpolated depth value seen so far, used for hidden-surface removal.
Depth precision
: the granularity with which distinct world-space depths can be distinguished by the depth buffer's representation.
Derivative (screen-space)
: the rate of change of an interpolated attribute (e.g. UV) with respect to screen x or y, used for LOD selection.
Early-Z
: performing the depth test before the fragment shader runs, skipping shading for occluded fragments.
Edge function
: a linear function of pixel position, zero on a triangle edge and of consistent sign on either side, used for the inside-triangle test (Pineda 1988).
Fill rate
: the number of fragments a rasterizer can shade and write per second.
Fill rule
: the convention deciding which triangle owns a pixel that lies exactly on a shared edge.
Fixed-point
: a numeric representation with an implicit fractional scale (here, 24.8 subpixel coordinates), as opposed to floating point.
Fragment
: a single candidate pixel-sample produced by rasterizing a triangle, prior to the depth test and shading.
Framebuffer
: the combined color and depth (and MSAA sample) storage for one rendered frame.
Frustum
: the truncated-pyramid view volume bounded by six clip planes.
Guard band
: an extended screen-space region within which triangles skip full clipping (P13).
Half-space test
: an equivalent name for the edge-function inside-triangle test, evaluating which side of each edge's line a point falls on.
Homogeneous coordinate
: a 4-component representation (x,y,z,w) of a 3D point or vector enabling projection to be expressed as a matrix multiply.
LOD (level of detail)
: the selected mip level for texture sampling, derived from screen-space derivatives (section 8).
Mipmap
: a precomputed pyramid of progressively downsampled texture images (Williams 1983).
MSAA (multisample antialiasing)
: sampling triangle coverage at multiple positions per pixel while shading only once per pixel (P10).
NDC (normalized device coordinates)
: the coordinate space after perspective divide, in [-1,1]³ (or [0,1] for depth, per convention).
Overdraw
: the number of times a given pixel is shaded across all triangles submitted in a frame, before depth testing eliminates the occluded ones.
Perspective-correct interpolation
: interpolating an attribute via the 1/w formula (section 8) so the result respects projective foreshortening.
Perspective divide
: dividing a clip-space coordinate's x, y, z components by w to obtain NDC.
Premultiplied alpha
: a color representation where RGB channels are pre-scaled by alpha, simplifying certain blend equations.
Primitive assembly
: grouping shaded vertices, via index triples, into triangles.
Quad (fragment quad)
: a 2×2 group of fragments processed together to enable finite-difference derivative computation (P6).
Resolve
: averaging MSAA samples (or copying tile-local buffers) into the final single-sample framebuffer.
Screen-space derivative
: see derivative (screen-space).
Setup rate
: the number of triangles per second a rasterizer can compute edge functions and bounding boxes for, independent of fill cost.
Sort-middle
: a parallel rasterization architecture partitioning work by screen-space tile after geometry processing but before final compositing (ADR-04).
Sort-last
: a parallel rasterization architecture partitioning work by primitive/thread, compositing full private framebuffers at the end (section 36).
Subpixel precision
: the fractional-bit resolution at which screen coordinates are quantized for rasterization (24.8, ADR-02/03).
Tile
: a fixed-size rectangular region of the framebuffer processed independently during rasterization (P11).
Tile scheduler
: the work-distribution mechanism (rayon's work-stealing scheduler, ADR-18) that assigns tiles to worker threads.
Top-left rule
: the specific fill-rule convention classifying a pixel on a shared edge as covered by the triangle for which that edge is a top or left edge (section 8).
Varying
: an interpolable per-vertex attribute passed from vertex shader to fragment shader (ShaderVaryings).
Viewport transform
: mapping NDC coordinates to screen-space pixel coordinates.
Watertight mesh
: a mesh whose triangles share edges exactly, with no gaps or overlaps, used as the basis for the I1 coverage invariant.
Winding order
: the vertex traversal order (clockwise or counter-clockwise) of a triangle, determining its facing direction.
w (homogeneous coordinate)
: the fourth component of a homogeneous vector, equal to −z_eye under this project's projection-matrix convention (section 8).
Z-buffer
: see depth buffer.
Z-fighting
: visible flicker between two near-coplanar surfaces whose depth values are indistinguishable at the buffer's precision (P4).
