35. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  • D1 — Wheel horizon. 64^6 ticks × TICK_NS (1 ms) = 68,719,476,736 ms ≈ 795 days ≈ 2.18 years (ADR-03, ADR-05). Exceeds every realistic TTL (seconds to weeks) by two to three orders of magnitude, justifying "no realistic deadline is ever rejected as past-horizon" (P8's rejection path is a safety net, not a common case).
  • D2 — RAM ceiling @ 100M keys. Per-key footprint: wheel Node (24 B, §8.2) + KeyState shard entry (12 B raw + ~36 B DashMap bucket/hash overhead ≈ 48 B) = 72 B/ key; × 1.33 allocator/alignment overhead factor ≈ 96 B/key. 100,000,000 × 96 B = 9.6 GB; × 1.25 operational margin (arena fragmentation, in-flight WAL buffer, metrics) ≈ 11.5 GB, rounded to the ≤ 12 GiB ceiling in §4.
  • D3 — Insert-cost budget from throughput. 1 / 5,000,000 inserts/sec = 200 ns average per-insert budget at M1's target — consistent with M4's < 100 ns p50 claim (leaves headroom for the p99 tail, M5).
  • D4 — Heap-top capacity vs. worst-case arrival. At M2's 800,000 inserts/sec floor, a 16 ms top_horizon window could theoretically see 800,000 × 0.016 = 12,800 inserts if every single one carried a sub-16ms TTL. ADR-04's fixed capacity of 256 is sized against the empirical short-TTL fraction (§17.4's sweep), not this theoretical worst case — the gap between 12,800 and 256 is exactly why the eviction-to-wheel overflow path (§17.2) exists as the correctness backstop.
  • D5 — WAL group-commit rate vs. device floor. ADR-09's 1 ms/1024-record window bounds commit rate to min(1000 commits/sec time-bound, arrival_rate/1024 count-bound). At M2's 800,000 inserts/sec, 800,000 × 0.001 s = 800 records accumulate per 1 ms window (under the 1024 cap), so the time bound dominates: ≈ 1,000 fsyncs/sec, 50× under the 50,000 fsyncs/sec NVMe fdatasync floor (§22.7) — comfortable headroom.
  • D6 — Recovery RTO independent of dataset size. M9 requires 1,000,000 records ≤ 5 s ⇒ ≥ 200,000 records/sec replay throughput. Since ADR-10 caps records-since-last- snapshot at ≤ 1,000,000 regardless of total keyspace size, RTO = records_since_snapshot / replay_throughput ≤ 1,000,000 / 200,000 = 5 s for any total dataset size — the formal derivation behind P10's boundedness claim.
  • D7 — Storage amplification. WAL bytes/record ≈ 1–2 B (varint) + 1 B (tag) + 28 B (key 8 + generation 4 + wall_ns i128 16) + 4 B (CRC32C) ≈ 35 B. A full insert+tombstone pair for 100M keys ≈ 100,000,000 × 2 × 35 B = 7 GB, vs. D2's 9.6 GB live-keyspace RAM footprint ⇒ ≈ 0.73× amplification, comfortably under the ≤ 1.2× ceiling in §4 (and bounded absolutely by ADR-10's snapshot cadence regardless of total keyspace size).
  • D8 — Connection-buffer memory budget. 65,536 max FDs (§4) × 32 KB (16 KB read + 16 KB write buffer per connection) ≈ 2.1 GB — a budget tracked separately from D2's keyspace RAM ceiling in total node sizing.
  • D9 — Allocation cost at fire-throughput scale. M3's 10,000,000 fires/sec at 1,000 ticks/sec (1 ms tick) implies ≥ 10,000 fires drained per tick during a synchronized expiry burst (e.g. midnight session expiry). At typical allocator latency (50–100 ns/ alloc), 10,000 allocations would consume 0.5–1 ms of the 1 ms tick budget alone — the exact arithmetic justifying why any allocation in the fire path (P4/I11) is unaffordable at this target throughput.
  • D10 — Replicated fire-accuracy bound. Configured NTP_skew_ms = 100 (§4) ⇒ M13's ≤ 2 × NTP_skew_ms = 200 ms bound derives from worst_case_error ≤ |primary_skew| + |follower_skew| ≤ 2 × max(individual configured skew), assuming both nodes are bounded by the same configured tolerance.
