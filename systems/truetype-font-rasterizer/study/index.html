Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an expert carries while building this system. Each: the model, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

MM1 — A font is a hostile program, not a document. A .ttf literally ships a Turing-ish bytecode interpreter and a graph of self-referential offsets; treat every length/offset field as attacker-chosen. Predicts: the parser's threat surface equals its entire input; the dangerous code is wherever a field indexes memory. Bug without it: the classic OOB-read CVE (e.g. fmt-4 idRangeOffset indexing past glyphIdArray). Applied: §Phase 0 (single Reader gate), §Phase 2 (cmap bounds), §Threat Model, invariants I1/I2.

MM2 — Offsets are edges in a graph that may have cycles. loca→glyf, composite→component, CFF subr→subr, hint CALL→FDEF are all pointers; the input controls the graph shape, including back-edges. Predicts: any pointer-following loop can be made to recurse forever or revisit nodes. Bug without it: stack-overflow / hang on a cyclic composite or self-calling subr. Applied: §Phase 4 (depth cap + cycle guard, I5/I6), §Phase 11 (CALL/LOOPCALL budget), §Phase 12 (subr depth cap).

MM3 — Coverage is an integral, computed by sweeping a derivative. Per-pixel alpha = the area of the glyph inside that pixel; the signed-area cell method accumulates a derivative (cover, area deltas as edges cross pixels) and integrates left-to-right per row to recover coverage in 1× memory. Predicts: AA quality and cost come from exact area math, not sample count; a sign error flips or doubles coverage. Bug without it: inverted/blotchy glyphs, or N× memory from supersampling. Applied: §Phase 8, ADR-9, invariant I14.

MM4 — Determinism is a property of the arithmetic, not the test suite. Float addition is non-associative and the compiler/CPU may reorder it; identical bitmaps across machines require an integer-only fill path. Predicts: any f32 in flatten/coverage produces per-platform pixel diffs that goldens will eventually catch as flakes. Bug without it: "works on my machine" golden-image flapping; an unsafe shared cache. Applied: §Phase 5 / §Phase 8 (fixed-point), invariant I21, Principle 4.

MM5 — Hinting is grid-fitting, and grid-fitting is a constrained move of points toward the pixel grid. The VM moves control points along a freedom vector by distances measured along a projection vector, snapping to the grid via a round state, then IUP drags the untouched points along. Predicts: hinting helps most where the grid is coarse (small ppem) and is nearly a no-op at large ppem; a bad IUP corrupts the whole contour, not one point. Bug without it: distorted hinted glyphs worse than unhinted. Applied: §Phase 11, paper P1, §11.6.

MM6 — The pixel grid has exactly one convention, chosen once. Sample point (pixel center), integer-pixel origin (lower-left vs top-left), y-direction (font y-up vs raster y-down), and bbox rounding must be fixed in one place; every metric (left,top,advance) inherits it. Predicts: any per-stage re-derivation introduces a 1-px shift or atlas seam. Bug without it: off-by-one seams, glyphs that don't sit on the baseline together. Applied: §Phase 7 (conventions), Principle 11, §8.10.

MM7 — There are two outline technologies under one container, and they meet at the rasterizer. TrueType glyf is quadratic with an integer hint VM; CFF/Type-2 is cubic with a charstring interpreter and subrs. Both reduce to an Outline of segments that flatten into the same edge/coverage path. Predicts: you can add CFF as a front-end module without touching the raster core; the boundary is the Outline/EdgeList type. Bug without it: a CFF path that re-implements rasterization and drifts from glyf. Applied: §Phase 12 (CFF feeds Phase 6/8), Principle 5, §3.1.

MM8 — The cache key is the rendering function's full input signature. A GlyphBitmap is a pure function of (glyph, ppem, transform-bits, hint-mode); the key must equal that tuple exactly, or two distinct renders collide. Predicts: any pixel-affecting input omitted from the key serves a wrong-but-plausible bitmap, silently. Bug without it: a 12-px 'A' served for a 13-px request after a transform change. Applied: §Phase 10, invariant I19, Principle 9.

MM9 — Soft-fail vs hard-fail is a safety/compatibility tradeoff, decided per anomaly. A wrong checksum or missing post names are cosmetic (the rest of the world renders the font) → warn + continue; an OOB offset or upem==0 is unsafe/incorrect → reject. Predicts: rejecting on cosmetic anomalies makes you incompatible with real-world sloppy fonts; soft-failing on unsafe ones reintroduces CVEs. Bug without it: either over-strict (blank text everyone else shows) or unsafe (renders a malformed glyph). Applied: Principle 13, §Phase 0 (checksum soft), §Phase 1 (upem hard).

MM10 — Tail latency is set by the worst glyph, not the average. Per-glyph cost scales with point/segment/cell count and hint instructions; a single complex CJK or adversarial glyph dominates p99/p999. Predicts: average throughput is a poor SLO; you must cap per-glyph work and measure Latin vs CJK tails separately. Bug without it: a service that meets p50 but blows p99 on one font's ideographs, or hangs on an instruction bomb. Applied: §Performance Engineering (tail latency), §4.1 (per-glyph caps), §Phase 11 (instruction budget, I13).
