32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1. A ray tracer is a Monte Carlo integrator with a geometry oracle — all image quality questions reduce to variance questions. Predicts: noise reduction always trades against more samples or smarter sampling, never a free lunch. Bug class without it: chasing "sharper" images by tweaking BSDF math instead of variance. Resolved in §8, §17.

MM2. Importance sampling does not make the answer better, it makes the same answer arrive sooner — every sampling change must leave the expectation untouched. Predicts: unbiasedness must be independently re-verified after any sampling change. Bug class without it: renders that look "cleaner" but are silently biased. Resolved in §20 (P3).

MM3. The BVH is a spatial index, not a scene graph — it must be validated against brute force, never trusted by construction. Predicts: periodic cross-checking catches silent correctness bugs invisible to screenshots. Bug class without it: false hits/misses corrupting shading with no visible artifact pattern. Resolved in §15 (P11).

MM4. Energy conservation is a conserved quantity, like momentum — every BSDF is a candidate suspect until the white-furnace test proves otherwise. Predicts: BSDF bugs manifest as brightness drift, not obvious visual artifacts. Bug class without it: subtle over/under- brightening across material combinations that a screenshot review misses. Resolved in §16, §18 (I1).

MM5. A path's throughput is the only memory a random walk carries forward — everything else must be re-derived at each vertex. Predicts: state that leaks across bounces (a stale reused PDF) causes correctness bugs. Bug class without it: MIS/PDF mismatches that only manifest as a weight-sum violation. Resolved in §20 (P9).

MM6. Determinism is a property of the whole pipeline — RNG, thread schedule, math library — not just the algorithm. Predicts: a "correct" renderer can still be non-reproducible. Bug class without it: flaky regression tests blamed on "noise" when the real cause is thread-order- dependent summation. Resolved in §27 (I9).

MM7. Radiometric units are conserved through every transform — texture, tone curve, and BSDF each have a defined place in the unit chain, and skipping one corrupts everything downstream. Predicts: gamma/tonemap bugs that look like material bugs. Resolved in §21, §22 (P10).

MM8. A tile is the unit of parallel failure isolation — cross-tile state is the only place data races can hide. Predicts: race bugs always trace to a violation of one-tile-one-thread ownership. Resolved in §13 (P14, I13).

Glossary (§38) · 56 terms · site-wide glossary

Radiance
flux per unit projected area per unit solid angle; the fundamental quantity the integrator estimates.
Irradiance
flux per unit area, the hemispherical integral of incident radiance.
Solid angle
the 2D angular measure dω = sinθ dθ dφ subtended on a sphere.
BRDF
Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function, describing reflection only.
BSDF
Bidirectional Scattering Distribution Function, generalizing BRDF to include transmission.
BTDF
the transmission-only component of a BSDF.
Rendering equation
Kajiya 1986's integral equation for outgoing radiance in terms of emission plus scattered incoming radiance.
Monte Carlo estimator
an average of f(X)/p(X) over random samples X ~ p, converging to the true integral.
Variance
the estimator's expected squared deviation from its mean; the quantity every noise-reduction technique targets.
Importance sampling
choosing a sampling density p that matches the integrand f to reduce variance.
Probability density function (PDF)
the density p(x) a sampling routine draws from.
Cosine-weighted sampling
sampling hemisphere directions with density proportional to cosθ, matched to the Lambertian integrand.
Low-discrepancy sequence
a deterministic sequence (Sobol, Halton) that fills sample space more uniformly than independent random draws.
Stratification
subdividing the sample domain into cells and drawing one jittered sample per cell.
Multiple importance sampling (MIS)
combining several sampling strategies with weights that sum to one, reducing variance below any single strategy alone.
Balance heuristic
the MIS weighting w_s = p_s / Σp_i, provably within 4× of optimal.
Power heuristic
the MIS weighting using squared PDFs, empirically often lower variance.
Russian roulette
probabilistic path termination that preserves the estimator's expectation via inverse-probability throughput scaling.
Unbiased vs. consistent
an unbiased estimator's expectation equals the true value at any sample count; a consistent estimator converges to the true value only as sample count grows to infinity.
Firefly
an isolated, very-bright pixel caused by a low-probability, high-contribution sample.
Shadow acne
self-intersection artifacts from insufficient ray-origin offsetting.
Epsilon offsetting
nudging a ray's origin along the geometric normal to avoid self-intersection.
BVH
Bounding Volume Hierarchy, the tree-structured acceleration structure this blueprint uses.
Surface area heuristic (SAH)
a cost model for choosing BVH split positions based on child surface area and primitive count.
Microfacet
a model treating a rough surface as a statistical distribution of microscopic mirror facets.
GGX / Trowbridge-Reitz
the microfacet normal distribution function used for the rough-metal and rough-dielectric BSDFs.
Smith masking-shadowing
the geometric attenuation term accounting for microfacet self-occlusion.
Fresnel equations
the physical law governing reflectance/transmittance as a function of incidence angle and index of refraction.
Schlick approximation
a cheap polynomial approximation to the Fresnel reflectance curve.
Dielectric
a non-conducting material (glass, water) exhibiting both reflection and refraction.
Conductor
a metal, whose complex index of refraction absorbs the transmitted component entirely.
White furnace test
a scene with uniform environment emission used to verify a BSDF conserves energy exactly.
Tone mapping
the pure-function transform from linear HDR radiance to a display-referred image.
Mipmap
a precomputed pyramid of progressively lower-resolution, filtered texture levels.
Ray differential
auxiliary rays tracked alongside a primary ray to estimate texture-footprint size for mip-level selection.
Next-event estimation (NEE)
explicitly sampling a light source at each non-specular path vertex rather than relying solely on BSDF sampling to find it.
Throughput
the accumulated product of BSDF-value-over-PDF factors along a path, scaling all future contributions.
Path tracing
the recursive/iterative Monte Carlo algorithm evaluating the rendering equation by sampling scattering directions.
Thin-lens camera
a camera model with a finite aperture producing depth-of-field blur.
Depth of field
the range of scene depths that appear acceptably in focus under a finite-aperture camera model.
Circle of confusion
the blur disk radius produced by an out-of-focus point under the thin-lens model.
Tangent space
the local coordinate frame (tangent, bitangent, normal) used to interpret a tangent-space normal map.
Normal mapping
perturbing the shading normal per-texel to simulate fine surface detail without added geometry.
Texture filtering
resolving a texture sample by averaging over a footprint rather than a single texel, avoiding aliasing.
Trilinear filtering
filtering that interpolates between two adjacent mip levels as well as within each level.
Spectral rendering
representing light as a function of wavelength rather than three RGB channels.
Gamma correction
the nonlinear encode/decode transform between linear light and a display-referred signal.
Linear color space
a color representation proportional to physical radiance, used internally throughout this system (P10).
sRGB
the standard display color space and its associated gamma-like transfer function.
HDR (high dynamic range)
image data whose values exceed the [0,1] display range, requiring tone mapping before display.
Tile scheduler
the component partitioning the film into work units and distributing them across threads.
Film accumulator
the per-pixel running sum (and sum-of-squares) of radiance samples.
Denoising
a post-process (typically biased) that trades some fidelity for reduced apparent noise at fixed sample count.
Seeded replay
re-running a render with an identical seed, scene, and thread count to verify reproducibility.
Chi-square test
a statistical test comparing an empirical sample distribution against an expected density, used here to validate BSDF/light PDF consistency.
Soak test
a long-duration continuous render used to detect memory growth or numerical drift. ---
