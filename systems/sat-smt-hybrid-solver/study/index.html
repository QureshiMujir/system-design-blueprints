Hard questions

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does deleting a learnt clause never cause a wrong answer, while a single bad theory explain() does — and what does that asymmetry imply for where you spend audit effort? (synthesize P4/P5 + I5 + MM2; grounded in §6 Design Principles + Mental Models)
  2. Watches survive backjumping untouched, yet the watcher invariant must still hold afterward. Reconcile these two statements and explain why "undoing" watch moves on backtrack is the classic killer bug. (Phase 2 N.6 + I2 + MM4)
  3. Why does the choice of branching heuristic (VSIDS vs LRB vs CHB) never appear as a correctness concern but always as a tuning concern, while clause deletion sits on the boundary? (Phase 3 N.9 + Phase 12 + MM2)
  4. Why does the blueprint build a deliberately slow DPLL baseline (Phase 1) before the fast CDCL core (Phase 2), and what would poison the entire test strategy if it were skipped or buggy? (Phase 1 N.9 + Phase 2 N.4 + Testing Strategy "differential")
  5. Trace how a theory.check() conflict becomes indistinguishable, to the SAT core, from a propositional learnt clause — and why that indistinguishability is the property that makes new theories pluggable. (§8.4 + Phase 6 + P12/MM5)
  6. Walk the EUF conflict a=b, b=c, f(a)≠f(c) from assertion to the learnt clause, naming the structure that produces the explanation and the invariant it must satisfy. (§8.5 + Phase 7 + I11/I5)
  7. Explain how the same propagate mechanism appears in both the Boolean core and a theory, and why a fast SMT solver maximizes propagation rate rather than check speed. (MM6 + P11 + Domain Deep Dive D.4 + Phase 8)
  8. Reconcile P11 (lazy full check) with P5 (theory lemmas are clauses): why is calling full check on every literal both unnecessary and harmful, and what cheaper mechanism fills the gap? (P5/P11 + Phase 6 + MM6)
  9. Nelson-Oppen may exchange only entailed shared equalities. Show how exchanging a merely model-true equality (the model-based shortcut) can produce a wrong UNSAT, and what non-convexity (LIA) forces instead. (I13 + MM9 + Phase 10 + Comparative note on Z3)
  10. Given a mixed QF_UFLIA formula, explain what purification does, why fresh shared variables are required, and the soundness bug a name capture would cause. (Phase 10 N.2/N.6 + I13 + Glossary "Purification")
  11. Why must arithmetic use bignum rationals rather than f64, and what specific failure (with a concrete value) does a float introduce in a decision procedure? (P1 + ADR-11 + I10 + MM7; Phase 8 N.6)
  12. Explain the role of δ-rationals in the simplex and what wrong answer their absence would produce on a strict inequality. (Domain Deep Dive D.6 + Phase 8 N.6 + Glossary "δ-rationals")
  13. Why does this blueprint emit DRAT proofs only for the SAT layer and unsat cores for SMT, and in what concrete downstream scenario would that be insufficient? (ADR-18/19 + Failure Modes incident note + Stretch Goals full SMT proofs)
  14. Identify the three places every silent-wrong bug in this project can live, and map each to the specific paranoid-mode audit and invariant that targets it. (Domain Deep Dive D.5 + I2/I5/I6 + Operational Readiness --paranoid)
  15. Why is determinism (seed + ordered tie-breaks) a prerequisite for debugging soundness bugs, not merely a reproducibility nicety — and what tool depends on it? (P7 + I14 + MM10 + Operational Readiness; tools/ddmin)
  16. Contrast why a watcher bug and a simplex cycling bug have opposite failure signatures (silent-wrong vs hang), and how each is detected and bounded. (Phase 2 N.9 + Phase 8 N.9 + I2/I10/I15)
  17. Given the §4 throughput target of 10M propagations/s, compute the implied memory bandwidth and explain why packed literals and a contiguous arena are correctness-irrelevant but performance-decisive. (Capacity C5 + MM3 + ADR-04/05)
  18. Quantify why LBD-based clause deletion is mandatory rather than a tuning knob, using the conflict rate and learnt-clause size. (Capacity C4 + Phase 3 + Scalability bottleneck #2)
  19. Given the §4 4 GB cap and Capacity C2, argue that the input clause-DB is not the memory risk — then identify what is, and the mechanism that bounds it. (Capacity C2/C4 + Scalability + ADR-10)
  20. Bound the clause blow-up of a 64-bit multiply from first principles, and use the result to justify when you would abandon bit-blasting for word-level reasoning. (Capacity C7 + MM8 + ADR-14 + Stretch Goals)
  21. The incremental contract claims a ~100× campaign speedup. Derive the order of magnitude and tie it to the design principle it justifies. (Capacity C10 + P10 + Scalability "incremental scaling")
  22. After (pop 1), the core returns to the level-0 state yet keeps some learnt clauses and drops others. State the rule that decides which, and why keeping the wrong one is invisible to one-shot tests. (Phase 11 N.6 + Domain Deep Dive D.8 + Failure Mode "Incremental wrong")
  23. The architecture has exactly one trust boundary. Name it, and explain why every downstream component may assume validated input — and what breaks if a second component starts consuming untrusted text. (§3 Architectural Overview + Threat Model T1)
  24. The blueprint rejects eager SMT (bit-blast everything to one SAT call) yet keeps bit-blasting inside the lazy framework. Explain how the same technique can be both the rejected architecture and an accepted component, and what distinguishes the two uses. (ALT-1 in Alternative Architectures + ADR-14 + MM5/MM8)
  25. Both stochastic local search and a portfolio could solve a satisfiable instance faster than this single-thread CDCL core, yet both are deferred or rejected. Give the distinct reason each is unsuitable as the foundation, tying each to a specific principle. (ALT-2/ALT-3 in Alternative Architectures + P1/P7 + I9/I14)

Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an expert solver engineer carries. Each model: the one-sentence intuition, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

Read these as a lens, not a glossary: MM1/MM4 are the propositional invariants (trail + watches), MM2 is the soundness asymmetry that ranks all audit effort, MM3 is the performance reality, MM5/MM6/MM9 are the DPLL(T) seam and theory cooperation, MM7/MM8 are the two theory-specific traps (exactness, blow-up), and MM10 is the discipline that makes every other bug debuggable. A reader who holds all ten can predict where a given solver design will break before reading its code.

MM1 — The trail is the program counter; everything else is a cache of it. The current partial assignment is the solver's state; watch lists, theory tableaux, activity scores, and learnt clauses are all derived views that must agree with the trail at every decision level.

  • Predicts: any structure that can desync from the trail is a latent wrong-answer bug, and the fix is always "reconstruct from / re-sync to the trail," never "patch the cache."
  • Bug class without it: you treat theory state, watches, or occurrence lists as independent state mutated ad hoc, and a backjump leaves them stale → phantom or missed conflicts.
  • Applied: P2, I1/I4 (trail), I6 (theory push/pop mirrors levels), Phase 2 (watcher invariant), Phase 6 (theory state tracks the trail), Domain Deep Dive D.5 (where bugs hide).

MM2 — A learnt clause is a proof step you are allowed to forget. Every clause CDCL learns is a resolvent entailed by the input; learning it is sound, deleting it is sound, the only thing that is not sound is learning a clause that is not entailed.

  • Predicts: clause deletion can never cause a wrong answer (only slowdown), but a single unsound learnt clause — propositional minimization gone wrong, or a bad theory explain() — causes wrong UNSAT; so audit what you learn far harder than what you delete.
  • Bug class without it: you fear deletion and hoard clauses (OOM) while under-checking explanation soundness (the actual danger).
  • Applied: P4/P5 (1-UIP + theory lemmas are clauses), P9 (minimization), I3/I5, Phase 3 (deletion is free), Phase 7/8/10 (explanation soundness is the risk).

MM3 — BCP is a memory-bandwidth problem, not a CPU problem. On industrial CNF the solver spends ~80% of its time in propagation, and that time is dominated by cache misses chasing watch lists and clause bodies, not by arithmetic.

  • Predicts: the layout of literals and watchers (packed u32, blocking literal, contiguous arena) moves the wall-clock far more than algorithmic cleverness in the inner loop; profiling will show LLC misses, not instruction count, as the ceiling.
  • Bug class without it: you micro-optimize branches in the propagate loop while pointer-chasing boxed clauses thrashes cache — a 5–10× loss invisible to instruction-level reasoning.
  • Applied: P3/P13, ADR-04/05/07, Phase 2 N.7, Performance Engineering (cache analysis), C1/C2/C5 (capacity model), MM10 below.

MM4 — Two-watched literals are an invariant, not an algorithm. You do not "run" watching; you maintain the property "each clause watches two literals that are unassigned, or the clause is already satisfied/unit/conflicting" across assign and backjump.

  • Predicts: the correctness question is never "did I propagate?" but "does the invariant still hold after this backjump?" — and because watches survive backjumping untouched (only the trail truncates), the audit is a pure structural check.
  • Bug class without it: you try to "undo" watch moves on backtrack (the classic killer bug), corrupting watch lists and silently dropping propagations → wrong SAT.
  • Applied: P3, I2, Phase 2 (the most important phase), Domain Deep Dive D.5, Consolidated Pitfall 1.

MM5 — DPLL(T) is the SAT core wearing a blindfold. The Boolean engine never learns what an atom means; a theory conflict reaches it as nothing but a new clause over proxy variables, indistinguishable from a learnt propositional clause.

  • Predicts: adding a theory cannot require touching the core — if it does, the abstraction has leaked; and every theory technique reduces to "produce a sound clause the blind core can resolve."
  • Bug class without it: you special-case theory knowledge inside the search (e.g. arithmetic hints in decide), and the engine ossifies — new theories become impossible.
  • Applied: P5/P12, I7, Phase 6 (Theory trait), Domain Deep Dive D.3, the "Final Notes" seam.

MM6 — Theory propagation is unit propagation in another language. Just as BCP forces a literal when a clause becomes unit, a theory can force a proxy literal when its constraints entail it (e.g. x≥2 ∧ x+y≤3 forces ¬(y≥2)), feeding the same trail.

  • Predicts: a fast SMT solver propagates cheaply and often so the expensive full check rarely discovers a conflict propagation didn't already prevent; the lever for SMT speed is propagation rate, not check speed.
  • Bug class without it: you rely on full check to catch every theory conflict, exploring exponentially more Boolean assignments than necessary.
  • Applied: P11, Phase 6 (propagate + lazy reason), Phase 8 (bound propagation), Domain Deep Dive D.4.

MM7 — Exactness is a soundness property, not a numerics preference. In a decision procedure the answer is a single bit; one rounding error in a simplex pivot does not "lose precision," it flips SAT↔UNSAT.

  • Predicts: floating point cannot appear anywhere a feasibility decision is made, no matter how much faster; bignum rational growth is the price of correctness and must be managed (normalize, bound — C8), never avoided by switching to floats.
  • Bug class without it: you reach for f64 in the tableau "for speed," and 0.1+0.2 ≠ 0.3 decides a benchmark wrong — silently.
  • Applied: P1, ADR-11, I10, Phase 8 N.6, Consolidated Pitfall 4, Comparative note on Yices/exact LRA (Comparative Architecture).

MM8 — Bit-blasting trades a hard theory for a huge propositional formula. Every QF_BV operation compiles to a circuit; the SAT core then reasons about gates with no idea it is doing arithmetic — correct, simple, and exponentially wasteful on wide multipliers.

  • Predicts: BV scaling is governed by generated clause count, and the multiplier (O(n²) partial products) is the cliff; you size the blast before you write it (C7) and switch to word-level reasoning when n is large.
  • Bug class without it: you bit-blast a 256-bit multiply, generate ~10⁵–10⁶ clauses, and swamp the core.
  • Applied: ADR-14, I12, Phase 9, C7 (multiplier capacity), Domain Deep Dive D.7, ALT-1 (eager bit-blasting, Alternative Architectures).

MM9 — Nelson-Oppen cooperation is the exchange of facts, never guesses. Combined theories agree on satisfiability by propagating only equalities over shared variables that each can prove entailed; a single guessed (non-entailed) equality is a wrong UNSAT.

  • Predicts: combination soundness lives entirely in the entailment check, and non-convex theories (LIA) cannot propagate a disjunction of equalities directly — they must hand the case split to the SAT core.
  • Bug class without it: you let a theory export an equality that merely holds in its current model (the model-based shortcut) without the entailment guarantee N-O requires → unsound combination.
  • Applied: P5, I13, ADR-16, Phase 10, Domain Deep Dive D.8, Comparative note on Z3 model-based combination (Comparative Architecture).

MM10 — Determinism is a debugging tool you build before you need it. A solver whose decisions depend on hash-iteration or address order produces bugs that vanish on the next run; making every tie-break a function of (formula, seed) means every bug reproduces exactly.

  • Predicts: the cost (explicit tie-break ordering) is paid once and repays itself the first time a soundness bug must be minimized with ddmin; conformance flakiness is always a determinism leak, not "a hard instance."
  • Bug class without it: a wrong-answer report cannot be reproduced, ddmin cannot shrink it, and the bug is effectively undebuggable.
  • Applied: P7, I14, ADR-21, Phase 3 (tie-breaks), Operational Readiness (reproducibility), Failure Mode "Non-determinism".

5. Required Reading

Books

  • Handbook of Satisfiability (Biere, Heule, van Maaren, Walsh) — chs. on CDCL, preprocessing, proof complexity. Extract: the canonical treatment of two-watched literals, 1-UIP, VSIDS, BVE. → applied in Phase 2 (CDCL core), Phase 3 (VSIDS/restarts), Phase 4 (BVE/BCE).
  • The Calculus of Computation (Bradley & Manna) — chs. 1–3 (logic), 8–11 (decision procedures, combination). Extract: EUF, arithmetic procedures, Nelson-Oppen — the SMT backbone. → applied in Phase 7 (EUF), Phase 8 (arithmetic), Phase 10 (Nelson-Oppen).
  • Decision Procedures: An Algorithmic Point of View (Kroening & Strichman) — chs. on EUF, arithmetic, bit-vectors, arrays, combination. Extract: implementation-level pseudocode for each theory. → applied in Phases 7–10 (every theory + combination).
  • Handbook of Practical Logic and Automated Reasoning (Harrison) — DPLL + first-order chapters. Extract: a from-scratch implementer's view of search + deduction. → applied in Phase 1 (DPLL baseline) and §8 Domain Model.
  • Numerical Linear Algebra / LP texts (e.g. Vanderbei, Linear Programming) — Simplex chapters. Extract: tableau, pivoting, anti-cycling — basis for the LRA solver. → applied in Phase 8 (Simplex) and Domain Deep Dive D.6 (worked pivot).
  • Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS) — union-find (ch. 21), basics. Extract: disjoint-set with union-by-rank + path compression for congruence closure. → applied in Phase 7 (EUF union-find) and §11 Core Schema (UnionFind).
  • SAT/SMT by Example (Yurichev) — applied encodings. Extract: how real problems become CNF/SMT; informs the conformance corpus. → applied in each phase's N.5 Conformance Fixtures and the Testing Strategy corpus.

Papers

  • GRASP: A Search Algorithm for Propositional Satisfiability (Marques-Silva & Sakallah, 1996/1999) — origin of conflict-driven learning + non-chronological backtracking. Relevance: the conflict-analysis loop. → applied in Phase 2 (analyze/1-UIP, P4) and Domain Deep Dive D.1–D.2.
  • Chaff: Engineering an Efficient SAT Solver (Moskewicz et al., DAC 2001) — two-watched literals + VSIDS. Relevance: the BCP + branching design. → applied in Phase 2 (BCP, ADR-07) and Phase 3 (VSIDS, ADR-06).
  • MiniSat (Eén & Sörensson, 2003) — the readable reference architecture. Relevance: clause DB, the clean core loop, the API you will mirror. → applied in Phase 2 (core loop) and §11 Core Schema (SatSolver trait).
  • Predicting Learnt Clause Quality / Glucose (Audemard & Simon, IJCAI 2009) — LBD ("glue") for clause deletion + restarts. Relevance: clause DB management. → applied in Phase 3 (LBD reduce + glucose restarts, ADR-09/10).
  • A Fast Linear-Arithmetic Solver for DPLL(T) (Dutertre & de Moura, CAV 2006) — the simplex variant every SMT solver uses. Relevance: Phase 8 (LRA/LIA) implementation, near-verbatim. → applied in Phase 8 (Simplex, ADR-12, I10).
  • DPLL(T): Fast Decision Procedures (Ganzinger, Hagen, Nieuwenhuis, Oliveras, Tinelli, CAV 2004) — the lazy SMT framework + theory propagation. Relevance: the theory interface. → applied in Phase 6 (DPLL(T) framework, P5/P11) and §8.4.
  • Simplify: A Theorem Prover for Program Checking (Detlefs, Nelson, Saxe, 2005) — E-graph + matching + combination in a real prover. Relevance: EUF + combination. → applied in Phase 7 (EUF, I11) and Phase 10 (combination).
  • Simplifying problems before they are solved / Effective Preprocessing (Eén & Biere, SAT 2005) — variable + clause elimination (BVE/SatELite). Relevance: preprocessing. → applied in Phase 4 (BVE/subsumption, ADR-07 occurrence lists).
  • The seminal paper on combining decision procedures (Nelson & Oppen, TOPLAS 1979) — equality-exchange combination. Relevance: Phase 10 (Nelson-Oppen) combination. → applied in Phase 10 (eq-exchange, ADR-16, I13).
  • Extended Resolution / DRAT proof system papers (Heule, Hunt, Wetzler) — clausal proofs + checking. Relevance: proof emission. → applied in Phase 5 (DRAT emit, ADR-18, I9).
  • Congruence closure papers (Nelson-Oppen 1980; Nieuwenhuis & Oliveras, proof-producing CC) — Relevance: explanations. → applied in Phase 7 (proof forest explain_eq, ADR-17, I11).

Reference Implementations

  • MiniSat — core/Solver.cc, mtl/ (Vec, Heap, clause alloc). Lesson: the canonical CDCL loop, watcher lists, VSIDS heap. Pitfall: its simplicity omits modern restarts/LBD — add those from Glucose. → applied in Phase 2 (core loop) and Phase 3 (heap); the differential oracle in Phase 1/2 N.4.
  • CaDiCaL (Biere) — inprocessing, modern heuristics. Lesson: state-of-the-art preprocessing + restart policies. Pitfall: large; read selectively (propagate, analyze, elim). → applied in Phase 4 (inprocessing schedule) and Phase 12 (vivification/rephasing).
  • Glucose — LBD + aggressive deletion + restarts. Lesson: clause-quality-driven DB management. → applied in Phase 3 (reduce.rs LBD, restart.rs glucose).
  • Z3 (de Moura, Bjørner) — src/smt, src/math/simplex, src/ast. Lesson: theory combination, simplex, term representation. Pitfall: very large; study one theory at a time, not the whole engine. → applied in Phases 7–10 (the differential oracle for every theory, N.4) and §11 term hash-consing.
  • CVC5 — theory engine + combination. Lesson: clean theory interface + sharing. Pitfall: avoid copying its proof machinery wholesale Phase 0. → applied in Phase 6 (Theory trait shape) and Phase 10 (combination).
  • Yices (Dutertre) — the simplex paper's home. Lesson: the LRA solver in practice. → applied in Phase 8 (Simplex, alongside Dutertre–de Moura, ADR-12).
  • DRAT-trim — the proof checker you emit for. Lesson: the DRAT format contract; run it in CI to verify UNSAT proofs. → applied in Phase 5 (proof emit + CI gate, ADR-18, I9).

RFCs / Specifications

  • SMT-LIB 2.6 Standard — command language, sorts, logics (QF_UF, QF_LRA, QF_LIA, QF_BV, QF_ABV), responses. The external contract. → applied in Phase 0 (parser) and Phase 11 (command loop), §8.2.
  • DIMACS CNF format — the SAT input contract. → applied in Phase 0 (parse/dimacs.rs), §8.1.
  • DRAT proof format — the clausal proof contract. → applied in Phase 5 (proof emission, ADR-18).
  • SMT-COMP rules + benchmark library (SMT-LIB benchmarks) — the conformance corpus + scoring (sat/unsat/unknown, time). → applied in each phase's N.5 fixtures and the Testing Strategy conformance suite.

Glossary · 60 terms · site-wide glossary

SAT
propositional satisfiability; find a truth assignment or prove none exists.
SMT
satisfiability modulo theories; SAT over first-order theory atoms.
CNF
conjunctive normal form (AND of OR-clauses).
DIMACS
the standard CNF text format.
SMT-LIB
the standard SMT command + term language.
CDCL
conflict-driven clause learning; modern SAT search.
DPLL
the backtracking-search ancestor of CDCL.
DPLL(T)
DPLL/CDCL driving theory solvers; the lazy SMT architecture.
Literal
a variable or its negation.
Clause
a disjunction of literals.
Trail
the assignment stack with levels + reasons.
Decision level
depth of decisions; backjump target unit.
BCP
Boolean constraint (unit) propagation.
Two-watched literals
lazy clause-watching scheme for fast BCP.
Blocker
cached watch literal to skip clause dereference.
Conflict clause
the all-false clause that triggers analysis.
1-UIP
first unique implication point; the literal a learnt clause asserts.
Backjump
non-chronological backtrack to the asserting level.
Learnt clause
clause derived from a conflict (added to DB).
VSIDS / EVSIDS
activity-based branching heuristic.
Phase saving
reuse a variable's last assigned polarity.
Restart
discard the decision stack (keep learnts) to escape bad subtrees.
Luby sequence
a restart schedule guaranteeing completeness.
LBD (glue)
distinct decision levels in a clause; clause-quality metric.
BVE
bounded variable elimination (preprocessing).
BCE
blocked-clause elimination.
Tseitin encoding
CNF conversion of arbitrary Boolean formulas.
DRAT / RAT
DRAT is the externally-checkable clausal proof format for UNSAT; RAT (resolution asymmetric tautology) is the redundancy property it certifies beyond plain resolution.
Unsat core
minimal unsatisfiable subset of assumptions/assertions.
Theory
a decision procedure for a fragment (EUF, LRA, BV, arrays).
EUF
equality + uninterpreted functions.
Congruence closure
deducing equalities of function applications.
Union-find
disjoint-set structure for equivalence classes.
Simplex
the LP/feasibility pivoting method (here, for satisfiability).
Bland's rule
anti-cycling pivot rule guaranteeing termination.
LIA / LRA
linear integer / real arithmetic.
Branch-and-bound / Gomory cut
integer-feasibility techniques.
Bit-blasting
compiling bit-vector ops to propositional circuits.
Read-over-write
the array axiom relating select and store.
Extensionality
arrays equal iff equal at every index.
Nelson-Oppen
combining theories via shared-variable equality exchange.
Purification
splitting mixed terms so each atom is single-theory.
Convex theory
entails an equality whenever it entails a disjunction of one; non-convex needs case-splitting.
Theory propagation
theory-implied literals fed to the SAT core.
Incremental solving
push/pop/assumptions reusing state across queries.
Assumption
a temporary unit constraint for one check-sat, not permanently asserted.
Assertion level
push/pop scope; bounds the lifetime of assertions + dependent learnt clauses.
Clause provenance
the assertion level a learnt clause depends on (governs incremental soundness).
Vivification
strengthening a clause by propagating its negated literals.
δ-rationals
value + infinitesimal δ encoding to handle strict < bounds in simplex.
Slack variable
a fresh variable equal to a linear term, carrying its bound.
Degeneracy
a simplex basis with zero-slack rows; the cycling risk Bland's rule removes.
Cube-and-conquer / portfolio
parallel strategies: split a formula into cubes solved in parallel, or race diverse solver configs on the same instance (deferred).
Hash-consing
interning structurally equal terms to one id (sharing + fast equality).
e-graph
equality graph; congruence-closed term DAG (here, union-find + signatures).
Tautological clause
a clause containing both x and ¬x; always true, droppable.
Equisatisfiable
same satisfiability (not same models); preprocessing's guarantee.
Soundness vs completeness
never wrong vs never says "unknown"; this solver prioritizes soundness, bounds completeness with budgets.
Resolution
the inference (A∨l) ∧ (B∨¬l) ⊢ (A∨B); the proof step underlying learning + DRAT.
Lemma
a learned clause (propositional or theory) added to prune future search.
