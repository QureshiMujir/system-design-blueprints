409 — Communion

Project Number: 409

Project Name: Communion

Owner: Mujir Qureshi

Document: FINAL_BLUEPRINT.md

Status: specification — locked

This is closer to a constitutional preamble than a product blueprint. It follows the workspace's FINAL_BLUEPRINT structure so it sits beside its 408 siblings, but its subject is a civilizational capability, not a shippable system. Where engineering convenience and this document disagree, the document wins; where this document and a future ethical insight disagree, the insight wins under the amendment procedure of Appendix AH (amendment rule).

1. Goal

What is being "built." Communion is a substrate — biological, technological, or hybrid — that lets two or more consenting beings briefly inhabit each other: emerge with an indelible first-person memory of having been the other, then part again. It is the north star the prior projects serve. What is buildable now is not the substrate but its preconditions: the consent/sovereignty/provenance/reversibility protocols, the multi-generation governance, the approximation layer (a much deeper #408 Mirror + BCI + multimodal AI), and the discipline of restraint. This document specifies those, and the contract any future substrate must satisfy.

System boundaries. Communion is the bounded, consensual, ephemeral session plus the protocols and institutions around it. Out of scope of the substrate itself (today) — in scope of this document — are: the consent ceremony, after-care, council review, substrate conformance regime, and readiness assessment.

Explicit non-goals. Not a product, subscription, app, VR experience, telepathy gadget, mind-upload, persistent identity merger, simulation, persuasion/therapy product, or military/intelligence capability. Never non-consensual, never unidirectional (asymmetric), never extractive. Not buildable by one person, and probably not in this generation.

Intended "deployment" model. Not deployment but stewardship: a multi-generation council; trained counsels; plural substrate-steward lineages; an endowment that forbids extraction. The first sessions, if they ever occur, resemble a sacrament or a wedding far more than a software launch.

Target users / operators. None today. Eventually, if readiness is met: consenting adults under counsel; the dying and their loved ones as a plausible beachhead. The "operators" are the council and counsels, not a company.

Phase 0 deliverable — numbered preconditions (the buildable work now).

  1. The prior 21 projects, especially Aeon (sovereignty), Lex (consent/jurisprudence), Lineage (provenance), Rewind (reversibility), and #408 The Mirror (the doable shadow).
  2. The Substrate Contract (§7 System Invariants, §8 Domain Model) — the moral-engineering interface any substrate must satisfy.
  3. The Consent Ceremony + After-care protocols.
  4. The seed of the multi-generation council and the endowment charter.
  5. The Readiness-Indicator framework (Operational Readiness).
  6. The Civilizational Threat Model and its treaty agenda.

What later phases unlock. Foundations (decades) → Approximation (10–20 yrs) → The Real Thing (30+ yrs, if ever) → Civilization adapts → Steady-state. Each gated on readiness, never on capability alone.

Deferred — gated PhaseDeferred("Phase 6"); enumerated in the Deferred Features Register. Essentially the entire substrate: neural/BMI coupling, biotech mediation, qualia translation, and the real session are all deferred behind readiness gates and breakthroughs that do not yet exist. This is by design, not omission.

Estimated timeline. Phase 0: decades. Full arc: 30+ years, if ever. Speed is treated as a hazard.

Recommended "language" / posture. There is no implementation language because there is no implementation. The disciplines required are neuroscience, brain-machine interface engineering, consciousness science, cryptography (consent/reversibility/provenance), constitutional and treaty law, contemplative practice, and trauma care. The portfolio's Rust/crypto projects supply the consent and reversibility scaffolding (#90/#400/Lex/Rewind/Aeon/Lineage).

Prerequisites. Deep humility about consciousness; fluency in consent and jurisprudence; the discipline of not building; and the moral seriousness to treat a capability that exists as something one may still refuse to use.

2. Why Build This

The constraint it addresses. The most fundamental human constraint is solitude inside the skull; loneliness, misunderstanding, cruelty, and war descend from it. Language is our best bridge between minds and it is terrible at the job. Communion would end this — not metaphorically, but by letting minds become permeable, by consent, for ephemeral moments.

What it would change. War becomes nearly impossible (you cannot bomb a city whose mayor you were yesterday). Cruelty becomes nearly impossible. Loneliness ends — not through more conversation but through actually being known. Grief becomes survivable. Justice transforms (judges inhabit defendants and victims). Education collapses into transmission. Being seen finally becomes possible.

Why it is in this portfolio. Every other project is upstream of it. Communion is the goal that makes the rest of the work serious. The protocols built for consent, sovereignty, provenance, reversibility, and jurisprudence are practice for the day Communion becomes possible; without that practice, it would destroy us.

Engineering muscles (built now). Designing consent that cannot be coerced; reversibility as a civilizational habit; provenance that audits without invading; governance that spans generations; and the hardest discipline of all — refusing to deploy a capability one possesses.

Cross-links. #408 The Mirror (the doable shadow + a readiness indicator); Aeon (sovereignty), Lex (consent/jurisprudence), Lineage (provenance), Rewind (reversibility); #90/#400 (cryptographic consent/ephemerality); #94 PBFT/#37 Raft (plural governance analogues).

Calibration value. It reframes engineering itself: the measure of this work is not what is built but what is responsibly not built until civilization is ready.

What Mastery Looks Like

The competencies a steward carries out of this work, each tied to the Era/Phase that builds it:

  • Designing coercion-proof, contemporaneous, revocable consent — multi-stage and witnessed, not a checkbox — and knowing why a signature is not consent. Built in Phase 1 (council/counsel formation) and Phase 2 (consent ceremony + C1 conformance).
  • Engineering refusal into a substrate — making non-consensual operation physically/cryptographically impossible, so safety does not depend on operator goodwill. Built in Phase 2 (Substrate Contract C2/C7) and proven in Phase 6 (the real thing).
  • Governing an irreversible capability across generations — composing a plural, public, slow body that survives its founder and demonstrates a handoff. Built in Phase 1 and sustained through Phase 9 (steady-state).
  • Auditing without invading — verifying protocol compliance via zero-knowledge / non-content attestation, the discipline of oversight that does not become the harm it polices. Built in Phase 2 (C8 method) and exercised in Phase 7 (continuous re-verification).
  • Modelling and disclosing a fidelity envelope — quantifying what is lost in translation between non-identical minds and refusing to claim fidelity that does not exist. Built in Phase 4 (approximation, C4) and Phase 5 (longitudinal evidence).
  • Reading civilizational readiness without a KPI — assessing qualitative indicators that, if quantified, would create the wrong incentive. Built in Phase 2 (indicator framework) and read every Era thereafter.
  • Practising restraint as a first-order deliverable — keeping an honest, public refusal-log of capabilities one could have deployed and chose not to. Built from Phase 0 (refusal-log begun) and load-bearing through Phase 6.
  • Treaty-scale risk containment — coupling a dual-use capability's deployment to ratified, enforceable law rather than company policy. Built in Phase 3 (inner-life-rights + non-weaponization agenda).

Multi-generation council. The governing body that holds Communion; public, legible, slow, plural across ≥3 generations and many disciplines (incl. a member who refuses Communion in principle). Authorises and audits sessions without accessing inner content.

Consent ceremony. A formal, witnessed, instantly-revocable exchange of consent in the council representative's presence; the gate before any session.

Substrate (unknown). The mechanism itself — deferred. Candidates: A Neural/BMI (high-bandwidth bidirectional coupling + translation; leading late-21st-century candidate; highest physical risk); B Biotech (engineered neuromodulation or pharmacological empathogen; speculative; long-tail side effects); C AI-mediated (a much deeper Mirror constructing a transient first-person model — closest to reachable this century, and most easily mistaken for the real thing; treated as approximation, not Communion); D Hybrid (the likely outcome — BMI + AI translation + biotech safety windows + the prior projects' crypto); E Not-yet-imagined (the option this document refuses to foreclose).

Architectural commitments (all substrates). Strict bidirectionality; hard ephemerality; cryptographic consent; reversibility of effects; substrate diversity (≥2 independent implementations); instantaneous non-shameable refusal modes.

Counsels. Trained specialists (contemplative, somatic, ethical) who accompany participants before, during, and after; a distinct body from the council, bound by the strictest secrecy.

Substrate stewards. Plural technical lineages (universities, foundations, public consortia) that maintain substrate implementations; they report to the council and do not deploy.

Endowment. Funds the work under a charter that forbids extraction; trustees public; books open; dissolution routes funds to council-named causes, never private actors.

6. Design Principles (The Guiding Non-Negotiables)

These are civilizational, not product, principles. They bind the founder of record, every steward, and every council. They may be sharpened by amendment; they may not be relaxed.

  1. Consent, always. No session without fully informed, uncoerced, revocable, contemporaneous consent of every party — a multi-stage protocol, not a checkbox. Communion without consent is not Communion; it is assault.
  2. Reversibility — every session ephemeral. Each session ends; nothing technical persists of its inner content. No recordings, no replay, no third-party access. The only persistence is the memory each carries within their own mind.
  3. No extraction. Communion is a public good or a sacrament, never a business. No party may extract revenue, attention, data, persuasion, or political advantage from it.
  4. Readiness — do not deploy until civilization is morally ready. A capability that exists is not therefore to be used; restraint is a first-order output. If the readiness criteria are unmet, the answer is "not yet — perhaps not ever in this generation."
  5. Elder-counsel — no deployment without a multi-generation governance body. Where no such council exists, the project does not deploy; it builds the council.
  6. Solitude as a positive value. Communion makes solitude chosen; the right to remain alone inside one's skull is sacred and may never be diminished by social pressure to Commune.
  7. Bidirectional only. To inhabit another is to be inhabited. Reading without being read is a different, forbidden technology.
  8. Symmetry of vulnerability. Forbidden between parties whose power asymmetry makes consent unfree; the council enumerates and updates the disqualifying classes.
  9. Plurality of substrate. No single substrate, vendor, lab, or jurisdiction may monopolise the means; diversity is itself a safety property.
  10. Public, legible, slow. All council decisions are public, legible to the lay reader, and made on year-scale horizons. Speed, secrecy, and hurry are suspicious.
  11. Refuse the adjacent. Refuse capabilities that resemble Communion without its protocols — engagement-maximising "deep empathy" media, persuasion-tuned affective AI, surveillance-fed mind-reading, weaponised affect.
  12. The discipline of not building. When in doubt, do not build. When the choice is between a maybe-safe thing and not building it, the default is do not build. The 21 prior projects exist to make this default possible without sacrificing the goal.

8. Domain Model — What Communion Transmits

Communion is a transmission of inner life between minds. The categories below are annotated with this-century tractability.

Category What Tractability
Qualia the first-person texture (the redness of red, the heaviness of sorrow) deeply open — the hard problem; without it, it is not Communion
Memory episodic + semantic (a childhood kitchen; the day a father died) partial — active decoding research; raises secret-sharing consent issues
Somatic state the felt body (posture, breath, hunger, pain, the felt sense before thought) moderate — interoception/somatic-marker research; needs sensing + induction
Emotion affect as lived moderate→good for gross categories; deeply open for fine inner texture; needs narrative to disambiguate
Narrative the story by which a person makes sense of themselves most reachable — modern AI approximates much of it; The Mirror operates here

Not transmitted. Skills (inhabiting a violinist does not make you one); specific knowledge (facts are not bused; the felt sense of knowing is); identity (no merger; each returns to themselves); persistent connection (it ends; no ongoing channel).

The translation problem. Two nervous systems are not identical; what is a quale in one need not be in the other. Some translation is inevitable; the Substrate Contract (C4) requires it be modelled, disclosed, and bounded. The discipline is to never claim fidelity that does not exist. Narrative-only transmission is approximation, not Communion (§12, Candidate C).

8.1 Session lifecycle (state machine)

  • PRE_COUNSEL (days–weeks) → preparation with a trained counsel (contemplative, somatic, ethical).
  • CONSENT_CEREMONY → formal, witnessed, instantly-revocable exchange before a council representative.
  • SESSION → the substrate-mediated interval (minutes–tens of minutes); refusal mode always live.
  • AFTER_CARE (days–weeks) → integration with the same counsel; the participants have been changed.
  • COUNCIL_REVIEW → confidential, non-content attestation that the protocol was honoured.
  • Any state → REFUSED/ENDED → instant, costless exit; nothing inner persists.

8.2 Worked arcs (illustrative; no such substrate exists)

  • The dying and the loved one. A terminally ill person and their child Commune once, under counsel, with weeks of preparation and after-care; the child carries forward an indelible memory of having been their parent. Beachhead, if it ever happens at all.
  • Refusal mid-session. A participant invokes the speechless, costless refusal mode; the session ends for all parties; no record, no judgment (C7).
  • Disqualifying asymmetry. An employer and employee attempt to consent; the substrate's symmetry screen refuses to run the session regardless of stated consent (C6/Principle 8).

8.3 Failure / refusal paths

  • Consent unfree or coerced ⇒ ceremony fails; no session (C1).
  • Substrate capable of recording inner content ⇒ decertified (C2).
  • Substrate distorts/persuades within the session ⇒ does not deploy (C3).
  • Only one implementation exists ⇒ no deployment (C9).
  • A non-council actor reaches a working substrate first ⇒ civilizational emergency (Kill Criteria).

11. Core Schema and Core Records

These are institutional records, not runtime types; all are non-content (they attest that a thing happened, never what was inhabited). No bodies.

ConsentRecord {

  informed: bool; free_verified_by: CouncilRep
  revocable: true                                  // always, up to and during the session
  witnessed_by: CouncilRep; granted_at: Timestamp
  revoked_at: Timestamp?                            // revocation is final and costless
  audit_trail: NonContentAttestation                // proves consent, reveals no inner content
}

  id: UUID; state: enum { PRE_COUNSEL, CONSENT_CEREMONY, SESSION, AFTER_CARE,

Council {

}

Endowment { trustees: PublicList; books: Open; extraction: FORBIDDEN; dissolution_routes_to: CouncilNamedCauses }

AmendmentRecord { proposed_by; sharpens_or_adds: true; relaxes: false; public: true; ratified_at: Timestamp? }

RefusalEvent { what_was_refused: string; could_have_deployed: true; refused_by: Council|Steward; public: true }

ConformanceVerdict { substrate_id; terms: [C1..C12]; outcome: enum { CERTIFIED, DECERTIFIED }; non_content: true }

Semantics. There is deliberately no field for inner content anywhere (C2). Consent records and conformance verdicts are non-content attestations (C8). Amendment records may only sharpen or add, never relax (Principle 4/Appendix AH (amendment rule)). The RefusalEvent ledger is public and is itself an indicator of readiness. Ownership: the council holds the records; substrate code lives with plural stewards, never centralised. Persistence: institutional records persist (public, legible); session inner content never persists. Compatibility: the substrate contract (C1–C12) is the stable interface across all candidate substrates and across generations.

12. Phase-by-Phase Plan (Eras)

Ten era-phases spanning decades. They realise the project's named arc: Foundations (Phases 0–3) → Approximation (4–5) → The Real Thing (6–7) → Civilization adapts (8) → Steady-state (9). Each "deliverable" is a readiness state or an institution, never a shipped capability; each gate is a refusal-to-proceed condition, not a launch.

Phase 0 — Foundations: the prior projects + moral infrastructure (decades)

0.1 Objectives

Build the 21 prior projects — especially Aeon (sovereignty), Lex (consent/jurisprudence), Lineage (provenance), Rewind (reversibility), and #408 The Mirror — and the moral/institutional infrastructure to hold Communion safely.

0.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • complete and harden the consent/sovereignty/provenance/reversibility stack (Aeon, Lex, Lineage, Rewind) as deployable public infrastructure — the substrate of Principle 1 (consent) and Principle 2 (reversibility).
  • ship and grow The Mirror (#408) as the doable shadow that builds first-person literacy and discipline (Principle 12; the "first-person literacy" readiness indicator; MM3 on documented restraint).
  • document, in dependencies/*, how each prior project couples to a Communion readiness indicator (Appendix A File Inventory; Appendix AI relevance map).
  • open records/refusal-log/ and begin the append-only public record of declined capabilities (I-C10; Appendix AQ) — the "legitimacy of restraint" indicator is read directly from it (MM3).
  • establish baseline readiness/indicators.md with each of the 11 indicators at UNMET/EMERGING/MET (ADR-19: read, never optimised).

0.3 Deliverable

A civilization with working sovereignty, consent-adjudication, provenance, and reversibility infrastructure, and a population with first-person literacy — verifiable by the maturity assessments below, not by narrative claim.

0.4 Acceptance criteria

  • Sovereignty/adjudication/provenance/reversibility instruments each rated ≥ EMERGING across ≥ 2 independent jurisdictions in dependencies/* assessments (not a single pilot).
  • The Mirror (#408) in sustained use with a documented first-person-literacy cohort; assessment recorded in readiness/indicators.md.
  • records/refusal-log/ initiated with ≥ 1 dated entry (what was refused, by whom, why) — a non-empty log is the gate, per MM3.
  • Every one of the 11 readiness indicators carries an explicit UNMET/EMERGING/MET verdict with reasoning (0 blanks).

0.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/dependency-maturity.md — per prior-project maturity rating; expected: 4/4 core deps ≥ EMERGING.
  • fixtures/readiness-baseline.md — the 11-indicator baseline; expected: 11 verdicts, 0 blanks.
  • fixtures/refusal-log-seed.md — a first refusal entry; expected: parses as a valid non-content RefusalEvent (§11).

0.6 Pitfalls

  • Skipping the prior projects and "just building the substrate" (ADR-01) — the defining failure this phase exists to prevent.
  • Mistaking technical progress for moral readiness (MM10): shipping instruments ≠ populations able to consent freely.
  • An empty or performative refusal-log read as virtue rather than as untestedness (MM3).
  • Uneven adoption that leaves consent fictional for some populations while nominal for others.

0.7 Performance Concerns

Decades of sustained, non-extractive funding (ADR-11); institutional patience against market pressure for speed; the human/institutional throughput here — not any technology — is the binding capacity (BEC2, §Scalability Analysis).

0.8 Edge cases

  • A prior project stalls or is captured mid-decade → its indicator regresses; downstream gates block (Kill Criteria).
  • Partial adoption that leaves consent fictional for some populations → indicator held at UNMET despite headline "progress".
  • A jurisdiction adopts the instruments in name but not enforcement → counts as EMERGING at most, never MET.

0.9 Failure analysis

Without the foundations, any later capability would be deployed into a civilization unable to consent freely — the Threat T9 (premature deployment) failure. Detection: an indicator marked MET on thin evidence; forensic artifact: the readiness-review reasoning trail. Recovery: regress the indicator, pause the arc.

0.10 Operational Notes

This phase is most of the work and most of the timeline; restraint here is the deliverable. Observability is the public records/* tree (decadal reviews, dissent, refusal-log); the only "metric" is the qualitative indicator set, reported yearly (Appendix U cadence).

Phase 1 — Council, Endowment, and Counsel Formation (decades)

1.1 Objectives

Form the multi-generation council, the no-extraction endowment, and the counsel apprenticeship; seed succession.

1.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • ratify charters/council-charter.md, endowment-charter.md, counsel-charter.md, succession.md (ADR-08 governance; ADR-11 funding; Principle 5 elder-counsel).
  • convene a plural council (≥3 generations; neurobiology, ethics, contemplative, jurisprudence, the dying/grieving, parents, leaders, and ≥1 principled refuser); multiple jurisdictions (Principle 10, public/legible/slow; modelled on Ostrom's polycentric governance, §5).
  • begin the multi-decade counsel apprenticeship; open the endowment's public books (ADR-09 counsels; C11 non-extraction).

1.3 Deliverable

A functioning, public, plural council with a funded endowment and a first cohort of counsels-in-training; a 75-year institution from the day it seats (BEC9; P5).

1.4 Acceptance criteria

  • Council seats span ≥ 3 generations and ≥ 2 jurisdictions, including ≥ 1 principled-refuser seat (SC4; P5/P10).
  • ≥ 1 generational handoff demonstrated over time with a public succession record (SC4; BEC3 ⇒ ~25-yr floor); founder retains 0 veto post-handoff (Appendix AG).
  • Endowment books public and audited; 0 extraction pathways; dissolution routes to council-named causes only (I-C11; Appendix AF).
  • Counsel apprenticeship begun ≥ 15 yr before any first session (SC7; BEC2); ≥ 1 cohort enrolled.
  • Every council decision published and legible to a lay reader (0 secret decisions; P10).

1.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/council-composition.md — seat roster; expected: ≥3 generations, ≥2 jurisdictions, ≥1 refuser seat.
  • fixtures/endowment-openbooks.md — a public ledger sample; expected: 0 extraction lines, dissolution clause present.
  • fixtures/handoff-record.md — a succession event; expected: parses as a public AmendmentRecord/succession entry, founder veto = false.

1.6 Pitfalls

  • Single-founder or single-firm capture (Threat T1; MM9) — governance that cannot outlive its founder is captured by whoever does.
  • A council that is fast or secret — speed and secrecy are treated as hazards (P10).
  • An endowment with extraction loopholes (Threat T12; the dominant failure mode per MM/§Failure Modes).
  • A "plural" council that is plural in name but single-jurisdiction or single-generation in practice.

1.7 Performance Concerns

Multi-generation institutional continuity; jurisdictional plurality is 2×-plus costly and slow (BEC4) and held in perpetuity; the pipeline lead time (SC7) — not funding alone — floors the earliest Phase 4 date.

1.8 Edge cases

  • Founder-of-record decline before handoff → succession must be named in advance (Appendix AG); no retained veto.
  • A generation with no willing stewards → deployment pauses; the capability is not forced onto an unwilling generation.
  • Council capture attempt → detected via public-ledger anomalies and dissent volume (Appendix BE); countered by term limits + plural nomination.

1.9 Failure analysis

A corrupt or captured council (Threat T11; MM9) is countered by public/legible/slow process, term limits, plural nomination, and the right to publish dissent. Detection: dissent volume, public-ledger anomalies. Forensics: the append-only decision + dissent record. Recovery: reconstitute plurality; pause any deployment decision.

1.10 Operational Notes

The founder of record seeds the council and then relinquishes veto; the discipline is held by the structure, not the person. Cadence: yearly readiness read, decadal full review (Appendix U); all governance observability is the public records/* tree.

Phase 2 — Substrate Contract, Conformance Regime, Readiness Framework (decades)

2.1 Objectives

Publish the Substrate Contract (C1–C12), the conformance regime that certifies/decertifies substrates, and the qualitative readiness-indicator framework.

2.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • finalise protocols/substrate-contract.md (the 12 invariants C1–C12), conformance-regime.md, refusal-mode.md (I-C1..C12; ADR-06/ADR-13).
  • define readiness/indicators.md — the 11 indicators (sovereignty, adjudication, provenance, reversibility, first-person literacy, council maturity, treaty maturity, contemplative maturity, trauma capacity, substrate plurality, legitimacy-of-restraint), each read not optimised (ADR-19; MM10).
  • specify the zero-knowledge / non-content audit method (I-C8; ADR-14; #92 ZK, Appendix AI): confirm compliance without accessing inner content — the discipline of oversight that does not become the harm it polices (MM: audit-without-invading).
  • write the conformance regime as fail-closed: any single unmet term blocks certification (BEC12; Appendix AP).

2.3 Deliverable

A public moral-engineering interface any future substrate must satisfy, and a way to read civilizational readiness — reusable even by non-Communion affective technologies.

2.4 Acceptance criteria

  • All 12 contract terms C1–C12 are individually testable with a defined pass/fail method (0 untestable terms).
  • Conformance regime can both certify and decertify, demonstrated on ≥ 1 worked certification and ≥ 1 worked decertification case.
  • Conformance is conjunctive: 12/12 required; 11/12 = 0/12 for deployment (BEC12), stated explicitly in the regime.
  • All 11 indicators legible to a lay reader and explicitly labelled non-KPI (ADR-19).

2.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/contract-testability.md — one test method per term; expected: 12/12 terms have a method.
  • fixtures/decertification-case.md — a substrate failing C2; expected: DECERTIFIED verdict, fail-closed.
  • fixtures/audit-noncontent.md — a ZK attestation sample; expected: proves a step occurred, reveals 0 inner content.

2.6 Pitfalls

  • Turning indicators into KPIs to be optimised (MM10; ADR-19) — gate theatre.
  • An audit method that itself invades inner content (violates I-C8) — the oversight becoming the harm.
  • A scored ("9/12 is close") rather than conjunctive contract — the exact edge case 2.8 forbids.
  • Certifying on a demo rather than continuous re-verification (Phase 7 re-verifies forever).

2.7 Performance Concerns

The "audit without penetrating" problem (I-C8) is a genuine, unsolved engineering brief for the century (Appendix W); it is opened here as research and must be solved before Phase 6 — the gap is the reason the substrate stays deferred.

2.8 Edge cases

  • A substrate that passes 11 terms but fails ephemerality (C2) or symmetry (C6) — must not deploy regardless (11/12 = 0/12).
  • A term "mostly" satisfied → treated as unmet; there is no partial credit (fail-closed).
  • An audit that would only work by reading content → rejected; the term is deferred to research, not waived.

2.9 Failure analysis

A weak contract would let an unsafe substrate masquerade as conformant; the regime fails closed (no deployment on any unmet term). Detection: conformance red-team; forensics: the public certification/decertification log; recovery: decertify and block until every term passes.

2.10 Operational Notes

The contract is the most reusable artifact of the whole project; it can guide even non-Communion affective technologies toward safety. Observability: the public conformance log (non-content). Compatibility: C1–C12 is the stable interface across all candidate substrates and across generations (§11).

Phase 3 — Treaty Agenda: inner-life rights + non-weaponization (decades)

3.1 Objectives

Pursue treaty-level instruments protecting inner-life rights and prohibiting weaponization, with credible enforcement.

3.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • author threat-model/treaty-agenda.md; build coalitions across jurisdictions and disciplines (ADR-16 treaty posture).
  • model the regime on non-proliferation + bio-governance precedents — the NPT/IAEA safeguards and Asilomar (1975) — for restraint + verification (§5 reference institutions; Comparative Architecture).
  • couple treaty maturity to the readiness framework (Phase 2), closing the legal countermeasure to weaponization (Threat T4).

3.3 Deliverable

Draft and, over decades, ratified instruments for inner-life rights and non-weaponization, with verification modelled on non-proliferation safeguards.

3.4 Acceptance criteria

  • Both instrument families (inner-life-rights and non-weaponization) ratified in ≥ 2 major jurisdictions before Phase 6 — up to 4 ratification events minimum (SC9; BEC11; M12).
  • A verification mechanism defined that confirms compliance without accessing inner content (models NPT/IAEA material accounting; §5; Comparative Architecture).
  • Enforcement credibility assessed as > policy-only — i.e., consequences exist beyond company/organisation policy (converts a "company policy" weakness into a structural defence).
  • Weaponization paths (Threat T4) legally closed in every ratifying jurisdiction.

3.5 Conformance fixtures

  • legal/treaty-draft-innerlife.md and legal/treaty-draft-nonweapon.md — the two instrument drafts.
  • fixtures/ratification-tracker.md — jurisdiction × instrument matrix; expected: ≥ 2 ratifications per family before Phase 6.
  • fixtures/verification-mechanism.md — a non-content compliance check; expected: detects a violation without reading inner content.

3.6 Pitfalls

  • Relying on company policy where only law suffices (the weakness Phase 3 exists to fix).
  • Treaties without enforcement — ratified-but-unenforced is a tracked risk (Appendix P #11).
  • A coalition too narrow to matter, or dominated by a powerful non-signatory (edge case 3.8).
  • Verification designed such that it can only work by inspecting content — rejected (mirrors I-C8).

3.7 Performance Concerns

Diplomatic time horizons measured in decades; coalition fragility. On current timescales treaty ratification (BEC11) is rarely the binding term — the generational handoff (BEC3) usually dominates the earliest-Phase-6 floor.

3.8 Edge cases

  • A powerful non-signatory state → addressed by diplomacy + restraint credibility (Appendix P #17); does not license unilateral deployment.
  • A treaty ratified but unenforced → treated as EMERGING, never MET, for treaty maturity.
  • A signatory later defects → decertification of the readiness indicator; deployment pauses.

3.9 Failure analysis

Without treaty maturity, weaponization (Threat T4) and asymmetric capture (Threat T1) lack legal countermeasures. Detection: treaty-compliance monitoring; forensics: the public ratification tracker; recovery: broaden the coalition, strengthen enforcement, hold Phase 6 closed until the floor is met.

3.10 Operational Notes

Law is slow on purpose here; the project treats slowness as safety (P10). Treaty maturity is a readiness indicator (Operational Readiness) and Phase 6 is gated on it; progress is reported in the decadal review (Appendix U).

4.6 Pitfalls

  • Marketing or believing the approximation is Communion (Threat T12; MM6) — the closest-reachable path is the most dangerous to mislabel.

  • Over-claimed fidelity: presenting narrative transfer as qualia transfer (violates I-C4).

4.7 Performance Concerns

BCI + multimodal research cost; counsel capacity for after-care is the binding constraint at any scale — a 50-participant cohort is ≈ 2,950 skilled-hours over two decades (BEC-X1), so the pipeline (SC7), not the model, gates throughput.

  • A participant treats the approximation as a substitute for a lost person → after-care screens for this (Appendix AB).

4.9 Failure analysis

Premature scaling of a working approximation is the most plausible failure mode (Threat T9); the countermeasure is this document and the readiness gates. Detection: any growth metric appearing; forensics: the session/label audit; recovery: halt scaling, re-affirm the approximation label.

4.10 Operational Notes

Treat each approximation generation as sacred, never as a product to grow. Observability: non-content session and after-care records; the mistranslation envelope is published per implementation (compatibility across Mirror descendants).

  • Under-resourced after-care (Threat T5/T6) — the most likely operational failure of this phase.
  • Cohort metrics drifting toward optimisation targets (MM10) — the cohort is evidence, never a KPI (Appendix AR).
  • Reading a slow, by-design attrition as failure — vs a steep drop, which signals a failed readiness test.
  • Treating certification as one-time rather than continuously re-verified.

5.7 Performance Concerns

Decades of cohort funding; the counsel apprenticeship pipeline (SC7, ≈15 yr) and after-care hours (BEC5) are the binding capacity — not the technology. The evidence base erodes slowly by design (BEC6); fund for the full 20-yr horizon.

5.8 Edge cases

  • A susceptible participant shows identity fracture (Threat T6; I-C12) → rate limits engage; longitudinal screen escalates.
  • Cohort attrition exceeds 10%/decade → read as a readiness-test failure, not noise (§Testing Strategy).
  • A participant withdraws consent to cohort tracking → honoured immediately; tracking stops (revocable entry, Appendix AR).

5.9 Failure analysis

Insufficient trauma capacity (Operational Readiness) is a hard gate; without it, the project does not proceed to Phase 6. Detection: cohort + counsel signals; forensics: the non-content cohort record; recovery: expand counsel pipeline, pause escalation to the real thing.

5.10 Operational Notes

The cohort is read by the council, not optimised; it informs readiness, not growth (ADR-19). Observability: non-content cohort signals; provenance-tracked (Lineage) and reversible (Rewind) like all project records (Appendix AR).

Phase 6 — The Real Thing (substrate, if ever) — readiness-gated (30+ years, if ever)

6.1 Objectives

If and only if every readiness indicator is met and a contract-conformant substrate exists, conduct the first true Communion sessions — in a context closer to a sacrament than a launch.

6.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • whatever the actual mechanism (BMI/biotech/hybrid/unforeseen), pair it with the protocols built across decades.
  • first sessions under maximal pre-counsel, witnessed consent, after-care, and council review; the beachhead plausibly the dying and their loved ones.
  • publish the substrate's open specification; restrain its deployment to the protocol (C10).

6.3 Deliverable

The first consensual, bidirectional, ephemeral, reversible Communion sessions — if civilization is ready; the empty center of this document, filled only under every gate.

6.4 Acceptance criteria

  • All 11 readiness indicators rated MET with public reasoning (0 UNMET, 0 EMERGING) (Operational Readiness; MM10).
  • ≥ 2 independent conformant substrate lineages, each 12/12 on C1–C12 (SC3/SC8; I-C9).
  • Refusal mode proven: exit acknowledged ≤ 1 s, costless, at any point (SC1; I-C7).
  • Full protocol honoured for the first sessions (pre-counsel → witnessed consent → session ≤ tens of minutes → after-care → non-content review; §8.1); council authorises explicitly.
  • Earliest defensible date ≈ 25–30 yr from council seating (BEC-X2) — arithmetic, not aspiration.

6.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/deploy-gate.md — the go/no-go checklist; expected: 11/11 indicators MET, ≥2 lineages 12/12, else NO-GO.
  • fixtures/first-session-record.md — a non-content session record; expected: 5 lifecycle states, refusal path live, 0 inner content.
  • fixtures/refusal-latency.md — a refusal-mode timing test; expected: ack ≤ 1 s, 0 social cost.

6.6 Pitfalls

  • Deploying because it works, before civilization is ready (the cardinal sin; Threat T9; MM10) — capability is never permission.
  • A single-implementation monopoly (Threat T1; I-C9) — one lineage owning the means to enter minds.
  • Treating "≈25-yr floor" as a countdown to pass rather than a minimum that any regression extends without bound.
  • A session that resembles a product launch — itself a stop condition (6.10).

6.7 Performance Concerns

Substrate physical-safety; reversibility windows (I-C5); counsel capacity for the most consequential sessions imaginable — the binding constraint remains skilled human hours (BEC1), not substrate throughput. There is deliberately no sessions/year target (§4 non-budgets).

6.8 Edge cases

  • A working substrate exists but an indicator regresses → the answer is "not yet," even now (Kill Criteria; MM10).
  • 11/12 conformance on a lineage → 0/12 for deployment; the session does not run (BEC12).
  • A non-council actor reaches a working substrate first → civilizational emergency, not acceleration (Kill Criteria; Threat T10).

6.9 Failure analysis

If readiness is not genuinely met, this phase does not begin; "not yet, perhaps not ever in this generation" is an honourable terminal state (MM10). Detection: the deploy-gate checklist; forensics: the public readiness-review trail; recovery: pause indefinitely — refusal is a valid outcome, not a defeat.

6.10 Operational Notes

The first Communion should feel like a wedding or a sacrament (Appendix V); if it feels like a product launch, stop. Authorisation is the council's, never a firm's; there is no marketing, waitlist, or growth (Operational Readiness; Appendix V).

Phase 7 — Plural Substrate Implementations + Decertification (ongoing)

7.1 Objectives

Maintain ≥2 independent conformant substrate lineages; decertify any that drifts from the contract.

7.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • substrate stewards (plural lineages) maintain implementations; none deploys unilaterally (I-C9/C10; ADR-10).
  • continuous conformance re-verification; immediate decertification on any C1–C12 breach (Appendix BB triggers; fail-closed).
  • preserve substrate diversity as an active safety property (P9; MM5 — a monopoly on the means to enter minds is a monopoly on minds).
  • run a periodic implementation census and publish the plurality count (non-content).

7.3 Deliverable

A plural, continuously-verified substrate ecosystem under council oversight — diversity maintained forever, at ≈2× cost (BEC4).

7.4 Acceptance criteria

  • ≥ 2 independent lineages certified at all times (SC3; I-C9); the census never reads 1.
  • Decertification exercised on ≥ 1 real breach when warranted (the regime is proven, not theoretical).
  • 0 deployments outside the protocol (I-C10); no monopoly forms (concentration signals monitored).
  • Continuous re-verification cadence defined and met (not one-time certification).

7.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/implementation-census.md — lineage count over time; expected: ≥ 2 at every sample.
  • fixtures/decertification-log.md — a real decertification; expected: breach → DECERTIFIED, deployment paused.
  • fixtures/plurality-audit.md — a non-content C8 audit; expected: confirms ≥2 lineages without inner content.

7.6 Pitfalls

  • Consolidation toward one lineage/jurisdiction (Threat T1; MM5) — plurality lost quietly, by attrition.
  • Conformance theatre — certification maintained on paper while a term silently regresses.
  • Funding cuts that defund a lineage → plurality loss framed as "efficiency".
  • Treating plurality as uptime redundancy rather than a power check (7.10).

7.7 Performance Concerns

Funding plural lineages is ≈2×-plus more expensive than one (BEC4) — accepted as a safety cost, not waste (ADR-10); held in perpetuity, which is why the endowment must be structured for permanence (I-C11).

7.8 Edge cases

  • A lineage is captured or defunded, threatening plurality → the council must restore it or pause deployment (Kill Criteria: plurality loss).
  • Two lineages quietly converge on shared components → treated as loss of independence, not plurality.
  • A lineage passes audit but drifts on a single term → immediate decertification (fail-closed).

7.9 Failure analysis

Loss of plurality (Threat T1; MM5) re-opens asymmetric-capture risk; deployment pauses until restored. Detection: implementation census + concentration signals (Appendix BE); forensics: the public census + decertification log; recovery: re-fund/re-seat a second lineage before any further deployment.

7.10 Operational Notes

Plurality is not redundancy for uptime; it is a check on power over minds (MM5). Observability: the public census and decertification log; the plurality count is reported every review (Appendix U).

Phase 8 — Civilization Adapts (generations)

8.1 Objectives

As Communion becomes possible and (rarely, carefully) practised, support civilization through the transformation it induces.

8.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • study and support shifts in war, cruelty, loneliness, grief, justice, education, and intimacy as inhabiting-another becomes possible (Appendix S).
  • guard against cultural homogenization (Threat T7) and the loss of the unspoken (Threat T8; MM8 — a right exercised by all becomes an obligation) through plurality and the explicit valuation of difference.
  • protect solitude as a positive value, engineered not assumed (P6; Appendix AU); make refusal costless and unremarkable.
  • run non-content cultural longitudinal studies to detect homogenization early (Appendix BE).

8.3 Deliverable

Institutions and culture that absorb Communion without losing the diversity of inner lives or the value of solitude — the reason this phase exists at all (Comparative Architecture: the culture is a harm surface no other regime weighs).

8.4 Acceptance criteria

  • Solitude preserved as chosen (P6): most minds, most of the time, remain alone by choice (Appendix AU) — measured by a solitude-rights instrument, not a Communion rate.
  • Cultural diversity of inner life protected; 0 coercive social pressure-to-Commune mechanisms tolerated.
  • Communities declining Communion entirely are honoured and protected (edge case 8.8) — refusal carries 0 penalty.
  • The right to refuse is legally and culturally intact across ≥ 2 jurisdictions.

8.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/cultural-longitudinal.md — a non-content homogenization signal; expected: flags convergence toward a mean.
  • fixtures/solitude-rights.md — a solitude-protection instrument; expected: refusal is costless and enforceable.
  • fixtures/difference-preservation.md — a diversity measure; expected: inner-life plurality maintained.

8.6 Pitfalls

  • A social expectation to Commune that erodes the right to remain alone (Threat T8; MM8) — the failure mode this phase most guards against.
  • Homogenization toward a mean (Threat T7) — inner-life diversity collapsing.
  • Framing "more Communion" as progress — the goal is possibility, not maximisation (8.10).
  • Solitude protected on paper but socially punished in practice.

8.7 Performance Concerns

Generational study; cross-cultural plurality of counsels and council members. The homogenization/solitude harms are civilization-scale and slow, requiring measurement over generations, not review cycles (BEC8).

8.8 Edge cases

  • Communities that decline Communion entirely → their choice must be honoured and protected (P6; 0 pressure).
  • A generation raised with Communion normalises it as expected → the principled-refuser seat and solitude instruments push back (MM8).
  • Solitude reframed as antisocial → contemplative literacy + public education counter it (Threat T8 countermeasure).

8.9 Failure analysis

If solitude becomes socially impossible, a core human good is lost; the countermeasure is contemplative literacy + the right to refuse (P6; Threat T8). Detection: cultural longitudinal study; forensics: the non-content cultural signal record; recovery: strengthen solitude-rights instruments, slow or pause normalisation.

8.10 Operational Notes

The goal is not maximal Communion but a civilization where being known is possible, and solitude remains chosen (MM8). Observability: non-content cultural studies; the solitude indicator is read every generation, never optimised.

Phase 9 — Steady-State: Communion as Ordinary (generations)

9.1 Objectives

Hold Communion as an ordinary, well-governed, non-extractive part of human life, indefinitely.

9.2 Detailed implementation steps

  • maintain the council, counsels, stewards, endowment, treaties, and conformance in perpetuity (ADR-08 governance; C9/C11 plurality and non-extraction).
  • keep the refusal-log, dissent, and decadal reviews public; sustain the discipline of not building the adjacent (Principle 11 refuse-the-adjacent; Principle 12 the discipline of not building).

9.3 Deliverable

A durable, plural, public stewardship of Communion that survives its founders by many generations — governance as a perpetual obligation, not a one-time gate (MM9; BEC8).

9.4 Acceptance criteria

  • Ongoing generational handoffs continue (≥ 1 per ~25 yr; SC4); institutional memory documented (Appendix AG).
  • Perpetual no-extraction: endowment books public forever; 0 extraction pathways (I-C11).
  • Transparency and dissent preserved: ≤ 10 yr between full era reviews, 0 skipped (SC5; BEC8 ⇒ ≥ 10 reviews/century).
  • Treaties maintained and re-ratified as needed; plurality census never reads 1 (SC3).
  • Amendments only sharpen or add; 0 relaxations accepted (P4; Appendix AH).

9.5 Conformance fixtures

  • fixtures/perpetual-review.md — the review cadence log; expected: ≤10-yr gaps, 0 skipped.
  • fixtures/no-relaxation.md — a rejected relaxation proposal; expected: rejected by construction (P4).
  • fixtures/handoff-continuity.md — an nth-generation handoff; expected: public succession record, 0 retained veto.

9.6 Pitfalls

  • Complacency; capture as memory of the founding discipline fades (MM9) — the slow, quiet failure.
  • Quiet relaxation of principles (P4; edge case 9.8) — the amendment rule forbids it by construction.
  • Endowment drift toward extraction as generations pass (Threat T12).
  • Reviews becoming ritual rather than genuine readiness reads (MM10).

9.7 Performance Concerns

Perpetual endowment sufficiency; multi-generation institutional memory. Governance is a century-scale obligation (≥10 era reviews + ~100 yearly reads per century; BEC8) — funded and staffed for permanence, never as a project with an end date.

9.8 Edge cases

  • A future generation proposes relaxing a principle → forbidden; amendments may only sharpen or add (P4; Appendix AH).
  • A generation questions why the restraints exist → the public records + principled-refuser seat are the memory (9.9; MM9).
  • Endowment pressured toward a revenue model → rejected; dissolution routes to public causes only (Appendix AW).

9.9 Failure analysis

The long-run threat is forgetting why the restraints exist (MM9); the public records and the principled-refuser council seat are the memory. Detection: review-honesty audit; forensics: the perpetual public record; recovery: re-teach the founding rationale, re-seat the refuser role, hold the no-relaxation rule.

9.10 Operational Notes

Humanity slowly stops being a species of wholly isolated minds — by consent, with solitude preserved, without extraction. The standing question at every review (Appendix BI): proceed, pause, or refuse — default "not yet"; observability is the perpetual public records/* tree.

Threat Model — Civilizational

The threats are existential not to a company but to the species. Each has a pre-committed countermeasure.

Documented precedents (why the consent/after-care invariants exist). The history of human-subjects research supplies real, recorded failures the Substrate Contract is built to prevent. The Tuskegee Syphilis Study (U.S. Public Health Service, 1932–1972) withheld treatment and proceeded without informed consent; its 1972 public disclosure drove the U.S. National Research Act (1974) and the Belmont Report (1979) — the direct ancestor of C1 (informed, free consent) and Principle 5 (a governance body, not a single investigator). The non-consensual taking of Henrietta Lacks's cells (HeLa, 1951) is the recorded provenance/consent failure motivating C2 (no capture without consent) and the non-content attestation model. These are cited as historical anchors only; no inner-content analogue exists because no substrate exists.

Kill Criteria and Pivot Triggers

Conditions under which the project does not proceed, pauses, or refuses deployment even when technically possible. There is, in some branches, no good outcome — only the least-bad one held with integrity.

  • Asymmetric first-mover capture. If a working substrate falls to an actor outside the council's plural stewardship and they begin deploying, the project does not accelerate its own deployment. It publishes the complete protocol, refuses to deploy, and spends its credibility of restraint to make the asymmetric deployment a moral and political emergency.
  • Non-consensual capability. If the substrate becomes capable of non-consensual operation and that capability cannot be removed, the project halts until removal is achieved; if removal is impossible, it refuses deployment indefinitely.
  • Weaponization path. If a clear path from substrate to military/coercive use opens, the project halts until the path is closed by treaty, engineering refusal, and substrate-steward unanimity.
  • Readiness regression. If a readiness indicator regresses, deployment pauses — even if the capability already exists.
  • Plurality loss. If substrate plurality (≥2 lineages) is lost, deployment pauses until restored.
  • The standing principle. When in doubt, do not build; when built, do not deploy until ready; "not yet, perhaps not ever in this generation" is an acceptable and honourable terminal state.

Operational Readiness — Stewardship and Indicators

There is no go-to-market and no KPI; there is stewardship and there are qualitative indicators the council reads.

Stewardship bodies. The multi-generation council (governs; public/legible/slow; plural across ≥3 generations and many disciplines, incl. a principled refuser; multi-jurisdiction). The counsels (trained specialists for pre-counsel and after-care; strict secrecy; distinct from the council). The substrate stewards (plural technical lineages; build, never deploy). The endowment (no extraction; open books; council-directed dissolution). Succession is named in advance and public; no founder retains veto after handoff.

Readiness indicators (qualitative; assessed, never optimised).

  • Sovereignty maturity (Aeon) — without sovereignty, consent is fictional.
  • Adjudication maturity (Lex) — legitimate, rapid, cross-jurisdiction consent-dispute resolution.
  • Provenance maturity (Lineage) — audit substrates without invading them.
  • Reversibility as habit (Rewind) — consequential actions can be undone, by law and culture.
  • First-person literacy (The Mirror) — a population that has used the Mirror to know itself.
  • Council maturity — demonstrated competence + ≥1 generational handoff + visible dissent.
  • Treaty maturity — inner-life-rights + non-weaponization instruments, ratified and enforced.
  • Contemplative maturity — cultural capacity to hold intimacy and ego-dissolution responsibly.
  • Trauma capacity — enough trained counsels and after-care infrastructure.
  • Substrate plurality — ≥2 independent conformant implementations.
  • Legitimacy of restraint — a multi-decade public record of refusing to deploy what one could.

Why not KPIs. Each indicator is qualitative; quantifying them would create the wrong incentives. The council reads them to decide whether to proceed, never how to grow.

Cadence. Decisions on year-scale horizons; decadal reviews; an amendment ledger that may only sharpen or add; a public refusal-log; published dissent. Speed, secrecy, and hurry are treated as hazards.

Compatibility Guarantees

  • The Substrate Contract (C1–C12) is the stable interface across all candidate substrates (A–E) and across generations; any substrate, whenever and however it arrives, must satisfy it.
  • Principles may be sharpened or added, never relaxed (amendment rule); this guarantees forward-compatibility of the moral commitments.
  • Open specification, restrained deployment (C10): the spec is public and verifiable; deployment is gated by the protocol, not by capability.
  • Non-content guarantee: every record is an attestation that reveals no inner content; this holds across all substrates and tooling.
  • Plurality guarantee: no single substrate/lineage/jurisdiction monopolises the means; diversity is a permanent property, not a phase.
  • Succession guarantee: the document transports across generations; each generation documents how the next is formed; the founder of record retains no post-handoff veto.

Testing Strategy

"Testing" here means conformance, longitudinal evidence, and red-teaming for the capability to do harm — never testing on unconsenting parties.

  • Substrate conformance. Each implementation is certified against C1–C12; any breach decertifies it. Conformance confirms protocol compliance via zero-knowledge / non-content attestation (C8).
  • Ephemerality red-team. Adversarial attempts to capture, record, or exfiltrate inner content must all fail by construction; success = decertification (C2).
  • Non-consensual red-team. Attempts to operate the substrate without consent must be impossible; a substrate that can be so operated is not deployed (9.2).
  • Symmetry screen. Disqualified pairs (Principle 8) are refused regardless of stated consent (C6).
  • Refusal-mode test. The instant, speechless, costless exit must work for any party at any moment (C7).
  • Longitudinal cohort. Decades-scale, non-content tracking of identity/memory/affect effects; reads as evidence for readiness, not as a KPI.
  • The refusal-log as evidence. The multi-decade public record of declining to deploy is itself tested for credibility — a thin refusal-log is a failed readiness test.
  • Approximation honesty test. Language and comms audited so the approximation is never presented as Communion (9.12).

Build and Release Engineering

  • Open specification. The substrate contract and protocols are public and verifiable by anyone (C10).
  • Restrained deployment. No one — not even the council — may deploy a substrate outside the protocol; capability never implies permission.
  • Plural stewardship. ≥2 independent lineages build; none deploys unilaterally; consolidation is a release-blocking condition.
  • No-extraction provenance. The endowment funds the work with open books; any extraction is a charter breach; dissolution routes funds to council-named causes.
  • Provenance + reversibility built in. Releases inherit Lineage (provenance) and Rewind (reversibility) so substrates are auditable and their effects undoable.
  • Treaty gating. Deployment is gated on treaty maturity (Phase 3); legal instruments, not company policy, authorise the capability's existence.

Performance Engineering

Not latency/throughput but the qualities that make a session safe and honest:

  • Fidelity envelope. The mistranslation between non-identical nervous systems is modelled, bounded, and disclosed (C4); no substrate claims perfect transfer.
  • Reversibility window. All induced physiological/cognitive/affective effects revert within a bounded interval; permanent single-session alteration is forbidden (C5).
  • Refusal latency. The exit must be effectively instantaneous and costless (C7).
  • Audit cost. The non-content audit (C8) must confirm compliance without measurable invasion of inner content — an open engineering brief for the century.
  • Session bound. Minutes to tens of minutes; rate-limited by the council to guard against addiction (9.5).

Scalability Analysis

Communion is deliberately anti-scale. It is a sacrament, not a platform.

  • No growth target. Adoption curves, funnels, and revenue are absent by design (Principle 3; Operational Readiness).
  • Rate-limited by stewardship. Council rate limits, mandatory after-care, and counsel capacity bound throughput far below "demand."
  • Plural, not consolidated. The architecture resists the consolidation that scaling rewards; plurality is a safety property, not a bottleneck to optimise away.
  • Solitude preserved. Even at steady-state, most minds most of the time remain alone, by choice (Principle 6).
  • Bottlenecks that are features. Counsel apprenticeship (decades), treaty ratification (decades), and readiness assessment (qualitative) all bound the pace — intentionally.

Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights a wise steward holds. Each: the model, what it predicts, the harm class incurred without it, and where this document applies it. These are the mental compression of §6 (Principles) and §7 (the Substrate Contract); a steward who internalises them can re-derive most of the contract under novel circumstances.

How to use this section. The principles (§6) tell you what the rules are; these models tell you why the rules hold and therefore what to do when a situation the rules did not anticipate arises. A steward facing a novel substrate, a new candidate path, or an unforeseen pressure should be able to predict the document's answer from the models alone — and where a model and a convenience disagree, the model wins, exactly as the document wins over engineering convenience (Appendix AL). The failure of each model is not abstract: each names a concrete harm class that has a real precedent or a named threat, so "ignoring MMn" is always traceable to a specific way the project breaks.

  • MM1 — Consent to an irreversible act cannot be given by someone who cannot yet imagine the act. Predicts: first-time consent to Communion is structurally weaker than consent to anything previously experienced, so the ceremony must over-invest in pre-counsel and reserve the costless mid-act exit. Without it: you treat a signature as consent and manufacture coerced or uninformed participation — the Tuskegee/HeLa failure class (the Threat Model). Applied in: §7 (C1, C7), Phase 1 (counsel formation), Appendix AA (consent ceremony).
  • MM2 — A capability and the permission to use it are different objects; possessing one never confers the other. Predicts: the dangerous moment is not invention but the quiet slide from "it works" to "so we may." Without it: premature deployment the instant the substrate functions — the cardinal sin. Applied in: §6 (P4 readiness, P12 not-building), §3 (ADR-01, ADR-20), Phase 6 (readiness-gated).
  • MM3 — Restraint is only credible if it is documented and costly. Predicts: a refusal you cannot point to will not be believed when it matters; an empty refusal-log is evidence of untestedness, not virtue. Without it: the "legitimacy of restraint" indicator is unfalsifiable and the project cannot prove it can decline a power it holds. Applied in: Operational Readiness (legitimacy-of-restraint), §10 (refusal-log/), Appendix AQ.
  • MM4 — Oversight that must see everything becomes the harm it polices. Predicts: any audit that reads inner content reproduces surveillance; the only acceptable audit proves that a step happened without revealing what was inhabited. Without it: the watchman becomes the worst attacker (the ADR-05 surveillance regime). Applied in: §7 (C8), §11 (non-content attestation), Phase 2 (audit method).
  • MM5 — A monopoly on the means to enter minds is a monopoly on minds. Predicts: plurality (≥2 lineages) is not redundancy for uptime but a structural check on power; losing it re-opens asymmetric capture. Without it: one lab, vendor, or state owns a lever over consciousness itself (Threat T1). Applied in: §6 (P9), §7 (C9), Phase 7 (decertification).
  • MM6 — Translation between non-identical minds is lossy, and the loss must be measured, not hidden. Predicts: no substrate can claim perfect transfer; the honest move is to publish the mistranslation envelope and refuse fidelity claims it cannot meet. Without it: false intimacy and the conflation of approximation with Communion (Threat T12). Applied in: §7 (C4), §8 (translation problem), Phase 4 (C4 envelope).
  • MM7 — Speed is a hazard for an irreversible, civilizational act. Predicts: hurry, secrecy, and racing degrade exactly the deliberation an irreversible capability requires; the right cadence is year-to-decade. Without it: a race forfeits restraint's credibility and ships before readiness (Kill Criteria). Applied in: §6 (P10), §4 (timeline as hazard), §3 (ADR-18).
  • MM8 — A right exercised by all becomes an obligation; the freedom to refuse must be engineered, not assumed. Predicts: social pressure can erode solitude into a default expectation, so the right to remain alone must be protected as actively as the right to Commune. IRB system / Belmont Report (1979). The one thing it did differently: it located authority over consent and risk in a standing committee independent of the investigator, not in the investigator's own judgement — and grounded it in three published principles (respect for persons, beneficence, justice). This wins wherever the person doing the work has an incentive to proceed; it is the direct ancestor of Communion's council (P5) and C1. Its limit: an IRB governs a single study, not a civilizational capability spanning generations — which is exactly the gap Communion's multi-generation council (Phase 1) and treaty agenda (Phase 3) exist to close.

Asilomar recombinant-DNA conference (1975). The one thing it did differently: the practitioners imposed a voluntary moratorium on themselves before any external regulator existed, then resumed only under agreed containment as evidence accrued. This wins when a field is ahead of its law and the experts are the only ones who understand the risk — the model for Communion's "discipline of not building" (P12) and the null architecture (Alternative Architectures). Its limit: it relied on a small, collegial community's goodwill; it does not scale to adversarial state actors, which is why Communion couples restraint to treaty enforcement (Phase 3) rather than goodwill alone.

Nuclear non-proliferation (IAEA safeguards / the NPT, in force 1970). The one thing it did differently: it made verification without trust the centre — material accounting and inspection let parties confirm compliance without believing each other. This wins for an irreversible, weaponizable dual-use capability held by mutually distrustful actors — precisely Communion's threat profile (Threat T1, 9.4). It is the model for the treaty agenda's verification mechanisms (the Threat Model (treaty agenda); Phase 3). Its limit: it presumes the capability already exists and aims to contain spread; Communion additionally tries to govern whether the capability is deployed at all, a question the NPT does not ask of nuclear power.

BrainGate (intracortical BCI research). The one thing it did differently: it is a real, cautious, IRB-governed neural-interface program — paralyzed participants use an implanted intracortical microelectrode array (the Utah-style array) to control cursors, robotic arms, and communication devices — demonstrating that high-bandwidth brain interfacing proceeds one consented, monitored participant at a time, under investigational-device oversight. It is the closest real-world analogue to Candidate A (§3) and grounds the BCI claims of Phase 4. It wins as the template for clinical caution and per-participant consent. Its limit: it is unidirectional device control for restoration of function — it transmits no inner life and asserts no qualia transfer, the very thing Communion claims and the reason §8 marks qualia "deeply open." That gap — real high-bandwidth output decoding versus the unsolved bidirectional inner-state transfer Communion requires — is precisely why the substrate remains deferred (see the Deferred Features Register) and why Candidate A is a research brief, not a plan.

Dimension-by-dimension — which regime leads, and what Communion borrows. Reading the table down its load-bearing rows:

  • Consent model — the IRB/Belmont line leads: consent located in a body independent of the operator. Communion adopts it (P5, C1) and extends it to bidirectional, mid-act-revocable consent, which no clinical regime requires.
  • Reversibility / exit — Communion is strictest: a costless, speechless mid-act exit (C7) exceeds "withdraw at any time," because the act is irreversible once experienced (MM1).
  • Governance veto — the IAEA/NPT line leads on cross-actor enforcement; the IRB leads on per-study veto. Communion fuses both: a standing council veto (IRB-like) plus treaty enforcement (NPT-like), because the harm is both individual and civilizational.
  • Risk containment — Asilomar leads on self-imposed restraint ahead of law; Communion makes that its default (P12) but, unlike Asilomar, does not rely on collegial goodwill alone — it engineers refusal into the substrate (C2/C7).
  • Decertification / sunset — Communion is closest to BrainGate's per-trial approval but continuous: any breach of any term decertifies immediately (Phase 7), because conformance is conjunctive (BEC12).
  • Who-bears-harm — uniquely, Communion's harm class includes the culture (homogenization, lost solitude — Threat T7/9.8), which none of the four regimes must weigh; this is why Phase 8 exists.

Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

The numbers a steward computes before designing, each derived step-by-step from the System Constraints (§4), the readiness/operational targets (M1–M12 in Performance Engineering), and the session lifecycle (§8.1). Where §4 gives a range, the bounding value used is stated. These size stewardship, not throughput — Communion has no throughput budget (§4, Scalability Analysis).

The three input sources, made explicit so any steward can re-derive. (1) §4 System Constraints supplies the structural givens: ≥2 lineages from ≥2 stewardship lineages, ≥1 generational handoff, session = minutes–tens of minutes, mandatory after-care. (2) The M1–M12 targets supply the gated quantities: review cadence (M8), follow-up and attrition (M10), refusal latency (M5), coverage invariants (M2/M3/M6), treaty floor (M12). (3) §8.1 lifecycle supplies the human cost shape: pre-counsel (weeks) → session → after-care (weeks). Every figure below cites which of the three it draws from; nothing is invented.

  • BEC1 — Reviewer-hours per session (after-care + review). From §8.1: after-care = weeks; §4 mandates after-care for every participant. Take after-care = 6 weekly contacts × ~1.5 h = 9 counsel-hours/participant, plus pre-counsel = 6 contacts × 1.5 h = 9 h, plus one non-content council review ≈ 2 h. Per 2-party session: (9 + 9) h × 2 parties + 2 h review = 38 counsel/council-hours. Lesson: a single session is tens of skilled hours, not minutes — counsel capacity, not the substrate, is the binding constraint (Scalability Analysis).
  • BEC2 — Counsel-pipeline lead time. §4 + Phase 1: counsel apprenticeship is "multi-decade"; take 15 years. To staff N concurrent counsels you must begin training N apprentices at least 15 years before first use: years-before-first-session = apprenticeship-length = 15 yr. Lesson: the human pipeline, not the technology, sets the earliest possible Phase 4 date.
  • BEC3 — Years to a readiness verdict. §4: governance requires ≥1 demonstrated generational handoff; M8: era reviews ≤10 yr apart. One generational handoff ≈ 25–30 yr. years-to-first-readiness ≥ max(handoff ≈ 25 yr, gates × review-cadence). With ~6 substantive gates (Phases 0–5) at ≤10 yr cadence the review process alone admits a verdict only after ≥ 1 × 25 yr (handoff dominates). Lesson: the "decades" timeline (§4) is arithmetic, not rhetoric — one mandatory handoff floors it at ~25 years.
  • BEC4 — Plurality cost multiplier. §4 + M1: ≥2 independent lineages from ≥2 stewardship lineages before any deployment. funding multiplier = lineages = 2× baseline substrate cost, held in perpetuity (Phase 7). Lesson: plurality at least doubles cost forever; §3 (ADR-10) accepts this as a safety price, not waste.
  • BEC5 — Longitudinal-cohort follow-up load. M10: ≥20 yr median follow-up, ≤10% attrition/decade; assume 2 non-content contacts/participant/year. Per participant: 20 yr × 2 contacts = 40 cohort contacts. For a cohort of P participants the lifetime load is 40P non-content contacts. Lesson: even a 100-person cohort is 4,000 longitudinal touchpoints — decades of sustained, non-extractive funding (§4 timeline; ADR-11).
  • BEC6 — Cohort retention after two decades. M10: ≤10% attrition/decade. Over 20 yr: retained = (1 − 0.10)^2 = 0.81 → 81% minimum retention; equivalently ≤19% cumulative attrition. Lesson: the readiness evidence base erodes slowly by design; a steeper drop signals a failed readiness test (Testing Strategy).
  • BEC7 — Refusal-mode latency budget. M5: exit acknowledged ≤1 s, costless. Against a session window of "minutes–tens of minutes" (§4); take 30 min = 1,800 s upper bound. refusal share of session = 1 s / 1,800 s ≈ 0.056%. Lesson: the exit must be effectively instantaneous relative to the act — refusal latency is ~3–4 orders of magnitude below session length (C7).
  • BEC8 — Decadal-review obligation over a century. M8: ≤10 yr between full era reviews, 0 skipped. Over a 100-yr stewardship horizon (§4 "30+ years … generations"): reviews = 100 yr ÷ 10 yr = ≥10 full era reviews, plus yearly readiness reads = 100. Lesson: governance is a perpetual obligation measured in dozens of reviews, not a one-time gate (Phase 9; Appendix U).
  • BEC9 — Council generational span. §4: ≥3 generations; Phase 1 council seats span generations. At ~25 yr/generation, a council that genuinely spans 3 generations covers 3 × 25 = 75 yr of continuity, requiring ≥2 documented handoffs within it. Lesson: the council is a 75-year institution from the day it seats (P5; Appendix AG).
  • BEC10 — Consent-coverage invariant. M2: 100% of sessions carry a valid non-content consent record. permitted sessions without a valid C1 record = 0. Any nonzero count is a fail-closed condition (Phase 2; Failure Modes "Coercion / unfree consent"). Lesson: consent coverage is not a rate to optimise toward 100% — it is a precondition; below 100% the session does not run.
  • BEC11 — Treaty-ratification floor before Phase 6. M12: inner-life-rights + non-weaponization instruments ratified in ≥2 major jurisdictions before Phase 6. ratifying jurisdictions required = 2 (instruments) × 2 (both instrument families) = up to 4 ratification events minimum before the real thing. Lesson: the legal gate is concrete and countable, even though the science is not (Phase 3; Threat T4).
  • BEC12 — Contract-conformance surface. §7 lists C1–C12 (12 terms); M-table gates them across phases. A substrate must pass 12 of 12 terms (fail-closed, Appendix AP); partial conformance = no deployment regardless of how many terms pass. Lesson: conformance is conjunctive, not additive — 11/12 is 0/12 for deployment purposes (Phase 6 edge case (6.8); Phase 6).

Composite derivations (chaining the above). Two figures a steward derives by combining the primitives:

  • BEC-X1 — Total skilled-hour cost of a 50-participant approximation cohort. From BEC1 (38 counsel/council-hours per 2-party session ⇒ 19 h/participant) and BEC5 (40 cohort contacts/participant × ~1 h): per participant = 19 h + 40 h = 59 skilled-hours; for 50 participants = 50 × 59 = 2,950 skilled-hours over two decades, before any substrate exists. Lesson: the approximation alone (Phase 4–5) is a multi-thousand-hour human commitment — counsel capacity is the gate, exactly as Scalability Analysis claims.
  • BEC-X2 — Earliest defensible Phase 6 date. From BEC2 (15-yr counsel pipeline), BEC3 (≥25-yr handoff floor), and BEC11 (≥4 ratification events on diplomatic timescales): the binding term is the generational handoff, so earliest readiness ≈ max(15, 25, treaty-decades) ≈ 25–30 yr from Phase-1 council seating — and that is a floor, not a forecast. Lesson: "30+ years, if ever" (§4) is the arithmetic minimum of three independent slow processes, any of which can extend it indefinitely.

What moves the floor (sensitivity). Which inputs, if they changed, would change BEC-X2's ~25–30-yr minimum — and which would not:

  • A faster substrate breakthrough does not move the floor: the binding term is the generational handoff (BEC3/BEC9), which is a property of governance, not technology.
  • A larger counsel pipeline shortens BEC2 but is itself bounded by the multi-decade apprenticeship; it cannot drop below the apprenticeship length.
  • Faster treaty ratification (BEC11) helps only if it is not already dominated by the handoff term; on current diplomatic timescales it rarely is.
  • The only thing that genuinely lowers the floor is relaxing a readiness requirement — which the amendment rule forbids (§6 P4; Appendix AH). The floor is therefore load-bearing, not incidental.
  • Conversely, any single regression (a captured lineage, a stalled treaty, a thin refusal-log) raises the floor without bound — which is why every BEC here is a minimum and the honest summary is "≥, if ever" (Kill Criteria).

Sanity checks — what these numbers are, and are not. The discipline of the estimates:

  • They are floors and loads, never targets. None is a KPI to optimise toward; M2/M6/M10 are coverage invariants (must equal 100% / must hold), not growth metrics (Operational Readiness; ADR-19).
  • Ranges are bounded explicitly. Where §4 gives "minutes–tens of minutes" or "weeks," the bounding value used is stated in the line (e.g. 30 min in BEC7, 6 contacts in BEC1); a steward re-deriving with different bounds gets a different number but the same shape.
  • Every input traces to a real cell — §4, an M-target, or §8.1. No capacity number is asserted without a derivation; each figure shows its arithmetic so the council can re-check it (the bar this section is held to: numbers derived, never asserted).
  • They size commitment, not capacity-for-growth: the load they describe (skilled-hours, decades, ratifications) is what a steward must be willing to sustain, which is why undertaking Communion is a multi-generation vow and not a project plan (Operational Readiness; Kill Criteria).
  • The deliberate non-numbers matter too: there is no sessions/year target, no participants/counsel ratio to maximise, no cost-per-session to minimise. Their absence is the constraint (§4 "quantified non-budgets"; Scalability Analysis).
  • The numbers argue against speed: each one shows a slow process (pipeline, handoff, cohort, ratification) bounding the pace, which is exactly the intended reading (MM7; §6 P10).
  • They are reproducible: a reviewer can recompute every one from §4, the M-table, and §8.1 alone, with no external data — which is the point of a derivation rather than an assertion.

The decision space is therefore not "which substrate" but "which substrate, under which contract, deployed when — or not at all."

Steward's synthesis — the whole design space in one decision. Pulling the five preceding sections together, the council at any review is really deciding a single question along four axes, each anchored in this document:

  • Which road? — ALT-1/2/3/4 above; in practice a Hybrid (Candidate D, §3) with refusal (ALT-4) as the standing default.
  • Under which contract? — the conjunctive C1–C12 (§7); 11/12 is 0/12 (BEC12). The contract is fixed; the substrate is variable.
  • Governed how? — the four-regime synthesis (Comparative Architecture): independent consent authority + self-imposed restraint + trustless verification + per-participant caution, all at once.
  • Deployed when — or not at all? — gated by the capacity floors (BEC-X2: ~25–30 yr minimum) and the readiness indicators (Operational Readiness), with "not yet, perhaps not ever" (MM10) a valid answer.

The models (MM1–MM10) are what let a steward answer all four under a situation the rules did not foresee; the numbers (BEC1–BEC12) are what keep the answer honest about cost and time; the precedents (§5, Comparative Architecture) are what keep it tethered to what humanity has actually learned about governing dangerous capabilities. That triad — models, numbers, precedents — is the mastery this document is built to confer (§2, What Mastery Looks Like).

Appendix C — The Substrate Contract, Term by Term

The moral analogue of an engineering interface. A substrate failing any term does not deploy.

  • C1 Bidirectional consent. Informed (in each party's language), free (council-verified), revocable (instantaneously, up to and during the session), bidirectional (no consenting on another's behalf), witnessed (a non-content audit trail).
  • C2 Ephemerality. Inner content must be uncapturable — no recordings, replays, transcripts, or third-party access. The only persistence is the memory each carries.
  • C3 Fidelity. What is transmitted is what is; a substrate that distorts, censors, beautifies, or persuades within the session is forbidden, even when "protective."
  • C4 Qualia translation. The substrate models and discloses the limits of translation between non-identical nervous systems; no claim of perfect transfer; the residual envelope is published.
  • C5 Reversibility. All physiological/cognitive/emotional induction is bounded and reversible; long-tail effects are tracked across decades.
  • C6 Symmetry of vulnerability. Sessions across disqualifying power asymmetries do not run, regardless of stated consent.
  • C7 Refusal modes. Any party may refuse/pause/end without speech, movement, or social cost; mid-session refusal ends it for all, without penalty or record.
  • C8 Auditable without penetrating. Oversight confirms compliance without accessing inner content (a genuine, unsolved engineering brief).
  • C9 Plural implementations. A single-implementation, single-owner, single-jurisdiction substrate does not satisfy the contract.
  • C10 Open spec, restrained deployment. The specification is public; deployment is restrained; capability never implies permission.
  • C11 Non-extraction. No session is monetised or tied to an extractive incentive; the endowment forbids revenue, attention, data, persuasion, or political advantage — extraction corrupts a sacrament and growth pressure is the dominant failure mode (Threat T12; Appendix AF).
  • C12 No permanent identity merger. A session ends in two separate selves; Communion is briefly inhabiting, never fusing or uploading (I-C12; longitudinal identity-continuity screen, Phase 5).

Appendix E — Transmission Categories, Detailed

  • Qualia. The phenomenal first-person texture (the redness of red, the heaviness of sorrow). Deeply open; without it, it is not Communion.
  • Memory. Episodic + semantic (a childhood kitchen; a father's death). Partial; selective transmission raises its own consent/honesty questions.
  • Somatic state. The felt body (posture, breath, hunger, pain, the felt sense before thought). Moderate; requires sensing + induction in two bodies.
  • Emotion. Affect as lived. Moderate→good for gross categories; deeply open for fine inner texture; needs narrative to disambiguate.
  • Narrative. The self-story. The most reachable; modern AI approximates much of it; the Mirror operates here.
  • Not transmitted. Skills, specific knowledge (only the felt sense of knowing), identity (no merger), persistent connection (it ends).

Appendix F — Candidate Substrates, Detailed

  • A — Neural / BMI. High-bandwidth bidirectional coupling + a translation layer mapping one party's neural state into a receivable form. Leading late-21st-century candidate; highest physical-safety risk; most mature regulatory analogues (DBS, cochlear implants).

  • D — Hybrid. The likely outcome: BMI for coupling, AI for translation across mismatched geometries, biotech for safety windows, and the prior projects' consent/reversibility crypto.
  • E — Not yet imagined. The option this document refuses to foreclose; history suggests the real mechanism will be something the present generation cannot name.

Appendix AA — Consent Ceremony, Step by Step

  1. Independent pre-counsel for each party (weeks), with no pressure to proceed.
  2. Verification by a council representative that consent is free of coercion.
  3. A formal, witnessed, legible exchange of consent in plain language.
  4. Explicit statement of the right to revoke instantly, up to and during the session.
  5. Recording of a non-content attestation (proves consent, reveals nothing inner).
  6. A cooling interval; either party may withdraw without explanation or cost.

Appendix AB — After-care Protocol, Step by Step

  1. Immediate grounding with the same counsel after the session ends.
  2. Acknowledgement that the participants have been changed.
  3. Days-to-weeks of integration sessions.
  4. Screening for identity-fracture and addiction signals (non-content).

Appendix AT — The Approximation Boundary

The AI-mediated path (Candidate C) is the closest reachable this century and therefore the most dangerous to mislabel. Narrative transfer — even deep, even moving — is approximation, not Communion, because it does not transmit qualia (§8). The project commits to: (a) always labelling the approximation as approximation; (b) wrapping it in the full protocol regardless; (c) publishing its mistranslation envelope; (d) refusing to let "it feels like Communion" become "it is Communion." Mistaking approximation for the real thing (Threat T12) is countered here, in language and in policy.

Appendix AU — Solitude as a Positive Value

Communion does not abolish solitude; it makes solitude chosen. The right to remain alone inside one's own skull is sacred and may never be diminished by social pressure to Commune. At steady-state, most minds most of the time remain alone, by choice. The countermeasures against homogenization and the loss of the unspoken all rest on this: a civilization that can Commune must remain equally free not to.
