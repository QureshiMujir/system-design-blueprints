  3. Why is a single vantage's DOWN verdict insufficient, and how does quorum decouple detection speed from false positives? (§4.3 + I-CONS)
  4. What is "split steering," why is it worse than a slow uniform failover, and what prevents it? (Design Principle 5 + §22 + I-ORDER/I-LEADER)
  5. How does the edge hot path stay lock-free at 50 k qps, and what does RCU/Arc-swap buy over a RWLock? (§3.5 + Performance Engineering + I-SNAP)
  6. Decompose the client-observed failover RTO into its four terms; which two does the GSLB control? (§4.1 + §2.7)
  7. Why is DNS steering "statistical, not per-request," and which mechanisms give per-request precision at what cost? (§2.7 + §20/§21)
  8. When is anycast withdrawal the right mechanism versus DNS versus HTTP redirect? (§8.8 + §21 + §2.1)
  9. How does flap damping trade reactivity for stability, and what is the penalty/decay behavior? (§4.3 + I-DAMP)

  18. How does a composite policy (geo → failover) evaluate deterministically, and which specificity rule fires? (§8.4–8.5 + I-DET)

Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an expert GSLB engineer holds. Each: the model, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and where this blueprint applies it.

  • MM1 — Steering is a decision; the mechanism is interchangeable delivery. The policy engine computes which endpoint; DNS/anycast/redirect just deliver it. Predicts: the same decision logic must serve all three mechanisms. Without it: policy logic baked into the DNS path, untestable and unreusable. Applied: Design Principle 1, §3.2, P4–P9.
  • MM2 — You steer through caches you do not control; the TTL is the reactivity floor. Predicts: failover RTO is bounded below by the cached TTL even with instant detection + propagation. Without it: you "fix" failover speed by tuning detection and wonder why clients still hit the dead region. Applied: §2.7, §4.1, I-TTL.
  • MM3 — DNS steering is statistical, not per-request. You steer the aggregate of clients behind a resolver/subnet, not an individual. Predicts: per-request precision needs anycast or HTTP redirect. Without it: you promise per-client DNS steering that physically cannot exist. Applied: §2.7 Consequence 2, P6/P8/P9.
  • MM4 — Without ECS the GSLB sees the resolver, not the client. ECS fixes geo accuracy but shatters the resolver cache by subnet. Predicts: accuracy is bought with cache cardinality + a privacy cost. Without it: you geo-route to 8.8.8.8's location and mis-steer the client. Applied: §2.1, §4.2, I-ECS.
  • MM5 — One vantage's "down" is noise; truth is a quorum. Predicts: a single broken probe path will report DOWN; only consensus should re-steer. Without it: one broken vantage flaps global traffic. Applied: §4.3, I-CONS.
  • MM6 — A flapping endpoint is a self-inflicted outage amplifier; damp it. Predicts: re-steering on every health flip thrashes traffic worse than the original blip. Without it: the GSLB amplifies transient instability into a global incident. Applied: Design Principle 4, I-DAMP.
  • MM7 — A half-propagated failover (split steering) is worse than a slow uniform one. Predicts: unordered/unbounded propagation lets some edges steer to a dead region while others don't. Without it: a failover that half-lands corrupts global state. Applied: Design Principle 5, §22, I-ORDER/I-LEADER.
  • MM8 — Stale-but-available beats fresh-but-unavailable. Predicts: an edge behind on propagation should serve its last-good snapshot, not fail. Without it: a propagation hiccup becomes a global DNS outage. Applied: Design Principle 3, I-FAILSAFE.
  • MM9 — The data plane is read-mostly; an RCU snapshot swap buys the sub-ms tail. Predicts: a per-query lock collapses the tail under QPS. Without it: the p99 SLO dies at load. Applied: Design Principles 2/9, §3.5, I-SNAP.
  • MM10 — One writer per generation (leader + epoch) is deliberate serialization for correctness, not a bottleneck. A generation advance is a cheap stamp; the heavy work is sharded. Predicts: two writers → conflicting global state. Without it: split steering at the source. Applied: Design Principle 12, §22, I-LEADER.

Required reading (§5)

5.1 Books

  • Liu & Albitz, DNS and BIND (5e) — ch.4–5 (zones, RRsets, TTL), ch.10 (advanced features). The authoritative-DNS + TTL mental model our answers live in. — Applied: §8.7, §18–§19, P6–P7
  • Beyer et al., Site Reliability Engineering (Google) — ch.19 (load balancing at the frontend), ch.20 (datacenter load balancing). The GSLB-vs-local-LB framing and Google's steering practice. — Applied: §2.1–§2.7, §3.2, §4.1, P0
  • Tanenbaum & van Steen, Distributed Systems (3e) — ch.6 (consistency + replication), ch.8 (fault tolerance, failure detection). The eventual-consistency + propagation foundation. — Applied: §8.12, §22, P10
  • Kurose & Ross, Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach — ch.2 (DNS), ch.4–5 (network/IP, routing, BGP). DNS + anycast + BGP basics. — Applied: §8.8, §20, §3.1, P8
  • Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications — ch.5 (replication), ch.9 (consistency + consensus). Generation/version propagation, leader/epoch reasoning. — Applied: §8.12, §22.2, P10
  • Cricket Liu, DNS on Windows / DNS & BIND Cookbook (recipes) — practical GSLB/geo-DNS + split-horizon patterns; TTL and negative-caching recipes. — Applied: §8.7, §19, P7

5.2 Papers / Engineering References

  • Contavalli et al., "Client Subnet in DNS Queries" (RFC 7871, 2016) — the ECS specification: option format, SCOPE PREFIX-LENGTH, cache semantics, privacy. The normative basis for P6. — Applied: §8.6, §18, P6
  • Calder et al., "Mapping the Expansion of Google's Serving Infrastructure" (IMC 2013) — how a global serving infrastructure + EDNS-client-subnet maps clients to frontends; the latency-map intuition. — Applied: §8.10, §15, P3
  • Chen et al., "End-User Mapping: Next Generation Request Routing for CDNs" (SIGCOMM 2015, Akamai) — ECS-driven end-user mapping; why resolver-based mapping is inaccurate and ECS fixes it. The geo/latency-routing blueprint. — Applied: §8.6, §8.10, P6
  • Schlinker et al., "Engineering Egress with Edge Fabric" (SIGCOMM 2018, Facebook) — controlling egress/steering with BGP at scale; the announcement-control lineage for the anycast crate. — Applied: §8.8, §20, P8
  • Krishnan et al., "Moving Beyond End-to-End Path Information to Optimize CDN Performance" (IMC 2009) — latency/RUM-driven server selection. The latency-map pipeline. — Applied: §8.10, §15, P3
  • Das, Gupta, Motivala, "SWIM: Scalable Weakly-consistent Infection-style Process Group Membership" (DSN 2002) — the failure-detection + membership patterns reused for vantage consensus + propagation (#197). — Applied: §8.2, §14, P2
  • Mockapetris, "Domain Names — Implementation and Specification" (RFC 1035, 1987) — DNS wire format, RR encoding, message structure. The answer-serialization contract (with #162). — Applied: §8.11, §18, §11.8, P6
  • Damas, Graff, Vixie, "Extension Mechanisms for DNS (EDNS(0))" (RFC 6891, 2013) — the OPT pseudo-RR that carries ECS; UDP payload size, the EDNS framing P6 must produce. — Applied: §8.6, §18, P6
  • Andrews, "Negative Caching of DNS Queries (DNS NCACHE)" (RFC 2308, 1998) — SOA-minimum, negative-answer TTL; the negative-caching strategy (§8.7). — Applied: §8.7, §19, P7
  • Abley & Lindqvist, "Operation of Anycast Services" (RFC 4786, 2006) — anycast operational practice; announce/withdraw, catchment, the basis for the anycast control crate (with #355). — Applied: §8.8, §20, P8

5.3 Reference Implementations

  • AWS Route 53 — Traffic Flow / routing policies (docs + behavior) — failover, weighted, latency, geolocation, geoproximity, multivalue, nested traffic policies. Study the policy model; do not copy. Lesson: composable nested policies. Pitfall: proprietary internals unknown. — Applied: §8.4, §9 ADR-5, P4–P5
  • Cloudflare Load Balancing (docs + blog) — origin health monitoring, steering policies (off/random/dynamic-latency/geo/proximity), pool/origin model. Lesson: the pool→origin health + steering shape. — Applied: §8.1, §8.9, P4
  • PowerDNS dnsdist + geoipbackend / Lua policies — open-source geo-DNS + ECS handling in a real authoritative server. Read closely — the closest open analogue. Lesson: ECS scoping + geo-backend integration. Pitfall: tied to PowerDNS internals (we plug into #162 instead). — Applied: §8.6, §18, P6
  • gdnsd — an authoritative DNS server built for GSLB (geoip + multifo/weighted + health checks). Lesson: health-check + weighted/geo answer generation in one daemon. Pitfall: monolithic (we separate control/edge). — Applied: §8.2, §13, P1
  • CoreDNS (loadbalance, geoip, plugins) — pluggable DNS with steering plugins. Lesson: the plugin/answer-pipeline shape, ECS plumbing. — Applied: §18, §9 ADR-7, P6
  • NS1 / Akamai GTM (docs) — filter-chain steering (NS1) + liveness/load-feedback GTM. Lesson: the filter-chain composition model and load-feedback steering. — Applied: §8.4, §17, P5

Glossary · 39 terms · site-wide glossary

GSLB (Global Server Load Balancing)
steering each client to the best datacenter/PoP globally, by health/proximity/policy (the system being built).
Steering mechanism
the way a decision is delivered: DNS answer, anycast announce/withdraw, or HTTP 3xx redirect.
Control plane
gtrd: owns config, runs the decision engine, aggregates health, drives propagation.
Edge responder
gtr-edge: answers DNS/HTTP queries at a PoP from a propagated snapshot.
Prober / vantage point
gtr-probe: runs health checks from a network location; its verdict is one vote in consensus.
Endpoint
a steerable target (IP/CNAME + location + weight + health-check ref).
Pool
a set of endpoints under one policy + one health-check spec + a TTL.
PolicySet
a top-level, possibly nested, named routing policy (Route-53 "traffic-policy" analogue).
Routing policy
the rule selecting endpoints: failover, weighted, latency, geolocation, geoproximity, multi-value, or composite.
Failover policy
primary endpoint, then secondaries in order, by health.
Weighted policy
distribute clients among endpoints by weight (seeded-deterministic per client).
Latency policy
steer a client network to its lowest-latency healthy endpoint (via the latency map).
Geolocation policy
map a client's region (continent/country/subdivision) to a target; most-specific wins.
Geoproximity policy
pick the nearest endpoint by great-circle distance + a per-endpoint bias.
Multi-value answer (MV)
return up to N healthy endpoints; the resolver/client picks.
Composite / nested policy
a policy whose targets are themselves policies/pools (bounded depth).
HealthState
the consensus health of an endpoint: Healthy / Degraded / Unhealthy / Unknown.
Vantage consensus
fusing per-vantage verdicts into one HealthState via a quorum (so no single path flaps an endpoint).
Flap damping
a BGP-style penalty/decay that suppresses an oscillating endpoint (stability over reactivity).
Quorum
the ≥ ⌈(V+1)/2⌉ agreeing vantages required to set a HealthState.
EDNS Client Subnet (ECS)
an EDNS0 option carrying the client's subnet so geo/latency steering targets the client, not the resolver (RFC 7871).
SOURCE PREFIX-LENGTH
the ECS query field: how many client-address bits the resolver reveals.
SCOPE PREFIX-LENGTH
the ECS response field: the prefix granularity at which the answer is valid (the resolver cache key).
Latency map
a client_network → endpoint → latency_estimate table aggregated from RUM, powering latency routing.
RUM (Real User Measurement)
client-observed latency samples feeding the latency map.
Geoproximity bias
a per-endpoint distance offset that expands/shrinks its effective catchment region.
Prefix trie / LPM
the longest-prefix-match structure mapping a client subnet to a location (I-LPM).
TTL (Time To Live)
how long a resolver may cache an answer; the floor on DNS-steering reactivity (§2.7).
Negative caching
caching NXDOMAIN/NODATA per the SOA-minimum TTL (RFC 2308).
Anycast catchment
the set of clients a PoP serves under a shared anycast prefix (shifts on withdrawal).
Announce / withdraw
advertising or retracting an anycast prefix from a PoP to steer at routing speed.
BGP control sink
the abstraction receiving announce/withdraw intents (a real BGP-4 speaker is deferred, §22.2).
Generation
the monotonic version number stamped on a propagated ResolvedPlan (leader-advanced).
Staleness bound
the max generations / seconds an edge may lag the leader before it is flagged/quarantined (I-PROP).
RCU snapshot (Arc-swap)
the immutable, atomically-swapped edge state a query reads (lock-free, no torn read, I-SNAP).
Leader / epoch
the single control-plane writer per epoch; epoch fencing rejects a stale leader (I-LEADER, no split steering).
Failsafe
returning a last-known/all-configured answer (never empty) when health/data is missing (I-FAILSAFE).
DecisionTrace
the per-query record of why an endpoint was chosen (policy path, location, ECS scope, health, generation, TTL).
Split steering
the failure where some edges steer to a dead region while others steer away (the worst failure mode, principle 5).
