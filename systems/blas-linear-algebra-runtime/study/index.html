Hard questions

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does a naïve triple-loop GEMM run at ~3 % of peak while a blocked, packed one reaches ~90 %, and which single architectural quantity explains most of the gap? (§8.3 AI taxonomy; §8.4 five-loop model; Back-of-Envelope item 2; MM1)
  2. Given a CPU with 8 f64 SIMD lanes, 2 FMA ports, 2.5 GHz, 64 KB L1d, 512 KB L2, derive its f64 peak and a sound mr×nr, kc, mc. Show why your mr×nr does not spill. (§4.1; Back-of-Envelope items 1,3,4,5; P4)
  3. A GEMM result differs in its low bits between 1-thread and 8-thread runs. Is this a bug? What exactly does the runtime promise, and how would you make the two runs bit-identical and at what cost? (§3.3 numerical boundary; P8/P13; ADR-15/ADR-24; I4)
  4. Why is GEMV permanently bandwidth-bound while GEMM can be compute-bound, expressed in flops/byte, and what does this imply for which LAPACK steps are the bottleneck? (§8.3; Back-of-Envelope item 2; §8.7 panel factorization; MM8)
  5. The micro-kernel is the only unsafe code. State the exact precondition that makes it sound, where it is established, and how the test suite proves it holds for shapes that are not multiples of the block sizes. (§8.5; I2/I3; P6; Phase 3 §15.4–§15.5; Phase 4 §16.5)
  6. Walk a single cblas_dgemm call from a row-major C caller through to the micro-kernel. At which step is row-major folded to column-major, and why does that fold cost nothing? (§3.4 data-flow; §8.1 row/col identity; P2/ADR-6; Phase 11 §23)
  7. Why does partial-pivot LU record a growth factor while Cholesky does not need pivoting at all, and what pathological matrix forces LU's worst case? (§8.7; I6/I7; Failure Modes "Documented precedents" — Wilkinson; Phase 9 §21)
  8. Classical Gram-Schmidt and Householder both produce a QR factorization. Why does the blueprint forbid CGS, expressed as an orthogonality bound, and which invariant encodes the requirement? (§8.7; I8; §22.6 pitfalls; Failure Modes "Documented precedents")
  9. Which of the five GEMM loops do you parallelize for a wide-and-short C, which for a tall-and-thin C, and why is parallelizing the k-loop a correctness hazard rather than just a perf choice? (§8.4; ADR-11; I11; MM6; Phase 6 §18)
  10. The pack buffers are ~8.5 MB per thread regardless of matrix size. Derive that number and explain why it does not grow with N, and what design principle this realizes. (Back-of-Envelope item 7; P11; §11.5; §3.3)
  11. A SYRK should be roughly half the flops of the equivalent GEMM. Where do those flops go, how does the macro-kernel skip them, and what correctness check guarantees the result is still a valid triangle? (§8.3; Phase 8 §20.2; I9; §20.4)
  12. Why is β=0 a special case that must not read C, and what numerical hazard does skipping the read avoid? Trace it to the invariant and the fixture that tests it. (§8.5; I5; Phase 3 §15.5 microkernel_beta0_nan; §8.8)
  13. A user reports GEMM at 45 % of peak on a new CPU with the same code that hits 90 % elsewhere. Give the three most likely causes in order and the telemetry/diagnostic that distinguishes them. (Phase 5 §17.6/§17.9; Failure Modes "perf regression"/"register spill"; P12 KernelTrace; MM4)
  14. Why does the runtime derive block sizes from a probe instead of hardcoding them, and construct a concrete CPU on which a hardcoded mc from one machine collapses throughput on another. (§4.1; P5/ADR-9; MM4; Phase 5 §17.6)
  15. Subnormal inputs flood a GEMM and throughput drops ~100×. Why, what is the opt-in mitigation, and why is it not the default? (Failure Modes "Documented precedents" — denormals; ADR-29; §5.1 book 7; Phase 1 §13.8)
  16. LAPACK's speed is attributed to its "level-3 fraction." Define it, show it tends to 1 as n grows, and name the serial bottleneck that caps factorization scaling. (§8.7; P7/ADR-18; Back-of-Envelope item 10; MM8; Phase 9 §21.7)
  17. A C caller passes lda smaller than the number of rows. What must happen, at which boundary, and what is the consequence if validation were skipped? (§8.8; I12/P14; ADR-13; Threat Model "OOB read/write"; Phase 11 §23)
  18. The runtime promises a backward-error bound ≤ c·n·u·‖A‖‖B‖ for GEMM but not bit-reproducibility. Reconcile these two statements using the non-associativity of floating-point addition. (§3.3; P8; MM7; Higham §5.1 book 2; ADR-16)
  19. Packing costs O(mc·kc) traffic. Prove it is negligible for large blocks and explain the regime where it is not, naming the alternative path. (Back-of-Envelope item 8; MM3; ADR-7; Phase 12 §24.2 small-matrix path; ADR-8)
  20. Multi-thread GEMM scales to ~0.85× linear, not 1.0×. What physical resource is the ceiling, how is it derived, and which block constant is the lever against it? (Back-of-Envelope items 6,9; Scalability Analysis; MM6; Phase 6 §18.7)
  21. Why is the naïve oracle compiled only into test builds, what property makes it trustworthy, and which historical hardware bug justifies validating even the FP path against it? (P1/ADR-20; Phase 2 §14.9–§14.10; Failure Modes "Documented precedents" — Pentium FDIV)
  22. TRSM is called the LAPACK workhorse. Why, what makes its diagonal block inherently sequential, and what is the block-size tradeoff that balances sequential cost against GEMM offload? (§8.7; Phase 8 §20.2/§20.6; Phase 9 §21; MM8)
  23. Complex GEMM typically reaches about half of real peak. Explain the two contributing causes and the layout decision that drives one of them. (§8.2; ADR-12; Phase 7 §19.6–§19.7; Back-of-Envelope item 1)
  24. A determinism golden hash breaks after a change to kc. Is this a regression? How does the versioning policy treat it, and what would a real regression look like instead? (P8/P13; I4; ADR-30; Compatibility Guarantees; Phase 13 §25.6)
  25. You must port the runtime to a new ISA with 32 vector registers (NEON/SVE). Which parts change, which stay, and how does the mr×nr optimum shift? (§8.5; P6/ADR-4; MM2; Phase 12 §24.6; Back-of-Envelope item 4)

Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — GEMM is memory-bound until you block for cache; the kernel's job is to hit the FLOP/byte ridge of the roofline. Insight: a matrix multiply does 2N³ flops on 3N² data, so its arithmetic intensity is O(N) — but only if the data is reused; without blocking, every flop re-fetches operands from DRAM and you run at bandwidth, not at the FLOP ceiling. Predicts: a naïve GEMM sits on the bandwidth-bound (left) side of the roofline at ~3 % of peak; a blocked one walks right along the ridge to ~90 %. Bug class without it: "optimizing" the inner loop (unrolling, intrinsics) while leaving operands strided in DRAM — you fight for 2× and leave 15× on the table. Resolved in: §8.3 (AI taxonomy), §8.4 (five-loop model), Phase 5 (§17); Back-of-Envelope items 1–2.

MM2 — The micro-kernel is a register-residency machine; the C tile lives in registers, A and B stream past it. Insight: the only way to amortize a load is to multiply it into many accumulators before evicting them; the mr×nr C tile is read and written kc times from registers, so the kernel's flops/load ratio is exactly mr·nr / (mr+nr). Predicts: throughput is capped by the vector-register file — pick mr,nr so mr·nr/vlen + nr ≤ vregs or you spill and fall off a cliff. Bug class without it: choosing mr×nr for "more parallelism" past the register budget → spills → silent 2–3× slowdown that no correctness test catches. Resolved in: §8.5 (micro-kernel contract), P4, Phase 3 (§15); Back-of-Envelope item 4.

MM3 — Packing is a bandwidth investment that pays off in TLB and cache hits, not a copy you can skip. Insight: copying a strided operand panel into a contiguous, aligned, panel-ordered buffer costs O(mc·kc) traffic once, but every subsequent micro-kernel pass reads it at unit stride with no TLB misses and no conflict evictions — amortized over O(mc·kc·nc) flops. Predicts: for large blocks pack_ns/compute_ns < 10 %; for tiny k the pack dominates and you should skip blocking entirely. Bug class without it: a "clever" pack-free kernel that reads user strides directly → TLB thrash → 5–10× slowdown on large matrices. Resolved in: §8.4, ADR-7, P3, Phase 4 (§16); Back-of-Envelope item 3.

MM4 — The cache hierarchy dictates the three block sizes; mc,kc,nc are not tuning knobs but derived constants. Insight: each block is sized so an operand fits one cache level — Bp(kc×nc) in L3, Ap(mc×kc) in L2, the micro-panel in L1 — so reuse happens at the level that can serve it without falling through. Predicts: given (L1,L2,L3) sizes and the element width, the near-optimal blocks are arithmetic; a probe reads the caches and computes them. Bug class without it: hardcoding blocks tuned on one CPU → on a CPU with half the L2, Ap evicts Bp and throughput collapses with no code change. Resolved in: §4.1 (derivation), P5, ADR-9, Phase 5 (§17); Back-of-Envelope items 5–6.

MM5 — Layout is strides; transpose and row/col-major are free re-interpretations, not data moves. Insight: a matrix is a base pointer plus two strides; transposing swaps the strides and the extents, and a row-major op equals the column-major op of the transpose (RowMajor C=AB ≡ ColMajor Cᵀ=BᵀAᵀ). Predicts: one kernel path serves all 16 {layout × transA × transB} combinations; transpose is O(1), never O(N²). Bug class without it: materializing op(A) into a temporary to "make it contiguous" → an O(N²) copy and O(N²) extra memory the streaming design was built to avoid. Resolved in: §8.1, P2, ADR-6, Phase 0 (§12), Phase 2 (§14).

MM6 — Parallelize the loop that partitions the output, so threads never collide on C. Insight: splitting jc (or ic) gives each thread a disjoint block of C and its own pack buffers — a race-free cover by construction; splitting pc (the k-dimension) makes every thread accumulate into the same C and forces a reduction. Predicts: the right loop to split depends on shape — wide → jc, tall → ic, both small / large-k → split-k with reduction. Bug class without it: parallelizing pc without a reduction → concurrent += into shared C → nondeterministic wrong results that pass single-threaded tests. Resolved in: ADR-11, I11, P13, Phase 6 (§18); Smith 2014 (§5.2 paper 4).

MM7 — Numerical results are a contract of bounds, not of bits; the accumulation order is part of the answer. Insight: floating-point addition is not associative, so changing kc blocking or thread count changes the rounding and hence the low bits — but the backward error stays bounded by c·n·u·‖A‖‖B‖. Predicts: you can promise reproducibility for a fixed (shape, threads, ISA) (a fixed order) or a bound across all of them, but not both for free. Bug class without it: asserting bit-equality between the threaded kernel and the oracle → flaky test failures on every legitimate blocking change. Resolved in: §3.3 (numerical boundary), P8, P13, I4, ADR-15/ADR-24; Higham (§5.1 book 2).

MM8 — LAPACK is fast because it spends its flops in GEMM; the panel is the tax, the trailing update is the payoff. Insight: a blocked right-looking factorization does a small, memory-bound, partly-serial panel factorization, then pushes the bulk of the work into a large level-3 trailing update — the "level-3 fraction" approaches 1 as n grows. Predicts: factorization throughput tracks GEMM throughput times the level-3 fraction; the serial panel is the Amdahl ceiling, attacked by look-ahead. Bug class without it: an unblocked (level-2) factorization → memory-bound → 10× slower than the blocked one despite identical flop counts. Resolved in: §8.7, P7, ADR-18, Phase 9 (§21), Phase 10 (§22); LAPACK design (§5.2 paper 6).

MM9 — unsafe is a seam with a contract, not a region of hope; everything above the micro-kernel is checked. Insight: memory safety in a kernel runtime reduces to one provable claim — packing produces buffers of exactly the size and alignment the micro-kernel reads (I2/I3) — so the unsafe block is sound iff that contract holds. Predicts: you can confine auditing to ~3 files (pack + micro-kernel) and fuzz tiny shapes to exercise the seam exhaustively. Bug class without it: unsafe pointer arithmetic sprinkled through the driver → OOB that ASAN finds only on the one shape your fuzzer missed. Resolved in: §8.5, P6, I2, I3, Phase 3 (§15); the safe/unsafe boundary in §3.3.

MM10 — Validate untrusted arguments at the boundary; a numerical library is a memory-safety surface the moment it has a C ABI. Insight: lda, dims, increments, and pointers from a C/Fortran caller are untrusted integers that index into memory — a negative or oversized lda is an OOB read/write unless rejected before any compute (the xerbla discipline). Predicts: every ABI entry needs a validation prologue; a panic must never unwind into C (UB). Bug class without it: trusting lda ≥ nrows → a malicious or buggy caller drives the kernel out of bounds; or a Rust panic crossing FFI → undefined behavior. Resolved in: §8.8 (failure-path examples), P14, I12, ADR-13, Phase 11 (§23).

Required reading (§5)

5.1 Books

  • Golub & Van Loan, Matrix Computations (4e) — ch.1 (matrix mult & block algorithms), ch.3 (LU), ch.4 (Cholesky/SPD), ch.5 (QR/Householder). The semantics oracle for every factorization. Applied: §8.7 factorization state machines; Phase 9 (§21) LU/Cholesky; Phase 10 (§22) QR.
  • Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms (2e) — ch.2–3 (FP & summation), ch.9 (LU), ch.10 (Cholesky), ch.19 (QR). Source of every error bound we assert. Applied: §4 numerical constraints; the error-bound formulas in Phase 1 (§13.4), Phase 9 (§21.4), Phase 10 (§22.4); Testing Strategy error-bound tolerances.
  • Demmel, Applied Numerical Linear Algebra — conditioning, backward error, blocked factorization intuition. Applied: §6 P8 numerics contract; Phase 9 (§21) growth-factor/conditioning; Failure Modes "numerical instability".
  • Hennessy & Patterson, Computer Architecture (6e) — ch.2 (memory hierarchy), App.B/C (caches, pipelining). Applied: §4.1 peak-FLOPS/block-size derivation; Phase 5 (§17) cache blocking; Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model.
  • Agner Fog, Optimizing subroutines in assembly + instruction tables — FMA latency/throughput, port pressure, the micro-kernel budget. Applied: Phase 3 (§15) micro-kernel register/port budget; docs/performance/microkernel-budget.md.
  • Drepper, What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory — cache, TLB, prefetch, NUMA. Applied: Phase 4 (§16) packing & prefetch; Phase 5 (§17) TLB/huge-page pitfalls; Scalability Analysis NUMA.
  • Intel/AMD optimization manuals — AVX2/AVX-512 latencies, alignment, store-forwarding. Applied: simd crate intrinsics (§10); Phase 3 (§15) and Phase 12 (§24) ISA kernels.
  • Trefethen & Bau, Numerical Linear Algebra — Householder QR, conditioning lectures; clear derivations. Applied: §8.7 QR state machine; Phase 10 (§22) Householder/WY reflectors.

5.2 Papers

  • Goto & van de Geijn, "Anatomy of High-Performance Matrix Multiplication" (ACM TOMS 2008) — the GEMM blueprint: packing + the loop ordering + register/cache blocking. Foundational. Applied: §8.4 five-loop model; ADR-7; Phase 4 (§16) packing; Phase 5 (§17) blocking; Mental Models MM2/MM4.
  • Van Zee & van de Geijn, "BLIS: A Framework for Rapidly Instantiating BLAS Functionality" (TOMS 2015) — the micro-kernel abstraction; mr,nr,mc,kc,nc and how all L3 ops reduce to one kernel. Applied: §8.5 micro-kernel contract; ADR-8; Phase 3 (§15); Phase 8 (§20) derived ops; Comparative Architecture Table (BLIS row).
  • Van Zee et al., "The BLIS Framework: Experiments in Portability" (TOMS 2016) — instantiating kernels across ISAs; informs AVX-512/NEON gating. Applied: ADR-4; Phase 12 (§24) AVX-512/NEON kernels; Deferred Features Register.
  • Smith et al., "Anatomy of High-Performance Many-Threaded Matrix Multiplication" (IPDPS 2014) — which loops to parallelize; the 5-loop parallelization map. Applied: ADR-11; Phase 6 (§18) loop-partition choice; Mental Models MM6.
  • Whaley & Dongarra, "Automatically Tuned Linear Algebra Software (ATLAS)" (SC 1998) — auto-tuning block sizes; motivates our probe. Applied: ADR-9; Phase 5 (§17) block-size derivation; Alternative Architectures (autotuned codegen).
  • Anderson et al., LAPACK Users' Guide (3e, SIAM 1999) / LAPACK Working Notes — blocked right-looking factorization, the level-3 fraction. Applied: ADR-18; §8.7; Phase 9 (§21) and Phase 10 (§22) blocked factorizations.
  • Demmel et al., "Error bounds from extra-precise iterative refinement" (TOMS 2006) — refinement to recover accuracy. Applied: Phase 10 (§22.2) iterative refinement; Failure Modes "numerical instability".
  • Higham, "The accuracy of floating point summation" (SISC 1993) — dot/nrm2 summation order. Applied: ADR-16/ADR-17; Phase 1 (§13) multi-accumulator dot and scaled nrm2; I4.
  • Williams, Waterman, Patterson, "Roofline: an insightful visual performance model" (CACM 2009) — the perf model the bench harness embodies. Applied: §8.3 arithmetic-intensity table; ADR-22; Phase 13 (§25) roofline; Performance Engineering.
  • Kågström, Ling, Van Loan, "GEMM-based level-3 BLAS" (TOMS 1998) — deriving SYMM/SYRK/TRMM/TRSM from GEMM. Applied: §8.3 L3 taxonomy; Phase 8 (§20) derived ops.

5.3 Reference Implementations

  • BLIS (kernels/haswell/3/, frame/3/gemm/) — micro-kernel + the 5 loops in clean form. Study; do not copy. Lesson: the macro/micro split. Pitfall to avoid: their CPP-macro kernel-gen — we write explicit Rust. Applied: §8.4–§8.5; Phase 3 (§15) micro-kernel; Phase 4 (§16) macro-kernel; Comparative Architecture Table.
  • OpenBLAS (kernel/x86_64/, interface/) — assembly micro-kernels, driver. Lesson: real-world ISA tuning. Pitfall: hand-asm is unreadable; we use intrinsics. Applied: Phase 12 (§24) perf hardening; LA_NUM_THREADS/OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS compat (Phase 11 §23.10); Comparative Architecture Table.
  • Reference BLAS/LAPACK (Netlib, Fortran) — the semantics oracle. Read dgemm.f, dgetrf.f, dpotrf.f, dgeqrf.f. Pitfall: it is the slow reference, not a perf model. Applied: ADR-20; Phase 2 (§14) oracle; the conformance suite (Phase 13 §25); Comparative Architecture Table (reference Netlib row).
  • Eigen (Core/products/GeneralMatrixMatrix.h) — C++ blocking. Lesson: another blocking taxonomy. Pitfall: expression templates hide the loops. Applied: Phase 5 (§17) blocking comparison; Comparative Architecture Table (Eigen row).
  • libxsmm — small-matrix JIT kernels. Lesson: small-m,n,k matters; informs our batched/small path. Applied: ADR-8 "when JIT wins"; Phase 12 (§24.2) small-matrix path; Deferred Features Register (batched-GEMM); Alternative Architectures (runtime JIT).
  • CBLAS reference header (cblas.h) — exact enum values & signatures for our ABI. Applied: §11.3 ABI-stable enums; Phase 11 (§23) CBLAS ABI; ADR-14.

Glossary

AI (arithmetic intensity) flops per byte of memory traffic; the roofline x-axis. axpy y:=αx+y. Backward error smallest input perturbation making the computed result exact. BLAS levels 1 vector / 2 matrix-vector / 3 matrix-matrix. Block reflector (WY) compact form of a product of Householder reflectors for level-3 QR update. mc/kc/nc cache-blocking sizes (A-rows / k-slice / B-cols). mr×nr register-blocking micro-tile of C. CBLAS C interface to BLAS. Cholesky A=LLᵀ for SPD A. Condition number κ(A) sensitivity of the solution to perturbations. Conformance validation against reference semantics. Differential testing compare against a trusted oracle. FMA fused multiply-add, single rounding. GEMM general matrix-multiply C:=αop(A)op(B)+βC. GEMV matrix-vector multiply. Growth factor (ρ) max element growth during LU; governs stability. Householder reflector H=I−τvvᵀ, orthogonal. info LAPACK status (0 ok, <0 bad arg, >0 singular/indefinite). ISA dispatch runtime selection of the widest available instruction set. lda/leading dimension stride between columns (col-major). Level-3 fraction share of factorization flops done in GEMM. LU PA=LU with partial pivoting. Macro-kernel loop over register tiles within a packed block. Micro-kernel the innermost ISA-specific register-blocked routine. MatView stride-based matrix descriptor. nrm2 Euclidean norm (scaled to avoid overflow). Oracle the naïve O(n³) reference implementation. Packing copying operand panels into contiguous aligned buffers. Peak FLOPS 2·vlen·FMA_throughput·clock·cores. Pivoting row interchange for stability. QR A=QR, Q orthogonal. Roofline perf-ceiling model (bandwidth vs FLOP). SPD symmetric positive-definite. STREAM memory-bandwidth benchmark. SYRK symmetric rank-k update. TRSM triangular solve with multiple RHS. τ (tau) Householder scalar. u (unit roundoff) 2⁻²⁴ (f32) / 2⁻⁵³ (f64). xerbla BLAS error handler for bad parameters. YMM/ZMM 256-/512-bit vector registers. DAZ/FTZ denormals-are-zero / flush-to-zero CPU modes that trade accuracy near zero for avoiding the subnormal stall (ADR-29). TLB translation lookaside buffer; packing keeps the hot operand in few pages to avoid TLB misses. xLASSQ the scaled sum-of-squares recurrence used by nrm2/asum to avoid over/underflow. Right-looking factorization that updates the entire trailing submatrix after each panel (maximizes the level-3 fraction; ADR-18). Level-3 fraction lever the panel width kb that trades serial panel cost against GEMM-offloaded trailing work. Roofline ridge point the AI at which a kernel transitions from bandwidth-bound to compute-bound (peak/bandwidth). SAFMIN smallest float whose reciprocal does not overflow; bounds safe division in triangular solves. Differential oracle the trusted naïve reference every kernel is diffed against (P1/ADR-20). Split-k parallelizing the k-loop with a final partial-sum reduction (ADR-11; wins for small m,n, large k).
