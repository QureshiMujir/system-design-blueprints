Hard questions (§34)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does the AES-256 key schedule need a SubWord-only branch that AES-128 does not, and what breaks if it is omitted? (§14, §31 MM3)
  2. Why is ShiftRows applied before SubBytes inside AES-NI's accelerated round instruction, yet the two operation orders produce bit-identical ciphertext to FIPS 197's textbook order? (§16, §8)
  3. Why is the bitsliced scalar path the default even though it is roughly 3× slower per block than table-driven AES? (§6 P2, §23 T1)
  4. Why does GCM restrict nonces to exactly 12 bytes when SP 800-38D permits variable lengths? (§6 P7, §8.6)
  5. Under what conditions does GCM-SIV's ~2× performance cost become the right tradeoff over plain GCM? (§9 ADR-08, §19)
  6. Why must GHASH's length block encode bit-lengths rather than byte-lengths, and what silent failure results from getting this wrong? (§18, §24 FM-9)
  7. Why is tag comparison required to be constant-time even though the tag itself is not secret? (§6 P9, §23 T3)
  8. Why does the design refuse to expose a chained ECB mode while still exposing encrypt_block as a public primitive? (§6 P11, §9 ADR-10)
  9. What is the mathematical relationship between GHASH and POLYVAL, and why does GCM-SIV need a different one? (§8.4, §19.2)
  10. Why does the CTR counter overflow specifically at 2³²−1 blocks, and what is the maximum safe plaintext length under one (nonce, iv) pair? (§17, §4, §33 item 4)
  11. Why is KW's integrity check a fixed constant recovered after six passes, and how does its failure mode differ diagnostically from a GCM tag failure? (§20, §24 FM-1 vs FM-4)
  12. Why does the threat model treat fault-injection/DFA attacks as out of scope while treating cache-timing as squarely in scope? (§23 T1 vs T9)
  13. Why is VAES's 2× throughput advantage sometimes the wrong choice in a shared-tenant cloud environment? (§9 ADR-05, §23 T12)
  14. What number of blocks must a batch contain before AES-NI's key-schedule setup cost is amortized below a 20% overhead, and why does this justify the 8-block batching principle? (§33 item 5, §6 P6)
  15. Why does the design defer FIPS 140-3 boundary code rather than build it into Phase 0? (§9 ADR-18, §36)
  16. How does bitslicing transform 8 blocks of state so that SubBytes becomes a fixed Boolean circuit, and why does this eliminate the cache-timing channel structurally rather than by obfuscation? (§8, §13)
  17. Why must the conformance report distinguish a Deferred bucket from a Fail bucket, and what breaks if the two are conflated? (§27, §36)
  18. Why does reusing a GCM nonce under one key break confidentiality of every message under that key, not merely the two ciphertexts that happened to collide? (§8.7, §23 T2)

31. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — "The state is a matrix, not a stream." Predicts: ShiftRows/MixColumns only make sense column-wise/row-wise, never as flat-byte operations. Bug class without it: byte-ordering confusion (interpreting the 16-byte state as a flat array instead of the column-major 4×4 matrix of §8.1). Applies in §8.
  • MM2 — "A round is a fixed-point-free permutation composed Nr times, with one truncated final round." Predicts: 10/12/14 rounds differ only in schedule length and round count, not round shape (except the final round drops MixColumns). Bug class without it: applying MixColumns to the final round, producing a plausible-looking but wrong ciphertext. Applies in §8, §15.
  • MM3 — "The key schedule is a linear-feedback recurrence over words, not bytes." Predicts why AES-256 needs an extra SubWord-only branch AES-128 does not (§8.3). Bug class without it: a truncated Rcon table or a missing i mod 8 = 4 branch. Applies in §14.
  • MM4 — "Constant-time is a property of the algorithm, not the language." Predicts: a bitsliced implementation is equally constant-time in C or Rust; a table-driven implementation leaks in either. Bug class without it: trusting Rust's memory safety to imply timing safety. Applies in §6 (P2), §22.
  • MM5 — "An AEAD is a MAC wrapped around a cipher, not a cipher with an add-on." Predicts why GCM/CCM/GCM-SIV all decompose into (encrypt, authenticate) despite interleaving differently. Bug class without it: returning plaintext before tag verification completes (a return-early bug). Applies in §8, §18.
  • MM6 — "A nonce is a coordinate, not a secret." Predicts: nonces may be sent in the clear; what must never happen is reuse under the same key. Bug class without it: encrypting the nonce or treating it as needing confidentiality. Applies in §6 (P7), §23 (T2, T10).
  • MM7 — "Hardware acceleration is a backend swap, never a semantic change." Predicts: AES-NI's internal ShiftRows-then-SubBytes order differs from FIPS 197's textbook order yet is provably equivalent (SubBytes is per-byte, so it commutes with the ShiftRows permutation). Bug class without it: "optimizing" by skipping the equivalence check between backends. Applies in §16, I8.
  • MM8 — "The key schedule is the expensive one-time setup; the bulk cipher is the cheap, parallel part." Predicts: batching amortizes the round-key derivation cost. Bug class without it: re-deriving the key schedule per block instead of per key. Applies in §6 (P6), §29 (M14).

5. Required Reading

Books

  • Daemen, J. & Rijmen, V. — The Design of Rijndael (Springer, 2002). Chapters 2–4: GF(2⁸) arithmetic, the wide-trail strategy, branch-number/non-linearity design rationale. Applied in §8 (Domain Model), §13 (Phase 1 GF/S-box).
  • Paar, C. & Pelzl, J. — Understanding Cryptography (Springer, 2010). Chapter 4, "The Advanced Encryption Standard (AES)" — a from-scratch derivation of every round operation. Applied in §13–§15 (Phases 1–3).
  • Ferguson, N., Schneier, B. & Kohno, T. — Cryptography Engineering (Wiley, 2010). Chapters 4 (block ciphers) and 5 (block cipher modes) — the engineering case for AEAD over bare CBC. Applied in §18–§20 (Phases 6–8), §23 (Threat Model).
  • Katz, J. & Lindell, Y. — Introduction to Modern Cryptography (CRC Press, 3rd ed.). Chapter on pseudorandom permutations and block-cipher-mode security definitions (IND-CPA, IND-CCA). Applied in §8 (Domain Model), §27 (Testing & Verification Strategy).
  • Menezes, A., van Oorschot, P. & Vanstone, S. — Handbook of Applied Cryptography (CRC Press, 1996). Chapter 2 (finite-field/mathematical background) for GF(2⁸)/GF(2¹²⁸) arithmetic; Chapter 7 (block ciphers) for the Feistel/SPN design-theory groundwork AES's SPN structure builds on. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), §8 (Domain Model).
  • Aumasson, J-P. — Serious Cryptography (No Starch Press, 2017). Chapters on block ciphers and authenticated encryption — the practitioner's framing of AEAD misuse resistance. Applied in §19 (Phase 7 GCM-SIV), §23 (Threat Model).

Papers

  • Bernstein, D. J. — Cache-timing attacks on AES (2005). The paper that demolished table-driven AES. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), §23 (T1).
  • Osvik, D. A., Shamir, A. & Tromer, E. — Cache Attacks and Countermeasures: the Case of AES, CT-RSA 2006. L1-cache architecture and attack detail. Applied in §23 (T1).
  • Bonneau, J. & Mironov, I. — Cache-Collision Timing Attacks Against AES, CHES 2006. The "even non-malicious co-tenants leak" result. Applied in §23 (T1, T12).
  • Käsper, E. & Schwabe, P. — Faster and Timing-Attack Resistant AES-GCM, CHES 2009. The bitsliced-AES + carryless-multiply GHASH technique this project's default scalar path implements. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), §31 (MM4).
  • Gueron, S. — Intel® Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) New Instructions Set, Intel White Paper, rev. 3.01 (2012). The AES-NI intrinsic semantics. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Joux, A. — Authentication Failures in NIST version of GCM, public comment to NIST (2006). The original "do not reuse a GCM nonce" analysis. Applied in §18 (Phase 6), §23 (T2).
  • Böck, H., Zauner, A., Devlin, S., Somorovsky, J. & Jovanovic, P. — Nonce-Disrespecting Adversaries: Practical Forgery Attacks on GCM in TLS, WOOT 2016. Nonce-reuse forgeries observed in the wild. Applied in §23 (T2), §35 (rejection rationale for variable-length nonces).
  • Ferguson, N. — Authentication weaknesses in GCM, public comment to NIST (2005). The tag-truncation-considered-harmful analysis. Applied in §6 (P9), §23 (T3).
  • Gueron, S. & Lindell, Y. — GCM-SIV: Full Nonce Misuse-Resistant Authenticated Encryption at Under One Cycle per Byte, CCS 2015. The GCM-SIV construction. Applied in §19 (Phase 7).
  • Boyar, J. & Peralta, R. — A new combinational logic minimization technique with applications to cryptology, SEA 2010. The minimized Boolean-circuit S-box this project's bitsliced path implements. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • **Reparaz, O., Balasch, J. & Verbauwhede, I. — Dude, is my code constant time?, DATE 2017.** The dudect statistical methodology. Applied in §22 (Phase 10).
  • **Vaudenay, S. — Security Flaws Induced by CBC Padding — Applications to SSL, IPSEC, WTLS..., EUROCRYPT 2002.** The padding-oracle attack class. Applied in §20 (Phase 8), §23 (T4).
  • Saarinen, M-J. O. — Cycling Attacks on GCM, GHASH and Other Polynomial MACs and Hashes, FSE 2012. The reason GHASH keys are derived fresh per session rather than reused across keys. Applied in §18 (Phase 6), §23 (T10).
  • Hamburg, M. — Accelerating AES with Vector Permute Instructions, CHES 2009. The vector-permute (vpaes) alternative to bitslicing. Applied in §35 (Alternative Architecture AA2).
  • Nikova, S., Rechberger, C. & Rijmen, V. — Threshold Implementations Against Side-Channel Attacks and Glitches, ICICS 2006. The masked-circuit technique this project deliberately does not implement. Applied in §35 (Alternative Architecture AA4), §36 (Deferred Features).

Reference Implementations

  • RustCrypto aes crate (github.com/RustCrypto/block-ciphers, aes/). Study the ni/soft/armv8 backend split. Lesson: backend selection as a compile-time-and-runtime hybrid. Pitfall not to copy: do not vendor — this project is clean-room (ADR-15). Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • BoringSSL (crypto/fipsmodule/aes/aes_nohw.c). Study the constant-time bitsliced reference. Lesson: a "no hardware" (nohw) name signals the constant-time-by-construction intent this project mirrors (P2). Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • OpenSSL (crypto/aes/aes_core.c, crypto/modes/gcm128.c). Study the historical table-driven core and the GHASH/GCM state-machine shape. Pitfall not to copy: the table-driven core is exactly the cache-timing liability Bernstein 2005 documents — reference only, never the default. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), §18 (Phase 6).
  • mbedTLS (library/aes.c, library/gcm.c). Study the no_std/embedded-friendly structuring. Lesson: a mode layer that stays allocation-free even under memory-constrained targets. Applied in §12 (Phase 0), §20 (Phase 8).
  • Linux kernel crypto subsystem (crypto/aes_generic.c, arch/x86/crypto/aesni-intel_glue.c). Study the runtime backend-priority/static_call dispatch pattern. Pitfall not to copy: the generic C table-driven path has the field's longest cache-timing CVE history — reference for dispatch architecture only. Applied in §16 (Phase 4), §21 (Phase 9).

Glossary (§37) · 48 terms · site-wide glossary

AES
Advanced Encryption Standard, FIPS 197's name for the Rijndael cipher restricted to a 128-bit block.
AAD
Additional Authenticated Data; authenticated but not encrypted.
AEAD
Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data.
AES-NI
x86_64 hardware instructions accelerating AES rounds.
Backend
the selected implementation strategy (Aesni/Vaes/Neon/ Soft, §11.4).
Bitslicing
representing N blocks' same-position bits packed into one machine word, enabling branch-free per-bit logic.
Block
the fixed 128-bit unit AES operates on.
Block cipher
a keyed, fixed-size permutation.
CAVP/ACVP
NIST's Cryptographic Algorithm Validation Program / Automated CAVP.
CBC
Cipher Block Chaining mode.
CCM
Counter with CBC-MAC, an AEAD mode (SP 800-38C).
Constant-time
an execution whose timing does not depend on secret data.
CTR
Counter mode.
dudect
a statistical constant-time test methodology (Reparaz et al. 2017).
ECB
Electronic Codebook mode (no chaining; exposed here only as a one-shot block primitive).
Equivalent inverse cipher
an AES decryption formulation reusing the encrypt round's structural shape (FIPS 197 section 5.3.5).
FIPS 197
the NIST standard defining AES.
GCM
Galois/Counter Mode, an AEAD (SP 800-38D).
GCM-SIV
nonce-misuse-resistant AEAD variant of GCM (RFC 8452).
GF(2⁸)
the finite field AES's byte arithmetic operates in.
GHASH
the GF(2¹²⁸)-based universal hash underlying GCM's authentication.
IV
Initialization Vector.
J0
GCM's derived initial counter block.
KAT
Known-Answer Test.
KEK
Key-Encryption Key (the key that wraps another key in KW/KWP).
Key schedule
the process deriving per-round keys from the master key.
KW
Key Wrap (RFC 3394).
KWP
Key Wrap with Padding (RFC 5649).
MixColumns
the AES round step mixing bytes within a column via GF(2⁸) matrix multiplication.
Nk
key length in 32-bit words.
Nonce
a value used once per key; not secret (MM6).
Nr
number of AES rounds (10/12/14).
NEON crypto extension
ARMv8-A instructions accelerating AES rounds.
Padding oracle
an attack exploiting distinguishable padding-validity error responses.
PCLMULQDQ
x86_64 carryless-multiplication instruction used to accelerate GHASH.
PMULL
the aarch64 equivalent of PCLMULQDQ.
POLYVAL
GCM-SIV's byte-reversed GHASH variant.
RCON
the round-constant table used in key expansion.
Rijndael
the cipher family AES selects a subset of.
Round key
the per-round key material XORed in via AddRoundKey.
S-box
the fixed byte-substitution table (SubBytes).
ShiftRows
the AES round step cyclically permuting state rows.
Side channel
an information leak via a non-primary channel (timing, cache, power).
SubBytes
the AES round step applying the S-box to every byte.
Tag
the fixed-length authentication value an AEAD produces.
VAES
Vector AES, an AVX-512 extension processing multiple blocks per instruction.
Wycheproof
Google's cryptographic test-vector corpus, including adversarial negative cases.
Zeroize
overwriting memory holding secret data before deallocation. ---
