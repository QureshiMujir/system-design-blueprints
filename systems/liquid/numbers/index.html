Provenance write success rate ≥ 99.99% Below this, the audit substrate cannot answer a regulator query with confidence (§26).
Compiler build time (single function, cold) ≤ 2 s Keeps liquidc check usable in a pre-commit hook.
LIR round-trip (serialize→deserialize) byte-identical, 100% of fixtures I1; any drift is an automatic compiler regression.
Network RTT assumption (adapter ↔ provider) 5 ms (same-region) – 150 ms (cross-region probe) Bounds the latency-feed's own measurement noise floor.
Availability budget (scheduler decision path) 99.9% (43m/month error budget) Scheduler degrades to last-known-good Plan rather than halting (I5, FM-9).
RTO for a poisoned/quarantined adapter ≤ 5 min (canary-probe cadence) Bounds blast radius of T1/T3 (§22).

The one defining property: the scheduler's routing decision is a pure, replayable function of logged inputs (I4) — every other constraint above exists to keep that property cheap enough to hold on every single invocation.

32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Derived from §4's System Constraints table; every number below shows its formula.

  1. Scheduler decision throughput per core. p50 decision latency 1 ms (§4) → 1,000 decisions/sec/core (single-threaded worst case); with Go's goroutine concurrency and the feed-cache hit path dominating, effective throughput ≈ 1,000 × (cache-hit ratio, assumed 0.95 given the 60 s TTL / 10 s poll cadence) ≈ 950 decisions/sec/core sustained.
  2. FD budget per adapter connection. 4,096 FD ceiling (§4) ÷ 2 FDs/adapter connection ÷ 3 default adapters ≈ 682 pooled connections per adapter before headroom exhaustion — informs the connection-pool sizing default in scheduler/internal/feeds/.
  3. Provenance ingestion rate at 10,000 concurrent invocations. 10,000 concurrent (§4) × assumed 50 ms average invocation duration → 200,000 Invocation rows/sec peak write rate into ClickHouse — sizes the ClickHouse ingestion-buffer capacity (ADR-06).
  4. LIR module storage footprint per 1,000 functions. 64 KiB/module ceiling (§4) × 1,000 → 64 MiB total LIR storage for 1,000 registered functions — trivially cacheable in the scheduler's in-memory Plan index.
  5. Adapter poll fan-out at PAC = 10. 10 adapters (Phase 3 target, §16.3) × 1 poll/10 s (§4) → 1 feed-poll request/sec aggregate — negligible network load even before the TTL cache.
  6. Cross-region probe cost bound. 150 ms worst-case RTT (§4) amortized over the 10 s poll interval → probe overhead is 1.5% of the poll cycle's wall-clock budget, never on the invocation-time critical path (I11's cache is what makes this arithmetic hold).
  7. Residency-receipt signature-verification overhead. Ed25519 verification ≈ 50–100 µs (well-documented cryptographic benchmark figure) → at 950 decisions/sec/core (item 1), adds ≈ 5–10% to decision latency in the worst case — still within the 8 ms p99 ceiling (§4) with over 100× headroom.
  8. Memory footprint at max concurrency. 256 MiB RSS ceiling (§4) ÷ 10,000 concurrent invocations (§4) ≈ 25.6 KiB per in-flight invocation's scheduler-side state — a tight but achievable budget given Go's per-goroutine stack starts at 2 KiB and grows on demand.
  9. Cost Model Service scrape volume. 10 adapters (item 5) × 2 independent sources per provider (T3 mitigation, §22) × 1 scrape/60 s (billing-meter granularity, §4) ≈ 0.33 scrapes/sec aggregate — a trivially small background load justifying a single-process Cost Model Service through at least Phase 7.
  10. Canary-probe overhead at PAC = 10. 1 probe per adapter per 5 min RTO cadence (§4) × 10 adapters ÷ 300 s ≈ 0.033 probe-invocations/sec aggregate — under 0.01% of any tenant's real traffic at even modest production volume (hundreds of invocations/sec).
