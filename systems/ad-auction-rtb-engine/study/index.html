Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an RTB-engine expert holds. Each predicts behavior, names the bug class you write without it, and points to where this blueprint applies it.

MM1 — The auction is a deadline, not a computation. Insight: the system's job is to return on time, and the auction is whatever you can compute before the clock runs out — bids are an optional input. Predicts: p99 is dominated by bidder wait, not compute (§4: p50/p95/p99 = 12/60/95 ms vs compute p99 < 5 ms). Bug without it: you optimize scoring while a single slow bidder blows the whole budget. Applied: Phase 5 (gate.rs, DeadlineClock), DP2, I10.

MM2 — A truthful payment is a threshold, not a price you pick. Insight: in a DSIC mechanism the winner's payment is the minimum bid at which it would still have won — it is determined by the others, not chosen. Predicts: if you compute the second-price payment from the runner-up's score without dividing by the winner's pCTR·quality, you break both IR and truthfulness simultaneously. Bug without it: mispriced "second price" that fails the property test. Applied: Phase 1 (second_price.rs), DP5, I1/I2; the threshold = Myerson's lemma payment (B3).

MM3 — Score ranks, money is computed separately, and never the twain in the same units. Insight: score = bid×pCTR×q is a ranking key in abstract units; a payment is a price in money units; converting between them always means dividing by that bidder's pCTR·q. Predicts: VCG externalities and GSP slot prices are in score units until divided back. Bug without it: you charge a score as if it were money (DP13 violation), corrupting billing and pacing feedback. Applied: Phase 2/Phase 3 (gsp.rs, vcg.rs), DP13, §11 Value/Price/Score types.

MM4 — Pacing is a price on your own budget, set by feedback. Insight: the multiplier μ is the opportunity cost of spending a dollar now; you bid in units of value − μ·price, i.e. shade by 1/(1+μ), and μ rises exactly when you spend too fast. Predicts: under sustained over-demand μ climbs until spend rate → B/T; mis-tuned gain → oscillation. Bug without it: you treat pacing as a hard gate and either overspend in bursts or stall. Applied: Phase 6 (dual.rs), DP8, I7; theory P6/P7/P8, B4/B5.

MM5 — A probabilistic counter that errs one way is safe; one that errs both ways is a liability. Insight: a Count-Min Sketch only ever over-estimates, so a frequency cap built on it is at worst slightly conservative — never under-enforced. Predicts: the breach bound is one-sided (§4: ≤ 0.1% over cap, conservative); over-suppression is the failure you tune against, not under-counting. Bug without it: you pick a sketch with two-sided error and show users too many ads (a policy breach). Applied: Phase 7 (cms.rs), DP9, I8; theory P12, B8.

MM6 — You only ever see your own wins, so naive statistics are biased. Insight: win/loss data is censored — you never observe competitors' bids — so fitting a win-rate model on won auctions alone systematically misestimates. Predicts: a model that ignores censoring over-predicts win rate at high bids and corrupts suggested_bid. Bug without it: over-bidding → overspend, blamed wrongly on pacing. Applied: Phase 8 (winrate.rs), I12; theory P9.

MM7 — Determinism is a precondition for both replay and testing, and floats/maps quietly destroy it. Insight: a pure mechanism core that is a function of (bids, mechanism, reserves, seed) can be replayed bit-exactly and property-tested; any f64, HashMap iteration, or wall-clock read silently breaks this. Predicts: a replay digest mismatch always traces to an un-recorded or nondeterministic input. Bug without it: irreproducible billing disputes you cannot debug. Applied: Phase 0/Phase 11 (result.rs::digest, prng.rs), DP1, I6; A05/A20/A30.

MM8 — Count money on confirmation, not on the auction. Insight: a win is a claim; the charge is real only when the billing notice confirms, so spend/cap must move on confirmation and unconfirmed wins must be reserved-then-released. Predicts: counting at auction time over-counts (the exchange may not confirm) and double-counting reservation + confirm over-charges the controller. Bug without it: phantom overspend or leaked reservations. Applied: Phase 6/Phase 9 (inflight.rs, notice.rs), I11, DP12.

MM9 — Hedge the tail, never the body. Insight: a backup request cuts the tail of a fan-out, but hedging every call doubles load; you hedge only past p95. Predicts: blanket hedging at 100K QPS (§4) is self-inflicted DOS; targeted hedging cuts p99 with bounded extra load. Bug without it: a "latency fix" that collapses throughput. Applied: Phase 5 (hedge.rs), P11 Tail-at-Scale; §4 fan-out degree 10–50.

MM10 — The hot path must not allocate, lock, or fsync. Insight: GC-free does not mean stall-free — an allocator storm, a contended mutex, or an inline fsync each produce tail latency just as bad as a GC pause. Predicts: per-bid Box, a global counter Mutex, or fsync on the auction task each show up as a p99 cliff under load. Bug without it: you blame the language for a tail you built. Applied: Phase 12 (bumpalo arena, sharded atomics, MPSC journal enqueue), DP14, A21/A22; §4 memory ceiling 8 GB.

Glossary · 51 terms · site-wide glossary

RTB
Real-Time Bidding: per-impression programmatic auction conducted in ~100 ms
OpenRTB
IAB protocol for RTB; defines BidRequest/BidResponse and notices
BidRequest
The ad-call describing an impression opportunity sent to bidders
BidResponse
A bidder's reply containing bids (seatbid)
Imp(ression)
A single ad slot opportunity within a BidRequest
DSP
Demand-Side Platform: a bidder buying impressions on behalf of advertisers
SSP / Exchange
Supply-Side Platform / exchange selling impressions; sends ad calls
tmax
Maximum time budget (ms) for the whole ad call; engine must respond within
First-Price
Auction where the winner pays its own bid; not truthful
Second-Price / Vickrey
Winner pays the second-highest (effective) bid; truthful (DSIC)
GSP
Generalized Second-Price: multi-slot, pay min-bid-to-hold-position; not truthful
VCG
Vickrey-Clarke-Groves: multi-slot, pay the externality imposed on others; truthful
DSIC
Dominant-Strategy Incentive Compatible: truthful bidding is optimal regardless of others
IR
Individual Rationality: a winner never pays more than its bid; utility ≥ 0
Revenue Equivalence
Under IPV/risk-neutral/symmetric, all standard auctions yield equal expected revenue
Myerson Optimal Auction
Revenue-optimal mechanism: second-price with reserve where virtual value = 0
Virtual Valuation φ(v)
v − (1−F(v))/f(v); optimal reserve r* solves φ(r*)=0
Reserve / Floor
Minimum price; hard (reject below) or soft (first-price below, second-price above)
Publisher / Exchange Floor
Floors set by publisher vs exchange; effective = max
eCPM
Effective cost per mille = bid × pCTR × 1000; expected value per 1000 impressions
pCTR
Predicted click-through rate; calibrated probability a shown ad is clicked
Score
Ranking key = bid × pCTR × quality
Quality Score
Per-advertiser/creative quality multiplier in the score
Clearing Price
The price the winner actually pays (settlement price)
Budget Pacing
Spending a campaign budget B smoothly over horizon T
Spend Curve S*(t)
Target cumulative spend over time (even pacing: B·t/T)
Pacing Multiplier μ
Lagrange multiplier on the budget constraint; shade bid/(1+μ)
Bid Shading
Lowering a bid below value to control spend (pacing) or strategy (first-price)
PID Controller
Proportional-Integral-Derivative feedback controller for spend tracking
Probabilistic Throttling
Entering the auction with probability p_t to control spend rate
Frequency Cap
Max impressions per (user, campaign) per time window
Count-Min Sketch (CMS)
Probabilistic counter with one-sided over-estimate; used for caps
HyperLogLog (HLL)
Probabilistic distinct-count (reach) estimator
Bloom Filter
Probabilistic set membership (e.g. "already capped")
Sliding Window
Time window that advances continuously for cap counting
Fan-out
Sending a BidRequest concurrently to many bidders
Hedged Request
A backup request to a second endpoint to cut tail latency
Circuit Breaker
Short-circuits calls to a failing bidder until it recovers
Partial Result
Running the auction over only the bids that arrived before the deadline
Late-Drop
Discarding bids that arrive after the collection deadline (I5)
NBR
No-Bid Reason code in a BidResponse
nurl / burl / lurl
Win / billing / loss notice URLs the exchange calls
Auction Journal
Append-only, billing-reconcilable log of every auction and notice
Reconciliation
Settling journaled charges exactly-once against the ledger
Censored Data
Win/loss observations without seeing competitors' bids; basis of win-rate models
Bid Landscape
Distribution `P(win
Micros
Integer money unit = 1e-6 currency; no float (DP4)
Determinism
Identical (bids, mechanism, reserves, seed) ⇒ identical AuctionResult (I6)
IPV
Independent Private Values: bidders' values are i.i.d. and privately known
Calibration
Adjusting predicted probabilities so they match observed frequencies (I12)
SLO / RTO
Service Level Objective / Recovery Time Objective
