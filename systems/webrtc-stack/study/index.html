Hard questions (§33)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does regular nomination (§16.2) wait for check-list settlement instead of nominating the first Succeeded pair, and what would break if it didn't? (§16.6, I9)
  2. Trace the exact byte transformation of one audio frame from rtc-codec-opus output to the wire — name every header prepended and every encryption applied. (§8.3, §8.4)
  3. Why is the DTLS exporter label a bigger single point of failure than the AES key size, given both are "just cryptographic parameters"? (§8.3, §18.6)
  4. Derive the SFU's 500-connection ceiling from first principles — which single number in §4 is the binding constraint, and why is it CPU rather than memory? (§4, §32 CM5)
  5. Why does GCC take the min() of two independent estimators (I6) rather than averaging them or trusting the more recent one? (§8.4, §20.2, Pa1)
  6. What specifically breaks if the jitter buffer's depth cap (§4) were removed and it were allowed to grow unbounded under sustained jitter? (§19.6, MM3)
  7. Why does SCTP ride inside the same DTLS connection as media (ADR-12) rather than a second, independent DTLS connection? (§3, §21.2, MM8)
  8. Compare the failure semantics of an SRTP auth-tag failure (I3) against a DTLS fingerprint mismatch (I8) — why does one drop silently and the other tear down the whole connection? (§18.9, §17.2, P5)
  9. Why is ICE-Lite (§14, §15) not simply "ICE with fewer candidates," and what production topology makes that distinction load-bearing? (§29, §31)
  10. Trace what happens to an in-flight DataChannel message if the underlying SCTP association aborts mid-send — is it retried, lost, or surfaced as an error, and where is that decided? (§21.8, I7, FM-7)
  11. Why does the Comparative Architecture Table (§31) show mediasoup rejecting P2P entirely rather than just deprioritizing it — what constraint makes that the right call for an SFU but wrong for this design? (§31, §1)
  12. Using §4's constraints and §32's formulas, derive the additional TURN-server capacity needed if the relayed-connection fraction doubled from 20% to 40%. (§4, §32 CM6)
  13. Why does the threat model (§22) treat a forged TWCC feedback message (T8) as a congestion-control problem rather than an authentication problem, given SRTCP already authenticates RTCP? (§22, §20.2)
  14. What is the concrete regime in which ADR-09's rejected alternative (REMB-only) is actually the correct choice, and how would you detect at runtime that you're in it? (ADR-09, §31)
  15. Why does I12's requirement that PeerConnection pass through Failed before Closed matter operationally, given the application arguably only cares that the call ended? (§21.6, FM-10, §24)
  16. Derive, from CM5 and M11, the point at which adding video transcoding (currently out of scope, §1) would break the 500-connection SFU ceiling — what CPU multiplier would transcoding introduce? (§32 CM5, §29)
  17. Why is the replay window (I3) a fixed-size bitmask rather than a growable set, and what attack does the fixed size specifically close off that a growable set would not? (§7, §22 T4, P7)
  18. Contrast the alternative architecture of wrapping libwebrtc via FFI (§34) against this design's from-scratch approach — under what deadline pressure does the FFI path actually produce a more correct system faster? (§34, ADR-01, ADR-13)

30. Mental Models & First Principles

  1. MM1 — ICE is a distributed consensus on one fact. Both agents must agree, without a coordinator, on which single (local,remote) pair is usable. Predicts: nomination must wait for check-list settlement, not race to the first success. Bug class without it: premature pair selection on a low-priority pair (§16.6). Applies at §16.
  2. MM2 — DTLS-SRTP is a key-agreement protocol wearing a media-transport costume. The handshake's only real job is producing symmetric keys; everything else (record framing) is TLS's usual job repurposed for datagrams. Predicts: the exporter label (§8.3) is the single most fragile string in the whole stack. Bug class without it: "SRTP just doesn't work" debugged as an RTP bug instead of a key-derivation bug. Applies at §18.
  3. MM3 — The jitter buffer is a queueing-delay budget, not a storage buffer. Growing it doesn't "fix" jitter; it trades network-jitter latency for buffer-induced latency (Pa4/Pa5's bufferbloat lesson). Predicts why depth is capped at 300 ms even under sustained jitter (§4). Bug class without it: unbounded depth growth chasing an unreachable "zero loss" goal. Applies at §19.
  4. MM4 — GCC is a control loop, not a formula. It has poles, gain, and stability margins like any feedback controller; treating its output as a one-shot calculation guarantees oscillation under real network noise. Predicts the tuning tradeoff in §20.6. Bug class without it: bitrate "flapping" visible as periodic quality dips. Applies at §20.
  5. MM5 — Multiplexed streams need an explicit fairness policy. SCTP streams share one association the way threads share a CPU; without an explicit policy one stream can starve another. Predicts why DataChannel message-size limits (I7) exist independent of association-level flow control. Bug class without it: a large unreliable stream starving a small reliable one. Applies at §21.
  6. MM6 — NAT traversal is probabilistic, not deterministic. No client-side algorithm guarantees P2P connectivity through an arbitrary NAT; TURN is mandatory infrastructure, not a rare fallback. Predicts the ~80% P2P failure rate without traversal cited in the domain literature. Bug class without it: shipping without a TURN deployment plan and discovering it in production. Applies at §15, §1.
  7. MM7 — Every layer boundary is a trust boundary until proven otherwise. Decrypted does not mean authenticated; parsed does not mean valid. Predicts where P5's fail-closed checks must live (every layer transition in §3). Bug class without it: trusting DTLS-decrypted-but-not-yet-Finished-verified data (I2). Applies at §8.6.
  8. MM8 — Reliability and congestion-safety are orthogonal axes. SCTP's reliability modes (§8.5) and DTLS's congestion behavior are independent knobs, not one dial; conflating them is why naive "just make media reliable" proposals break real-time latency. Predicts ADR-12's SCTP-over-DTLS choice over a simpler always-reliable transport. Bug class without it: adding TCP-like reliability to the media path and reintroducing head-of-line blocking. Applies at §21, ADR-12.

Reference Implementations

  • libwebrtc (webrtc.googlesource.com/src) — study p2p/base/p2p_transport_channel.cc (ICE state machine) and modules/congestion_controller/ (GCC). Lesson: strict layering between transport and media. Pitfall not to copy: the monolithic build graph makes it nearly impossible to embed only the ICE agent. Applied in §16, §20.
  • Pion (github.com/pion/webrtc) — study icetransport.go and sctp/. Lesson: small composable packages per RFC. Pitfall: Go GC pauses surface as p99 jitter-buffer latency spikes under load. Applied in §9 ADR-01, §19.
  • str0m (github.com/algesten/str0m) — study its sans-IO core. Lesson: a pure Rust state machine with no socket ownership is independently fuzzable and testable. Pitfall: the caller must correctly drive the event loop, a foot-gun for new integrators. Applied in §3.1, §9 ADR-03.
  • aiortc (github.com/aiortc/aiortc) — study its rtcdtlstransport.py and rtcsctptransport.py. Lesson: a from-scratch SCTP implementation scoped to the WebRTC subset is tractable in a few thousand lines. Pitfall: pure-Python asyncio ties DTLS timers to the event loop, causing retransmission jitter under load. Applied in §21.
  • mediasoup (github.com/versatica/mediasoup) — study its ICE-Lite + SFU-only focus. Lesson: server-side WebRTC has a fundamentally different candidate-gathering story than P2P (§1, §29). Pitfall: assuming SFU learnings transfer unchanged to P2P client code. Applied in §14, §29.
  • coturn (github.com/coturn/coturn) — the reference TURN server; we implement only the TURN client (ADR-14) and validate against coturn in the interop conformance bucket. Applied in §15.

JavaScript Session Establishment Protocol (RFC 8829); SDP negotiation model.
