34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Minor-GC pause (survivor-proportional, P1): pause_minor = survivors * copy_cost_per_object. At 5,000 survivors and 40 ns/object copy cost (sequential write, cache-friendly per §30): pause_minor = 5,000 * 40ns = 200 microseconds — well under the 1 ms target (M1), and critically independent of nursery size (a 4x larger nursery with the same survivor count yields the same 200 microseconds, the direct demonstration of P1).

  2. Minor-GC frequency: frequency = alloc_rate / nursery_size. At 1.5 GB/s allocation rate (M6) and a 64 MB nursery: frequency = 1.5e9 / 64e6 = ~23 minor collections/second.

  3. Write-barrier throughput tax: overhead_pct = (pointer_stores_per_sec * barrier_cycles) / cpu_frequency_hz. At 200 million pointer stores/sec, 5 cycles/store (M4, card-mark), 3 GHz CPU: overhead_pct = (2e8 * 5) / 3e9 = ~33% of one core's cycle budget — meaning card-mark barrier cost alone justifies keeping the fast path at ≤5 cycles (M4's target is load-bearing, not arbitrary).

  4. Concurrent-mark race constraint (§8.6, I5): mark_rate >= headroom * (alloc_rate / avg_obj_size). At headroom 1.3, 1.5 GB/s allocation, 64-byte average object size: minimum required mark_rate = 1.3 * (1.5e9 / 64) = ~30.5 million objects/sec — compared against M7's 2 GB/s graph-traversal target (at 64-byte objects, ~31 million objects/sec), showing the design sits right at its margin at this allocation rate, which is exactly why the pacing controller (Phase 7) exists as an active control loop rather than a fire-and-forget assumption.

  5. Remembered-set/card-table size: card table at 1 byte per 512-byte card over a 32 GB old generation: card_bytes = 32e9 / 512 = 62.5 MB (~0.2% of heap, matching §4's ≤8% RS-overhead ceiling with wide margin — precise RS would instead scale with old→young edge count, not old-gen size).

  6. Promotion rate and old-gen fill time: at a 5% promotion rate (§4) of a 1.5 GB/s allocation rate: promotion_bytes_per_sec = 0.05 * 1.5e9 = 75 MB/s; a 32 GB old gen with 50% headroom before major-GC trigger (16 GB fillable) fills in 16e9 / 75e6 = ~213 seconds between major cycles under steady-state promotion.

  7. Concurrent-mark duration for that same old gen: at M7's 2 GB/s traversal rate and a 16 GB live set at trigger time: mark_duration = 16e9 / 2e9 = 8 seconds wall-clock, comfortably inside the 213-second inter-cycle interval from step 6 — confirming I5's margin holds at steady state (the same inequality as step 4, now checked at the cycle-duration timescale rather than the instantaneous-rate timescale).

  8. Pause-time-vs-heap-size relationship motivating regionalization: STW-compact worst case (M12) scales roughly linearly with old-gen live-set size at a fixed per-object relocation cost; at ~40 ns/object (survivor-copy-equivalent cost) and a 64 GB old gen at 80% live occupancy (~51.2 GB live, ~800 million 64-byte objects): pause_compact = 8e8 * 40ns = 32 seconds — far beyond M12's 200 ms ceiling, which is exactly why full-heap STW compaction is reserved for rare, worst-case fragmentation events (§8.5 step 5) rather than routine operation, and why region-based/colored-pointer designs (§31, §33, §36) exist for heaps where this arithmetic stops being acceptable at all.

  9. Minimum concurrent-mark worker count (§8.6's w_min equation): reusing step 4's numbers (headroom 1.3, 1.5 GB/s allocation, 64-byte objects, required mark_rate ≈ 30.5 million objects/sec) against a measured per_worker_rate of 8 million objects/sec (a single marker thread's sustained traversal rate under realistic cache-miss conditions, lower than the aggregate M7 target because M7 already assumes multiple workers): w_min = ceil(30.5e6 / 8e6) = 4 marker threads — a concrete, checkable output of the pacing controller's control loop (Phase 7) rather than an arbitrary default worker-pool size.

  10. Read-barrier throughput tax in concurrent-compaction mode (Phase 10, optional): analogous to step 3's write-barrier tax but applied to loads rather than stores, since concurrent-compaction mode's read barrier fires on every mutator load, not just every store. At 800 million pointer loads/sec (typically higher than store rate — most workloads read more than they write), 8 cycles/load (a representative colored-pointer/Brooks-pointer check cost, cheaper per-op than the SATB store barrier but far higher aggregate frequency), 3 GHz CPU: overhead_pct = (8e8 * 8) / 3e9 = ~213% — i.e. more than two full cores' worth of cycle budget, illustrating numerically why ADR-11 treats this as the single most expensive opt-in in the system and reserves it for deployments that have already exhausted STW-compaction's acceptable range (per step 8).
