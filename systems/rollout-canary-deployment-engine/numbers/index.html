Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Every number derives from the §4 System Constraints table; formula shown. These are the figures an expert computes before committing to the design.

  1. Controller working-set RAM (steady state). concurrent_rollouts × mem_per_rollout = 1000 × 2 MB = 2 GB. The §4 target of ≥1000 rollouts at <2 MB each sets the controller's baseline heap; node sizing starts here.
  2. Event-log memory ceiling (all rollouts). concurrent_rollouts × event_ring_cap × bytes_per_event. With ~512 B per CloudEvent JSON record: 1000 × 10 000 × 512 B ≈ 5.1 GB if every ring is full — i.e. the event log, not the rollout state, dominates memory, justifying the 10 000-entry ring cap and async batch writes (Scalability Analysis).
  3. Reconcile throughput ceiling (single worker). 1 / p99_reconcile_latency = 1 / 50 ms = 20 reconciles/s worst-case per serialized worker. To converge 1000 rollouts each needing ~6 reconciles, a single worker takes 1000 × 6 / 20 = 300 s — which is why §4's bounded-concurrency workqueue (semaphore ~200) fans reconciles out rather than serializing them.
  4. Steady-state reconcile rate (resync). With a default resync near the 15 s leader TTL: concurrent_rollouts / resync_interval = 1000 / 15 s ≈ 67 reconciles/s baseline load — comfortably under the 20/s-per-worker ceiling once fanned across ~4 workers (67 / 20 ≈ 3.4).
  5. Prometheus query fan-out at peak. concurrent_rollouts × metrics_per_run = 1000 × 3 = 3000 concurrent queries if every rollout analyses at once; the §4 analysis path therefore caps in-flight queries with a semaphore (max_concurrent_queries ≈ 100), bounding provider pressure to 100 / 0.2 s = 500 queries/s at a 200 ms budget.
  6. Analysis scheduling precision. §4 requires analysis jitter <1 s against a measurement interval. For a 60 s interval that is 1 / 60 ≈ 1.7% allowed drift per tick — only achievable with absolute sleep_until, not relative sleeps (Phase 8 — canary-analysis).
  7. Webhook dispatcher saturation. webhook_timeout × max_in_flight bounds tail exposure: at a 10 s per-hook timeout and a fan-out semaphore of 20, a wave of failing hooks costs at most 10 s wall time per batch of 20, so 1000 pending notifications drain in (1000 / 20) × 10 s = 500 s worst case — hence dispatch must be non-blocking to reconcile (§6 P15-adjacent, Phase 11).
  8. Leader renewal overhead. Renew at TTL / 2 = 15 s / 2 = every 7.5 s ⇒ 1 / 7.5 s ≈ 0.13 store writes/s for the lease — negligible against reconcile write volume, confirming store-CAS election (ADR-07) is essentially free.
  9. Failover blast-radius window. Worst-case time with no single writer = one full leader_TTL = 15 s (§4). During this window weights are frozen, not wrong (MM1), so the user-visible impact is delayed convergence, not traffic loss.
  10. Binary + image footprint. canaryd <30 MB stripped (§4); on a distroless cc base (~25 MB) the deployable image is ≈ 55 MB, keeping cold-start pulls fast for the <500 ms startup target in Performance Engineering.
  11. Revision-history storage per rollout. revisionHistoryLimit × rs_record_size. At the default limit 10 and ~4 KB per serialized RS record: 10 × 4 KB = 40 KB/rollout, or 40 MB across 1000 rollouts — trivial, which is why GC targets correctness (no dangling refs, §7 I6) over space.
  12. Watch fan-out memory. watch_subscribers × per_sub_buffer. With a 1024-slot broadcast channel and ~512 B per buffered WatchEvent: 1024 × 512 B = 512 KB per hot rollout's channel — bounded, with lagged subscribers pruned rather than blocking (Scalability Analysis).
