Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Uplink bytes per client per round (uncompressed). Start: model size m = 10⁶ params (§4.1 mid-range), 32-bit fixed-point quantization (4 bytes/param). Raw delta: 10⁶ × 4 = 4 MiB/client. With top-k=10% sparsification: 0.1 × 4 MiB + 4-byte indices ≈ 0.8 MiB/client. Design consequence: chunking threshold at 4 MiB per §4.1 is exactly one uncompressed delta; compression is mandatory at the upper model range (m = 10⁷ → 40 MiB uncompressed).

  2. Total uplink bytes per round (cohort). Start: cohort = 100 (cross-device, §4.1), uplink per client = 4 MiB. Total uplink: 100 × 4 MiB = 400 MiB/round. At T=200 rounds: 80 GiB total uplink traffic. Design consequence: subsampling rate q = 100/10⁶ = 10⁻⁴ is the primary cost lever — doubling the cohort doubles the bill linearly.

  3. SecAgg share volume per client per round. Start: cohort = 100 (§4.1), each client shares two 32-byte secrets (b_u, s_u^SK) to all other n−1 = 99 peers via Shamir. Each share ≈ 64 bytes (32-byte point + 32-byte value). Total shares per client: 2 × 99 × 64 = 12,672 bytes ≈ 12.4 KiB. Over cohort: 100 × 12.4 KiB = 1.24 MiB of share traffic. Design consequence: share overhead is ~0.3% of uncompressed model traffic for m = 10⁶; it grows linearly with cohort, not with model size — the Bell-2020 log-degree graph (deferred Phase 13) targets this term at m_c ≥ 10⁴.

  4. Server pairwise bookkeeping memory. Start: cohort = 100, model m = 10⁶, 4 bytes/param, per §4.1 server memory = O(m + cohort²). Model store: 10⁶ × 4 = 4 MiB. Pairwise share store: cohort² × 64 bytes = 100² × 64 = 640 KiB. Total: ≈ 4.6 MiB. At cohort = 1000: pairwise store = 10⁶ × 64 = 64 MiB — still manageable but grows quadratically. Design consequence: O(cohort²) is the SecAgg-specific ceiling flagged in Scalability Analysis; cohort = 10⁴ → 6.4 GB of share state.

  5. PRG mask expansion cost. Start: mask expansion target ≥ 10⁷ params/s/core (§4.1), cohort = 100 clients, model m = 10⁶. Each client must PRG-expand cohort − 1 = 99 pairwise masks: 99 × 10⁶ = 9.9 × 10⁷ params of PRG output per round per client. At 10⁷ params/s/core: 9.9 s/core/client. Vectorized AES-NI can reach ≥ 10⁸ params/s; at that rate: ~1 s/client. Design consequence: the PRG expansion dominates client CPU in SecAgg rounds; the deferred Rust/C PRG extension (Phase 12) targets the 10× speedup needed at m = 10⁷.

  6. Noise multiplier σ for a target ε=3, δ=10⁻⁵ over T=100 rounds. Start: cohort rate q = 100/10⁶ = 10⁻⁴ (§4.1 cross-device pool N=10⁶, cohort=100). Using the subsampled-Gaussian RDP bound (Mironov–Talwar–Zhang 2019), subsampling amplification reduces the effective RDP by ≈ q = 10⁻⁴. A rough calibration (the calibrate_sigma binary search per Phase 9): σ ≈ 1.1 achieves ε ≈ 3 at these parameters where without subsampling the same T and σ would exhaust ε ≈ 300. Design consequence: subsampling amplification is the difference between a practical and an impractical DP campaign; q is the first hyperparameter to tune.

  7. Ring size R to avoid sum overflow. Start: cohort = 100 (§4.1), 32-bit quantization, max per-parameter absolute value after quantization = 2³¹ − 1 ≈ 2.1 × 10⁹ (§4.1 masking ring). True sum maximum: cohort × max_quantized = 100 × 2.1 × 10⁹ = 2.1 × 10¹¹. R = 2³² = 4.3 × 10⁹ — too small! Required: R > 2.1 × 10¹¹ → R = 2⁶⁴ for cohort = 100 at 32-bit quant, OR reduce per-parameter quantization range by clipping updates harder. Design consequence: the §4.1 note "R = 2³² default (or 2⁶⁴)" is load-bearing: cross-silo cohorts at 32-bit quantization mandate R = 2⁶⁴ unless the clip bound B is tightened.

  8. Round latency floor (WAN). Start: SecAgg = 4 sequential rounds (§4.1), each round requires one server↔all-clients RTT. WAN RTT assumption (cross-device, explicit in §4.1 round deadline 60–600 s): assume 100 ms RTT per hop. Latency floor = 4 × 100 ms + serialization/crypto overhead ≈ 400 ms minimum per FL round; with chunking overhead for m = 10⁶ at 4 MiB: chunking adds ≈ 1 × 4 MiB / (10 Mbps uplink) = 3.2 s. Total floor per round: ~4–5 s LAN, 10–600 s WAN. Design consequence: 200-round campaign takes 33–120 min minimum ignoring training time; deadline tuning is the primary latency lever (§4.1 configurable deadline).

  9. Privacy budget burn rate per round. Start: σ = 1.1 (from item 6), q = 10⁻⁴, T = 100 rounds. Gaussian RDP per round ≈ α/(2σ²) = α/2.42 at order α; with subsampling the effective per-round charge is ≈ q²·α/(2σ²) (leading term). Over 100 rounds: total RDP ≈ 100 × 10⁻⁸ × α/2.42. After conversion to (ε,δ), the ledger charges roughly ε/T = 3/100 = 0.03 ε-units/round. Design consequence: the server's pre-round gate (I13) checks the ledger with a one-round look-ahead; running "just 10 more rounds" at the same σ costs ≈ 0.3 additional ε — non-trivial if the target ε is 3.

  10. Cross-device vs cross-silo cohort sizing. Start: cross-silo cohort = 10–50, cross-device cohort = 50–10⁴ (§4.1), pool N = 10⁶. Cross-silo: with cohort = 20, t = 10, dropout tolerance = 9 (up to 9 clients may drop). Cross-device: with cohort = 200, t = 100, dropout tolerance = 99. Comm ratio: cross-device round delivers 200/20 = 10× more gradient signal but generates 200² / 20² = 100× more pairwise share traffic at the server. Design consequence: the cross-device regime is where the O(cohort²) ceiling bites first and the Bell-2020 log-degree graph (deferred) is most urgent.
