Decisions (§9) · 24 ADRs

ADR-01

Core language

Chosen

TypeScript 5.x everywhere

Rejected

Go, Rust, Python

When the rejected choice is right

A hot-path packet-level crypto engine owned by a dedicated team — Rust wins

Why: Single-language monorepo lets a small team move across web/services without context switch; structural typing catches schema drift at compile time

ADR-02

Monorepo tooling

Chosen

pnpm workspaces + Turborepo

Rejected

Polyrepo + Bazel

When the rejected choice is right

Teams > ~50 engineers with independent release cadences — polyrepo + Bazel wins

Why: Shared schema/types must stay byte-identical across web, services, conformance suite; Turborepo remote cache keeps CI fast

ADR-03

Dependency policy

Chosen

Pinned lockfile + Changesets-gated upgrades + weekly Dependabot/Snyk scan

Rejected

Always-latest floating deps

When the rejected choice is right

A throwaway prototype where velocity dominates reproducibility risk — floating deps win

Why: A 100-year-horizon codebase cannot tolerate supply-chain surprises

ADR-04

Metadata store

Chosen

Postgres 16 (Neon Phase 1–8, RDS Phase 9)

Rejected

DynamoDB, FoundationDB

When the rejected choice is right

Write throughput must exceed single-primary Postgres by 10×+ with simple key-value access — DynamoDB wins

Why: Relational integrity for schedule/identity/audit joins; pgvector co-location for AI Echo embeddings

ADR-05

Ciphertext object storage

Chosen

AWS S3 + KMS, multi-region, Glacier mirror

Rejected

Self-hosted MinIO, IPFS

When the rejected choice is right

Data-residency law forbids any US-hyperscaler dependency — sovereign self-hosted MinIO wins

Why: 11-nines-class durability is a vendor commitment the team cannot self-certify at this scale

ADR-06

Serialization

Chosen

JSON with explicit schemaVersion

Rejected

Protobuf, Avro

When the rejected choice is right

Wire volume reaches millions of events/sec and binary compactness dominates cost — Protobuf wins

Why: Human-inspectable audit trails matter more than wire-size at messages/second (not millions/second) scale

ADR-07

Internal RPC

Chosen

tRPC (internal), REST (institutional)

Rejected

GraphQL

When the rejected choice is right

Third-party consumers need flexible partial-field queries across many resource types — GraphQL wins

Why: End-to-end TS inference eliminates contract-drift bugs; REST is lowest-friction for partners' existing tooling

ADR-08

Runtime

Chosen

Node.js 22 LTS

Deno, Bun

When the rejected choice is right

Cold-start latency or single-binary edge deployment is the dominant constraint — Bun/Deno wins

ADR-21

Generational key escrow

Chosen

When the rejected choice is right

Shard-holders must jointly compute over the secret without reconstructing it (high-frequency threshold signing) — MPC wins

ADR-22

Canonical clock

Chosen

TAI-rooted monotonic clock, explicit leap-second table

When the rejected choice is right

Sub-second wall-clock display precision to humans matters more than century-scale monotonicity — UTC display (converted from TAI at render) wins

Backpressure policy

Bounded BullMQ concurrency + queue-depth-triggered slow-dispatch (P12)

Unbounded worker autoscaling

When the rejected choice is right

The bottleneck is definitively stateless compute, not a downstream dependency — autoscaling wins

Why: Autoscaling masks the true bottleneck (Identity Service/KMS); bounded concurrency surfaces it as measurable jitter

34. Alternative Architectures (Roads Not Taken)

Alt 1 — Decentralized ledger / smart-contract time-lock as primary durability + scheduling.

[sender] → [encrypt off-chain] → [Arweave/IPFS ciphertext]
                                        │
                              [on-chain time-lock contract]
                                        │
                          (unlocks at block-height/timestamp)
                                        ▼
                                  [recipient claims]

Regime where it wins: fully trustless multi-party scenarios with no single operator anyone trusts (e.g. dead-man's-switch inheritance among mutually distrusting parties — embodied by real decentralized dead-man's-switch protocols on Ethereum, and by Arweave's permanent-storage model). Rejected: on-chain data is public or requires a heavy off-chain encryption bridge for confidentiality; low throughput and gas cost are unsuited to consumer compose volume (§4); regulatory ambiguity conflicts with the fiduciary/estate integration Phase 9 requires (§21).

Alt 2 — Fully client-side, local-first architecture with no server-held ciphertext.

[device A] ⇄ CRDT sync ⇄ [device B] ⇄ CRDT sync ⇄ [device C]
   (no central server ever holds ciphertext)

Regime where it wins: maximal-privacy personal note-taking where the user's own device availability across decades is an acceptable assumption — the real "local-first software" lineage (Kleppmann et al. 2019, §5). Rejected: a device-only architecture cannot self-trigger a delivery at an exact future date without a central wake mechanism (the sender's device may be off, lost, or the sender deceased) — defeats I3/I11 by construction; it also removes the server-mediated trust anchor the identity ladder (§15/§20) depends on.

Alt 3 — Single-tenant monolith without service separation ("majestic monolith").

[one process] → [one Postgres DB] → [one deploy target]

Regime where it wins: pre-product-market-fit stage with very low message volume and no compliance/crypto-isolation requirement — the real, publicly documented pattern behind many early-stage Rails/Basecamp-style companies. Rejected: Echoes' crypto-isolation (I1) and AI Echo tenant-isolation (I8) requirements need Vault Service and the Echo Trainer to be structurally separate trust/sandbox boundaries — collapsing them into one process would violate P4/P9 by construction, not merely by convention.

Alt 4 — Durable-execution workflow engine (Temporal.io) instead of BullMQ+Redis.

[workflow: escrow-reconstruction] → [activity: contact custodian 1..5]
                                   → [activity: verify quorum]
                                   → [activity: unwrap key]

Regime where it wins: complex, long-running, deeply branching workflows with compensation logic across many services — real system: Temporal.io. The Phase 9 escrow-reconstruction and multi-decade key-rotation sequences are plausible Temporal workflows. Rejected at Phase 1–8 scale: BullMQ+Redis is operationally simpler for the current team size (§9 ADR-09); Temporal's own persistence store and worker-versioning discipline are justified only once Phase 9's generational workflows grow complex enough — flagged as a re-evaluation trigger (§35).
