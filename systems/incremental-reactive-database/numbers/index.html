Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Numbers an expert computes before designing, each derived from §4.1. Assume an Update triple ≈ 32 B on the wire/log (data key + time + diff + framing).

  • C1 — Input ingest bandwidth. 500 K updates/s × 32 B = 16 MB/s sustained per view (§4.1 throughput). A single NVMe and one core absorb this; CPU consolidation, not I/O, is the limit.
  • C2 — WAL write bandwidth. 16 MB/s × 1.5 (amplification, §4.1) = 24 MB/s to the WAL; with group-commit fsync this is well inside NVMe write budgets and bounds the p99 20 ms latency during fsync.
  • C3 — Arrangement memory, pre-compaction. A 16 GB raw arrangement (§4.1 tested ceiling) × 3 (all time versions before compaction) = up to 48 GB transient; compaction must keep pace or the box OOMs — motivates logarithmic merge (MM6, Phase 6).
  • C4 — Arrangement memory, steady state. 16 GB × 1.1 = 17.6 GB after compaction (§4.1); the 0.1 overhead is the index/antichain bookkeeping.
  • C5 — Checkpoint size & recovery I/O. Checkpoint = 1.2 × 17.6 GB ≈ 21 GB; to recover a 10 GB arrangement in < 60 s (§4.1) requires ≥ 170 MB/s restore throughput — feasible on NVMe, not on spinning disk.
  • C6 — File-descriptor budget. 128 base + 2 × 1000 changefeeds + 4 × WAL/checkpoint files ≈ 2 150 FDs at max subscribers (§4.1); requires ulimit -n ≥ 4096.
  • C7 — Join cost. At 100 K delta-pairs/s (§4.1) and ~10 matches/probe average, each side's arrangement is probed 100 K × 10 = 1 M lookups/s; the L2-resident cursor (≤ 256 KB working set, §4.1) keeps this cache-friendly.
  • C8 — Changefeed fan-out. 1 000 subscribers × notification ≤ 2 ms after frontier advance (§4.1): a single waker sweep over 1 000 wakers must complete inside the 2 ms budget ⇒ ≤ 2 µs/waker — argues for batched wake, not per-subscriber syscalls.
  • C9 — Multi-view memory. 100 active views (§4.1), even at a modest 256 MB arrangement each, = 25.6 GB resident; large arrangements force fewer concurrent views or eviction (deferred partial state).
  • C10 — Reduce throughput vs input. Reduce sustains 200 K key-deltas/s (§4.1); against 500 K input updates/s, a reduce-heavy plan is the bottleneck at 0.4× the ingest rate — plan accordingly.
  • C11 — Recovery replay tail. WAL decode ≈ 100 K rows/s (§4.1 glossary): a 1 M-row WAL tail replays in ≈ 10 s, matching the stated recovery target.
  • C12 — Frontier antichain size. For a single-input linear chain the frontier is a single time (antichain size 1); for an n-way join it is bounded by the product of input frontiers — kept small (≤ a few elements) by advancing inputs in lock-step (MM3, MM10).
