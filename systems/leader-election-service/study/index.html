30. Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — Leadership is a lease, not a crown. Insight: a leader holds time-bounded, renewable authority, never permanent ownership. Predicts: every leader must renew and every consumer must tolerate a leader vanishing. Bug without it: a zombie ex-leader keeps writing after a partition. Resolved in §17 (Phase 5), P7.
  • MM2 — A fencing token turns an unsafe lock into a safe one. Insight: attach a monotonically increasing epoch to leadership and check it at every downstream mutation (Kleppmann DDIA Ch 8, Chubby sequencers §5). Predicts: without it, a delayed request from an old leader corrupts state. Bug without it: dual-writer corruption. Resolved in §17, I2.
  • MM3 — FLP means you buy liveness with timeouts and randomness. Insight: with async + one crash, no protocol is both safe and live (FLP 1985); Raft keeps safety always and adds randomized timeouts for probabilistic liveness. Predicts: deterministic timeouts → perpetual split votes. Bug without it: livelocked elections. Resolved in §15 (Phase 3), P1.
  • MM4 — Quorum intersection is the whole safety argument. Insight: any two majorities of the same configuration share a node, so two leaders in one term is impossible (I4); the danger is two configurations with disjoint majorities. Predicts: even-size clusters and unsafe reconfig cause split-brain. Bug without it: two independent quorums elect two leaders. Resolved in §16 (Phase 4), I11.
  • MM5 — The clock is a liveness device, never a safety device. Insight: wall-clock decides when to try to elect/expire, never whether something is committed; safety uses term/index order (Lamport 1978). Predicts: skew must only delay, never violate. Bug without it: wall-clock lease TTL grants two leaders under skew. Resolved in §17, P11.
  • MM6 — Failure detection is a probability, not a boolean. Insight: phi-accrual outputs a continuous suspicion level over inter-arrival statistics (Hayashibara 2004), so a GC pause raises suspicion gradually instead of flipping "dead". Predicts: fixed timeouts flap under pauses. Bug without it: election storms on a hiccup. Resolved in §16 (Phase 4), FM-13.
  • MM7 — Commit means durable on a majority, not written locally. Insight: an entry is committed only once a quorum has fdatasync'd it (I10); local write is not commit. Predicts: acking early loses data on failover. Bug without it: acknowledged leadership disappears (RPO break). Resolved in §15/§14, P8.
  • MM8 — Snapshot and log must compose exactly once. Insight: state = snapshot(@N) + log(N+1..); the boundary must be exact (Ongaro thesis Ch 5). Predicts: an off-by-one at the boundary drops or double-applies an entry. Bug without it: replay gap after compaction. Resolved in §18 (Phase 6), I12.
  • MM9 — Even reads need consensus. Insight: a leader that was silently deposed will happily serve stale reads; a linearizable read must confirm current leadership (read-index barrier) or go through the log. Predicts: "just read locally" leaks stale ownership. Bug without it: a client acts on a leadership view that a new leader already superseded. Resolved in §15.2 (Phase 3), I4.
  • MM10 — Membership is just another log entry. Insight: reconfiguration is committed through the same log as data, so the config in effect at index i is well-defined and replays deterministically; the only special rule is quorum-overlap on transition (I11). Predicts: treating membership as out-of-band state creates disjoint-quorum races. Bug without it: two configs active at once → split-brain (MM4). Resolved in §16 (Phase 4), ADR-21.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017) — Ch 5 (Replication), Ch 8 (Trouble with Distributed Systems — clocks, fencing), Ch 9 (Consistency & Consensus). Extract: the fencing-token argument and the leader-lease hazard. Applied in §17 (Phase 5), P7, MM2.
  • Petrov, Database Internals (O'Reilly, 2019) — Part II Ch 8–9 (failure detection, leader election), Ch 13–14 (consensus, Paxos/Raft). Extract: phi-accrual and log-matching mechanics. Applied in §15/§16 (Phases 3–4), I6.
  • van Steen & Tanenbaum, Distributed Systems, 3rd ed. (2017) — Ch 6 (coordination: Bully & Ring election), Ch 8 (fault tolerance, failure masking). Extract: classical election algorithms as the baseline rejected in §34. Applied in §34, ADR-03.
  • Ongaro, Consensus: Bridging Theory and Practice (Stanford PhD, 2014) — full: membership changes (Ch 4), log compaction/snapshots (Ch 5), client interaction & leases (Ch 6). Applied in §15/§18 (Phases 3, 6), ADR-21.
  • Bos, Rust Atomics and Locks (O'Reilly, 2023) — Ch 2–3 (atomics, memory ordering), Ch 4 (building locks). Extract: Acquire/Release discipline for the apply loop. Applied in §12/§15 (Phases 0, 3), P2.
  • Gregg, Systems Performance, 2nd ed. (2020) — Ch 2 (methodology: USE), Ch 6–8 (CPU, memory, file systems). Extract: tail-latency and fsync analysis method. Applied in §28, §14 (Phase 2).
  • Fall & Stevens, TCP/IP Illustrated, Vol. 1, 2nd ed. (2011) — Ch 12–17 (TCP timers, retransmission). Extract: RTT/timeout assumptions behind C2/C18. Applied in §13 (Phase 1), ADR-05.
  • Herlihy & Shavit, The Art of Multiprocessor Programming, 2nd ed. (2020) — Ch 3 (concurrency correctness: linearizability), Ch 7 (spinning/locks). Extract: the precise definition of linearizability used for the ownership register (§8 read model). Applied in §8, §26 (Jepsen checker).

Papers

  • Ongaro & Ousterhout, "In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm (Extended Version)" — USENIX ATC 2014. Contribution: Raft; the five safety properties. Relevance: the core of raft-engine. Applied in §15, I4–I8.
  • Chandra & Toueg, "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems" — JACM 43(2), 1996. Contribution: ◇W is the weakest detector for consensus. Relevance: justifies the detector's design. Applied in §16, MM6.
  • Burrows, "The Chubby Lock Service for Loosely-Coupled Distributed Systems" — OSDI 2006. Contribution: coarse-grained locks, sequencers (fencing), operational lessons. Applied in §17, §23, T8.
  • Junqueira, Reed & Serafini, "Zab: High-performance broadcast for primary-backup systems" — DSN 2011. Contribution: primary-order broadcast, epoch numbers. Relevance: contrast with Raft term. Applied in §34, ADR-03.
  • Fischer, Lynch & Paterson, "Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process" — JACM 32(2), 1985. Contribution: FLP impossibility. Relevance: why timeouts/randomness are mandatory. Applied in §30 MM3, P1.
  • Hunt, Konar, Junqueira & Reed, "ZooKeeper: Wait-free coordination for Internet-scale systems" — USENIX ATC 2010. Contribution: znode API, ephemeral-sequential election recipe. Applied in §31, §34.
  • Lamport, "The Part-Time Parliament" — ACM TOCS 16(2), 1998. Contribution: Paxos. Relevance: the alternative rejected in ADR-03. Applied in §9 (ADR-03), §34.
  • Chandra, Griesemer & Redstone, "Paxos Made Live — An Engineering Perspective" — PODC 2007. Contribution: consensus engineering (disk faults, master leases, snapshots). Applied in §18, §23, ADR-22.
  • Hayashibara, Défago, Yared & Katayama, "The φ Accrual Failure Detector" — SRDS 2004. Contribution: suspicion as a continuous value. Applied in §16 (Phase 4), MM6, FM-13.
  • Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" — CACM 21(7), 1978. Contribution: happens-before, logical clocks. Relevance: why term/index order, not wall-clock, defines truth. Applied in §8, MM5.
  • Gilbert & Lynch, "Brewer's Conjecture and the Feasibility of Consistent, Available, Partition-tolerant Web Services" — ACM SIGACT News 33(2), 2002. Contribution: CAP formalization. Applied in §4 (defining property), P1.
  • Howard, Schwarzkopf, Madhavapeddy & Crowcroft, "Raft Refloated: Do We Have Consensus?" — ACM SIGOPS OSR 49(1), 2015. Contribution: reproduces Raft, quantifies election-timeout tuning. Applied in §15 (Phase 3), M13.
  • Oki & Liskov, "Viewstamped Replication: A New Primary Copy Method to Support Highly-Available Distributed Systems" — PODC 1988. Contribution: view-change / primary-copy replication predating Paxos. Relevance: the ancestor of leader-based consensus, contrasted in §34. Applied in §34, ADR-03.
  • Moraru, Andersen & Kaminsky, "There Is More Consensus in Egalitarian Parliaments" (EPaxos) — SOSP 2013. Contribution: leaderless, low-latency WAN consensus via command dependency graphs. Relevance: the deferred multi-leader alternative. Applied in §34 (Alt. B), ADR-03.

Reference Implementations

  • etcd-io/etcd — raft/ (raft library: raft.go, raftpb), server/etcdserver/, server/lease/lessor.go, server/storage/mvcc/. Lesson: separating a pure raft state machine from IO. Pitfall not to copy: early etcd's unbounded watch buffers causing OOM. Applied in §15, §17, §19.
  • hashicorp/raft — snapshot/, fsm.go, log_store (BoltDB). Lesson: SnapshotStore + FSM separation, InstallSnapshot. Pitfall: BoltDB fsync amplification under high write rate. Applied in §18 (Phase 6), ADR-06.
  • tikv/raft-rs — the Rust Raft port; harness/ deterministic test network. Lesson: driving raft with an injectable clock/network for replay. Pitfall: leaving ProgressTracker inflight windows unbounded. Applied in §12 (sim-harness), §15.
  • apache/zookeeper — FastLeaderElection.java, Leader.java, ephemeral/sequential znode handling. Lesson: the herd effect and its getChildren-watch fix. Pitfall: session-expiry semantics that surprise clients. Applied in §19, §31, §34.
  • hashicorp/consul — agent/consul/session*, Serf/memberlist (SWIM). Lesson: session TTL + health-check invalidation; gossip membership. Pitfall: coupling app health to lock liveness too tightly. Applied in §16 (ADR-08), §17.
  • kubernetes/client-go — tools/leaderelection/ (LeaderElector, LeaseLock, RenewDeadline, LeaseDuration). Lesson: the client-side lease renewal contract clients expect. Pitfall: RenewDeadline set too aggressively → controller flapping. Applied in §21 (Phase 9), FM-8.
  • logcabin/logcabin — Ongaro's own reference Raft implementation; Server/RaftConsensus.cc, Storage/. Lesson: the canonical mapping from the Raft paper's pseudocode to a real server, including snapshotting and client sessions. Pitfall: its threading model is dated — do not copy the coarse locking. Applied in §15, §18.

Glossary (§36) · 53 terms · site-wide glossary

Acquire
client request to become owner of an election group; commits an ACQUIRE entry and, on success, returns a lease + fence token.
AppendEntries
Raft RPC carrying log entries + heartbeat from leader to followers; also the consistency check (I6).
Apply loop
the single-writer thread that applies committed entries to the state machine in index order (I3, I8).
Availability (CP sense)
ability to serve within a partition; sacrificed on the minority side to preserve safety (P1).
Backpressure
bounded-queue load-shedding that returns BackpressureExceeded rather than growing memory (P5, ADR-23).
Bully algorithm
classical election where the highest-id live node wins (§34 D).
Commit index
highest log index durably replicated on a quorum (I10); source of fence tokens (ADR-10).
Conformance fixture
a named test input + expected output mapped to an invariant (§26).
CP
Consistent + Partition-tolerant (CAP); this service's classification (§4, P1).
Election group
an independent coordination domain with its own ownership FSM (group_id).
Election timeout
randomized interval (150–300 ms, C18) after which a follower becomes a candidate (MM3).
Ephemeral-sequential znode
ZooKeeper election recipe primitive (§34 A).
Fail closed
on uncertainty, revoke/refuse rather than risk unsafe action (P9).
Fence token
monotonic u64 ownership epoch from the commit index; checked at every mutation (I2, P7, MM2).
fdatasync
POSIX call flushing file data (not metadata) to stable storage; the durability primitive (ADR-07).
FLP impossibility
no async consensus is both safe and live with one crash (MM3, §5).
Follower / Candidate / Leader
Raft roles (RaftRole).
Group commit
batching many appends into one fdatasync to amortize cost (§14, §32 #5).
Heartbeat
periodic leader→follower AppendEntries (100 ms, C18) proving liveness.
Learner
non-voting node catching up before promotion to voter (§16, I11).
Lease
time-bounded ownership grant (owner, fence_token, expiry) (§8).
Linearizable
reads/writes appear to take effect atomically in real-time order.
Log matching
Raft property: equal (index,term) ⇒ identical entries and prefixes (I6).
Membership change
single-server add/remove preserving quorum overlap (ADR-21, I11).
Monotonic clock
CLOCK_MONOTONIC; used for lease timing, never for safety (P11, ADR-22).
mTLS
mutual TLS; both peers authenticate (P14, ADR-14).
Phi-accrual
failure detector emitting a continuous suspicion value (ADR-08, MM6).
Quorum
majority ⌊n/2⌋+1 of voters (C1); any two intersect (MM4).
Quorum intersection
the property that two majorities share a node, ensuring single leadership (I4, I11).
RequestVote
Raft RPC by a candidate seeking votes, subject to the up-to-date-log restriction (I7).
Revision
monotonic per-group version used by watch cursors (§8, §19).
Ring algorithm
classical election over a logical ring (§34 D).
RPO
recovery point objective; 0 committed entries lost here (C12, I10).
RTO
recovery time objective; < 60 s worst case (C11, M11).
Session
client liveness context that renews leases and expires on missed heartbeats (§8).
Snapshot
checksummed capture of applied state enabling compaction + fast recovery (§18, I12).
Split-brain
two nodes believing they are leader simultaneously; prevented by quorum + fencing (FM-4, MM4).
Term
Raft logical clock / election epoch; never decreases (I14, MM5).
Torn write
a partially written record detected by CRC and truncated on replay (I15, §14).
WAL
write-ahead log; append-only durable record of entries (§14).
Watch cursor
per-subscriber stream position for resumable delivery (§19, WatchCursor).
Zab
ZooKeeper Atomic Broadcast; the primary-order protocol contrasted in §31/§34.
Zombie leader
an ex-leader still acting after losing leadership; neutralized by fencing (MM1, MM2).
Pre-vote
a Raft round where a candidate checks it could win before bumping its term, preventing disruptive elections by rejoining nodes (§15.2).
Read-index
a barrier that lets a leader answer a linearizable read after confirming it still leads, without appending a log entry (§15.2, MM9).
Leadership transfer
a leader handing off to a caught-up follower via TimeoutNow, avoiding a timeout-driven election gap (§24).
Learner lag
how far behind a non-voting node's match-index is from the leader's commit index (§16).
Joint consensus
the alternative multi-server reconfiguration protocol rejected in ADR-21 in favor of single-server change.
Load shedding
rejecting excess work with BackpressureExceeded rather than queueing unboundedly (P5, ADR-23).
Suspicion (phi)
the continuous failure-detector output; higher phi = higher probability the peer is down (MM6).
Sequencer
Chubby's name for a fencing token handed to clients and validated by downstream services (§5, MM2).
Dedup cache
the replicated per-session (request_id → result) map giving exactly-once semantics across retries (§8).
Bounded-stale read
a follower read tagged with its commit index so a client can reject reads older than a bound (§8).
