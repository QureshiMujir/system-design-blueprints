34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Run wall clock: population × generations × eval-seconds ÷ workers. At population 10,000, generations 500, eval time 1/800 s/eval (M1) fanned over 64 workers: (10,000 × 500 × 0.00125 s) ÷ 64 ≈ 97.7 s of pure eval time — but the heavy-fitness case (M2, 5 eval/s) dominates in practice: (10,000 × 500 × 0.2 s) ÷ 64 ≈ 15,625 s ≈ 4.3 hours, consistent with the 30 s–120 s per-generation cadence (§4/M9) × 500 generations ≈ 4.2–16.7 hours.

Host RAM: sandbox RSS × concurrency. At 64 MiB per WASM sandbox (§4 default ceiling) × 64 concurrent sandboxes/node ≈ 4 GiB/node just for candidate linear memory, plus Wasmtime engine/module-cache overhead (~500 MiB/node estimated) ≈ 4.5 GiB/node for the sandbox pool alone.

Archive disk: genome bytes × archive entries × generations retained. At ~4 KiB/cell (§4) × 10,000 cells ≈ 40 MiB per generation snapshot (§4's own figure); retaining 500 generations of full snapshots naively would be 500 × 40 MiB = 20 GiB, which is why ADR-23's WAL-plus-periodic-compaction model retains only the latest snapshot plus the append-only edge log, not 500 full snapshots.

Lineage store: ≈120 bytes/edge (§4) × 200,000 genomes/generation × 500 generations ≈ 120 × 10^8 bytes ≈ 12 GB, matching §4's own lineage-graph-growth figure exactly — confirming the retention-horizon compaction (§18.6, §37) is necessary well before a multi-week 500-generation run at full population completes.

Sandbox spawn throughput vs evaluations/second required: at population 10,000 and a 60 s target generation cadence, required throughput = 10,000 ÷ 60 ≈ 167 eval/s aggregate; at M1's 800 eval/s per warm-cache worker, that requires ⌈167 ÷ 800⌉ = 1 worker in the trivial-fitness case, but at M2's 5 eval/s (heavy fitness) it requires ⌈167 ÷ 5⌉ = 34 workers to hit the same cadence — the heavy-fitness case, not the trivial case, sizes the fleet.

Scheduler message rate: one dispatch + one result message per evaluation; at 34 workers × 5 eval/s (M2 fleet-sized case) ≈ 170 evals/s ⇒ 340 scheduler messages/s, well within gRPC/Protobuf (ADR-08/ADR-09) throughput headroom, confirming the scheduler is not the bottleneck at this scale (§31's bottleneck ordering).

Checkpoint size and write time: population_size genome hashes (32 bytes BLAKE3 each) + archive (§4's 40 MiB/generation figure) ⇒ 10,000 × 32 bytes ≈ 320 KB for population membership, dominated by the ≈40 MiB archive snapshot; at a conservative 200 MB/s durable-volume write throughput, a 40 MiB checkpoint write completes in ≈0.2 s, well inside the 30 s RTO budget (M11) even accounting for fsync overhead and CRC computation.

Dedup ledger memory footprint: (genome hash 32 bytes + generation 8 bytes + attempt 1 byte + status flag 1 byte) ≈ 42 bytes/entry × in-flight generation width (population 10,000) ≈ 420 KB resident, negligible against the ≈4.5 GiB/node sandbox-pool RAM figure above.

Fleet-wide FD budget check: 4,096 concurrent sandboxes (§4 M10) × ≈16 FDs/sandbox (§4) = 65,536 FDs, exactly the per-worker ulimit (§4) at the default 64-workers-×-64-sandboxes topology — confirming the FD ceiling is reached, not exceeded, at the documented default fleet size, leaving zero headroom without raising the ulimit or reducing per-sandbox FD usage.

WASM module-compile cache size: at an estimated 50 KB compiled-module size per distinct genome shape and a working set of ~2,000 distinct shapes per generation (population 10,000 with substantial structural reuse from crossover/mutation locality), the warm cache footprint is ≈100 MB/node — well within a typical worker node's available RAM alongside the 4.5 GiB sandbox-pool figure.

Novelty-scoring cost at reservoir cap: k=15 nearest-neighbor lookups against a 5,000-entry reservoir-capped novelty archive (§20.6), each a behavior-descriptor distance computation of O(descriptor dimensionality) ≈ 10 dimensions ⇒ 15 × 5,000 × 10 ≈ 750,000 floating-point distance-term operations per candidate; at 10,000 candidates/generation this is ≈7.5 × 10^9 operations/generation, parallelized across the same worker fleet doing sandbox evaluation, budgeted well inside the 30 s–120 s generation cadence (M9) on modern multi-core hardware.

Lineage WAL group-commit batch size: at 200 bytes/edge-record on the wire (§18's ≈120 bytes/edge store figure plus framing overhead) × 10,000 offspring/generation ≈ 2 MB per generation's batched WAL write; at a conservative 200 MB/s durable-volume throughput (same figure used for the checkpoint estimate above) this fsyncs in ≈10 ms, negligible against the 30 s–120 s generation cadence (M9).
