Hard questions

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why is direct worker-to-worker messaging forbidden, and what two things break if you allow it? (§6 P2, §3.)
  2. How does the kernel distinguish a worker that crashed from one that chose not to bid, and what disambiguates them? (§8.3, §15.6.)
  3. Trace the exact sequence when a budget is exhausted mid-LLM-call: what is cancelled, in what order, and what is the worst-case overage? (§16.2, Capacity #6.)
  4. Why is a blackboard write and a bus message never interchangeable, and where is the confusion forbidden? (§6 P4, §18, MM5.)
  5. What guarantees does the bus give about message order, and why per-thread rather than global? (§14, ADR-21, I7.)
  6. How does the kernel survive a coordinator crash mid-DAG, and what is the RPO/RTO? (§4, §17.2, I9, F7.)
  7. After a network partition, two Results arrive for one task — what happens and why is double-completion impossible? (§8.5, I8, MM6.)
  8. Why is determinism a testing strategy rather than a runtime property, and what is asserted stable when text varies? (§6 P7, §29, MM9.)
  9. Where does prompt injection enter, how does it propagate, and what bounds it? (§25.1–§25.2, I2, MM4.)
  10. Why does the budget watchdog matter more than bus throughput, with numbers? (Capacity #1, #2, #10, MM3.)
  11. How does a loop in the DAG terminate, and what two fields must every loop carry? (§17.2, I11, §21.6.)
  12. Why does the kernel never call asyncio.Task.cancel(), and what leaks if it does? (§6 P5, I4, MM7.)
  13. What makes a Pattern fail at construction rather than at first dispatch, and why is that better? (§6 P10, I10, §21.)
  14. How is a deferred surface kept from silently becoming a half-feature? (§6 P12, I15, Deferred Features Register.)
  15. Why is cost recorded as a tuple, and what analysis becomes impossible if you collapse it to a number? (§20.6, I13, Phase 8.)
  16. How does the kernel enforce tenant isolation on the blackboard, and where is the check? (§18, I12, §25.2.)
  17. What is the contract-net escalation path after repeated failure, and why manager-choice? (§8.3, §15.2.)
  18. Why is the worker abstraction a thin wrapper, and what coupling does crossing it introduce? (§6 P1, ADR-05, §3.)
  19. How does the trace let you debug a divergence without re-running the run? (§2 Mastery, Phase 8, MM8.)
  20. Why is horizontal scale-out additive rather than a rewrite, and what contract makes that true? (§23.2, §32.)
  21. What stops a dead-letter flood from taking down the kernel? (§25.2, F3.)
  22. How does memory retrieval avoid becoming a per-message LLM-cost amplifier? (§19.2, §19.6.)
  23. Why does the conformance suite measure patterns against a recorded reference rather than the LLM judge? (§21.6, §24.2.)
  24. What is the worst-case extra cost the watchdog can let slip, and why is it bounded? (Capacity #6, #7.)
  25. Why is the in-process bus 2500× over-provisioned relative to LLM-bound completion, and what does that imply for where to spend engineering effort? (Capacity #10, MM3.)

Mental Models & First Principles

  • MM1 — Coordination is the product; the agent is a dependency. Predicts: most "multi-agent bugs" are coordination bugs, not model bugs. Without it you keep tuning prompts while a bus race eats your weekend. Applied in §2, Phase 4.
  • MM2 — A multi-agent system is a distributed system with an LLM-shaped failure injector. Predicts: every classic distributed-systems failure (partition, duplicate, lost update) reappears, plus prompt injection. Without it you under-build the dead-letter, dedup, and CAS paths. Applied in §25, Phase 2, Phase 6.
  • MM3 — Dollars are the tightest resource, not CPU. Predicts: the budget watchdog matters more than throughput optimization. Without it a stuck loop bills $200. Applied in §4, Phase 4, §32.
  • MM4 — Provenance beats trust. Predicts: you cannot make an LLM trustworthy, but you can tag every message with where it came from and enforce it. Without it, role hallucination and injection propagate silently. Applied in I2, §25.
  • MM5 — State and communication are different physics. Predicts: mixing the blackboard and the bus produces races debuggable only by trace archaeology. Without the separation you re-derive it after the first incident. Applied in P4, Phase 6.
  • MM6 — The result is the only truth; everything else is a hint. Predicts: heartbeats and acks tell you about liveness, never completion. Without it you lose acked-but-undelivered tasks on partition. Applied in P11/I8, Phase 11.
  • MM7 — Structured cancellation or leaks. Predicts: ad-hoc task.cancel() orphans children and leaves half-written state. Without TaskGroups you leak FDs and corrupt the blackboard. Applied in P5/I4, Phase 1, Phase 4.
  • MM8 — The trace is the source of truth for debugging, so build it first-class. Predicts: if a dialogue node has no span, it is invisible exactly when you need it. Without span-per-message you debug by re-running and praying. Applied in P9, Phase 8.
  • MM9 — Determinism is a testing strategy, not a runtime property. Predicts: you make tests deterministic with cassettes and assert structural stability, not text equality. Without it conformance is flaky and incidents are irreproducible. Applied in P7, §29.
  • MM10 — Defer with a typed anchor, never with a TODO. Predicts: an un-anchored deferral becomes scope creep or a silent half-feature. Without the register and PhaseDeferred, the Sealed gate is unenforceable. Applied in P12/I15, Deferred Features Register.

5. Required Reading

You will not write a correct contract-net implementation without reading these. Read them in order; do not skip the foundational papers. Each entry names the §/phase where its idea is applied.

Books

  • Wooldridge, M., 2009, An Introduction to MultiAgent Systems (2nd ed.). §1–§4. The academic baseline; supervisor-worker vs peer-to-peer topology informs §3 and Phase 4.
  • Tanenbaum & Steen, 2017, Distributed Systems (3rd ed.). §6 synchronization, §8 fault tolerance. The kernel is a distributed system in miniature; applied in Phase 2 (delivery semantics) and the Deferred distributed-worker surface.
  • Kahneman, D., 2011, Thinking, Fast and Slow. System-1/System-2 distinction is the prior for the fast/slow worker split in the swarm pattern (Phase 9).
  • Kleppmann, M., 2017, Designing Data-Intensive Applications. Ch. 5 (replication) and Ch. 9 (consistency) inform the blackboard CAS and per-thread ordering (Phase 6, Phase 2).
  • Newman, S., 2021, Building Microservices (2nd ed.). Boundary/contract discipline informs the §22 normative interfaces and the REST/WS boundary (Phase 10).
  • Hohpe & Woolf, 2003, Enterprise Integration Patterns. Dead-letter channel, idempotent receiver, message router — directly applied in Phase 2.

Papers

  • Smith, R. G., 1980, The Contract Net Protocol. The TaskAnnounce/Bid/Award/Result envelope and deadlock conditions; the basis of Phase 3.
  • Wu et al., 2023, AutoGen. §3 conversation protocol, §4 patterns. The contemporary multi-agent surface; calibration for Phase 9.
  • Hong et al., 2023, MetaGPT. §3 SOP layer; how role-based prompts compose. Applied to SoftwareEngineeringTeam (Phase 9).
  • Qian et al., 2023, ChatDev. The design/coding/testing/documentation phase decomposition is the template for SoftwareEngineeringTeam (Phase 9).
  • Li et al., 2023, CAMEL. Role-playing prompt structure; template for the Debate pattern (Phase 9).
  • Khattab et al., 2023, DSPy. Typed-signature approach informs the Worker interface (Phase 1).
  • Yao et al., 2022, ReAct. The reason-act loop is the assumed per-agent loop the Worker wraps (Phase 1).
  • Shinn et al., 2023, Reflexion. Verbal self-critique is the prior for the critic loop-back in ResearchTeam (Phase 5, Phase 9).

Reference implementations & specs

  • LangGraph documentation. Persistence and human-in-the-loop sections; the model for the DAG executor's pause/resume (Phase 5).
  • OpenDevin / OpenHands. Agent-loop and runtime sections; the production software-engineering-team reference (Phase 9).
  • AutoGen-Bench (Microsoft, 2024) and AgentBench (Liu et al., 2023). The conformance corpus is a curated subset (§24, Conformance Suite).
  • FIPA Agent Communication Language specifications (FIPA-ACL, FIPA-Contract-Net-Protocol). The envelope is FIPA-shaped (§8, Phase 3).
  • OpenTelemetry semantic conventions for AI/agent workloads (current draft). The span attributes follow these conventions (Phase 8); deviations documented in docs/observability.md.
  • Anthropic, 2024, Building effective agents. Calibration for which patterns are worth shipping; "evaluator-optimizer" → CodeReviewCommittee, "orchestrator-workers" → ResearchTeam (Phase 9).

The literature changes faster than this document. If a post-2026 paper supersedes one of these for our purposes, an ADR captures the change.

Read in two passes. First pass, for the shape of the system: Smith 1980 (allocation), Wooldridge §1–§4 (topology), and Anthropic's "Building effective agents" (which patterns are worth shipping) — these three set the boundaries the rest of the blueprint fills in. Second pass, for the mechanics under load: Tanenbaum & Steen §8 and Hohpe & Woolf (failure and messaging), Kleppmann Ch. 9 (consistency for the blackboard), and the OTEL AI conventions (the observability contract). The AutoGen/MetaGPT/ChatDev/CAMEL/LangGraph/OpenDevin implementations are reference reading throughout — read the code, not just the papers, when a phase's design choice is unclear.

Glossary · 47 terms · site-wide glossary

Agent
A single-agent runtime from the Agentic-Framework (#26). The kernel sees only a Worker wrapper.
Arbitration policy
The kernel's response to ambiguity/conflict (stuck loops, dead-letter floods, conflicting writes). Default: rule-based with LLM-judge fallback.
At-least-once
Delivery mode (default): the consumer acks after handling; redelivery on no-ack.
At-most-once
Fire-and-forget delivery; no redelivery.
Award
The coordinator's selection of the winning bidder; step 3 of the contract net.
Backpressure
The sender awaits when a bounded queue is full; no hidden buffer absorbs bursts.
Bid
A worker's offer to take a task; step 2 of the contract net.
Blackboard
A shared versioned KV store for state more than one agent reads/writes; per-run by default.
Budget
The cap on a run: max cost, wall-clock, steps, tokens. Enforced by the watchdog.
Bus
The typed message channel; in-process by default, cross-process via #58.
Cancellation
Cooperative termination, AnyIO TaskGroup-shaped; parents cancel children, not vice versa.
CAS
Compare-and-set: a write carries an expected_version; mismatch is rejected.
Cassette
A recorded LLM-call replay used to make tests deterministic.
Contract Net
Smith's 1980 allocation protocol: announce, bid, award, result.
Coordinator
The single orchestrator: compiles patterns, dispatches, runs the contract net, watches budgets/deadlines, arbitrates.
Cost ledger
The per-call record of (provider, model, tokens, cost, run, task, role).
DAG
Directed Acyclic Graph (with optional back-edges marking loops); the internal form of a Pattern.
Dead letter
A message that exceeded its delivery attempts; stored on a separate topic.
Deferral
A surface not shipped at Phase 0, anchored via PhaseDeferred("Deferred Features Register").
Delivery mode
at-most-once / at-least-once / exactly-once-via-dedupe.
Determinism
Same (pattern, inputs, seed, replays) ⇒ same trace structure.
Envelope
The frozen wrapper around every bus message; carries identity, routing, timing, and a typed payload.
Episodic memory
A per-agent verbatim log of received/emitted messages.
Event
A coordinator output element (TaskAnnounced, Awarded, Done, …) streamed to the application.
Exactly-once-via-dedupe
Delivery mode using a bounded LRU of recent msg_ids to drop duplicates.
HITL
Human-in-the-loop: a DAG checkpoint that pauses for an external Resume.
Idempotency-Key
A header making run creation idempotent; duplicates return the same run id.
LLM-judge
An arbitration policy where a manager LLM ranks bids or verdicts.
Loop
A DAG back-edge with max_iterations and terminate_when.
Manager-choice
An award strategy deferring winner selection to the LLM-judge policy.
Pattern
A typed dataclass naming roles, the graph, and the default budget; the user-facing surface.
PhaseDeferred
The typed deferral exception; its string anchors at the Deferred Features Register.
Provenance
The sender_role/sender_worker tags carried by every envelope.
Result
The outcome of an awarded task; step 4 of the contract net; the only completion signal.
Role
A named registration on the bus; patterns declare roles, workers register against them.
Run
A single coordinator invocation, identified by a RunId.
Semantic memory
A per-agent distilled-fact store on the Vector-Database.
Shared memory
A team-level store on the Vector-Database.
Span
An OpenTelemetry trace node; the kernel emits span-per-run/task/message/tool.
Sticky session
Pinning all messages in a thread to one worker (stateful workers).
Stuck loop
A repeated (sender, recipient, payload-hash) tuple within the sliding window.
Subscription
A worker's registration to be notified on blackboard writes matching a prefix.
TaskAnnounce
Step 1 of the contract net: a broadcast to a role.
Thread
A sub-conversation identified by a ThreadId; per-thread FIFO order is guaranteed.
TTL
An optional per-blackboard-key expiry.
Watchdog
The budget/deadline enforcement loop (1-Hz heartbeat + pre-dispatch).
Worker
A Worker ABC subclass wrapping an Agent (LLM) or Tool (non-LLM).
