32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — "PPO's clip is a trust region enforced by refusing to reward off-policy drift, not by measuring it." Predicts: KL can still drift unboundedly even while the clip term looks well-behaved. Bug class without it: policy collapse from an unbounded inner-epoch loop that trusts the clip alone. Resolves in §17.

MM2 — "The rollout buffer is a data contract, not a data structure." Predicts: any component that can be swapped (env-runner language, backend) needs only agree on buffer shape and dtype. Bug class without it: training logic coupled directly to env internals, breaking P1. Resolves in §11.

MM3 — "GAE is a recursion with a direction, not a formula." Predicts: any implementation computing deltas without walking backward first will silently mis-bootstrap. Bug class without it: a forward-computed "advantage" that is actually a disguised n-step return. Resolves in §16.

MM4 — "terminated and truncated answer two different questions: did the world end, or did we just stop watching." Predicts: bootstrapping through truncation is correct; through termination it is not. Bug class without it: FM-3's bootstrap leak. Resolves in §8 and §16.

MM5 — "Normalization statistics are part of the model, not preprocessing." Predicts: a checkpoint without them is a materially different function than the one that trained. Bug class without it: the eval-degradation failure named in P5 and FM-9. Resolves in §18 and §22.

MM6 — "Vectorization amortizes interpreter overhead; it does not parallelize computation." Predicts: throughput plateaus once GIL contention dominates, regardless of how many envs are added. Bug class without it: assuming linear scaling to num_envs=1024 on a single sync process. Resolves in §19 and §31.

MM7 — "Every implementation detail is a hypothesis until it's ablated on your own benchmark." Predicts: a cited paper's finding may not transfer to a different env/seed distribution. Bug class without it: cargo-culting a hyperparameter without re-verifying its direction locally. Resolves in §20.

MM8 — "Determinism is a property of the whole system, not of any one seed call." Predicts: one un-seeded RNG anywhere breaks the reproducibility guarantee for the entire run. Bug class without it: FM-10's dependency-induced nondeterminism. Resolves in §22.

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Sutton, R. S. and Barto, A. G. — Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed. (MIT Press, 2018). Chapters 3 (MDPs), 6 (TD learning), 9 (function approximation), 13 (policy gradient). Extract: the MDP formalism, the policy-gradient theorem, baseline subtraction. Applied in §8.
  • Szepesvari, C. — Algorithms for Reinforcement Learning (Morgan & Claypool, 2010). Extract: convergence proofs for value iteration and the contraction-mapping view of the Bellman operator. Applied in §8.
  • Bertsekas, D. P. — Reinforcement Learning and Optimal Control (Athena Scientific, 2019). Chapters on approximate policy iteration and actor-critic methods. Extract: the rigorous derivation of advantage as a variance-reduced baseline. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Puterman, M. L. — Markov Decision Processes: Discrete Stochastic Dynamic Programming (Wiley, 1994). Extract: the formal (S, A, P, R, gamma) tuple and the terminating-vs-continuing MDP distinction used to ground the terminated/truncated split. Applied in §8.
  • Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y., and Courville, A. — Deep Learning (MIT Press, 2016). Chapters 7 (regularization) and 8 (optimization). Extract: the Adam algorithm and gradient-clipping rationale that justify ADR-13 and ADR-14. Applied in §17 (Phase 5).
  • Kleppmann, M. — Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017). Chapters 3, 5, 9. Extract: the durability/versioning/consistency framing applied to the checkpoint store and telemetry pipeline. Applied in §26.

Papers

  • Schulman, J., Wolski, F., Dhariwal, P., Radford, A., and Klimov, O. (2017). Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms. arXiv:1707.06347. Contribution: the clipped surrogate objective. Applied in §17 (Phase 5).
  • Schulman, J., Moritz, P., Levine, S., Jordan, M., and Abbeel, P. (2016). High-Dimensional Continuous Control Using Generalized Advantage Estimation. ICLR. Contribution: the GAE(gamma, lambda) recursion. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Schulman, J., Levine, S., Moritz, P., Jordan, M., and Abbeel, P. (2015). Trust Region Policy Optimization. ICML. Contribution: the KL-constrained natural-gradient step PPO approximates. Applied in §8 (Domain Model).
  • Mnih, V., Kavukcuoglu, K., Silver, D., et al. (2015). Human-Level Control through Deep Reinforcement Learning. Nature. Contribution: DQN, the off-policy discrete-control baseline. Applied in §36.
  • Mnih, V., Badia, A. P., Mirza, M., et al. (2016). Asynchronous Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning. ICML. Contribution: A3C/A2C, the direct ancestor of the vectorized rollout loop. Applied in §14 (Phase 2).
  • Williams, R. J. (1992). Simple Statistical Gradient-Following Algorithms for Connectionist Reinforcement Learning. Machine Learning. Contribution: REINFORCE, the policy-gradient baseline PPO builds on. Applied in §8 (Domain Model).
  • Engstrom, L., Ilyas, A., Santurkar, S., et al. (2020). Implementation Matters in Deep RL: A Case Study on PPO and TRPO. ICLR. Contribution: proof that code-level optimizations, not the algorithm, drive PPO's measured gains. Applied in §20 (Phase 8).
  • Andrychowicz, M., Raichuk, A., Stanczyk, P., et al. (2021). What Matters in On-Policy Reinforcement Learning? A Large-Scale Empirical Study. ICLR. Contribution: the empirical ablation of network/objective choices. Applied in §20 (Phase 8) and §9.
  • Huang, S., Dossa, R. F. J., Raffin, A., Kanervisto, A., and Wang, W. (2022). The 37 Implementation Details of Proximal Policy Optimization. ICLR Blog Track. Contribution: the operational checklist of PPO code-level details. Applied in §20 (Phase 8).
  • Henderson, P., Islam, R., Bachman, P., et al. (2018). Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters. AAAI. Contribution: documents seed/implementation fragility of reported RL results. Applied in §28.
  • Brockman, G., Cheung, V., Pettersson, L., et al. (2016). OpenAI Gym. arXiv:1606.01540. Contribution: the historical 4-tuple env-step API. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Towers, M., Terry, J. K., Kwiatkowski, A., et al. (2023). Gymnasium. Contribution: the maintained 5-tuple (terminated/truncated) env API this project targets. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Raffin, A., Hill, A., Gleave, A., Kanervisto, A., Ernestus, M., and Dormann, N. (2021). Stable-Baselines3: Reliable Reinforcement Learning Implementations. JMLR. Contribution: the reference "well-engineered" PPO implementation. Applied in §23 (Phase 11).
  • Weng, J., Chen, H., Yan, D., et al. (2022). Tianshou: A Highly Modularized Deep Reinforcement Learning Library. JMLR. Contribution: a modular policy/data-batch abstraction. Applied in §33.
  • Espeholt, L., Soyer, H., Munos, R., et al. (2018). IMPALA: Scalable Distributed Deep-RL with Importance-Weighted Actor-Learner Architectures. ICML. Contribution: V-trace off-policy correction for distributed rollouts. Applied in §36.

Reference Implementations

  • CleanRL (vwxyzjn/cleanrl), files cleanrl/ppo.py, cleanrl/ppo_continuous_action.py. Lesson: a single-file, top-to-bottom-readable PPO makes the 37-details checklist auditable line by line. Pitfall: do not copy its module-level args singleton pattern — it violates P12 (no-leaky-globals). Applied in §17 (Phase 5).
  • Stable-Baselines3 (DLR-RM/stable-baselines3), dirs stable_baselines3/ppo/ppo.py, stable_baselines3/common/buffers.py. Lesson: RolloutBuffer pre-allocation and compute_returns_and_advantage's GAE walk. Pitfall: its state-dependent-exploration (SDE) noise sampling is a rabbit hole not needed here. Applied in §16 (Phase 4).
  • Gymnasium (Farama-Foundation/Gymnasium), dir gymnasium/vector/ (sync_vector_env.py, async_vector_env.py). Lesson: the exact terminated/truncated propagation through VectorEnv.step. Pitfall: AsyncVectorEnv's shared-memory obs buffer is a throughput optimization, not a correctness requirement. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Tianshou (thu-ml/tianshou), dir tianshou/policy/modelfree/ppo.py. Lesson: cleanly separating the policy/value network protocol from the update loop. Pitfall: its generic Batch abstraction over-generalizes for a from-scratch single-algorithm build. Applied in §15 (Phase 3).
  • openai/baselines (openai/baselines), dir baselines/ppo2/. Lesson: the original pessimistic value-clip formula and the orthogonal-init gains. Pitfall: its TensorFlow 1.x session/graph patterns do not translate to eager PyTorch. Applied in §17 (Phase 5) and ADR-05, ADR-11.

Glossary (§38) · 58 terms · site-wide glossary

MDP
Markov Decision Process; the (S, A, P, R, gamma, rho0) tuple (§8).
Return
the discounted sum of future rewards, G_t.
Discount factor (gamma)
the per-step weight applied to future reward.
Advantage
Q(s,a) - V(s); how much better an action is than the state's average.
GAE
Generalized Advantage Estimation; the lambda-weighted advantage estimator (§16).
TD error (delta)
the one-step temporal-difference residual feeding the GAE recursion.
On-policy
learning only from data generated by the current (or very recently current) policy.
Off-policy
learning from data generated by a different (often older or replayed) policy.
Clip ratio (eps)
the PPO surrogate's bound on the probability ratio, default 0.2.
KL divergence
a distributional distance used here to bound policy drift per update (P7).
Entropy bonus
a reward for policy stochasticity, encouraging exploration (P9).
Value bootstrapping
estimating a return using a learned value function instead of a full rollout.
terminated
the episode ended because a true terminal state was reached (I4).
truncated
the episode was cut off by a time limit or external stop, not a true terminal (I4).
Rollout
one T-step, N-env batch of collected experience (§8).
Minibatch
a subset of a flattened rollout batch used for one gradient step.
Epoch (update)
one full pass over all minibatches of a rollout during a PPO update.
Vectorized env
an env wrapper exposing N parallel instances through one batched API (§13).
Autoreset
a vectorized env's automatic per-instance reset upon episode end (§8, §19).
Normalizer statistics
the running mean/variance used to normalize observations or rewards (P5).
Explained variance
the fraction of return variance the value function accounts for.
Clip fraction
the fraction of samples where the PPO clip was actually active.
Approx KL
the cheap, unbiased KL approximation used for the early-stop check (§5 citation, Schulman 2020 blog).
Orthogonal initialization
a weight-init scheme preserving vector norms; used with specific gains (ADR-11).
safetensors
a checkpoint serialization format that avoids arbitrary code execution on load (ADR-17).
Run manifest
the recorded config + derived values + seed + git SHA written at run start (P11).
Checkpoint
a persisted snapshot of weights, optimizer state, RNG state, normalizer stats, and config (I10).
RTO
Recovery Time Objective; the target time to resume training from a checkpoint (§4).
SBOM
Software Bill of Materials; a manifest of all dependencies in a release (§29).
SLSA
a supply-chain provenance/attestation framework referenced for release integrity (§29).
Conformance suite
the fixture-driven test harness validating the env/VecEnv API contract (§13).
Differential testing
comparing this codebase's behavior against a reference implementation on fixed seeds (§23, §28).
Fuzzing
feeding a parser or dispatch function large volumes of generated input to find unhandled crashes (§23, §28).
Chaos testing
deliberately injecting a fault (e.g. a hanging env) to verify recovery behavior (§28).
Soak test
a sustained-load test run over a long duration to surface slow leaks or drift (§28).
Seed
the integer from which all of a run's randomness is deterministically derived (P3).
RNG
Random Number Generator; this project uses per-component seeded Generator instances (I6).
Policy gradient
the family of methods that directly optimize a parameterized policy via the gradient of expected return.
Actor-critic
a policy-gradient method that uses a learned value function (critic) to reduce variance in the policy (actor) gradient.
REINFORCE
the foundational Monte-Carlo policy-gradient algorithm (Williams 1992, §5).
Baseline (variance reduction)
a state-dependent term subtracted from the return to reduce gradient variance without introducing bias.
Trust region
a bounded region of policy-parameter space within which an update is trusted to improve the policy (TRPO, §8).
Surrogate objective
a tractable proxy objective (here, the clipped surrogate) standing in for the true, expensive policy-improvement objective.
Value function (V)
the expected return from a state under the current policy.
Q-function (Q)
the expected return from a state-action pair under the current policy.
Categorical distribution
the discrete-action probability distribution produced by the policy head (§8 taxonomy).
Gaussian policy
the diagonal-Normal continuous-action distribution used in Phase 9 (§21).
Log-std
the log of the Gaussian policy's standard deviation parameter.
Bellman equation
the recursive relationship defining V/Q in terms of immediate reward plus discounted future value.
GIL
the CPython Global Interpreter Lock; the source of the sync-vectorization throughput ceiling (§19, §31).
IPC
Inter-Process Communication; relevant to the deferred async/subprocess vectorization backend.
Backpressure
a system's mechanism for slowing producers when consumers cannot keep up (ADR-05).
SemVer
Semantic Versioning; the MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH scheme governing this project's compatibility guarantees (§27).
Canary deployment
validating a change on a small scope before full promotion (§26).
Telemetry
the structured metrics/logging surface used for debugging and monitoring (§7, §26).
Span (tracing)
a named, timed unit of work within the training loop (ADR-20, §26).
Running mean/std
an incrementally updated estimate of a data stream's mean and variance (RunningMeanStd, §11).
Welford's algorithm
the numerically stable incremental variance-computation method used by RunningMeanStd (§18).
