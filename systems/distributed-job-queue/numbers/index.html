32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. Steady-state in-flight leases (Little's Law): L = λ × W = 50,000 jobs/s (§4 dequeue ceiling) × 30 s (§4 default lease) = 1,500,000 concurrent leases at saturation → requires ≥150 worker processes at the 10,000-lease-per-process ceiling (§4).
  2. Redis ingress bandwidth: 100,000 enqueue/s (§4) × 1 KiB typical payload (well under the 256 KiB default cap, §4) = 100 MB/s sustained ingress.
  3. Leased-set memory: 1,500,000 in-flight leases (#1) × ~150 bytes/zset-member (job_id + worker_id + token) ≈ 225 MB resident in Redis's leased zset.
  4. Dead-letter growth rate: 0.1% assumed terminal-failure rate × 100,000 enqueue/s (§4) = 100 dead-letters/s × 2 KB/record × 2,592,000 s (30-day retention, ADR-16) ≈ 518 GB worst-case archival storage.
  5. Idempotency index worst case: 100,000 enqueue/s (§4) × 86,400 s (24 h dedup window, ADR-14) × 64 bytes/entry ≈ 553 GB if every job carried a unique key — sizes the ceiling operators must plan for even though real-world key adoption is a fraction of this.
  6. Postgres table growth: 10,000 enqueue/s (§4 Postgres ceiling) × 200 bytes/row × 86,400 s/day ≈ 173 GB/day raw growth before autovacuum reclaims completed rows.
  7. FD budget rationale: 10,000 leases/process (§4) would be 10,000+ FDs at 1:1, but heartbeat batching (§18.7) multiplexes renewals onto ~10 pooled connections, holding the actual ceiling at 12,000 FDs (§4) rather than 10,010+.
  8. Cron bucket-collision probability: skew_bound / bucket_width = 1 s (§4 clock-skew assumption) / 60 s (default cron bucket) ≈ 1.7% raw double-fire probability per handover event, reduced to an observed 0% after the I12 idempotency key absorbs it (§19.4, §28 M7).
