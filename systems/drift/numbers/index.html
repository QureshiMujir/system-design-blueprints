34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

Weekly validation wall clock (single repo, Phase 0–9): advisories/repo/week = 500/hour steady state × 24 h × 7 d ÷ 1 repo, but bounded by risk-filtered volume, not raw ingest — using the M3 p50 fix-attempt latency (8 min) as the per-validation wall-clock proxy: 1 repo × ~50 advisories/week (typical risk-filtered rate, not raw 500/hour ingest) × 8 min ÷ 8 sandbox slots (§4 max concurrency) = 400 sandbox-minutes ÷ 8 = 50 minutes of wall-clock queue time/week at typical load — negligible against the 5 min RTO (§4) and far below saturation.

Sandbox host RAM: per-validation RSS ceiling (§4: 4 GiB) × 8 concurrent validations (§4 max concurrency, single host) = 32 GiB minimum host RAM provisioned for the sandbox pool alone, before control-plane and OS overhead (add ~4 GiB operational headroom → 36 GiB target host size).

Event-store bytes (single repo, steady state): 5 MiB/day (§4, M13's per-repo basis) × 90-day retention (default operational retention window) = 450 MiB/repo retained; at 500-repo fleet scale (§4) with per-repo sharding (ADR-05): 500 × 450 MiB = 225 GiB fleet-wide steady-state retained event data.

Forge API budget: ≤ 400 calls/repo/day (§4, M11) vs 10 PRs/day/repo governor default (§4) → 40 calls/PR budget; GitHub's REST default quota is 5,000 calls/hour/installation, i.e. 120,000 calls/day — 400 calls/repo/day means a single installation token could in principle serve up to 300 repos before hitting the raw GitHub quota ceiling, well above the Phase 0–9 single-repo scope and consistent with the 500-repo fleet target needing the governor's fair-share allocation (§22) rather than raw quota headroom.

Advisory ingest rate vs feed size: 500/hour steady state (§4) × 24 h = 12,000 advisories/day processed; osv.dev's full export is on the order of low hundreds of thousands of records fleet-wide across all ecosystems — at 12,000/day a full re-ingest of a 300,000-record snapshot would take 25 days, confirming the ingester (§12) must be cursor/delta-based, not full-rescan, for steady state.

Dependency-graph memory: a 5,000-node lockfile (§4/M8 basis) × ~200 bytes/node (name + ecosystem + version strings + depth int, excluding parent/child edge lists) ≈ 1 MiB base node storage; edges at an average fan-out of 4 children/node × 5,000 nodes × 8 bytes/edge (int64 node ID) ≈ 160 KiB; total in-memory DependencyGraph for one (repo, SHA) ≈ 1.2 MiB, well within the 3 s p95 graph- build latency target (§4/M8) for a single-pass build.

Sandbox spawn throughput vs M6/M7 target: 8 concurrent validations (§4) × 1 spawn/2.5 s (§4/M6 p95) = a sustained sandbox-pool spawn rate of ~3.2 spawns/second at full saturation — this is the ceiling a warm-pod pool (§22.6) must absorb during a Kubernetes Job-scheduled burst before M7's 2.5 s p95 target degrades.

Justification signing throughput: 12,000 advisories/day ingested (above) but only a small risk-filtered fraction (~50/week per repo, above) ever reach Justified; at M10's < 2 s signing latency, even the full 5,000/hour burst ingest rate (§4) converted 1:1 to signings (worst case, never actually reached given the funnel) would demand 5,000 × 2 s ÷ 3,600 s ≈ 2.8 signer-seconds of work per wall-clock second — comfortably parallelizable across a small signer pool, confirming signing is never the bottleneck relative to sandbox concurrency.
