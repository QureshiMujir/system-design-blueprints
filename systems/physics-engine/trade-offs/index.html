ADR-01

Language / runtime

Chosen

Python 3.11+

Rejected

C++, Rust, C

When the rejected choice is right

A shipped console title needs sub-millisecond native step times

Why: Inspectability and REPL-steppability of the solver math is the pedagogical point

ADR-02

Numeric core strategy

Chosen

NumPy SoA bulk state + pure-Python scalar solver loop

Rejected

Fully vectorized NumPy solver, upfront Cython rewrite

When the rejected choice is right

Contact count exceeds ~5,000/step and step time is the bottleneck (PhaseDeferred("§30 Performance Engineering"))

Why: Solver inner loop is data-dependent and does not vectorize; premature native rewrite hides the pedagogy

Chosen

Rejected

When the rejected choice is right

36. Alternative Architectures (Roads Not Taken)

A1. Global LCP/MLCP solved via a direct pivoting method (e.g., Lemke's algorithm).

[body velocities] --Jacobian--> [LCP: w = Mz+q, w>=0, z>=0, w.z=0]
                                         │
                                  direct pivot solve
                                         │
                                 exact simultaneous z

Winning regime: small (tens of bodies), tightly-coupled scenes needing an exact simultaneous solution (offline research validation, near-singular configurations). Embodying system: research LCP solvers built on Stewart & Trinkle (1996) / Anitescu & Potra (1997)'s formulations. Rejected because pivoting methods scale poorly with contact count (worse than linear) and offer no natural warm-start analog — wrong tradeoff for this blueprint's real-time, large-contact-count target (§4).

A2. Position-based dynamics / XPBD.

[positions] --project constraints directly--> [corrected positions]
                          │
                  (no separate velocity solve;
                   velocity derived from Δposition/dt)

Winning regime: highly deformable, mass-varying, or very stiff systems (cloth, soft bodies — this blueprint's explicit non-goals) where unified position-space projection outperforms a velocity-space solver. Embodying system: XPBD as used in cloth/soft-body engines. Rejected here because this blueprint's domain (rigid bodies only) doesn't need XPBD's chief advantage (unconditional stability for arbitrarily stiff, deformable constraints) and would sacrifice the well-understood, directly citable sequential-impulse lineage (Catto's papers, §5) for a formulation this blueprint's rigid-body-only scope doesn't require.

A3. Fully implicit/variational integrator with Newton iterations (MuJoCo's convex-optimization approach).

[state] --formulate contact as smooth, convex optimization--> [Newton solve]
                                         │
                              implicit, per-step convergence
                                         │
                                  next state (soft contact)

Winning regime: contact-rich robotic manipulation research needing smooth gradients through contact (differentiable simulation) and tolerating higher per-step cost for accuracy. Embodying system: MuJoCo. Rejected because Newton-iteration-per-step cost is incompatible with §4's 16 ms real-time budget at the target body/contact counts, and this blueprint's teaching goal (an inspectable sequential-impulse solver, §2) is better served by the simpler, more widely-cited Catto lineage.

A4. Penalty/spring-damper contact model.

[penetration depth] --k*depth + c*velocity--> [applied force, no LCP]
                                         │
                                 integrated like any other force

Winning regime: extremely simple engines where implementation speed outweighs stability guarantees, or soft, squishy contact is the desired visual (arcade-style games). Embodying system: early physics engines and many simplified game-jam implementations use penalty forces directly. Rejected because penalty forces require a stiff spring constant to avoid visible sinking, which in turn demands a very small dt or an implicit integrator for stability (Press et al., ch. 16) — reintroducing exactly the stiffness-vs-stability tradeoff this blueprint's sequential-impulse solver (ADR-10) was chosen specifically to avoid.
