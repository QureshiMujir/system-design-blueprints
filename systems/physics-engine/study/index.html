32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1. A constraint solver is Gauss-Seidel on a sparse system with a deadline. Predicts: every visual artefact (jitter, softness, slow settling) is a convergence story, not a physics story. Bug class without it: chasing a "physics bug" by tweaking restitution/friction coefficients when the real issue is iteration count or ordering. Resolved in §19, §20.

MM2. Restitution and friction are inequalities you project onto, not forces you apply. Predicts: the solver clamps, it doesn't compute a closed-form force. Bug class without it: trying to "add a friction force" directly to the integrator instead of bounding an impulse in the solver. Resolved in §8, §19.

MM3. Determinism is a property of the whole pipeline's order, not just its arithmetic. Predicts: two runs with identical float operations in a different order can diverge. Bug class without it: assuming IEEE 754 compliance alone guarantees replay-safety, then being surprised by hash- map iteration nondeterminism. Resolved in §15, §7 (I4).

MM4. Broad phase trades false positives for zero false negatives. Predicts: a broad phase that looks "wasteful" (many rejected candidate pairs) is working correctly; a broad phase that looks "efficient" (few candidates) but misses one is catastrophic. Bug class without it: tightening AABBs to "optimize" and silently reintroducing missed contacts. Resolved in §8, §15 (P5).

MM5. Warm starting is memory across an otherwise-stateless iteration. Predicts: solver quality at a fixed iteration count is bounded by how well last frame's answer seeds this frame's guess. Bug class without it: doubling iteration count to fix jitter when the real fix is restoring warm-start continuity. Resolved in §20 (P4).

MM6. An island is the unit of independence, and static bodies are not nodes. Predicts: two physically separate structures sharing one static anchor must simulate as if they've never met. Bug class without it: a shared floor silently serializing unrelated stacks, then blaming the solver for a slowdown that's actually a partitioning bug. Resolved in §21 (I13).

MM7. Sleeping is a claim about the whole island, not a body. Predicts: one twitching body keeps 19 settled neighbors "awake" on paper even though nothing is visibly moving. Bug class without it: per-body sleep heuristics that let a sleeping body silently sit inside a still-settling neighbor. Resolved in §21 (P8).

MM8. A support function is the only vocabulary collision detection needs to speak. Predicts: adding a new convex shape should never require touching SAT, GJK, or the solver. Bug class without it: shape-specific narrow-phase special cases that quietly diverge from the general path on edge cases the general path already handled. Resolved in §14, §18 (P6).

5. Required Reading

Books

  • Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection — ch. 4 (bounding volumes), ch. 5 (basic primitive tests), ch. 9 (GJK), ch. 6 (BVH construction). Extract: the support-function abstraction and the BVH-vs-SAP tradeoff table. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §15 Phase 3, §18 Phase 6.
  • Baraff & Witkin, SIGGRAPH physically-based-modelling course notes — the constraint Jacobian/effective-mass derivation and the Lagrange-multiplier view of contact. Extract: the general constraint formulation this engine's ConstraintJacobian specializes. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §19 Phase 7.
  • Millington, Game Physics Engine Development — ch. 3–5 (particle → rigid body progression), ch. 13 (contact resolution architecture). Extract: the phased build order this blueprint's arc mirrors. Applied in §12 Phase 0, §13 Phase 1.
  • Eberly, Game Physics, 2nd ed. — ch. 2 (rigid body state, inertia tensor transform), ch. 4 (numerical integration of orientation). Extract: the world-space inertia-tensor update and quaternion renormalization discipline. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §14 Phase 2.
  • Bourg & Bywalec, Physics for Game Developers — ch. 5 (collision response), ch. 8 (friction and restitution intuition). Extract: the intuitive derivation of the impulse-restitution formula before the rigorous Jacobian form. Applied in §13 Phase 1.
  • Press et al., Numerical Recipes — ch. 16 (integration of ODEs, stability of explicit vs. implicit methods). Extract: the stiffness/stability argument used to justify semi-implicit Euler over RK4 for constrained systems. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §13 Phase 1.
  • Hairer, Lubich & Wanner, Geometric Numerical Integration — ch. 6 (symplectic methods, near-conservation of energy). Extract: why semi-implicit Euler is quasi-symplectic and the bound this gives on long-run energy drift. Applied in §8 Domain Model, §34 Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model.

Papers

  • Gilbert, Johnson & Keerthi, 1988, "A Fast Procedure for Computing the Distance Between Complex Objects in Three-Dimensional Space," IEEE J. Robotics and Automation — the GJK algorithm. Applied in §18 Phase 6.
  • van den Bergen, 2001, "Proximity Queries and Penetration Depth Computation on 3D Convex Objects" — the EPA formulation used for penetration depth. Applied in §18 Phase 6.
  • van den Bergen, 1999, "A Fast and Robust GJK Implementation for Collision Detection of Convex Objects" — numerical-robustness practice for simplex reduction. Applied in §18 Phase 6.
  • Catto, 2005, GDC, "Iterative Dynamics with Temporal Coherence" — warm starting. Applied in §20 Phase 8.
  • Catto, 2011, GDC, "Soft Constraints: Reinventing the Spring" — the CFM/ERP soft-constraint formulation studied against Baumgarte. Applied in §21 Phase 9.
  • Catto, 2009, GDC, "Modeling and Solving Constraints" — the sequential-impulse contact/joint formulation this engine's solver follows. Applied in §19 Phase 7.
  • Baumgarte, 1972, "Stabilization of Constraints and Integrals of Motion in Dynamical Systems," Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering — the position-stabilization bias term studied and rejected as the primary mechanism (ADR-13). Applied in §21 Phase 9.
  • Stewart & Trinkle, 1996, "An Implicit Time-Stepping Scheme for Rigid Body Dynamics with Inelastic Collisions and Coulomb Friction," Int. J. Numerical Methods in Engineering — the LCP formulation of contact studied as an alternative solver family. Applied in §9 Tech-Stack ADR Table, §36 Alternative Architectures.
  • Anitescu & Potra, 1997, "Formulating Dynamic Multi-Rigid-Body Contact Problems with Friction as Solvable Linear Complementarity Problems" — the LCP-with-friction formulation. Applied in §36 Alternative Architectures.
  • Mirtich & Canny, 1995, "Impulse-based Simulation of Rigid Bodies" — impulse-based dynamics as an alternative to force-based constraint solving; informs the CCD re-integration step. Applied in §22 Phase 10.
  • Guendelman, Bridson & Fedkiw, SIGGRAPH 2003, "Nonconvex Rigid Bodies with Stacking" — stacking stability for nonconvex geometry, informing the island/stack acceptance criteria. Applied in §21 Phase 9.
  • Lin & Canny, 1991, "A Fast Algorithm for Incremental Distance Calculation" — closest-feature tracking, the studied alternative to per-frame GJK. Applied in §18 Phase 6.
  • Gottschalk, Lin & Manocha, SIGGRAPH 1996, "OBBTree: A Hierarchical Structure for Rapid Interference Detection" — oriented-bounding-volume hierarchy construction, informing the dynamic BVH design. Applied in §15 Phase 3.

Reference Implementations

  • Box2D, src/dynamics/ (b2_contact_solver.*, b2_island.*) and src/collision/ (b2_collide_polygon.*) — lesson: the NGS position-solver split from the velocity solver, and canonical contact-ordering by body index. Pitfall not to copy: Box2D is 2D-only; do not port its manifold clipping code as-is into a 3D SAT routine without re-deriving the reference-face/incident-face selection in 3D. Applied in §19 Phase 7, §21 Phase 9.
  • Bullet, src/BulletCollision/NarrowPhaseCollision/ (btGjkEpaSolver3.*, btPersistentManifold.*) — lesson: manifold persistence via feature-id matching with a maximum point count and worst-point-replacement heuristic. Pitfall: Bullet's GJK has documented convergence issues at extreme scale (§11 Real Engine Bugs lineage) — do not copy its scale-dependent epsilon without documenting the scene scale it assumes. Applied in §18 Phase 6.
  • Chipmunk2D — lesson: the "arbiter" as a first-class persistent-contact object is a clean way to model manifold persistence explicitly rather than implicitly. Pitfall: its 2D-only manifold clipping does not generalize directly to 3D face clipping. Applied in §17 Phase 5.
  • Rapier — lesson: a modern Rust engine's island/parallelism split, useful as the parallelism target this Python engine explicitly does not reach in its core phases (ADR-20). Pitfall: do not assume Rapier's zero-cost abstractions translate to Python; the GIL constraint is real and documented in ADR-20. Applied in §15 Phase 3, §33 Comparative Architecture.
  • PyBullet — lesson: a Python binding's query-interface ergonomics (raycast, overlap) as the shape of this engine's own RaycastHit/query API. Pitfall: PyBullet wraps a C++ core for speed; this engine's pure-Python core will not match its throughput, and §30 must not pretend otherwise. Applied in §23 Phase 11.
  • MuJoCo — lesson: a fully implicit, convex-optimization contact model as the alternative-architecture case study. Applied in §36 Alternative Architectures.

RFCs / Specifications

  • IEEE 754 (binary64 floating point) — governs every arithmetic operation in this engine and is the basis of the same-platform determinism guarantee (§4). Applied in §4 System Constraints, §27 Compatibility & Versioning.

Constraint Force Mixing / Error Reduction Parameter; the soft-constraint alternative to Baumgarte (Catto 2011, §8).
Conservative advancement
the TOI-search procedure used for CCD (§8, Mirtich & Canny 1995).
Contact manifold
the set of persisted contact points between two bodies (§8, §11).

the |impulse_t| ≤ μ·impulse_n friction law (§8).
Degrees of freedom (DOF)
the independent motion parameters a constraint restricts or leaves free (§22).
Determinism
bit-exact reproduction given identical (state, dt, ordering) on one platform (§4, ADR-30).
Distance joint
a joint preserving a fixed anchor-to-anchor length (§11).

the scalar mass term reducing a constraint to a 1-DOF impulse problem (§8).

Expanding Polytope Algorithm; penetration-depth computation from a GJK simplex (§8, §18).
Feature-id
a stable identifier for the geometric feature underlying a contact point, used for manifold matching (§8, P4).
Friction cone
the exact Coulomb bound; approximated here by a pyramid (§8, ADR-14).
GJK
Gilbert-Johnson-Keerthi distance algorithm (§8, §18).
Inertia tensor
the 3×3 matrix mapping angular acceleration to torque (§8).
Island
a connected component of contact/joint-linked bodies, solved independently (§8, §21).
Jacobian
(constraint) — the matrix relating body velocities to a constraint's rate of change (§8).
LCP
Linear Complementarity Problem; the mathematical formulation underlying contact resolution (§8, §36).
Manifold persistence
carrying contact points and their impulses across frames by feature-id (§8, P4).
Minkowski difference
the set {a - b : a∈A, b∈B}, whose containment of the origin indicates overlap (§8, §18).
Narrow phase
the exact per-pair collision-detection stage (§8).
NGS
Nonlinear Gauss-Seidel; this engine's position-correction pass (ADR-13, §21).
Penetration slop
the tolerated residual penetration below which correction stops (I2, §4).
Projected Gauss-Seidel (PGS)
the iterative method underlying sequential impulses (§8, ADR-10).
ProxyId
a broad-phase-internal handle for a tracked AABB (§11).
Quaternion drift
numerical departure of a quaternion's norm from 1.0 under integration (§8, I7).
Resting contact
a contact with near-zero approach velocity, triggering restitution's special case (§8, ADR-15).
Restitution
the bounciness coefficient governing post-collision separation velocity (§8).
Revolute joint
a hinge joint, one free rotational DOF (§11, §22).
SAT
Separating Axis Theorem (§8, §17).
Semi-implicit Euler
the integrator using post-update velocity for the position step (§8, P10, ADR-05).
Sequential impulse
the per-constraint iterative impulse solver (§8, ADR-10).
Simplex
a 1–4 point set (point/line/triangle/tetrahedron) used by GJK (§8, §18).
Sleeping
suspending integration/solving for a settled island (§8, P8, I14).
Spatial hash
the uniform-cell broad-phase structure, this engine's initial backend (§8, ADR-07).
StepStats
the per-step telemetry record (§11, §26).
Support function
the direction → extreme point contract every convex shape implements (§8, P6).
Symplectic integrator
an integrator approximately preserving a Hamiltonian system's structure, bounding energy drift (§8, Hairer/Lubich/Wanner).
Time of impact (TOI)
the fractional-step time at which two swept shapes first touch (§8, §22).
Warm starting
applying the previous step's accumulated impulse as this step's initial guess (§8, P4, ADR-12).
Weld joint
a joint locking all six relative DOF between two bodies (§11, §22).
Witness point
a point on a shape's surface identified by GJK/EPA as the closest/deepest feature (§8, §18).
XPBD
Extended Position-Based Dynamics; the position-space alternative studied in §36.
