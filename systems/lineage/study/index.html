34. Mental Models & First Principles

# Insight Predicts Bug Class Without It Resolving §
MM1 A manifest is a claim, not a fact — verification converts a claim into a fact Never render "verified" UI state without a completed signature+replay+schema check Trusting an unverified kind field as if it were checked §8, §18
MM2 Influence is a distribution, not a boolean coverage_score is always < 1 in sampled mode; top-k is a lossy summary Treating the top-1 shard as the sole cause of a generation §8, §14
MM3 Canonicalisation is the precondition for signing, not a formatting nicety Two semantically-identical manifests must canonicalise byte-identically or every signature breaks Signature verification flaking under key-ordering differences §11, §12, §13
MM4 A verifier is an oracle you must not blindly trust — it is itself a trust domain A local signature check must remain possible without the verifier A single point of forgeable trust (T7) §6 (P2), §26
MM5 A sampling strategy trades fidelity for tractability along a knob the manifest must disclose coverage_score must always accompany a sampled kind Silently treating a sampled trace as a full one §8, §14
MM6 A protocol's moat is adoption cost, not cleverness The spec must be adoptable unilaterally by one lab Designing features only a multi-lab coalition could use §2, §35
MM7 Cryptographic agility is a versioning problem, not a crypto problem The algorithm field must be signed material itself, else a downgrade is possible Assuming Ed25519 forever and hard-coding it past the schema layer §7 (I-list), §9 (ADR-05)
MM8 Revocation is a timeline, not a boolean — a shard's past manifests remain historically valid revoked_after does not retroactively invalidate a signature Breaking P6's no-retroactive-breaking-changes guarantee on revocation §8.7, §27

Required reading (§5)

Books

  • Moreau, L. & Groth, P. — Provenance: An Introduction to PROV (Morgan & Claypool, 2013). Read: Ch. 2–4 (the PROV-DM entity/activity/agent model). Extract: the vocabulary Lineage's InfluenceVector/TrainingShardRef model specialises. Applied in §8 (Domain Model).
  • Kleppmann, M. — Designing Data-Intensive Applications (O'Reilly, 2017). Read: Ch. 5 (replication), Ch. 9 (consistency/consensus). Extract: replica-consistency reasoning for the verifier's Postgres/Redis state. Applied in §18–§19 (Phase 6–7).
  • Ferguson, N., Schneier, B., Kohno, T. — Cryptography Engineering (Wiley, 2010). Read: Ch. 4 (hash functions), Ch. 12 (key negotiation). Extract: signature-suite agility and key rotation discipline. Applied in §13 (Phase 1).
  • Wong, D. — Real-World Cryptography (Manning, 2021). Read: Ch. 3 (asymmetric crypto), Ch. 13 (zero-knowledge proofs). Extract: Ed25519 operational trade-offs; ZK proof-system selection criteria. Applied in §13, §22 (Phase 1, Phase 10).
  • Petrov, A. — Database Internals, Vol. 1 (O'Reilly, 2019). Read: Ch. 7 (B-tree storage), Ch. 11 (replication). Extract: verifier registry storage-engine trade-offs. Applied in §18 (Phase 6, ADR-06).
  • Beyer, B., Jones, C., Petoff, J., Murphy, N. (eds.) — Site Reliability Engineering (O'Reilly, 2016). Read: Ch. 4 (SLOs), Ch. 6 (monitoring). Extract: error-budget discipline. Applied in §28 (Operational Readiness).
  • Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y., Courville, A. — Deep Learning (MIT Press, 2016). Read: Ch. 8 (optimisation, gradients). Extract: the gradient machinery TRAK and influence functions are built on. Applied in §14 (Phase 2).

Papers

  • Koh, P.W. & Liang, P. (2017). Understanding Black-Box Predictions via Influence Functions. ICML. Applied in §8, §14 — the original per-example influence-function formulation.
  • Park, S.M., Georgiev, K., Ilyas, A., Leclerc, G., Madry, A. (2023). TRAK: Attributing Model Behavior at Scale. ICML. Applied in §9 (ADR-09), §14 — the v1.0 default method.
  • Ilyas, A., Park, S.M., Engstrom, L., Leclerc, G., Madry, A. (2022). Datamodels: Predicting Predictions from Training Data. ICML. Applied in §8 — an alternate declared influence_method.
  • Grosse, R. et al. (2023, Anthropic). Studying Large Language Model Generalization with Influence Functions. arXiv:2308.03296. Applied in §14, §27 — IF at production scale, and its documented failure modes.
  • George, T., Laurent, C., Bouthillier, X., Ballas, N., Vincent, P. (2018). Fast Approximate Natural Gradient Descent in a Kronecker-Factored Eigenbasis. NeurIPS. Applied in §8 — if_ekfac, one of the declared InfluenceMethod values.
  • Bernstein, D.J., Duif, N., Lange, T., Schwabe, P., Yang, B.-Y. (2012). High-speed high-security signatures. J. Cryptographic Engineering 2(2). Applied in §9 (ADR-04), §13 — Ed25519.
  • Groth, J. (2016). On the Size of Pairing-Based Non-interactive Arguments. EUROCRYPT. Applied in §9 (ADR-12), §22 — Groth16, the Phase-10 default proof system.
  • Green, T.J., Karvounarakis, G., Tannen, V. (2007). Provenance Semirings. PODS. Applied in §8 — the algebraic model for combining influence contributions.
  • Cui, Y., Widom, J., Wiener, J. (2000). Tracing the Lineage of View Data in a Warehousing Environment. ACM TODS 25(2). Applied in §8 — the classical data-lineage problem this protocol generalises to model outputs.
  • Moreau, L. et al. (2015). PROV-DM: The PROV Data Model. W3C Recommendation. Applied in §8, §11 — the entity/activity/agent vocabulary underlying the schema.

Reference Implementations

  • MIT TRAK (github.com/MadryLab/trak) — dirs: trak/. Lesson: efficient random-projection Jacobian estimation. Pitfall: assumes per-example gradient access; does not itself shard-fingerprint or sign — Lineage's Phase 2 wraps it. Applied in §14.
  • OpenLineage / Marquez (github.com/OpenLineage/OpenLineage, github.com/MarquezProject/marquez) — dirs: spec/, api/. Lesson: event-based lineage metadata model at job/dataset granularity. Pitfall: not cryptographically signed, not designed for per-token attribution. Applied in §35 (Comparative Architecture Table).
  • C2PA reference implementation (github.com/contentauth/c2pa-rs) — dirs: sdk/src/claim.rs, sdk/src/manifest.rs. Lesson: signed claim/assertion manifest structure for media. Pitfall: single-author asset model does not generalise to per-token model output. Applied in §35.
  • Sigstore / cosign (github.com/sigstore/cosign) — dirs: cmd/cosign/cli/sign/. Lesson: practical signing + transparency-log (Rekor) pattern for supply-chain provenance. Pitfall: transparency-log write-amplification at very high issuance rates. Applied in §9 (ADR-04), §35.
  • DataHub (github.com/datahub-project/datahub) — dirs: metadata-models/. Lesson: metadata graph + lineage edges at schema/column granularity. Pitfall: eventual-consistency lineage graph unsuited to per-request cryptographic verification. Applied in §35.
  • Apache Atlas (github.com/apache/atlas) — dirs: repository/src/main/java/.../lineage. Lesson: type-system-driven lineage classification. Pitfall: JanusGraph backend heavier than the verify-latency budget (§4) tolerates. Applied in §9 (ADR-06), §35.

Glossary (§40) · 43 terms · site-wide glossary

Attestation Registry
public registry of issuer DIDs, public keys, and key-history log.
ADR
Architecture Decision Record; this document's §9 table, ADR-01…ADR-24.
BLAKE3
the hash function used for shard fingerprinting (I9, ADR-11).
Canonicalisation
the deterministic transform (URDNA2015) from a manifest to signable bytes (P13, MM3).
Coverage Score
real number in [0,1]; the L1-share of top-k influence mass relative to the full distribution (I3).
DID
Decentralised Identifier (W3C); Lineage uses did:web by default.
Domain Separation
cryptographic technique ensuring a hash computed for one context (e.g. one shard_set) cannot be confused with a hash from another (I9).
Downgrade Attack
forcing use of a deprecated or weaker cryptographic primitive (T11).
Ed25519
the default v1.0 signature algorithm (ADR-04, Bernstein et al. 2012).
FD Budget
the file-descriptor ceiling per verifier instance (§4).
Groth16
the zero-knowledge proving system used for selective disclosure v1.0 (ADR-12).
Hover-UI
the client library rendering per-sentence provenance on hover (§20).
Influence Function
a technique estimating the change in model output from upweighting/removing a training example (Koh & Liang 2017, §5).
Influence Tracer
the model-side instrumentation producing InfluenceVectors (§14, §11).
InfluenceVector
the per-output-span schema element listing top-k influencing shards (§11).
Invariant
one of the 13 I1…I13 correctness properties enforced end-to-end (§7).
Issuer
the producing entity (lab, hosted-inference operator) signing a manifest.
Lineage Manifest
the signed JSON-LD document attesting provenance for an output (§8).
Model Prior
attribution category for influence not traceable to a specific shard, retrieval document, or system prompt.
Nonce
a 128-bit random value bound to one (issuer, nonce) pair, defending against replay (I5).
P50 / P95 / P99
latency percentiles used throughout §4 and §32's targets.
Principle
one of the 13 P1…P13 design principles (§6).
PROV / PROV-DM
the W3C provenance data model (Moreau et al. 2015) underlying the domain model's entity/activity vocabulary (§8).
Quorum Verification
chaining ≥2 independent verifiers and requiring agreement before trusting a verdict (§19, MM4).
Replay Cache
the verifier-side store of seen (issuer, nonce) pairs (I5, §18).
Royalty Clearinghouse
the Phase 12 service converting verified influence counts into per-shard royalty events (§24).
RTO
Recovery Time Objective; ≤ 15 min for verifier region failover (§4).
Selective Disclosure
proving influence without revealing the underlying shard, via a zero-knowledge proof (§22, P4).
Semiring Provenance
the algebraic model (Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen 2007) for composing multi-source influence contributions (§8.6).
Shard ID
the BLAKE3-256, domain-separated fingerprint of a training shard (I9).
ShardRevocation
the lifecycle event flagging a shard revoked_after a given time without invalidating past manifests (I13, §8.7).
Signed Attestation
the cryptographic block over the canonicalised manifest (§11).
SLO
Service Level Objective; e.g. 99.9% verifier availability (§4, §28).
TG-VLM/wk
tokens generated per week with a verifiable Lineage manifest attached; the protocol's adoption metric.
Tracer Conformance Suite
the mandatory, binary-pass test pack every conformant tracer must clear (§15).
Training Shard
an addressable, fingerprinted unit of training data.
TrainingShardRef
the schema element identifying a shard, its licence, and visibility (§11).
TRAK
Tracing with the Randomly-Projected After Kernel (Park et al. 2023); the v1.0 default InfluenceMethod.
Two-Stage Retrieval Strategy
the default sampling approach for kind:"sampled" manifests: coarse retrieval then fine influence (§14, ADR-10).
URDNA2015
the W3C RDF dataset canonicalisation algorithm used prior to signing (ADR-03).
Verification Simulator
the deterministic harness exercising one assertion per invariant (§7's debugging methodology, §30).
Verifier Service
the hosted/self-hostable endpoint validating signatures and dereferencing shards (§18–§19).
ZkInfluenceProof
the schema element carrying a zero-knowledge selective-disclosure proof (§11, §22).
