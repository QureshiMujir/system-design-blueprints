Hard questions (§35)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does serving a fetch above the high watermark violate linearizability even though the record genuinely exists on the leader's disk? (§8.5, §7 I2/I4)
  2. Under what exact ISR/min.insync.replicas configuration does acks=all degrade to the same RPO as acks=1, and how would an operator detect they were in that state before an incident? (§8.7, §18.9)
  3. Why is leader-epoch-based truncation immune to a class of bugs that timestamp-based truncation is not, given that both are trying to answer "how far back do these two logs agree"? (§8.6, §32 MM4)
  4. What specific invariant would break if HW were computed over all replicas instead of only ISR members, and why would the failure be a latency problem before it became a durability one? (§8.5, §18.6)
  5. Why does this design's compaction guarantee ("latest value per key") not imply "audit trail of every value a key ever had," and what happens to a consumer that assumed otherwise? (§8.10, §32 MM6)
  6. Trace exactly how a producer's idempotence guarantee (I5) and a consumer group's generation check (I11) are structurally the same defense (bounded-window deduplication keyed by an identity+sequence pair) applied to two different actors. (§8.8, §8.9)
  7. Why does the controller's separation from the data plane (P8) mean a controller-quorum outage is a bounded degradation rather than a full outage, and what specifically remains available during it? (§20.9, §25 FM-11)
  8. What would have to be true about a workload for per-partition Raft (§36) to produce lower tail latency than this design's ISR/HW scheme, given that Raft adds a round trip this design avoids? (§9 ADR-12, §36)
  9. Why is "the log is the database" (Aurora, Verbitski et al. 2017) a different claim than "this broker's log is a database," and where exactly does this system's model stop short of Aurora's? (§32 MM1, §33)
  10. Explain why a decompression bomb (T3) is simultaneously a security threat and a performance pitfall, and why the same mitigation addresses both. (§24, §13.6)
  11. Why does zero-copy fetch (P11) being "defeated by TLS" not mean TLS should be avoided, and what is the actual engineering answer? (§16.7, §23.7, §32 MM8)
  12. Given the capacity model's finding that partition count, not total bytes, binds first (§31, §34), what two independent scaling levers does an operator have before hitting that ceiling, and which one does this blueprint recommend reaching for first? (§31, §37)
  13. Why does an unclean leader election "lose" data that was already acknowledged, and in what precise sense was that data never actually as durable as the acknowledgement implied? (§8.13, §32 MM5)
  14. Why is a rebalance storm (FM-5) a symptom of a configuration choice rather than a bug, and what does that imply about how it should be fixed versus how a log-divergence bug (FM-3) should be fixed? (§25, §21.6)
  15. How does the metadata log's own use of the log-as-primitive principle (P1, ADR-16) let the controller be verified with the same tooling (§28's property tests) used for the data plane, rather than needing a bespoke correctness framework? (§20.2, §28)
  16. Why must a Jepsen-style test exist as a named deliverable rather than being subsumed into "more unit tests," given what §7's four fault-requiring invariants actually require to falsify? (§7, §28)
  17. What is the exact difference between what min.insync.replicas bounds and what replication factor bounds, and why does confusing them lead an operator to overestimate durability? (§8.7, §4)
  18. Why does tiered storage (§37) not change any of §7's invariants, even though it fundamentally changes where bytes physically live? (§31, §37)
  19. Trace why the transaction protocol's two-phase control-record mechanism (§8.8) avoids holding a cross-partition lock, and what would happen to producer throughput if it did. (§8.8, P13)
  20. Why is "consumer lag" and "under-replicated partitions" the specific pair of metrics singled out as the two alerts that matter, rather than, say, raw throughput or CPU? (§26, §18.10, §21.10)

32. Mental Models & First Principles

MM1. A log is a queue that forgot to delete, and that forgetting is what buys replay, fan-out, and time travel. Predicts: any consumer can rewind to any retained offset at zero marginal server cost. Bug class without it: building server-side per-consumer delivery state (reinventing a queue) because the engineer assumes "consumed" must mean "removed." Resolved in §1, §8.10 (Kreps 2013).

MM2. The high watermark is a promise about what cannot be un-said — reads stop there because durability is not yet decided above it. Predicts: consumers reading up to LEO instead of HW will eventually observe a record that unclean failover erases. Bug class without it: treating LEO and HW as interchangeable "current position" numbers. Resolved in §8.5, §7 (I2, I4).

MM3. Sequential disk access beats random memory access at scale, which is why this design fights for the page cache instead of building a cache. Predicts: a user-space cache re-implementing what the OS already does will underperform, not outperform, a well-sized page cache. Bug class without it: engineers build a custom LRU cache "for control" and find it strictly slower once measured. Resolved in §3, §9 (ADR-06), §30.

MM4. Leader epoch is a fence, not a clock — it answers "which history are we in," never "when did this happen." Predicts: any truncation logic keyed on timestamps instead of epochs will misbehave under clock skew. Bug class without it: the historic Kafka log-divergence bug class. Resolved in §8.6, §19 (P5).

MM5. Durability is a purchase, not a default — acks and min.insync.replicas are the receipt. Predicts: an operator who never reads the receipt will eventually be surprised by an RPO they didn't choose. Bug class without it: silent acks=1 deployments treated as "basically durable." Resolved in §4, §8.7 (P3).

MM6. Compaction is a promise about keys, not about history — it never claims to remember what a value used to be. Predicts: using a compacted topic as an audit log of every historical value will silently lose intermediate states. Bug class without it: assuming compaction is "just retention with extra steps." Resolved in §8.10, §22 (P7).

MM7. A consumer group's rebalance protocol is a distributed lock service in disguise — one partition, one owner, enforced by generation, not by trust. Predicts: any offset commit or processing logic that doesn't check its generation id will eventually double-process or skip data across a rebalance boundary. Bug class without it: the exact FM-9 offset-commit race. Resolved in §8.9, §21 (I11).

MM8. Zero-copy is a chain, and a chain breaks at its weakest interposed layer. Predicts: adding TLS, compression re-encoding, or any userspace transform anywhere on the fetch path silently reintroduces the copy the whole storage design was built to avoid. Bug class without it: "why did throughput halve after we enabled TLS" incidents. Resolved in §16, §23 (P11).

Reference Implementations

  • Apache Kafka — core/src/main/scala/kafka/log/ (segment/log manager), core/src/main/scala/kafka/cluster/Partition.scala (ISR/HW/leader logic), clients/src/main/java/org/apache/kafka/common/record/ (record-batch format). Lesson: canonical ISR/HW state machine and record-batch layout. Pitfall: the legacy ZooKeeper controller carries split-brain edge cases KIP-500 exists to retire — do not resurrect them. Applied in §8, §17, §18, §20.
  • Redpanda — thread-per-core storage engine, per-partition Raft replication. Lesson: replacing ISR/HW with per-partition Raft for synchronous quorum replication. Pitfall: full Kafka-protocol drop-in compatibility is an enormous surface — do not attempt it in an educational build. Applied in §17, §36.
  • Apache Pulsar — segment-oriented storage split from serving brokers (Apache BookKeeper). Lesson: decoupling compute (broker) from storage (bookie ledgers). Pitfall: the added coordination hop and two-clustered-systems operational burden. Applied in §33, §36.
  • NATS JetStream — streaming layer atop NATS core, R3 Raft groups per stream. Lesson: a simpler at-least-once streaming model. Pitfall: weaker offset/consumer-group semantics for high-fan-out CDC use cases. Applied in §33.
  • RabbitMQ Streams — log-structured extension to a broker-managed-queue system. Lesson: retrofitting log semantics onto a per-message-ack broker. Pitfall: running two delivery models (classic queues + streams) in one broker doubles protocol/operational complexity. Applied in §33, §36.

Glossary (§38) · 56 terms · site-wide glossary

Topic
a named, configured collection of ordered partitions.
Partition
the unit of parallelism, ordering, and replication; an append-only offset-addressed log.
Offset
a monotonic integer address of a record within a partition (P1).
Log-end offset (LEO)
the next offset a replica will write; its private write-progress pointer.
High watermark (HW)
the highest offset replicated to every ISR member; the fetch visibility boundary (P4).
Log- start offset
the lowest offset still retained; advances as retention deletes segments.
Segment
a bounded-size append-only file plus its index files; the retention/compaction unit.
Sparse index
an index recording one entry per configured byte interval rather than per record, trading lookup precision for memory (ADR-05).
Offset index / time index
the two sparse index files mapping offset→byte-position and timestamp→offset respectively.
Record batch
the wire/storage unit containing one or more records plus a shared header, CRC, and compression (§8.1).
Magic byte
the record-batch format version field.
Tombstone
a record with a null value, marking a key for eventual removal under compaction.
Leader
the replica accepting writes for a partition.
Follower
a replica replicating from the leader.
Replica
any broker hosting a copy (leader or follower) of a partition.
In-sync replica (ISR)
a replica caught up within the configured lag bound; membership in the durability contract (§8.5).
ISR shrink / expand
eviction/re-admission of a replica from the ISR set.
Under-replicated partition
a partition whose ISR size is below its replication factor; the primary alerting signal (§26).
Leader epoch
a monotonic counter fencing stale leaders and resolving log divergence (P5).
Truncation
a follower discarding log tail beyond the point it and the new leader agree on.
Unclean leader election
electing a leader from outside the last known ISR, trading availability for possible data loss (§8.13).
Acks
the producer-selected acknowledgement level (0/1/all) defining the durability contract (P3).
min.insync.replicas
the minimum ISR size required for an acks=all write to succeed.
Idempotent producer
a producer whose retries are deduplicated by sequence number, never producing duplicates (P6).
Producer epoch
fences a producer's stale/zombie incarnation.
Sequence number
per-partition, per-producer-epoch monotonic counter used for dedup (I5).
Transaction coordinator
the broker role driving a transaction's two-phase commit.
Control record
a marker record signaling a transaction's commit/abort to a partition's consumers.
Consumer group
a set of consumer instances cooperatively consuming a topic, one partition owned by at most one member at a time.
Group coordinator
the broker role managing a group's membership and offsets.
Generation
a monotonically increasing id for a group's current membership epoch, gating offset commits (I11).
Rebalance
the protocol re-assigning partitions among group members on membership change.
Cooperative rebalance
an incremental rebalance variant moving only affected partitions (KIP-429).
Session timeout
the heartbeat-miss duration after which a member is considered dead.
Offset commit
a consumer's durable record of its next-to-read position per partition.
Consumer lag
the gap between a partition's high watermark and a consumer group's committed offset; the second headline alert (§26).
Retention
the time/size policy governing segment deletion, independent of consumer position (I12).
Compaction
background rewriting that retains only the latest record per key (P7).
Dirty ratio
the fraction of a partition's bytes written since the last compaction pass; the compaction trigger.
Zero copy
transferring data from page cache to socket without a userspace buffer copy (sendfile/splice, P11).
Page cache
the OS's cache of recently-accessed file bytes; this design's sole read cache (ADR-06).
Tiered storage
archiving cold segments to object storage, deferred in this blueprint (§37).
Quota
a per-principal byte-rate budget enforced by response delay (P12).
Throttle time
the delay imposed on a quota-exceeding client's response.
Controller
the cluster role owning partition assignment, leader election, and ISR bookkeeping via a replicated metadata log (P8).
Metadata log
the controller's own replicated event log of cluster state (ADR-16).
KRaft
the self-managed metadata-Raft design (KIP-500) replacing an external coordination service.
Produce/fetch purgatory
the parking structure holding requests pending a condition (HW advance, timeout) before responding.
Long poll
a fetch that blocks up to a bound waiting for data rather than returning immediately empty.
CRC-32C
the Castagnoli CRC variant (RFC 3385) used for record-batch integrity (ADR-09).
Varint
a variable-length integer encoding used throughout the record layout (§8.1.1).
Compression codec
the pluggable algorithm (gzip/Snappy/LZ4/Zstandard) compressing a record batch.
read_committed
a consumer isolation level excluding uncommitted/aborted transactional records.
Last stable offset (LSO)
the boundary up to which read_committed consumers may read, gated by open transactions.
Static membership
a consumer identifying itself with a stable instance id to survive brief disconnects without triggering a rebalance.
Rack-aware placement
spreading a partition's replicas across failure domains (racks/zones) during assignment (§20). ---
