Hard questions (§37)

Self-examination questions from the blueprint; each points at the section where the answer lives.

  1. Why does a "convolution" that omits the kernel flip pass every test built on Gaussian and box kernels yet fail on a Sobel kernel? What single fixture catches it, and what is the cost of the flip on the FFT path vs the spatial path? (§13.2/§13.6 conv-vs-corr, I2; §16 Sobel; MM1)
  2. Derive the kernel size at which FFT-conv overtakes the separable path for a 4K image, and explain why that crossover is larger than the FFT-vs-dense crossover. Which §4 number does your derivation reproduce? (§36 BoE #1/#4; §4 crossover row; MM4)
  3. A Canny edge map is 2 pixels wide in places. Is the bug in NMS, hysteresis, or the gradient stage? Describe the fixture that localizes it and the orientation-convention dependency that could also produce it. (§17.2/§17.6, I8; §16 orientation, I9; MM3/MM6)

34. Mental Models & First Principles

The compressed insights an expert holds. Each: the insight, what it predicts, the bug class you write without it, and the § where this blueprint applies it.

MM1 — Convolution is separable iff the kernel is rank-1. A 2-D kernel that factors as an outer product K = c·rᵀ (rank 1) can be applied as a row pass then a column pass. Predicts: a k×k Gaussian/box/Sobel costs O(2k) per pixel, not O(k²) — the win grows linearly with k. Bug without it: applying every kernel densely, paying k² when k of the kernels you ship are separable, then "optimizing" by lowering quality instead of exploiting structure; or detecting rank-1 with too tight a tolerance and silently diverging from the dense path. Applied: §13.2 (convolve::separable, I3), §14.2 (Gaussian), §32 (the headline perf win).

MM2 — A prefix-sum turns any rectangle reduction into four lookups. The integral (summed-area) image lets the sum over any axis-aligned window be read as S(D)−S(B)−S(C)+S(A). Predicts: a box filter (and Harris's windowed tensor, and NCC's local means) costs O(1) per pixel regardless of window size; doubling the blur radius costs nothing. Bug without it: an O(window) box filter whose runtime balloons on large windows, plus an integer-overflow bug when the running sum of u8 over a megapixel window exceeds u16. Applied: §14.2 (box_integral, I13), §21 (structure-tensor windowing).

MM3 — Gradient orientation is a single pinned convention, or every downstream stage disagrees. atan2(gy,gx) in radians on (−π,π] is one choice; swapping arguments, using degrees, or a [0,2π) range are all "correct" yet mutually incompatible. Predicts: NMS thins along the wrong axis and Hough votes the wrong angle the instant the convention drifts between producer and consumer. Bug without it: a Canny that produces edges rotated 90°, or a circle-Hough whose gradient-direction votes scatter, with no single line of code obviously wrong. Applied: §16.2 (orientation, I9), §17 (NMS), §19 (gradient-guided circle votes).

MM4 — Large-kernel convolution is multiplication in the frequency domain. The convolution theorem says spatial convolution = pointwise product of FFTs. Predicts: cost becomes O(W·H·log WH) independent of kernel size, so above a crossover (k ≳ 15 here) FFT-conv beats the spatial path; below it, FFT's constant factors lose. Bug without it: either never using FFT (paying k² on a 31×31 kernel) or always using it (paying FFT overhead on a 3×3), plus a circular-vs-linear convolution wraparound artifact when you forget to zero-pad. Applied: §13.2 (fft_conv_369, #369), §36 (the crossover number), §38 (FFT-pipeline alternative).

MM5 — The Hough transform is a duality: a point in image space is a curve in parameter space. Each edge pixel maps to the locus of all shapes through it — a sinusoid in (ρ,θ) for lines, a cone for circles — and collinear/co-circular pixels' curves intersect at one parameter point. Predicts: shapes appear as accumulator peaks; quantization (bin size) directly trades peak sharpness against merging. Bug without it: treating Hough as magic, then choosing a resolution that merges two real lines into one peak or splits one line into two, with no model of why. Applied: §18.2 (line accumulator, I6), §19.2 (circle), §37 Q on resolution.

MM6 — Hysteresis is transitive connectivity, not a threshold. Canny keeps a weak edge pixel iff it is reachable from a strong pixel through a chain of weak pixels — a flood fill over 8-connectivity, not a per-pixel test. Predicts: a single strong seed can rescue a long faint contour; raising only the high threshold fragments contours that the low threshold would have linked. Bug without it: implementing hysteresis as "keep weak if a neighbor is strong" — the classic Canny bug — yielding fragmented edges that look like a smoothing or threshold problem. Applied: §17.2 (hysteresis, I8), §27 (fragmented-edges failure class), §37.

MM7 — The structure tensor's two eigenvalues classify local geometry. M = Σw·∇I∇Iᵀ summarizes the gradient distribution in a window; both eigenvalues small ⇒ flat, one large ⇒ edge, both large ⇒ corner. Predicts: Harris (det − k·tr²) and Shi-Tomasi (min λ) are two scalar summaries of the same tensor, and the same tensor drives optical flow's aperture problem. Bug without it: tuning a corner detector by magic constants without understanding that k only shapes the det/tr² isocontours, or computing eigenvalues inconsistently from the analytic 2×2 form vs #349. Applied: §21.2 (harris/shi_tomasi), §8.6 (the tensor formula), §37.

MM8 — Geometric warps must sample backward (dst→src), never scatter forward. For each destination pixel, invert the transform to find the source coordinate and interpolate; forward-mapping each source pixel leaves holes and overlaps. Predicts: the transform must be invertible (a singular homography cannot warp), and output quality is set by the interpolation kernel, not the mapping direction. Bug without it: a forward-mapped warp full of unwritten-pixel holes and double-written seams, "fixed" by post-hoc hole-filling instead of inverse mapping. Applied: §23.2 (warp_*, I14), §8.6 (inverse-map formula), §27 (warp-holes failure class).

MM9 — Erosion and dilation are dual under complement; everything else composes from them. erode(I) == complement(dilate(complement(I))) for a symmetric SE; opening = erode∘dilate, closing = dilate∘erode, gradient/top-hat/black-hat are differences. Predicts: a single correct min/max core gives every morphological operator; a duality-test failure localizes the bug to that core or the border convention. Bug without it: implementing erode and dilate as independent code paths that disagree at borders (erode must pad with +∞/255, dilate with −∞/0), so opening is no longer idempotent. Applied: §20.2 (shared min/max core, I10), §15.2 (minmax), §37.

MM10 — You must blur before you subsample, because subsampling aliases. Downsampling samples below the Nyquist rate of the image's high-frequency content; a low-pass (Gaussian) blur first removes the frequencies that would otherwise fold into false low-frequency artifacts. Predicts: a Gaussian pyramid is anti-aliased and a naïve decimation is not; a multi-scale detector built on the latter finds phantom structure. Bug without it: a "pyramid" that just drops every other pixel, producing moiré on textured regions and unstable scale-space keypoints. Applied: §22.2 (pyr_up_down, anti-alias fixture), §38 (tiled-pipeline alternative).

Required reading (§5)

5.1 Books

  • Gonzalez & Woods, Digital Image Processing (4e) — ch.3 (spatial filtering/convolution → §13/§14), ch.9 (morphology → §20), ch.10 (edge detection, Hough, thresholding → §17/§18/§19). The primary semantics oracle for nearly every primitive here; applied across §13–§20.
  • Szeliski, Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications (2e) — ch.3 (image processing, pyramids, filters → §14/§22), ch.4 (feature detection/matching — Harris/FAST/ORB → §21), ch.8/11 (geometric transforms, homography, RANSAC → §23). The modern integrative reference; applied in §14, §21, §22, §23.
  • Forsyth & Ponce, Computer Vision: A Modern Approach — linear filters and edges (→ §14/§17), the projective-geometry chapters (→ §23). Complementary derivations applied in §14, §17, §23.
  • Hartley & Zisserman, Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision — the DLT, homography estimation, Hartley normalization, RANSAC (ch.4). The reference for the §23 (Phase 11) geometry; the normalization in §23.2 and reprojection invariant I15 come from here.
  • Burger & Burge, Principles of Digital Image Processing (Core / Advanced) — implementation-level convolution, morphology, edges. Applied in §13 (convolution/border conventions), §17 (Canny stages), §20 (morphology).
  • Davies, Computer and Machine Vision — Hough transforms (lines/circles/generalized) and practical detector engineering. Applied in §18 (line accumulator) and §19 (circle gradient method).
  • Press et al., Numerical Recipes — SVD, least-squares, bicubic interpolation. Applied in §23.2 (the #349-facing DLT/SVD numerics) and §23.2 (bicubic sampler).
  • Nixon & Aguado, Feature Extraction and Image Processing — edges, Hough, features with a from-scratch slant. Applied in §17 (edges), §18 (Hough), §21 (features).

5.2 Papers

  • Canny, "A Computational Approach to Edge Detection" (TPAMI 1986) — the Canny edge detector: the optimality criteria, NMS, hysteresis (P5 core).
  • Duda & Hart, "Use of the Hough Transformation to Detect Lines and Curves in Pictures" (CACM 1972) — the ρ-θ parameterization we implement (P6).
  • Hough, US Patent 3,069,654 (1962) — the original transform (historical context for §18; the Duda-Hart ρ-θ form we actually implement supersedes it, applied in §18.2).
  • Matas, Galambos, Kittler, "Robust Detection of Lines Using the Progressive Probabilistic Hough Transform" (CVIU 2000) — PPHT (line segments, P6).
  • Harris & Stephens, "A Combined Corner and Edge Detector" (Alvey 1988) — the structure-tensor corner response (P9).
  • Shi & Tomasi, "Good Features to Track" (CVPR 1994) — the min-eigenvalue corner measure (P9).
  • Rosten & Drummond, "Machine Learning for High-speed Corner Detection (FAST)" (ECCV 2006) — the segment-test detector (P9).
  • Rublee, Rabaud, Konolige, Bradski, "ORB: an efficient alternative to SIFT or SURF" (ICCV 2011) — oriented FAST + rotated BRIEF (P9 descriptor).
  • Calonder et al., "BRIEF: Binary Robust Independent Elementary Features" (ECCV 2010) — the binary descriptor (P9).
  • Lowe, "Distinctive Image Features from Scale-Invariant Keypoints (SIFT)" (IJCV 2004) — scale space + DoG keypoints; the Lowe ratio-test matching applied in §21.2; the scale-space substrate in §22.2 (SIFT full pipeline deferred, §39).
  • Fischler & Bolles, "Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC)" (CACM 1981) — robust model fitting (P11 homography).
  • Suzuki & Abe, "Topological Structural Analysis of Digitized Binary Images by Border Following" (CVGIP 1985) — the contour-tracing algorithm (P12).
  • Marr & Hildreth, "Theory of Edge Detection" (Proc. R. Soc. 1980) — LoG zero-crossing edges (P5 alternative).

5.3 Reference Implementations

  • OpenCV (modules/imgproc/src/{filter,smooth,canny,hough,morph,color}.cpp; modules/features2d; modules/calib3d/src/fundam.cpp findHomography) — the differential oracle, applied in every phase's N.3/N.4 and the §25 conformance suite. Study filter engines (§13/§14), Canny (§17), the Hough accumulator (§18), morphology (§20), ORB (§21), RANSAC homography (§23). Study; don't copy (BSD; we re-derive in Rust). Pitfall: heavily optimized + many border/rounding conventions — extract the algorithm + match the conventions deliberately.
  • scikit-image (skimage/filters, feature, transform, morphology) — readable Python reference for the same primitives; cleaner than OpenCV for understanding the algorithms before §13–§23. Pitfall: different defaults (we pin OpenCV's, ADR-10/25).
  • Leptonica (src/{convolve,edge,morph,rop}.c) — C image library (the #387/Tesseract substrate): morphology and connected components, applied in §20 and §24.2. Lesson: rasterop/morphology engineering.
  • CImg / Halide apps — compact reference filter implementations + the performance-DSL view of separable/tiled filtering, informing §13/§32 performance work (Halide as the fused-pipeline contrast in the Alternative Architectures section). Pitfall: Halide itself is out of scope (ADR-1).
  • VLFeat (vl/{sift,covdet}.c) — scale space + SIFT/covariant detectors; the reference for the §22.2 scale space and the deferred SIFT pipeline (§39). Pitfall: SIFT patent era (now expired) — ORB default in §21.
  • PCL / Eigen geometry — homography/affine + RANSAC reference, applied in §23 (we use #349 for the linear algebra). Pitfall: heavy templates.

Glossary (§40) · 60 terms · site-wide glossary

Anchor
the kernel/SE reference point aligned to the output pixel (usually the center).
Bilateral filter
edge-preserving smoothing weighting by both spatial and intensity distance.
Border mode
the policy for off-image pixel reads (constant/replicate/reflect/wrap).
Canny
the multi-stage edge detector: smooth → gradient → NMS → hysteresis.
Connected components
maximal sets of connected foreground pixels (4/8-connectivity).
Convolution / correlation
sliding-kernel sum; convolution flips the kernel first.
DLT
Direct Linear Transform; solves a homography from point correspondences via SVD.
Distance transform
per-pixel distance to the nearest background pixel.
Erosion / dilation
morphological min / max over a structuring element.
FAST
a high-speed corner detector using a Bresenham-circle segment test.
Gaussian / Laplacian pyramid
multi-resolution image stacks (low-pass / band-pass).
Harris / Shi-Tomasi
structure-tensor corner detectors (response / min-eigenvalue).
Homography
a 3×3 projective transform between two image planes.
Hough transform
detecting shapes by voting in a quantized parameter space.
Hysteresis
Canny's edge-linking: keep weak edges connected to strong ones.
Integral image
prefix-sum image enabling O(1) rectangle sums (box filter).
Interpolation
estimating pixel values at non-integer coordinates (nearest/bilinear/bicubic).
Non-maximum suppression (NMS)
thinning by keeping only local maxima (edges/peaks/keypoints).
ORB / BRIEF
oriented FAST + rotated binary descriptor / the binary descriptor itself.
RANSAC
robust model fitting by random sampling + inlier counting.
Separable kernel
a rank-1 kernel = outer product of a row and a column vector (O(k) apply).
Sobel / Scharr / Prewitt
discrete derivative operators for image gradients.
Structure tensor
the local gradient covariance matrix (corner/flow analysis).
Structuring element
the neighborhood shape for morphological operations.
Suzuki border following
the contour-extraction algorithm with hierarchy.
Template matching
locating a patch by sliding-window similarity (SSD/NCC).
Warp (affine/perspective)
geometric image transformation via inverse mapping + interpolation.
Aliasing
artifacts from sampling above the representable frequency (avoided by blur-before-downsample).
Band-pass
a Laplacian-pyramid level: image content between two adjacent scales.
Bresenham circle
the integer pixel ring FAST tests around a candidate corner.
Hu moments
seven translation/scale/rotation-invariant shape moments.
Inlier / outlier
a correspondence consistent / inconsistent with the fitted model (RANSAC).
Intensity centroid
a patch's intensity-weighted center, giving an ORB keypoint its orientation.
Octave
a pyramid level where the image is halved in each dimension.
Popcount / Hamming distance
the bit-count metric for binary (BRIEF/ORB) descriptors.
Ratio test (Lowe)
accept a match only if the best descriptor distance is well below the second-best.
Reflect101
the OpenCV default border mode (mirror without repeating the edge pixel).
Reprojection error
the pixel distance between a transform-mapped point and its target (homography quality).
Saturation arithmetic
clamping integer results to the dtype range instead of wrapping (pinned, ADR-32).
Scale space
the continuum of progressively blurred images for multi-scale detection.
van Herk algorithm
O(1)-per-pixel min/max over a 1-D window (fast flat-SE morphology).
Zero-crossing
the sign change in the LoG output that marks a Marr-Hildreth edge.
DoG (Derivative-of-Gaussian)
a kernel that smoothes and differentiates in a single separable pass; avoids numerical differentiation of a noisy image (§14).
Douglas-Peucker
polyline simplification by iterative farthest-point elimination; reduces contour vertex count (§24).
F32 image
pixel buffer in 32-bit float; required for signed/high-precision intermediates (gradients, bilateral, warp); tolerance-matched to OpenCV (ADR-17).
Gray8 / Gray16
single-channel 8-bit (u8) / 16-bit (u16) images; the primary pixel dtypes for most CV primitives.
Hartley normalization
translate/scale point sets to have centroid at origin and mean distance √2 before DLT; conditions the SVD to avoid near-singular A matrices (§23.2, Hartley & Zisserman).
Idempotence
open(open(I)) == open(I); holds because erosion cannot remove pixels already minimal; a self-test that needs no oracle (§20.4, I10).
im2col
the layout transformation that unfolds a convolution into a GEMM; avoids it here (P12) to prevent O(k²·W·H) memory blowup.
Inverse mapping
computing each output pixel by tracing backward through the transform to the source; the only way to avoid holes and overlaps in a warp (MM8, I14).
L2 gradient
Euclidean gradient magnitude √(gx²+gy²); the accurate measure used by Canny; the L1 option |gx|+|gy| is faster but shifts thresholds (§17.2).
LoG (Laplacian of Gaussian)
the Marr-Hildreth edge operator: smooth with a Gaussian then compute the Laplacian; edges are zero-crossings of the result (§17.2).
NCC (Normalized Cross-Correlation)
template-match score that divides by local standard deviations, making it invariant to affine intensity change; zero-variance patches are a NaN guard (§24.6).
Photometric invariance
a descriptor's resistance to illumination change; ORB/BRIEF rely on binary intensity comparisons (relative, not absolute), giving partial invariance.
PPHT (Progressive Probabilistic Hough Transform)
the Matas et al. 2000 randomized variant that samples edges rather than voting all; outputs segments (endpoints), not infinite lines (§18.2, ADR-12).
Rank-1 matrix
a matrix equal to an outer product of two vectors; the algebraic condition for kernel separability (MM1, I3).
ROI (Region of Interest)
a sub-image view; ImageView<'a,T> borrows the parent Image with zero copy, enforcing read-only aliasing (§11.1, P3).
Stride
the byte step between successive image rows; must equal or exceed width×channels; padding is common (I13 row-coord arithmetic).
Tiling
decomposing an image into fixed-size tiles (e.g. 256×256 + halo); each tile is an independent unit of parallel work and fits in cache (I11, §38 Alt 2).
Xoshiro256*\
* the seeded PRNG used for RANSAC/ORB sampling; deterministic per seed, period 2²⁵⁶−1, passes PractRand (I7, ADR-21).
