34. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  1. End-to-end lag budget = decode + transport + apply = 2 ms (M2) + 10 ms (intra-region network, §4) + 10 ms (M3, sink-db worst case) = 22 ms typical — roughly 9x headroom under the 200 ms p50 target (§4), the remainder absorbed by batching/queueing delay.

  2. Slot retained-WAL growth if the pipeline stalls = source_write_rate × avg_event_size × stall_duration. At 50,000 events/sec (M1) × 500 bytes/event = 25 MB/s WAL production. Time to the 10 GB alert threshold (§4, I10) = 10×10⁹ / 25×10⁶ = 400 s (≈6.7 min); time to the 50 GB hard-abort = 50×10⁹ / 25×10⁶ = 2,000 s (≈33 min).

  3. Snapshot wall clock = (rows × per_row_read_time) / chunk_parallelism. From the §4 target (1M rows / 90 s at 10 workers): per_row_read_time ≈ (90 s × 10) / 1,000,000 = 0.9 ms/row/worker. A 10M-row table at the same parallelism: (10,000,000 × 0.9 ms) / 10 = 900 s (15 min).

  4. Throughput ceiling from the single-writer source log = source_wal_bandwidth / avg_event_size. At a typical NVMe-backed OLTP write ceiling of ~100 MB/s: 100×10⁶ / 500 = 200,000 events/sec — 4x this pipeline's own per-connector target (M1), meaning the pipeline's decode/apply path, not the source, is the binding constraint at that hardware profile until the fleet scales out (§31).

  5. Offset-commit overhead vs lag = commit_latency / (commit_cadence_events). At the 500 ms/1,000-event cadence (§4) and 50,000 events/sec, each commit batches ≈25,000 events; at a 5 ms commit latency, per-event overhead = 5 ms / 25,000 = 0.0002 ms/event — negligible next to the 2 ms decode latency, validating the cadence choice (§16.7).

  6. Sink apply workers needed to keep up = source_rate / (batch_size / batch_latency). At the sink-db target (100 rows / 10 ms, M3): 100/0.01 = 10,000 rows/sec/worker; to sustain 50,000 events/sec (M1) requires ≥5 concurrent apply workers.

  7. DLQ growth under a poison-event rate = event_rate × poison_rate × avg_dlq_entry_size. At an isolated-bug rate of 0.01% (1/10,000) and 50,000 events/sec: 5 events/sec × 2 KB = 10 KB/s (≈864 MB/day) — comfortably inside the 5 GB/7-day budget (§4). At a systemic 1% rate (FM-10): 500 × 2 KB = 1 MB/s (≈86.4 GB/day), blowing the budget within 90 minutes — the exact reason dlq_growth_rate, not just dlq_depth, must be alertable (§21.10, §25).

  8. Memory for in-flight events under backpressure = max_in_flight_events × avg_event_footprint. At the 50,000-event/node bound (§4) and ~1 KB/event: 50,000 × 1 KB = 50 MB — well inside the 512 MB per-connector ceiling, leaving headroom for the window buffer (Phase 6) and connection-pool overhead.
