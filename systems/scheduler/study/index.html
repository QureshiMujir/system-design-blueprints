Mental Models & First Principles

MM1 — The runqueue is the policy. A scheduling discipline is fully determined by the shape of its runnable-set data structure and its extract-next rule. Predicts: you can add a policy by adding a runqueue type, touching nothing in the dispatch loop. Without it: you build a god-object Scheduler that re-derives fairness per call and regresses silently. Applied: P1, §11.3, Phases 3/5/6/10.

MM2 — Fairness is bookkeeping, not intention. "Fair" means a concrete, integer-valued ledger (vruntime) advanced in inverse proportion to weight, with the smallest balance running next. Predicts: the CPU shares equal the weight ratios, computable by hand. Without it: you hand-tune priorities and chase oscillations forever. Applied: Phase 6, §8 worked example.

MM3 — Locality is partition, not cleverness. Cache locality comes from where a task is enqueued (per-CPU, last-CPU hint), not from a smart dispatch loop. Predicts: the win is in the enqueue/steal decision, measured by L3-miss-local ratio. Without it: you optimize the dispatch loop and wonder why migrations still trash the cache. Applied: P2/P3, Phase 9.

MM4 — Preemption is a request, delivery is the OS's. Decouple "should yield" (a flag) from "made to yield" (a signal). Predicts: the common path costs a flag check; signals are rare. Without it: you signal on every preempt and live in async-signal-unsafe context. Applied: P4, Phase 8.

MM5 — Real-time guarantees are proofs, and proofs have premises. EDF/RMS guarantees hold only under the admission test's assumptions (disjoint CPU, no interloper). Predicts: a best-effort task on an RT CPU voids the proof — so forbid it. Without it: you mix pools and ship deadline misses you cannot explain. Applied: P6/ADR-11, Phase 10.

MM6 — Steal half. The victim's queue depth is the load signal; stealing half balances in O(log N) steal cycles. Predicts: a 1M-task burst on one worker rebalances within 100 ms. Without it: steal-one thrashes the deque; steal-all ping-pongs the load. Applied: P3, Phase 9 (Cilk/kernel lineage).

MM7 — The half in sched_latency/2 is load-bearing. Wake compensation must give a woken task min_vruntime − sched_latency/2, not min_vruntime. Predicts: getting it wrong lets a long-blocked task hog CPU on wake — a documented historical kernel bug. Without it: fairness fails precisely on the bursty-IO workload that matters most. Applied: I3, Phase 6.

MM8 — Bounded inversion still exists. PIP removes unbounded priority inversion but leaves inversion equal to the longest critical section. Predicts: a hard-deadline task can still miss if a held lock's section is long. Without it: you "fixed" priority inversion and are surprised by misses. Applied: I14, Phase 10.

MM9 — Everything terminates or has a deadline. Any wait on uncooperative code (STW, forced preempt) needs an escape. Predicts: a tight-FFI task will starve a STW unless the deadline aborts it. Without it: one misbehaving task wedges the whole runtime. Applied: I11, Phases 8/11.

MM10 — Time is monotone or it is a lie. Use CLOCK_MONOTONIC for every deadline and slice. Predicts: CLOCK_REALTIME jumps under NTP and parks wake early/late. Without it: you get rare, unreproducible scheduling glitches at NTP-step boundaries. Applied: Phase 7.

5. Required Reading

You will not write a correct scheduler without these. Read in order; do not skip the foundational papers.

Books

  • Tanenbaum & Bos, Modern Operating Systems, 4th ed., ch. 2. Vocabulary (runqueue, time slice, priority class, multilevel feedback) and the canonical state machine diagram. Applied in §8 (state table) and Phase 3 (MLFQ).
  • Arpaci-Dusseau & Arpaci-Dusseau, Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces, ch. 7–10. Ch. 9 (CFS) is the best short explanation of vruntime accounting and the RB-tree. Applied in Phase 6 (CFS).
  • Liu, Real-Time Systems, ch. 5–6. EDF and RMS, and the schedulability proofs. Applied in Phase 10 (schedulability.rs).
  • Herlihy & Shavit, The Art of Multiprocessor Programming, ch. 7, 9, 10. Lock-free queue and parking-lot abstractions. Applied in Phase 5 (runqueue) and Phase 7 (parker).
  • Tanenbaum & Bos, ch. 6 (deadlock). The deadlock detection that rides alongside the PIP chain walk. Applied in Phase 10 (pip.rs).
  • Drepper, What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory. Cache-line behaviour motivating the per-CPU runqueue and the 64 B TaskHeader. Applied in §11 (TaskHeader) and Phase 9 (cache-locality).

Papers / RFCs

  • Sha, Rajkumar, Lehoczky, 1990, Priority Inheritance Protocols. Priority inversion and the inheritance protocol that fixes it (and the Mars Pathfinder bug). Applied in Phase 10 (pip.rs is line-for-line this paper).
  • Frigo, Leiserson, Randall, 1998, The Implementation of the Cilk-5 Multithreaded Language. The work-stealing-deque lineage and the lazy-task-creation argument. Applied in Phase 9 (work_stealing.rs).
  • Chase & Lev, 2005, Dynamic Circular Work-Stealing Deque. The data structure shipped in chase_lev.rs; the head/tail bounds checks are load-bearing. Applied in Phase 9 (ChaseLevDeque, replacing the Phase 3 FifoQ baseline).
  • Anderson et al., 1992, Scheduler Activations. Argues kernel and userland scheduling must communicate. Applied (as a design constraint, not a mechanism — no kernel upcalls) in Phase 7 (parker).
  • Bonwick & Adams, 2001, Magazines and Vmem. The per-CPU magazine pattern in the task/stack allocator. Applied in Phase 2 (stack pool, ADR-22).
  • Boyd-Wickizer et al., 2010, An Analysis of Linux Scalability to Many Cores. Cache-line bouncing on the kernel runqueue motivates per-CPU runqueues. Applied in ADR-02 and Phase 4 (the per-CPU worker fleet).
  • Lozi et al., 2016, The Linux Scheduler: a Decade of Wasted Cores. NUMA load-balancing failure modes. Our affinity heuristics in Phase 9 are designed against them (the 1.5x overload threshold).
  • Liu & Layland, 1973, Scheduling Algorithms for Multiprogramming in a Hard-Real-Time Environment. The RMS utilization bound. Applied in Phase 10 (schedulability.rs).
  • Lehoczky, Sha, Ding, 1989, The rate monotonic scheduling algorithm: exact characterization. Response-time analysis tighter than Liu-Layland. Applied in Phase 10 (RTA admission test).

Reference implementations

  • Linux kernel source: kernel/sched/fair.c (CFS), kernel/sched/rt.c (SCHED_FIFO/RR), kernel/sched/deadline.c (EDF since 3.14), version 6.6 LTS. Reference for Phase 6 / Phase 10, and the conformance baseline.
  • Tokio source: tokio/src/runtime/scheduler/multi_thread/ (per-worker runqueues, steal protocol) and .../park.rs. Applied in Phase 9 (steal protocol) / Phase 7 (parker) engineering tricks. Version 1.38.
  • Go runtime source: runtime/proc.go (M/P/G), runtime/preempt.go (signal preemption since 1.14), runtime/runtime2.go. The preemption design reference for Phase 8. Go 1.22.
  • Cilk-5 runtime: the work-stealing deque and lazy task creation. Reference for Phase 9.
  • intrusive-collections crate: the RBTree with inline links used by CfsTree. Reference for Phase 6.

The literature on scheduling is large and ancient. If a paper after 2026 supersedes one of these for our purposes, an ADR captures the change.

Glossary · 48 terms · site-wide glossary

Admission test
the schedulability check a real-time spawn must pass before acceptance (EDF: ΣC/T ≤ 1).
Affinity
a hint or constraint on which CPU a task may run on (Any/Preferred/Pinned).
Aging
promoting a long-waiting task to a higher MLFQ class so it cannot be starved.
Best-effort (BE)
non-real-time tasks; run on the BE CPU pool under CFS/MLFQ/RR.
CAS
compare-and-swap; the atomic primitive guarding state transitions (I2).
CFS
Completely Fair Scheduler; vruntime-keyed RB-tree fairness.
Chase-Lev deque
lock-free double-ended queue for work-stealing; owner one end, stealers the other.
cgroup v2
the unified Linux control-group hierarchy; source of cpu.max/cpu.weight.
Context switch
saving one task's execution state and restoring another's.
Cooperative preemption
a task yields at its own safepoint when preempt_requested is set.
Deadline
the absolute time by which an EDF task must complete.
Dispatch loop
the worker's pop→run→re-enqueue cycle.
EDF
Earliest-Deadline-First; dispatches the nearest-deadline runnable task.
EEVDF
Earliest Eligible Virtual Deadline First; CFS's successor in recent kernels.
Effective priority
a task's priority after PIP inheritance.
Futex
Linux fast userland mutex primitive; underlies the parker.
HDR histogram
bounded-memory, lossless-quantile latency histogram.
Injection queue
the global queue for tasks spawned from outside the worker fleet.
Leftmost cache
the cached pointer to the RB-tree's lowest-vruntime node (O(1) dispatch read).
loom
deterministic concurrency-schedule explorer for Rust.
MLFQ
Multi-Level Feedback Queue; class-based scheduling with aging.
Monotonic clock
CLOCK_MONOTONIC; never jumps backward, used for all deadlines.
NUMA
Non-Uniform Memory Access; memory cost varies by socket distance.
Park / unpark
a worker sleeping on / being woken from the parker.
PIP
Priority Inheritance Protocol; prevents unbounded priority inversion.
Poll budget
the max future polls before a forced preempt check (default 128).
Preemption
forcing a running task to yield.
Priority inversion
a high-priority task blocked by a low-priority lock holder.
Quiescent
a worker state where every running task is parked at a safepoint (for STW).
Real-time (RT)
tasks with deadlines; run on the disjoint RT pool under EDF/RMS.
Refcount
the per-task reference count (runqueue + join handle + pending I/O).
RMS
Rate-Monotonic Scheduling; static priority by period (shorter → higher).
RTA
Response-Time Analysis; the tight RMS schedulability test (Lehoczky-Sha-Ding).
Runqueue
the per-worker data structure holding runnable tasks.
Safepoint
a point in code where the runtime can interrupt a task safely.
sched_latency
the CFS target window over which all runnable tasks should run once (6 ms).
SIGURG
the Linux signal claimed for forced preemption (ADR-08).
Slice
the CPU-time quantum a task may run before yielding.
Stackful / stackless
task with its own switchable stack vs a pure polled future.
Steal-half
stealing half a victim's queue to balance in O(log N) cycles.
STW
Stop-The-World; every mutator parked at a safepoint for the GC.
Treiber stack
a lock-free stack; backs the global stack-overflow pool.
vruntime
virtual runtime; CFS's weighted measure of consumed CPU.
Work-stealing
idle workers steal from busy peers' runqueues (the load balancer).
Worker
an OS thread affined to a CPU, owning a runqueue.
Weight (CFS)
the nice-derived integer that sets a task's vruntime accrual rate and CPU share (PRIO_TO_WEIGHT).
Yield point
a location in code where a task voluntarily gives up the CPU (safepoint's cooperative counterpart).
Zero-copy handoff
a stolen task's TaskRef moves ownership without copying its body; only the pointer crosses worker boundaries.
