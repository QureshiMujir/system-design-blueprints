36. Alternative Architectures (Roads Not Taken)

A. Pure upstream/ISP filtering with BCP 38 everywhere.

[Attacker] --spoofed src--X  [ISP edge router: BCP38 drop]
[Attacker] --real src------->[ISP edge router: pass]------->[Internet]-->[Victim]
  • Winning regime: a world with universal BCP 38/BCP 84 ingress-filtering adoption across all transit and access networks, which would end IP-spoofing-based attacks (SYN floods with spoofed sources, most reflection/amplification) at their origin.
  • Embodying reference: no single system embodies this at scale — it is a network-operator practice (RFC 2827/RFC 3704), partially adopted (Jonker et al. 2017 documents the incomplete-adoption reality).
  • Why rejected here: undeployable as this project's architecture because it requires universal adoption across networks this project does not control (§1 boundaries — delegates BCP 38 assumptions to the network, per ADR-17's probabilistic treatment); building a mitigation platform that assumes it would leave every non-adopting network's victims undefended.

B. Cloud always-on inline model vs on-demand BGP-diversion scrubbing center.

Always-on:  [Client]-->[Inline XDP filter, every packet]-->[Origin]
On-demand:  [Client]-->[Origin] (baseline)
            [Client]-->[BGP-diverted]-->[Scrubber]--GRE-->[Origin] (under attack)
  • Winning regime: always-on wins when the protected service cannot tolerate any activation delay (financial trading infrastructure); on-demand wins when the protected service can tolerate a convergence-time gap but must not pay inline latency tax on 100% of baseline traffic (a latency-sensitive but rarely-attacked service).
  • Embodying systems: Cloudflare (always-on XDP, §33) vs Akamai Prolexic / Arbor-Netscout (on-demand, §33) — the two dominant industry models.
  • Why not chosen exclusively: this blueprint implements both behind ADR-11 as a deployment-time policy rather than picking one, because the tradeoff is customer/workload-specific, not a correctness question (MM7).

C. ML/behavioral-classification-first approach.

[Traffic]-->[Feature extraction]-->[Trained classifier model]-->[Verdict]
                                         ^
                              [Labeled training data, retrained periodically]
  • Winning regime: an environment with continuously available, high-quality labeled attack/legitimate traffic and tolerance for periodic retraining drift, where attack diversity has outpaced hand-tuned statistical signatures (ADR-06's "when rejected choice wins" column).
  • Embodying system: none of the cited reference implementations centers this approach at production scale in a documented, publicly-verifiable way at the time of this blueprint (most published ML-DDoS-detection work is academic/research-stage, distinct from the cited operational systems in §5/§33).
  • Why rejected here: training-data drift risk, interpretability loss (a false-positive investigation needs the "Detection"/"Telemetry" evidence trail in §7, which a black-box classifier complicates), and latency of retraining relative to an adversary that adapts within a single attack campaign (P3) — the statistical + sketch combination (ADR-06 locked choice) is interpretable and retrain-free by construction.

D. Proof-of-work / client-puzzle-first line making every request costly.

[Every client]-->[Mandatory PoW puzzle, all traffic]-->[Origin]
  • Winning regime: an environment where all legitimate clients are known to have surplus compute (server-to-server API traffic, not resource-constrained mobile/IoT clients) and where the operator is willing to impose baseline latency cost on 100% of legitimate traffic in exchange for near-total bot-cost-asymmetry.
  • Embodying system: client-puzzle proposals in the DDoS-defense literature (Kaufman/Perlman/Speciner-class cryptographic-puzzle designs, §5) applied as a universal front door rather than a targeted L7 challenge.
  • Why rejected here: this blueprint uses PoW only as a targeted, escalating L7 challenge (Phase 8, I11) applied to suspect fingerprint clusters, not as a mandatory universal front door — imposing it on all traffic would violate P2 (false-positive-as-outage) for every legitimate low-power client at baseline, when no attack is even occurring.
