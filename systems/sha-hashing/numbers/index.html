32. Back-of-Envelope Capacity Model

  • CM-1 — SHA-256 scalar block rate. 400 MB/s ÷ 64 B/block = 6.25M blocks/s (§4 scalar throughput floor ÷ block size) — the basis for the 160 ns/block p50 latency in §4.
  • CM-2 — SHA-256 SHA-NI speedup factor. 2 GB/s ÷ 400 MB/s = 5× (§4 M1 ÷ M2) — the ratio this blueprint's targets imply, consistent with Gueron's SHA-NI white paper's reported 3–4× on earlier microarchitectures and higher on newer ones.
  • CM-3 — Multi-buffer per-lane throughput. 4 GB/s (M5) ÷ 4 lanes = 1 GB/s/lane — 2.5× single-thread scalar (M2), the parallelism-amortization win of ADR-07.
  • CM-4 — HMAC-SHA-256 overhead tax. 1 − (M9 1.5 GB/s ÷ M1 2 GB/s) = 25% — the double-hash structure's cost (§19.7) expressed as a fraction of bare-hash throughput.
  • CM-5 — RAM for 10,000 concurrent Sha256 instances. 10,000 × 256 B/instance (§4 memory ceiling) = 2.56 MB — dominated by the 64-byte block buffer and 8×4-byte state array.
  • CM-6 — RAM for 10,000 concurrent Keccak instances. 10,000 × 200 B (I12 state size) = 2.0 MB — smaller per-instance than SHA-256 despite the larger raw state, because Keccak carries no separate block-buffer-plus-chaining-value split.
  • CM-7 — Conformance corpus size. ~10 hash variants × ~1,000 vectors/variant (CAVP ShortMsg+LongMsg+Monte average) × ~200 B/vector ≈ 2 MB on disk (§18.2, §26).
  • CM-8 — 24-hour fuzz canary exploration volume. ~5,000 exec/s (libFuzzer overhead on a scalar SHA-256 target, ~200 ns/exec) × 86,400 s/day ≈ 4.3×10⁸ inputs explored per 24-hour canary (M11, §21.2).
